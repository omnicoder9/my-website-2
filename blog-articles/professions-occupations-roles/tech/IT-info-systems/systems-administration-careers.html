1. Scope and method

This article covers the Systems Administration family of roles, the second subgroup of Enterprise IT and Information Systems in the series taxonomy. These are the people who install, configure, secure, patch, monitor and recover the servers, operating systems, directories, collaboration platforms, endpoints, hypervisors, storage arrays and backup systems an organisation runs on. The U.S. Department of Defense's System Administrator work role captures the core of the family: "setting up and maintaining a system or specific components of a system", including installing and updating hardware and software, managing user accounts, overseeing backup and recovery, and implementing security controls.[1]

Each role is described along the series' five independent dimensions:

A caution on job titles. Government classifications have one main code for this whole family: network and computer systems administrators (SOC 15-1244). BLS notes that within it, network administrators mainly manage routers, switches and cabling, while systems administrators manage servers, desktop and mobile equipment, software, email and data storage.[2] None of the thirteen titles has its own code. O*NET lists "Systems Engineer" as a reported title under 15-1244 as well as under computer systems engineers/architects (15-1299.08), which shows how loosely the title is used.[3,4] Where this article quotes pay or growth for a role, it names the code used as the nearest proxy.

Four points stand out. First, the core occupation is projected to shrink: BLS expects employment of network and computer systems administrators to fall 4% (about 13,200 jobs) from 2025 to 2035, but still projects about 13,400 openings a year, all from replacing workers who leave.[2] BLS attributes the decline to DevOps-oriented software developers taking over some administrative tasks, outsourcing to networks-as-a-service providers, and administrators automating routine work.[2] Second, the occupations that experienced administrators commonly move into are growing: information security analysts by 21%, network architects by 8% and IT managers by 16%.[6–8] Third, the largest employers of administrators are computer systems design and related services (16%), educational services (13%), finance and insurance (9%), state and local government (8%) and information (7%).[2] Fourth, the education picture is less settled than the "bachelor's degree" headline suggests. BLS reports that some employers accept a postsecondary certificate or associate's degree,[2] and in O*NET's survey of the occupation 30% of respondents said new hires need an associate's degree, 27% a bachelor's degree and 20% a postsecondary certificate; O*NET places the occupation in Job Zone Three (medium preparation).[3]

4. Frameworks, standards and processes

Systems administration has no single governing body of practice comparable to ITIL for service management. Instead, administrators work to a mix of workforce frameworks, security baselines, continuity standards and vendor guidance. The ITIL and ISO/IEC 20000 processes described in Part 1.1 (incident, change, configuration) still apply, and administrators are usually the people executing them.[5]

4.1 Workforce frameworks: SFIA, NICE and the DCWF

SFIA 9 describes the family's core work through skills such as system software administration (SYSP), infrastructure operations (ITOP), identity and access management (IAMT), storage management (STMG) and continuity management (COPL), each defined at several of SFIA's seven levels of responsibility.[5] The NICE Framework (NIST SP 800-181 Rev. 1) provides a common language of tasks, knowledge and skills for cybersecurity work, and its task statements for this area include "Install systems and servers", "Develop group policies and access control lists", "Maintain baseline system security" and "Determine the effectiveness of data redundancy and system recovery procedures".[9,10] The DoD Cyber Workforce Framework maps this work to role 451, System Administrator, which falls in the IT skill community and the "Operate and Maintain" category; its core tasks include managing accounts, network rights and access, installing and troubleshooting systems, and maintaining baseline system security.[1,11] The DCWF career pathway defines entry, intermediate and advanced proficiency as assisting with, independently performing, and leading or approving each task, a useful model for the organizational level dimension.[1]

4.2 Hardening and configuration baselines

NIST SP 800-123 sets out the basic tasks of securing servers that deliver network services: planning, hardening the operating system, securing server software and maintaining security over time.[12] In practice administrators implement these through prescriptive baselines. The CIS Benchmarks are consensus-developed configuration guides covering operating systems, server software, cloud providers, mobile devices, network devices, desktop software and storage devices, with a separate category of benchmarks aligned to DISA STIGs.[13] Among the CIS Critical Security Controls, Control 4 (secure configuration of enterprise assets and software) and Control 5 (account management) map directly onto daily administrative work.[14,15] In NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 the corresponding controls include baseline configuration (CM-2), configuration settings (CM-6), account management (AC-2), flaw remediation (SI-2) and system backup (CP-9).[16]

4.3 Patch and vulnerability management

NIST SP 800-40 Rev. 4 frames patching as preventive maintenance and "a normal cost of operating", and recommends an enterprise-wide strategy that simplifies patching while reducing the risk of breaches and outages.[17] Platform lifecycles drive much of this work. Windows 10 reached end of support on 14 October 2025, after which only organisations paying for Extended Security Updates receive security fixes,[18] and Exchange Server 2016 and 2019 reached end of support on the same date.[19]

4.4 Privileged access and identity

Because administrators hold the most powerful credentials in an organisation, how they themselves are administered is a core competency. Microsoft's AD DS tier model separates the identity control plane (Tier 0: domain controllers, AD FS, AD CS, Microsoft Entra Connect and the systems that manage them) from enterprise servers (Tier 1) and end-user devices (Tier 2), each with its own administrative accounts and privileged access workstations, so that compromise of a lower tier cannot be used to take over a higher one.[20] Microsoft's guidance for cloud roles recommends least privilege, just-in-time access through Privileged Identity Management, multifactor authentication for all administrators, recurring access reviews, fewer than five Global Administrators, and two cloud-only emergency access accounts.[21] For authentication assurance more generally, NIST SP 800-63-4 (July 2025) superseded SP 800-63-3 and sets requirements for identity proofing, authenticators and federation.[22]

4.5 Continuity, backup and recovery

NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1 describes how to develop information system contingency plans and how they relate to wider resilience planning, and provides a business impact analysis template.[23] ISO 22301:2019 is the certifiable standard for business continuity management systems, amended in 2024 for climate action.[24] The CIS Controls make data recovery a control in its own right (Control 11), calling for practices sufficient to restore in-scope assets to a trusted, pre-incident state.[25] Ransomware has made backup isolation a central concern: the joint CISA, MS-ISAC, NSA and FBI #StopRansomware Guide advises maintaining "offline, encrypted backups of critical data" and regularly testing their availability and integrity, keeping golden images of critical systems, and keeping offline copies of infrastructure-as-code templates.[26]

4.6 Virtualisation and storage security

NIST SP 800-125 addresses the security of full virtualisation for server and desktop environments.[27] NIST SP 800-209 covers storage infrastructure, noting that the shift to networked, software-defined and cloud storage increases management complexity and the risk of configuration errors, and recommends controls for data protection, isolation, restoration assurance and encryption.[28] ISO/IEC 27040:2024 gives requirements and guidance for storage security, explicitly addressing storage administrators among its audiences.[29]

4.7 Site reliability engineering and toil

Google's Site Reliability Engineering defines toil as operational work that is "manual, repetitive, automatable" and describes a goal of keeping it below 50% of each engineer's time.[30] That framing, together with BLS's observation that automation and DevOps practice are absorbing routine administrative tasks,[2] explains why scripting and configuration-as-code now feature in almost every administrator credential discussed below.

5. Role profiles

Each profile gives a five-dimension summary, then responsibilities, skills and core competencies, tools, processes, qualifications and credentials, and career moves. "Typical" statements about level and industry are the author's synthesis of the cited sources. Unless stated otherwise, the pay proxy for every role is network and computer systems administrators (15-1244), median $99,130 in May 2025.[2]

5.1 Systems Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT infrastructure, mixed platforms
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization Generalist: servers, accounts, backups, email, storage
  • Level Entry (junior) to senior
  • Industry All; largest employers are IT services, education and finance[2]

The systems administrator is the generalist at the centre of the family. BLS describes systems administrators as managing servers, desktop and mobile equipment, software, email and data storage,[2] and the DCWF definition adds backup and recovery and the implementation of operational and technical security controls.[1] In smaller organisations one person may cover every specialism in this article; in larger ones the generalist title often denotes a junior or rotating role from which specialists emerge.

Responsibilities

  • Determine system needs and install operating system and application hardware and software; add users and assign security permissions.[2]
  • Diagnose and fix problems reported by users or by automated monitoring; evaluate and improve performance.[2]
  • Perform data backups and disaster recovery operations.[3]
  • Maintain baseline system security according to organisational policy, and conduct functional and connectivity testing.[1]
  • Configure, monitor and maintain email applications and anti-malware software; keep logs and maintenance records.[3]

Skills and core competencies

  • Analytical, communication, multitasking and problem-solving skills, which BLS lists as important qualities.[2]
  • Server hardware, administration, security and disaster recovery, and troubleshooting (the four CompTIA Server+ domains; troubleshooting and administration together carry 58% of the weight).[33]
  • Scripting in PowerShell and Bash, both listed by O*NET as in-demand technologies for the occupation.[3]
  • SFIA: SYSP, ITOP, USUP, CFMG.[5]

Tools

  • Windows Server, Red Hat Enterprise Linux, Microsoft Active Directory, ServiceNow, Ansible, SSH, Nagios, VMware and Veritas NetBackup, all named in O*NET's technology data for 15-1244.[3]

Processes

  • Incident, change and configuration processes (see Part 1.1); patch management;[17] contingency planning and backup testing.[23]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Education: BLS gives a bachelor's degree as typical but notes some employers accept a postsecondary certificate or associate's degree.[2]
  • CompTIA Server+ (SK0-005; recommends A+ and two years' server experience), CompTIA Linux+ or Security+.[33–35]
  • A platform credential matching the employer's estate (Microsoft, Red Hat, Linux Foundation), since BLS notes employers may require certification in the products they use.[2]

Career moves

  • Up: senior administrator, systems engineer, infrastructure manager; BLS notes that administrators may move into IT management positions.[2]
  • Across: any specialist role below, security operations, cloud operations or site reliability engineering.

5.2 Linux Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, Linux servers and cloud instances
  • Function Operate, protect, some build
  • Specialization Red Hat Enterprise Linux and derivatives, Ubuntu, SUSE; shell and automation
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All; heavy in finance, telecoms, IT services and cloud-native firms

Linux administrators run Linux servers and virtual machines, whether on premises or in public cloud. The role has shifted from hand-maintained servers to fleets managed through automation, a shift visible in the Linux+ exam, where automation, orchestration and scripting now form 17% of the content.[34]

Responsibilities

  • Install and maintain systems: software from RPM and Flatpak repositories, boot targets, services, logs, scheduling, bootloaders and time synchronisation.[36]
  • Manage local storage and file systems: partitions, LVM, ext4, XFS, NFS and autofs.[36]
  • Manage users, groups, password ageing and privileged access; configure firewalld, SSH keys and SELinux.[36]
  • Automate configuration and deployment, for example with Ansible (the basis of Red Hat's RHCE).[36]

Skills and core competencies

  • Linux+ (XK0-006) domains: system management (23%), troubleshooting (22%), services and user management (20%), security (18%), and automation, orchestration and scripting (17%).[34]
  • Distribution-independent operations, networking, storage, essential commands and user management (LFCS domains).[37]
  • The Filesystem Hierarchy Standard, GNU and Unix commands, shells and scripting, and system services (LPIC-1 topics).[38]
  • SFIA: SYSP, ITOP, CFMG.[5]

Tools

  • Red Hat Enterprise Linux, Bash, Ansible, Chef, Puppet, Git, Docker and Red Hat OpenShift, all in O*NET's technology data for 15-1244.[3]
  • RHEL 10 (May 2025) introduced "image mode" for container-style operating system builds and RHEL Lightspeed, a generative AI command-line assistant.[39]

Processes

  • Configuration management and drift control, patching, hardening to CIS Benchmarks, log review.[13,17]

Qualifications and credentials

  • CompTIA Linux+ V8 (XK0-006, launched 15 July 2025; up to 90 questions in 90 minutes; 12 months' Linux server experience recommended).[34]
  • Red Hat RHCSA (EX200, a hands-on, performance-based exam now on RHEL 10), prerequisite for RHCE.[36]
  • Linux Foundation LFCS (two-hour, performance-based, distribution-independent; valid two years).[37]
  • LPI LPIC-1 (exams 101-500 and 102-500, 60 questions in 90 minutes each; valid five years).[38]

Career moves

  • Up: senior Linux engineer, platform or infrastructure engineer.
  • Across: site reliability or DevOps engineering, Kubernetes administration (CKA), cloud operations (AWS CloudOps Engineer).[40,41]

5.3 Windows Systems Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, Windows Server estates (on premises and hybrid)
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization Windows Server, Group Policy, file services, Hyper-V, PowerShell
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All; dominant in government, education, healthcare and mid-sized enterprises

Windows systems administrators run Windows Server and the services built on it. Microsoft's own role definition is now explicitly hybrid: the candidate for its Windows Server Administrator Associate certification manages Windows Server on premises and in Azure, handling identity, security, compute, networking, storage, monitoring, high availability and disaster recovery.[42]

Responsibilities

  • Deploy and manage AD DS; manage Windows Server instances and workloads in a hybrid environment; manage virtual machines.[43]
  • Implement and manage on-premises and hybrid networking, storage and file services.[43]
  • Secure Windows Server infrastructure; monitor and troubleshoot.[43]
  • Plan feature adoption and upgrades. For example, Windows Server 2025 adds an optional 32k-page AD database, hotpatching for Azure Arc-connected servers (in preview) and SMB over QUIC in Standard and Datacenter editions.[44]

Skills and core competencies

  • AD DS deployment (20–25% of AZ-802), storage and file services (15–20%) and monitoring and troubleshooting (15–20%).[43]
  • PowerShell automation, a hot technology for the occupation.[3]
  • Privileged access discipline under the AD DS tier model.[20]
  • SFIA: SYSP, ITOP, IAMT.[5]

Tools

  • Windows Admin Center, PowerShell, Azure Arc, Azure Policy, Azure Monitor, Azure Update Manager, Microsoft Defender for Identity and Microsoft Defender for Cloud.[42]
  • Hyper-V, which in Windows Server 2025 supports up to 4 PB of memory and 2,048 logical processors per host.[44]

Processes

  • Monthly patch cycles, Group Policy baselines, high availability and disaster recovery, server migration.[17,42]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Microsoft Certified: Windows Server Administrator Associate, now earned with a single exam, AZ-802 Administering Windows Server. Its predecessor exam AZ-800 retired on 30 September 2026.[42,43,45]
  • Microsoft Certified: Azure Administrator Associate (AZ-104) for hybrid roles.[46]
  • CompTIA Server+.[33]

Career moves

  • Up: senior Windows engineer, infrastructure engineer.
  • Across: Active Directory or identity administration, Azure administration, Microsoft 365 administration.

5.4 Unix Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, proprietary Unix on vendor hardware
  • Function Operate
  • Specialization IBM AIX on Power, Oracle Solaris on SPARC or x86, HP-UX; high-availability clustering
  • Level Intermediate to senior (rarely entry)
  • Industry Banking, insurance, telecoms, airlines, government and large ERP estates

Unix administrators look after certified Unix systems, a smaller and older but still business-critical segment. "UNIX" is a registered trademark of The Open Group, which certifies products against the Single UNIX Specification.[47] The platforms remain supported: IBM continues to release AIX 7.3 technology levels, with fix support for TL4 (released December 2025) scheduled until 31 December 2028,[48] and Oracle has stated Premier Support for Solaris 11 to 2031 and Extended Support to 2034.[49]

Responsibilities

  • Install, patch and upgrade the operating system on a vendor's maintenance cadence; for AIX this means tracking technology levels, service packs and end-of-fix-support dates.[48]
  • Manage capacity, logical partitions and clustering for high-availability workloads such as databases and ERP.
  • Maintain backups and run disaster recovery tests.[3]
  • Plan migrations from Unix to Linux or cloud as platforms approach end of life.

Skills and core competencies

  • POSIX shell scripting and Unix command-line fluency, transferable from Linux (LPIC-1's "GNU and Unix commands" topic).[38]
  • Vendor-specific volume management, virtualisation and service frameworks.
  • SFIA: SYSP, AVMT, CPMG.[5]

Tools

  • UNIX shell, Perl and SSH (in O*NET's technology data for 15-1244).[3]
  • Vendor management and service tools for AIX and Solaris.[48,49]

Processes

  • Lifecycle and fix-level planning, change control for production systems, availability and capacity management.[48]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Few current vendor-neutral Unix credentials exist; employers generally treat Linux credentials (LPIC-1, RHCSA) plus platform experience as the qualifying evidence.[36,38] This is the author's observation, not a published standard.

Career moves

  • Up: senior Unix engineer, platform architect.
  • Across: Linux administration and engineering, the most common path as Unix estates migrate.

5.5 Active Directory Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, on-premises directory services
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization AD DS forests and domains, Group Policy, DNS, AD CS, AD FS, hybrid sync
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All Windows-centric organisations

Active Directory administrators run Active Directory Domain Services, the identity store at the heart of most Windows networks. Microsoft classes domain controllers, AD FS, AD CS and Microsoft Entra Connect as Tier 0, the identity control plane, and lists built-in groups such as Backup Operators and Server Operators as Tier 0 accounts, so the role carries unusual security weight.[20] The 2023 rename of Azure AD to Microsoft Entra ID did not affect Windows Server Active Directory, which Microsoft continues to support and develop.[50]

Responsibilities

  • Design and maintain forests, domains, sites, replication and functional levels; Windows Server 2025 introduces a new functional level that enables a 32k database page size.[44]
  • Manage accounts, groups, delegation and Group Policy; develop group policies and access control lists.[10]
  • Keep the Tier 0 population small (Microsoft cites fewer than five Domain Admin-equivalent people as a common target) and enforce privileged access workstations.[20]
  • Operate hybrid synchronisation to Microsoft Entra ID; plan forest recovery.

Skills and core competencies

  • Deep AD DS knowledge, the largest skill area of AZ-802 (20–25%).[43]
  • Understanding of new security defaults, for example LDAP signing required by default for new Windows Server 2025 deployments and RC4 ticket-granting tickets no longer issued.[44]
  • SFIA: IAMT, SYSP, SCAD.[5]

Tools

  • Microsoft Active Directory, Windows Server and PowerShell.[3]
  • Microsoft Defender for Identity for directory threat detection.[42]

Processes

  • Access request, joiner-mover-leaver and periodic access review; privileged access management; directory backup and forest recovery.[15,21]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Microsoft Certified: Windows Server Administrator Associate (AZ-802).[43]
  • Microsoft Certified: Identity and Access Administrator Associate (SC-300) for hybrid identity.[51]

Career moves

  • Up: identity engineer, identity architect.
  • Across: identity administration, security operations, Microsoft 365 administration.

5.6 Microsoft 365 Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, software-as-a-service collaboration
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization Microsoft 365 tenant, Entra ID, Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint, Purview, Defender XDR, Copilot
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All; often the main administrative role in smaller organisations

Microsoft 365 administrators run a cloud tenant rather than servers. Microsoft describes the role as the "integrating hub" across Microsoft 365 workloads, coordinating with the people responsible for identity, security, compliance, endpoints and applications.[52] The role is being redefined around AI: Microsoft's new Microsoft 365 and AI Services Administrator Associate exam (AB-650) gives 35–40% of its weight to managing and securing AI services in Microsoft 365.[53]

Responsibilities

  • Deploy and manage a Microsoft 365 tenant; implement and manage Microsoft Entra identity and access.[54]
  • Manage security and threats with Microsoft Defender XDR, and compliance with Microsoft Purview.[54]
  • Govern and secure tenants and workloads, including AI services.[53]
  • Assign admin roles under least privilege, with Privileged Identity Management and emergency access accounts.[21]

Skills and core competencies

  • Functional experience across all Microsoft 365 workloads and Entra ID, plus working knowledge of networking, AD DS, DNS and PowerShell (Microsoft's stated prerequisites).[52]
  • Governance and security: 40–45% of AB-650.[53]
  • SFIA: SYSP, IAMT, SCAD.[5]

Tools

  • Microsoft 365 admin center, Entra admin center, Defender XDR, Purview, PowerShell.[54]

Processes

  • Role and licence assignment, access reviews, data retention and eDiscovery, incident response with security teams.[21,54]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Microsoft 365 Certified: Administrator Expert (MS-102), which requires one of four associate certifications first. Microsoft has announced that MS-102 retires on 30 November 2026.[52,54]
  • Microsoft 365 Certified: Microsoft 365 and AI Services Administrator Associate (AB-650), the newer route.[53]
  • Associate prerequisites for MS-102 include Endpoint Administrator (MD-102), Identity and Access Administrator (SC-300) and Teams Administrator.[52]

Career moves

  • Up: Microsoft 365 engineer or architect, modern workplace lead.
  • Across: identity, endpoint, compliance or security administration.

5.7 Exchange Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, messaging
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization Exchange Server, Exchange Online and hybrid; mail flow and email authentication
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry Government, defense, healthcare and other sectors that keep mail on premises; elsewhere merged into Microsoft 365 roles

Exchange administrators run Microsoft's messaging platform. The role has narrowed as mail moves to Exchange Online, but on-premises and hybrid Exchange remains common and is a frequent attack target. CISA issued emergency directives on Exchange in March 2021 and again in August 2025, the latter (ED 25-02) for a hybrid-configuration vulnerability that could allow an attacker with on-premises admin access to move into Microsoft 365.[55,56]

Responsibilities

  • Configure, monitor and maintain email applications.[3]
  • Keep servers on supported versions: Exchange Server 2016 and 2019 reached end of support on 14 October 2025, and Exchange Server Subscription Edition (SE) was released on 1 July 2025.[19,57]
  • Apply security and hotfix updates; Exchange SE is serviced through security updates and hotfix updates rather than cumulative updates.[57]
  • Manage hybrid configuration and its credentials, as ED 25-02 required of federal agencies.[56]

Skills and core competencies

  • Mail flow, transport, DNS and certificates; email authentication standards including DMARC, now a Proposed Standard as RFC 9989 (May 2026), which obsoletes RFC 7489.[58]
  • Windows Server administration (Exchange SE supports Windows Server 2019, 2022 and 2025, with Server Core recommended).[19]
  • SFIA: SYSP, ITOP, SCAD.[5]

Tools

  • Microsoft Exchange (in O*NET's technology data for 15-1244), Exchange Management Shell, Microsoft's Health Checker script.[3,56]

Processes

  • Patch and lifecycle management, migration to Exchange Online or Exchange SE, mailbox retention and recovery.[17,19]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Microsoft's dedicated messaging exam (MS-203) retired on 31 December 2023, so there is no current Exchange-specific Microsoft certification.[59]
  • Candidates typically show Exchange skill through MS-102 or AB-650 and Windows Server credentials.[43,53,54]

Career moves

  • Up: messaging architect, collaboration lead.
  • Across: Microsoft 365 administration (the most common destination), email security.

5.8 Identity Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT and cybersecurity overlap: identity and access management (IAM)
  • Function Protect, operate
  • Specialization Single sign-on, MFA, conditional access, provisioning, identity governance, privileged access
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All; most formalised in finance, healthcare and government

Identity administrators run the systems that decide who can sign in to what. Compared with an Active Directory administrator, the role is platform-neutral and spans cloud identity providers, federation and lifecycle automation. CISA lists identity management as a core competency of system administration,[11] and the work sits close to security: information security analysts (15-1212), projected to grow 21% to 2035, are a natural adjacent occupation.[6]

Responsibilities

  • Implement and manage user identities, authentication and access management, workload identities and identity governance (the four SC-300 skill areas).[51]
  • Integrate directories (AD, LDAP) and applications through SAML and OIDC single sign-on; configure MFA, lifecycle management and access policies (Okta's administrator exam topics).[60]
  • Run access reviews and just-in-time privileged access.[21]
  • Set authentication assurance in line with NIST SP 800-63-4 where required.[22]

Skills and core competencies

  • Federation protocols, authentication strength, provisioning and governance.[22,51]
  • Account management practice (CIS Control 5) and access control management (Control 6).[15,31]
  • SFIA: IAMT, SCAD.[5]

Tools

  • Microsoft Entra ID with Privileged Identity Management; Okta; Active Directory.[3,21,61]

Processes

  • Joiner-mover-leaver lifecycle, access certification, privileged access management, break-glass account control.[21]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Microsoft SC-300 (100-minute exam, renewed every 12 months by a free online assessment).[51]
  • Okta Certified Professional, then Okta Certified Administrator.[61]
  • IDPro CIDPRO, a vendor-neutral certification supported by IDPro's free Body of Knowledge.[62]

Career moves

  • Up: IAM engineer, identity architect, IAM manager.
  • Across: security engineering and governance (later parts of this series).

5.9 Endpoint Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT, end-user computing
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization Unified endpoint management: Windows, macOS, iOS, Android; app packaging and compliance
  • Level Entry–intermediate to senior
  • Industry All; scale matters most in education, healthcare, retail and government

Endpoint administrators manage the fleet of laptops, desktops and mobile devices centrally. Microsoft Intune, for example, is a cloud service that enrols, configures, secures and updates devices across Android, iOS/iPadOS, Linux, macOS and Windows, using mobile device management for whole devices or mobile application management for work apps on personal devices.[63] The role is the natural step up from desktop support (Part 1.1).

Responsibilities

  • Prepare infrastructure for devices; manage and maintain devices; protect devices; manage and secure applications.[64]
  • Optimise endpoint operations through automation, monitoring and reporting.[64]
  • Run operating system upgrades and retirements, such as moving devices off Windows 10 after its October 2025 end of support.[18]
  • Maintain the device inventory and secure configuration baselines.[14]

Skills and core competencies

  • Device enrolment, compliance and configuration policy, application deployment, patch rings.[63,64]
  • Apple device management for mixed estates.[65]
  • SFIA: SYSP, CFMG, DEPL, ASMG.[5]

Tools

  • Microsoft Intune; Jamf Pro for Apple devices.[63,65]

Processes

  • Standard changes, phased deployment, asset lifecycle, vulnerability remediation.[14,17]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Microsoft 365 Certified: Endpoint Administrator Associate (MD-102; English version updated 27 October 2026).[64]
  • Jamf Certified Associate and Jamf Certified Tech (Jamf 100 and 200 courses; the Tech badge is valid three years).[65]
  • CompTIA A+ at the entry point, as in Part 1.1.

Career moves

  • Up: endpoint engineer, end-user computing architect.
  • Across: Microsoft 365 or identity administration, security operations.

5.10 Virtualization Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT infrastructure, compute virtualisation and private cloud
  • Function Operate
  • Specialization VMware vSphere and VMware Cloud Foundation, Microsoft Hyper-V, KVM-based platforms
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All organisations with data centres; IT service providers

Virtualization administrators run the hypervisors and management layers on which most enterprise servers now live. NIST notes that full virtualisation lets several operating systems run on simulated hardware and is used to improve operational efficiency, including in cloud computing.[27] The market has been unsettled since Broadcom completed its acquisition of VMware on 22 November 2023 and focused the product line on VMware Cloud Foundation for private and hybrid cloud.[66]

Responsibilities

  • Build and operate hypervisor clusters, templates and virtual networks; manage virtual machines and containers.[42,43]
  • Plan capacity and availability across hosts and clusters.[5]
  • Secure the hypervisor and management plane, which the Microsoft tier model places alongside other systems able to control Tier 0 assets.[20,27]
  • Coordinate VM-level backup and disaster recovery.[42]

Skills and core competencies

  • Compute, storage and network virtualisation; high availability and live migration (Windows Server 2025 adds GPU partitioning with live migration).[44]
  • Platform evaluation and migration skills in a changing licensing market.[66]
  • SFIA: SYSP, CPMG, AVMT.[5]

Tools

  • VMware (in O*NET's technology data for 15-1244), Hyper-V, Windows Admin Center.[3,42,44]

Processes

  • Capacity management, host patching and lifecycle, change control for cluster-wide changes.[17]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Broadcom VMware Certified Professional tracks: VCP-VCF Administrator and VCP-VVF Administrator, with VCAP advanced certifications above them.[67]
  • Microsoft Windows Server Administrator Associate for Hyper-V estates.[42]

Career moves

  • Up: virtualisation engineer, private cloud or infrastructure architect (Part 1.3).
  • Across: cloud operations, Kubernetes platform engineering.[40,41]

5.11 Storage Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT infrastructure, storage
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization SAN and NAS arrays, file services, object storage, replication and snapshots
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry Data-heavy sectors: finance, healthcare imaging, media, research, cloud and hosting providers

Storage administrators provision and protect the capacity on which applications and backups depend. NIST SP 800-209 observes that storage has moved from direct-attached systems to networked and cloud-based access with software abstraction, which adds management complexity and raises the risk of configuration errors.[28]

Responsibilities

  • Provision and manage block, file and object storage; plan capacity and performance.[5]
  • Configure replication, snapshots and data protection; provide restoration assurance and encryption.[28]
  • Secure storage management interfaces, media and end-of-life disposal.[29]
  • Manage storage and file services on general-purpose servers.[36,43]

Skills and core competencies

  • SAN/NAS, host operating systems, data protection and high availability (NetApp's stated foundations for its administrator credential).[68]
  • Storage security concepts in ISO/IEC 27040:2024.[29]
  • SFIA: STMG, CPMG, AVMT.[5]

Tools

  • Vendor array management (for example NetApp ONTAP), Windows Server storage and file services, Linux LVM and XFS.[36,43,68]

Processes

  • Capacity management, configuration control, data protection testing, secure sanitisation.[28]

Qualifications and credentials

  • NetApp Certified Data Administrator, ONTAP (six to twelve months' ONTAP experience recommended).[68]
  • VCAP VCF Storage for VMware-centred storage.[67]

Career moves

  • Up: storage engineer, storage architect (Part 1.3).
  • Across: backup and recovery, cloud storage operations.

5.12 Backup and Recovery Administrator

  • Domain Enterprise IT infrastructure, data protection and resilience
  • Function Operate, protect
  • Specialization Backup software, immutable and offline copies, disaster recovery orchestration
  • Level Intermediate to senior
  • Industry All; mandated in finance, healthcare and energy

Backup and recovery administrators make sure data and systems can be restored. The work has moved from a routine operations task to a front-line ransomware defence: CISA's guidance stresses offline, encrypted backups that are regularly tested,[26] and several regulations now require documented and tested backups (see section 10).

Responsibilities

  • Perform data backups and disaster recovery operations.[3]
  • Maintain offline, encrypted backups and test their availability and integrity in a disaster recovery scenario; maintain golden images.[26]
  • Establish data recovery practices sufficient to restore assets to a trusted, pre-incident state.[25]
  • Contribute to contingency plans and business impact analyses.[23]

Skills and core competencies

  • Recovery objectives, backup architecture and recovery testing; continuity management.[23,24]
  • Monitoring, management and orchestration of backup platforms (the course areas required for Veeam's VMCE+).[69]
  • SFIA: STMG, COPL, ITOP.[5]

Tools

  • Veritas NetBackup (in O*NET's technology data for 15-1244); Veeam Backup & Replication, Veeam ONE and Veeam Orchestrator.[3,69]

Processes

  • Backup scheduling and verification, restore testing, disaster recovery exercises, retention management.[23,25]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Veeam: VMCE and VMCA have been retired; VMCE+ and the newer VMCSE require attendance at specified Veeam courses before the exam, and Veeam certifications do not expire.[69]
  • Continuity knowledge aligned to ISO 22301.[24]

Career moves

  • Up: data protection engineer, resilience or disaster recovery architect.
  • Across: storage administration, business continuity management, security operations.

5.13 Systems Engineer

  • Domain Enterprise IT infrastructure, design and integration
  • Function Build, operate
  • Specialization Infrastructure design, automation, integration across compute, storage, identity and cloud
  • Level Intermediate to principal
  • Industry All; also IT services and defense contractors

In enterprise IT, "systems engineer" usually means a senior, design-oriented administrator. O*NET's computer systems engineers/architects occupation "designs and develops solutions for complex application problems, system administration issues, or network concerns" and performs systems management and integration.[4] The same title has a different meaning in aerospace, defense and manufacturing, where systems engineering is the discipline certified by INCOSE (whose CSEP requires at least 60 months of systems engineering experience).[70] Job seekers should check which meaning an advert intends.

Responsibilities

  • Communicate with staff or clients to understand system requirements; evaluate existing systems and recommend improvements.[4]
  • Configure servers and maintain hardware and software; design and run hardware and software tests.[4]
  • Document system designs and train users.[4]
  • Recommend configuration changes and the hardware or software needed for them.[3]

Skills and core competencies

  • Infrastructure design, integration and automation; infrastructure as code.[5,26]
  • Cloud operations: monitoring, reliability and business continuity, deployment and automation (22% each of AWS's CloudOps Engineer exam).[41]
  • SFIA: IFDN, SINT, DESN.[5]

Tools

  • Configuration management and version control (Ansible, Chef, Puppet, Git), AWS CloudFormation, Docker, Red Hat OpenShift.[3]

Processes

  • Design review, change and release, capacity and availability planning, toil reduction.[30]

Qualifications and credentials

  • Senior platform credentials: RHCE, VCAP, AWS CloudOps Engineer – Associate (SOA-C03, which replaced SysOps Administrator – Associate in September 2025), Azure Administrator, CKA.[36,40,41,46,67,71]
  • INCOSE CSEP for the aerospace and defense sense of the title.[70]

Career moves

  • Up: infrastructure or solutions architect, principal engineer, IT manager.
  • Across: site reliability, platform or cloud engineering.

Pay proxy: computer occupations, all other (15-1299), median $116,580 in 2025, a broad group that O*NET uses for 15-1299.08.[4]

Operating systems, storage, networking, virtualisation, identity, and scripting and automation, which appear in Server+, Linux+, LFCS, RHCSA, AZ-802 and the AWS CloudOps exam.[33,34,36,37,41,43]

Hardening, least privilege, patching and recoverable backups, set out in NIST, CIS and CISA guidance.[12,13,17,21,26]

Analytical, communication, creative, multitasking and problem-solving skills, which BLS lists as important qualities for the occupation.[2]

The diagram shows four common progressions. They are the author's synthesis of the role profiles above, not a published standard.

Open-systems track

  1. IT support / junior systems administrator
  2. Linux or Unix administrator
  3. Senior Linux engineer / systems engineer
  4. Site reliability, platform or cloud engineer (other series articles)

Microsoft and identity track

  1. Desktop support / endpoint administrator
  2. Windows, AD or Microsoft 365 administrator
  3. Identity administrator / identity engineer
  4. Identity architect or security engineer

Infrastructure track

  1. Systems administrator
  2. Virtualisation, storage or backup administrator
  3. Infrastructure engineer
  4. Infrastructure architect (Part 1.3)

Management track

  1. Senior administrator / team lead
  2. Infrastructure or operations manager
  3. Head of infrastructure
  4. IT director → CIO

The labour market data support moving along these tracks. The core administrator occupation is projected to shrink 4%, while the occupations at the far end of the identity, infrastructure and management tracks are projected to grow: information security analysts 21%, network architects 8%, IT managers 16%.[2,6–8] Median pay rises from $99,130 for administrators to $129,180–$134,050 for security analysts and architects and $175,140 for IT managers, although architect and management roles typically require five or more years of related experience.[2,6–8] BLS itself notes that network administrators may advance to network architect and that administrators may move into IT management.[2]

11. Trends shaping the field

  • Automation absorbs routine work. BLS attributes the projected 4% decline partly to administrators automating routine tasks and to DevOps-oriented developers taking on administrative work.[2] Credential content reflects this: automation is 17% of Linux+, and in 2025 AWS renamed its SysOps Administrator – Associate certification to CloudOps Engineer – Associate.[34,71] The likely effect, in the author's view, is fewer purely manual administrator roles and more demand for administrators who write code.
  • Hybrid by default. Microsoft's Windows Server credential is now defined around on-premises and Azure management, with tools such as Azure Arc and Azure Update Manager.[42]
  • Identity as the control plane. Microsoft's tier model treats identity infrastructure as Tier 0, and its cloud guidance centres on least privilege and just-in-time access.[20,21] NIST SP 800-63-4 updated federal authentication guidance in 2025.[22]
  • Ransomware-resilient backup. Offline, encrypted and tested backups, golden images and recoverable infrastructure-as-code are now core recommendations from CISA, the NSA and the FBI.[26]
  • Platform churn. Windows 10 and Exchange 2016/2019 reached end of support in October 2025;[18,19] Exchange moved to a Subscription Edition;[57] and Broadcom's acquisition of VMware reshaped the leading virtualisation platform.[66] Lifecycle planning and migration are significant parts of the workload.
  • AI in administration. RHEL 10 ships a generative AI command-line assistant,[39] and Microsoft's newest Microsoft 365 administrator exam devotes over a third of its weight to managing and securing AI services.[53]

12. Limitations

Labour statistics are U.S.-specific and occupation-based, so they approximate rather than measure the titles in this family, and the 15-1299 figures used for systems engineers cover a much broader group.[2,4] Credential details change frequently. This article reflects issuer pages as of 2 November 2026, including the retirement of AZ-800 and the pending retirement of MS-102.[45,54] Some ISO and NIST SP 800-53 content is paraphrased from summaries or control catalogues. SFIA and DCWF level mappings, cluster groupings, career paths and framework crosswalks are the author's analytical synthesis and are labelled as such.
