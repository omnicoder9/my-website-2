Stylometry is usually taught as a ladder of linguistic levels: characters, words, morphemes, syntax, meaning, discourse, pragmatics. The ladder is a description of linguistics, not of measurement. This article re-describes the same seven levels by the question a working analyst actually faces, which is what has to be true before a number can exist at all. A character frequency requires that the bytes decoded correctly. A part-of-speech trigram requires that a tagger trained on somebody else's corpus was right about three tokens in a row. A sarcasm rate requires that a human being made a judgement and wrote it down.

Twenty-seven feature families are placed on an eight-step derivation scale and given a fixed set of fields: what they count, what one observation is, what machinery they need, what their variance is mostly about, and how they fail. Each is measured on a common offline corpus set with real annotation as ground truth: the Brown corpus with gold word classes, the Penn Treebank sample with gold parses, the NPS Chat corpus with gold dialogue acts, and two speech corpora with known speakers. Ten experiments run at build time and every figure in the article is drawn from their output.

The results do not support the intuition that deeper features are more powerful. They support a different claim: level and derivation depth are not the same axis, and where they come apart, the shallow feature usually wins. The last three characters of a word recover 29 per cent of the distance from a trivial baseline to a full word-class lexicon. Parse-tree depth correlates with the logarithm of sentence length at r = 0.68, so a five-layer feature carries information available at one layer. On a single attribution task, accuracy falls monotonically from 79.7 per cent at the character level to 49.2 per cent at the pragmatic level, and the ordering tracks how many countable events each level offers per thousand words rather than how much linguistic structure it encodes. What derivation depth buys is interpretation. What it costs is a longer chain of assumptions, each of which can be wrong in a way correlated with the very thing being measured.

1The question the levels do not answer

A stylometric result is a number attached to a text and a claim attached to the number. The number is almost always a rate: so many of this per thousand of that. The claim is that the rate is a property of the person who wrote the text rather than of the subject, the occasion, the medium, the editor or the software. Two centuries of work have established that such claims can be true, that they can be made to carry weight in scholarship and in court, and that they are fragile in specific, catalogued ways.12345

The claims have a history, and the history is instructive about which levels people reached for first. Mendenhall counted word lengths in 1887 because word lengths could be counted by hand.1 Ellegård built a synonym-choice profile for the Junius letters because a small set of alternations could be tabulated across a corpus.6 Morton counted sentence lengths and the positions of common particles in Greek prose.7 Mosteller and Wallace chose thirty function words for the disputed Federalist papers and modelled their rates as Poisson-like variables, which remains the single most influential design decision in the field.82 What unites these choices is not a theory of style but a practical constraint: they counted things that could be counted reliably without a machine to intervene. The constraint has been lifted, and this article is an argument for remembering what it was protecting.9101112

Underneath the whole enterprise is an empirical premise: that a person's writing carries a stable, measurable signature, a stylome, in the way a body carries a genome. The premise has been tested directly and it survives, but with a finding that is easy to skip past. When inexperienced writers were distinguished on a large feature inventory, the number of features required was an order of magnitude larger than the tradition had assumed.13 A signature exists; it is distributed across many weak indicators rather than concentrated in a few strong ones. That result is what makes observation density the governing constraint of this article, because a signal spread across many weak features can only be read from enough text.

The catalogue of features is conventionally organised by linguistic level. Characters at the bottom, then words, then morphology, syntax, semantics, discourse and pragmatics at the top. Every survey of the field uses some version of this arrangement,4141516 and the arrangement is not wrong. It corresponds to real strata of linguistic description, and it usefully predicts which features tend to be correlated with which others. But it is a taxonomy borrowed from linguistics, and it answers a linguist's question: what kind of thing is this feature about?

The analyst's question is different. Before you can count anything above the character level, something has to have decided where the words are. Before you can count a part-of-speech trigram, something has to have decided what class each of three consecutive tokens belongs to. Before you can count an instance of sarcasm, someone has to have judged that a sentence was sarcastic. Each of those decisions is made by a machine or a person that is not the author, using evidence that is not in the document, according to a scheme that was fixed for some other purpose. Each is a place where the measurement can acquire variance that has nothing to do with the writer.

So this article asks a different question of the same seven levels. Not what is this feature about, but: what has to be true before you can count it? The answer is given as a position on an eight-step derivation scale, D0 to D7, where each step is a representation that must be correct for the number to mean what it is said to mean. The scale is not a difficulty ranking and not a quality ranking. It counts interposed machinery.

Three things follow from asking the question this way, and the rest of the article is an attempt to substantiate them.

The first is that level and derivation depth are different axes. Paragraph structure is a discourse feature that needs nothing but preserved line breaks. Sentence length is a syntactic feature that needs only a sentence splitter. Suffix rates are morphological features computable from raw characters. Conversely, a great deal of what is sold as lexical analysis quietly depends on a lemmatiser, and any feature involving rare words depends on an external frequency list, which is another corpus wearing a disguise. When the two axes come apart, the shallow implementation is often the one that survives contact with real data.

The second is that the levels differ enormously in how many observations they supply. Characters arrive at about 5,300 per thousand words. Sentences arrive at about 45. Paragraphs at 1.3. Hedges at 1.9. Questions, in prepared political prose, at 0.32. Since the precision of a rate estimate scales with the square root of the number of events, this is not a minor difference in convenience. On the corpus used here, detecting a twenty per cent difference in the rate of a character-level feature takes 82 words; doing the same for the rate of questions takes over half a million. Most published pragmatic and discourse results are computed on far less text than that, and the arithmetic is rarely shown.1718

The third is that the confounds get worse as the derivation deepens, but not in the way the folklore suggests. The folklore says that errors compound: a tagger with five per cent error destroys trigram features because only 86 per cent of trigrams survive intact. The experiment below does not find that. Rate-based features are remarkably robust to unbiased annotation noise, because noise dilutes a signal without moving it. What actually damages deep features is that annotation error is not unbiased. It is a function of the domain, and the domain is exactly what a stylometric study is usually trying to hold constant.192021

The seven levels of the title are treated in the order the field lists them, and every sub-feature in the standard inventory is given a row in the register at the head of its section. What changes is the reading. Each family gets a plate, and each plate carries the same five fields and the same gauge, so that families from different levels can be laid side by side and compared on the only dimension that matters when you are deciding what to compute: the length of the chain of things that must be true.

2A scale of derivation, D0 to D7

The scale below has eight steps. Each names a representation, states what must be true of it, and names what supplies it. A feature sits at the depth of the deepest representation it requires. The step numbers are ordinal, not interval: D5 is not five times as uncertain as D1.

Two properties of the scale deserve emphasis because they are what make it useful.

It is cumulative and it is not optional. A part-of-speech feature at D4 does not merely require a tagger. It requires that the tokeniser at D1 divided the text the way the tagger's training data was divided, that the normalisation at D2 did not merge forms the tagger distinguishes, and that D0 decoding preserved the characters the tagger was trained on. Every layer inherits the failures of the layers beneath it. This is why tokenisation, which looks like a preliminary, is the single most consequential decision in most stylometric pipelines.222324

Depth is not the same as difficulty, and it is not the same as sophistication. A closed list of function words sits at D2 because it requires only that surface forms be identified and normalised. A pretrained sentence encoder sits at D6 because it requires a model with its own training corpus, its own tokenisation and its own inductive biases.2526 The list is older and cruder and its provenance is fully inspectable; the encoder is newer and stronger and its provenance is not. Neither of those facts is captured by calling one lexical and the other semantic.

A distinction cuts across the scale and is worth naming separately. Some features require a model, which is fitted to data and has an error rate that can in principle be measured. Others require an inventory, which is stipulated by somebody and has no error rate at all, only a coverage profile and a set of theoretical commitments. Function-word lists, hedge lists, connective inventories and politeness markers are all stipulated. So are sentiment lexicons, which are stipulated lists dressed in the vocabulary of measurement.2728 When such a feature behaves oddly, there is no accuracy figure to consult: you have to read the list.

The registers in the level sections mark each of the standard sub-features with its minimum depth. Where two depths are given, as in D1/D5 for clause counting, the first is the depth of the cheap surface proxy and the second the depth of the quantity the proxy is standing in for. Section 11 shows how far apart those two can be.

3How to read a plate

Every feature family in sections 5 to 11 gets a plate. A plate has a fixed set of fields, always in the same order, so that families can be compared rather than merely described.

  • counts — the quantity, stated so that someone could recompute it.
  • one observation is — the unit whose repetitions supply the statistical power. This is the field most often left implicit, and the one that decides how much text the family needs.
  • derivation — the minimum depth, the depth of the usual implementation, and what supplies the difference.
  • moves with — the source of variation that dominates the feature when nothing is held constant. Not the source it is intended to measure: the one it actually tracks.
  • fails as — the characteristic failure. Not a general warning: the specific way this family misleads.

The gauge beside each plate is a nest of eight concentric frames, one per step of the derivation scale, drawn from the inside out. Solid frames are layers the feature cannot do without. Hatched frames are layers the usual implementation adds, whether or not the definition demands them. Hairline frames are layers it never touches.

Each plate also carries an observation rate, measured on the same corpus with the same denominator so that the rates are comparable, and the amount of text that rate implies for a modest comparison. Those numbers come from the lab described next, not from the literature, and they are properties of one corpus of prepared English political prose. A different register would move them, in some cases by orders of magnitude, which is itself one of the article's points.

Four ground truths are available in this material, and the experiments are built around them. Brown supplies gold word classes, so a tagger's error can be measured rather than assumed.29 The Penn Treebank sample supplies gold constituent structure, so a surface proxy for clause count can be compared with the thing it proxies.30 NPS Chat supplies gold dialogue-act labels and speaker identifiers, so pragmatic features can be counted rather than inferred.31 The two speech corpora supply known speakers across two genres, so the same authors can be attributed within a genre and across one.

The classifier throughout is a Delta-style nearest centroid: features are converted to rates, standardised on the training partition only, and the test item is assigned to the class whose centroid is nearest under the mean absolute difference. This is Burrows's Delta generalised beyond word frequencies,323334 chosen because it is transparent, has no hyperparameters to tune and does not have the capacity to memorise. A stronger classifier would raise every number in the article; the comparisons between levels are what the experiments are for, and those are what a transparent classifier protects.3536

One further property of this material deserves comment, because it is the property that makes the deep levels measurable at all. Gold annotation is not the truth about a text; it is a scheme applied consistently. The Penn Treebank's constituent labels, the Universal Dependencies relations and the Penn Discourse TreeBank's sense hierarchy are each a theory of the phenomenon, written down and enforced by guidelines.303738 Where two schemes disagree, features defined over them are not comparable, however similar the names. A study that reports a subordination ratio has reported a ratio under a particular definition of subordination, and the derivation scale marks that dependence as part of the cost of depth. The tooling used to read these corpora here is deliberately minimal and stands in for the far larger toolchains most studies use.3940

One piece of arithmetic recurs in every section and is stated here once. Where a feature occurs at rate λ per thousand words, and two writers are to be compared on a rate ratio R with a two-sided test at the conventional levels, the text needed from each is approximately T = (z0.975 + z0.80)2(1/λ1 + 1/λ2) / (ln R)2, in the units in which λ was measured. With R = 1.2, five per cent significance and eighty per cent power, this reduces to roughly 433 divided by the rate per thousand words, in thousands of words. Every "words needed" figure in this article is that calculation, applied to a rate measured on the same corpus.1817 It assumes events arrive independently, which for word-level features they do not: rates are bursty, so the true requirement is larger, and the figures below should be read as floors.4142

Three limitations should be stated before any of the results are used. First, the speaker corpora are political addresses, which are drafted collaboratively; the attributed entity is an office and its staff, not a single writing hand. This weakens no comparison between levels, since every level faces the same target, but it means the absolute accuracies are not estimates of individual authorship. Second, the tagger used on unannotated text is a most-frequent-tag lexicon with a suffix backoff, which is far below the state of the art;4344 where its weakness matters, the experiment says so. Third, the corpora are English, and English has thin inflectional morphology, which systematically deflates the morphological level relative to what it would supply in a morphologically rich language.4546

5Character-level features

The character level is the only one at which nothing has been inferred. A character frequency is a count over the decoded contents of a file. That is its strength and the whole source of its reputation: character n-grams are, empirically, among the most effective features in authorship attribution across languages, genres and text lengths.47484950 They are also the hardest to interpret, because a character n-gram that discriminates two authors could be picking up a favoured word stem, a punctuation habit, a suffix or an editorial convention, and the number itself does not say which.51

Two qualifications belong at D0 and are routinely skipped. The first is that decoding is not free. A byte stream becomes characters only under an encoding assumption, and Unicode normalisation determines whether an accented letter is one character or two, and whether an emoji is one or several.5253 A study that counts characters without stating its normalisation form has left a free parameter in the measurement. The second is that what reaches the analyst has usually passed through editing, transcription, or optical character recognition, all of which rewrite exactly this level.5455 Capitalisation and punctuation are the first things a copy editor changes.

What the level does supply is density. On the corpus used here, characters arrive at 5,308.64 per thousand words, so a twenty per cent difference in a character rate can be detected on 82 words of text. That is a sentence or two. No other level in this article comes within an order of magnitude, and the practical consequence is that character features are the ones that still work on short messages.5649

The importance of what happens at the boundary between D0 and D1 is easiest to see by changing nothing but the boundary rule. Four defensible tokenisers were run over the same eighteen books: whitespace splitting, alphanumeric runs, words with internal apostrophes, and a Penn-style tokeniser that separates clitics and treats punctuation as tokens. No byte of text changed.

Mean vocabulary per book ranges from 6,760 types to 12,639, a difference of 87 per cent. Mean token length ranges over 20.7 per cent. The hapax share of types moves by 14.3 percentage points. These are the raw materials of the lexical level, and they are not properties of the books. When the same four tokenisations are used for an attribution task on the speech corpus, accuracy is similar in each case, between 64.3 and 71.4 per cent, but the answers are not the same answers: the whitespace and Penn-style pipelines disagree about 10.7 per cent of the addresses, and the apostrophe-sensitive pipeline disagrees about 14.3 per cent. Where a case turns on one document, the tokeniser is a party to the verdict.

6Lexical features

The lexical level is where stylometry began and where most of it still lives. Counting the frequencies of common words, most of them grammatical, and comparing profiles across candidate authors is the method of the Federalist study and of every descendant of it.82643265 Its durability is not an accident. Function words are frequent, so they are densely observed; they are only loosely tied to subject matter, so they transfer between topics; and they are produced below the level of conscious style, so they are hard to suppress.666768

The level divides sharply in two, and the division matters more than the label "lexical" suggests. Frequency features over grammatical words behave one way. Features that depend on the tail of the distribution — vocabulary richness, hapax legomena, rare-word rates — behave completely differently, because the tail of a word distribution is not a stable object.6942

Richness measures are the clearest case of a unit problem. A type-token ratio is not a count of events; it is one number per document. Yule's K, MTLD and MATTR are attempts to remove the length dependence that makes the raw ratio uninterpretable,70717273 and they succeed to varying degrees, but none of them changes the fact that the sample size for comparing two writers on richness is the number of documents, not the number of words. On the corpus here that is 0.16 observations per thousand words. The corresponding requirement is 2.67 million words to detect a twenty per cent difference by the same arithmetic used elsewhere in this article, which is a way of saying that richness comparisons between two writers are underpowered unless each has produced a shelf of books.

The transfer property of function words can be measured directly. Nine works by three authors were divided into samples of four sizes, and each sample was attributed twice: once against training material from the same work, and once with the whole of its own work held out, so that training and test material share an author but not a subject.

At 300-token samples, content words lose 10.9 points when the work changes, from 51.6 to 40.7 per cent; function words lose 0.4 points, from 85.6 to 85.2. At 150 tokens content words are at 26.6 per cent, below the 33.3 per cent chance level, while function words are at 74.1. By 1,200 tokens both feature sets are above 92 per cent and the difference has mostly closed. This is the empirical content of the standard advice, and it comes with a boundary: the advantage of grammatical words is an advantage in short samples and across topics, and it disappears when there is enough text that topic vocabulary alone identifies the writer.747576

The distance is part of the feature

A lexical result depends on three separable choices: which words, how the rates are scaled, and what distance is used to compare profiles. Burrows's Delta fixes the second and third as standardisation followed by a mean absolute difference,32 and the variants that followed changed one at a time: Delta prime and its relatives adjust the scaling,7778 and the cosine formulation, which normalises profile length, turns out to be the change that matters most.7934 The practical consequence is that two studies using the same word list and different distances are not replications of each other. Comparative work across genres and languages shows the same sensitivity to how many words are taken and how the samples are built.808182

Underneath all of it is the shape of the word-frequency distribution itself. Word rates are not well behaved: common words are burstier than a Poisson process, content words cluster in the documents that are about them, and the vocabulary of a corpus grows indefinitely with its size.83414284 Sequence features such as frequent word n-grams inherit the same behaviour in a sharper form.85 This is why so many lexical measures are length-dependent, and why the sample-size arithmetic used in this article, which assumes only that events arrive at some rate, is a lower bound on the text a real comparison needs rather than an estimate of it.8687

7Morphological features

Morphology is the level where the gap between the linguistic label and the derivation depth is widest. A suffix is a morpheme, and identifying morphemes requires an analyser that knows the language's inflectional and derivational system. A word-final trigram is a string, and identifying it requires nothing. Most published work described as morphological stylometry counts strings.5190

The question is how much is lost by the substitution, and in English it can be measured against gold annotation. A most-frequent-class table was built over the last three characters of each word in one half of the Brown corpus and evaluated on the other half, against a full word lexicon built the same way.

Word class from the last three characters alone is 45.8 per cent accurate on 490,861 held-out tokens, against 26.2 per cent for always guessing the commonest class and 93.1 per cent for a full lexicon. The suffix rule recovers 29.0 per cent of the distance from the trivial baseline to the lexicon while consuming no annotation at all. On the words the lexicon has never seen, which are 4.2 per cent of tokens and the ones that matter, the suffix rule is 79.6 per cent accurate, which is why every real tagger has a suffix backoff in it.4344

Two conclusions follow, and they pull in opposite directions. Positively, a character-level feature set is already carrying a large part of the morphological and grammatical signal, which helps explain why character n-grams perform as well as they do without any linguistic processing.5149 Negatively, this means that a study which reports character n-grams and morphological features as separate evidence is double-counting: the second is largely a re-encoding of the first.

Contractions deserve a note of their own, because they are the family most completely at the mercy of the layer beneath. Whether don't is one token or two is a tokeniser setting, and subword segmentation schemes used in modern pipelines make yet another decision.9192 The rate is also strongly bound to formality and medium rather than to the person: in 364,163 words of prepared political address the corpus here contains 29 contractions, a rate of 0.08 per thousand words, implying that no feasible sample would support a comparison between two speakers on that basis. The same writers contracting freely in email would supply a usable rate. The feature has not changed; the channel has.

The English caveat matters most here. In agglutinative and richly inflecting languages, morphological features carry proportionally far more of the burden, and word-frequency features carry less, because the type inventory explodes.454693 The derivation-depth argument is unchanged: the deeper analysis is required for a larger part of the signal, and the cost of the analyser being wrong rises with it.

A final point about the level as a whole. Morphological features are rarely used alone. They appear inside large mixed inventories, where hundreds of features from several levels are pooled and a verification decision is made on the profile as a whole.9413 That is a reasonable design, and it makes the derivation question sharper rather than softer: a mixed inventory inherits the deepest dependency in it, so a profile containing one parser-derived feature is a parser-dependent profile.

The register shows the spread. Sentence length sits at D1: it needs a sentence splitter and nothing else, and it is the oldest quantitative style statistic there is.197 Part-of-speech sequences sit at D4 and require a tagger trained on somebody's annotated corpus.4398 Parse-tree patterns, voice and named constructions sit at D5 and require a parser trained on a treebank whose annotation scheme defines what a construction is.3099100101 The literature on syntactic stylometry stretches across all three depths and does not always distinguish them.102103104

Take the deepest claim first. If a parse buys access to structure that surface features cannot reach, then tree-shape features should carry information that sentence length does not. On 3,884 sentences of the Penn Treebank sample, tree depth correlates with the logarithm of sentence length at r = 0.682, so 46 per cent of the variance in a D5 feature is accounted for by a D1 one.

The proxy runs the other way as well, and worse. A surface clause count built from subordinating conjunctions and semicolons correlates with the gold count of S and SBAR nodes at r = 0.522, understates it substantially, and orders 16.5 per cent of sentence pairs the wrong way round. So the cheap proxy and the parsed quantity are not two measurements of one variable. They are two variables, and a study that treats a conjunction count as a measure of subordination is measuring something else, related but distinct.

Now the compounding question. A tagger with error rate ε leaves only (1−ε)5 of its five-grams intact, which at ε = 0.30 is 16.8 per cent. If that arithmetic governed accuracy, deep sequence features would be worthless under realistic error. Tag streams from the Brown corpus were corrupted at rates from 0 to 0.30 by replacing tags at random from the tag marginal, and a fifteen-way register classification was rerun at each rate. Part-of-speech trigram accuracy falls from 39.6 per cent to 36.0 per cent across that whole range. The arithmetic is right about intact n-grams and wrong about the classifier: noise drawn independently of class dilutes every profile in the same direction, and a distance-based classifier is comparing profiles, not counting intact events.

The damage comes from somewhere else. A tagger was trained on the 88 press documents of the Brown corpus only, and applied to all fifteen categories, where gold tags are available for comparison.

Accuracy is 95.7 per cent inside the training domain and 93.4 per cent outside it, and the unseen-word rate rises from zero to as much as 10.7 per cent. The estimated verb rate is biased downward almost everywhere, by 2.6 per thousand tokens on average. In this case the bias is small relative to the 48.0 per thousand spread between categories, and only two of 105 category pairs are put in the wrong order, so the measurement survives. That is a reassuring result and it should be read narrowly: the error is systematic, its size depends on how far the target text is from the tagger's training domain, and the correct response is to measure it on annotated material from the target domain rather than to assume it is small.192021

There is a tradition that treats syntax functionally rather than distributionally, counting the choices a writer makes among grammatical alternatives that express roughly the same content: active against passive, gerund against infinitival complement, one ordering of adjuncts against another. Because both members of the alternation are available at every point, a preference is harder to explain away as an effect of subject matter, and the approach has been used both in forensic settings and in historical work on individual variation.105106107 Its cost is that identifying the alternation site requires the parse, so the derivation is deep even when the resulting feature is a single ratio, and the number of sites is small: passive constructions arrive here at 6.70 per thousand words, which implies about 65,000 words per writer for a modest comparison.

At the semantic level the machinery stops being a model of the language and starts being a model of what the language is about. That change of object is the whole problem. Every feature in this section is, in the general case, a measurement of subject matter, and subject matter is the confound that authorship analysis exists to escape.

Category-based features count tokens belonging to stipulated classes: emotion words, cognition words, social words, positive and negative polarity.2811011127112 They are interpretable in a way that character n-grams never are, which is why they dominate psychological and forensic applications. The cost is that the categories are somebody's theory, the coverage is uneven across registers, and there is no accuracy figure to consult because nothing was estimated. A lexicon has a coverage profile, not an error rate.

Topic models and embeddings differ from lexicons in that they are fitted, but they inherit the same problem in a stronger form. A topic distribution is by construction a measure of what a document is about.113 It can identify authors well, and it does, precisely to the degree that the authors write about different things.114 Pretrained encoders behave the same way unless their objective is explicitly designed to strip content, which is now an active line of work.2611576 Embeddings also break the audit property that every other family in this article retains: nothing is counted, so nothing can be recounted, and a disagreement between two studies cannot be traced to a definition.

The transfer experiment isolates the cost. Eleven speakers with addresses in two genres were used: the classifier was trained on policy addresses and tested on ceremonial ones by the same speakers, with everything else held fixed.

Semantic features fall from 64.4 per cent within genre to 46.7 per cent across it. That is a substantial loss, but it is not the largest in the figure, and the ordering of the levels is instructive. The largest loss belongs to the discourse level, 59.3 to 20.0 per cent, which is below chance-adjusted usefulness. Syntactic features lose 26.2 points. Morphological features lose the least of the four shallowest levels, 14.6 points. The character and lexical levels each land at 60.0 per cent. Nothing here supports treating "topic" as the only thing that fails to transfer: what fails to transfer is whatever the genre fixes, and genre fixes discourse organisation more tightly than it fixes vocabulary.10911668117

A companion measurement makes the same point without a classifier. For each level, the share of a feature's variance attributable to author and to genre was computed over 1,030 samples from eighteen literary works.

Every level explains more author variance than genre variance in this corpus, which is expected since the corpus contains many authors and four genres. The informative comparison is the ratio and the ordering. Syntactic features lead on author variance at 0.407, with character features at 0.374 and pragmatic at 0.345; semantic and discourse features trail at 0.202 and 0.192. When the comparison is restricted to prose fiction, so that genre is held constant, author variance falls for every level, by the largest margin at the pragmatic level, 0.345 to 0.209. That is what a genre-bound feature looks like from the inside: much of what appeared to be a difference between writers was a difference between the kinds of text they wrote.

One more caution about stipulated categories. Polarity and subjectivity lexicons were built for opinion mining, on product reviews and film reviews, where the task was to recover a rating.27118119 Applied to political address or to nineteenth-century fiction, they are being used far outside the distribution they were validated on, and their coverage falls unevenly: the words a lexicon lacks are not a random sample of the words a text contains. Because a lexicon has no error rate, this failure is invisible in the output. The only diagnostic available is the coverage rate itself, which should be reported alongside any lexicon-derived feature and almost never is.

The first problem is observation density. Discourse units are large, and large units are rare. In the corpus here, paragraphs arrive at 1.29 per thousand words and explicit connectives at 16.21. Detecting a twenty per cent difference in paragraph length at conventional power requires roughly 335,000 words per writer, which is more than most authorship cases have in total. Cohesion measured over adjacent sentence pairs is denser at 45.04 per thousand words, but a sentence pair is not an independent observation of the same kind as a token, and treating it as one overstates the precision.

The second problem is that the cheap version of every discourse feature is a different feature from the expensive one. Counting the string while is not counting a contrastive connective: the disambiguation between connective and non-connective uses needs syntax, and even with syntax it is imperfect.12938 Counting lexical repetition between neighbouring sentences is not measuring coherence: the entity-grid formulation of coherence requires coreference resolution,130128 and coreference is among the least reliable annotation layers in the whole stack.131 Where the expensive version has been used properly for attribution it does carry signal,132133 but the reported gains are modest and the pipeline is long.

Both problems show up in the transfer result. The discourse level loses 39.3 points when the genre changes, the largest drop of any level, ending at 20.0 per cent on a task where chance is 10 per cent. This is exactly what would be expected if discourse features are mostly measuring the conventions of the text type: an inaugural address and a policy address are organised differently no matter who delivers them.134116 The same logic applies to narrative organisation, which is a property of a genre before it is a property of a person.135

There is a third difficulty, quieter than the other two, which is that the annotation schemes at this level are themselves contested. Rhetorical Structure Theory and the Penn Discourse TreeBank describe overlapping phenomena with different primitives: one builds a tree over the whole text, the other annotates local relations anchored to connectives.12713638 Agreement between trained annotators is materially lower here than at the syntactic level, and the disagreements are not evenly distributed: they concentrate on implicit relations, which are exactly the ones a stylistic account would care about, because an explicit connective is a choice the writer already made visible.137129 A discourse feature therefore inherits not just the error of a parser but the residual disagreement of the scheme the parser was trained to apply. That is a different kind of uncertainty from a tagger's five per cent, and it does not shrink with more data.

Paragraph structure earns a separate warning because it is the one discourse feature that sits at D0. It needs no model, only preserved line breaks, which makes it attractive. But it is also the feature most likely to have been rewritten between composition and analysis. A paragraph break survives a plain-text export, a copy-edit, a conversion to a word processor format and a scrape in whatever condition each of those left it, and there is no way to recover the original from the artefact. A discourse feature at D0 is cheap to derive and expensive to trust.

Humour and irony are the limiting case and deserve to be stated plainly. There is no unsupervised counter for them. A feature family whose rate cannot be computed without first commissioning annotation is a different kind of object from a character frequency, and the derivation scale exists to keep that difference visible.

The pragmatic level is also where the most seductive claims in applied stylometry live, because its categories map onto psychological ones. 12Seven levels on one task

The level sections treated each family on its own terms. This section puts them on one task, with one classifier, one corpus and one protocol, so that the differences are attributable to the features.

The task is to attribute 59 State of the Union addresses to eleven speakers, leaving one address out at a time. Chance is 9.1 per cent and the majority-class baseline is 13.6 per cent. Each level supplies its own feature set, selected by frequency on the training partition only.

Accuracy falls monotonically with level: 79.7 per cent for character features, 78.0 for lexical, 74.6 for morphological, 72.9 for syntactic, 64.4 for semantic, 59.3 for discourse, 49.2 for pragmatic. Every level is far above chance, which is worth stating: even the pragmatic level, on 21 features counting sparse markers, identifies the speaker of half the addresses.

The monotone ordering is not, however, evidence that shallow features are inherently more informative. Two controls show what is driving it. The first is feature count. When every level is restricted to its twenty most frequent features, the ordering compresses sharply: character 71.2 per cent, syntactic 66.1, lexical 64.4, semantic 50.8, discourse 50.8, pragmatic 50.8, morphological 47.5. Much of the character level's advantage at full width is that it affords 300 usable features where the pragmatic level affords 21. A level's capacity to supply many weakly informative features is a real advantage, but it is a different advantage from any single feature being better.157158

The second control is text length. The same task was rerun on truncated addresses.

At 250 words nothing works well: the best level is character at 45.8 per cent and the discourse level is at 10.2, barely above chance. At 4,000 words the spread is 81.2 per cent for character features against 39.6 for pragmatic. Every level improves with length and the higher levels improve later, which is what the observation-density argument predicts. A level with 1.3 observations per thousand words cannot say anything about a 250-word sample because it has not seen anything yet.

Figure 6 puts all twenty-seven families on the same density axis with the sample size each implies. The finite requirements span nearly five orders of magnitude: 82 words for a character feature, 3,225 for hapax legomena, 64,608 for passive constructions, 334,698 for paragraph length, 2,671,908 for a document-level richness index and 5,435,950 for the contraction rate of prepared political prose. Two families, argument structure and irony, have no finite requirement at all, because they have no countable observation without annotation. This figure, more than any other in the article, is the practical content of the levels: they are not gradations of insight, they are gradations of how often the thing you want to count actually happens.

Three questions, not one

The comparison above is a closed-set attribution: eleven candidates, one of whom is the speaker. Most real questions are not of that shape, and the shape changes what the levels are worth. Verification asks whether two documents share an author with no candidate list at all, which is a one-class problem and a considerably harder one.159160 Attribution in the open world adds the possibility that the true author is absent, which requires a confidence threshold and a way to abstain.161 Profiling asks not who wrote it but what kind of person did, and it is a different task with different confounds.162163164

Two effects of task shape are worth carrying away. First, accuracy in a closed set falls as the candidate list grows and as the text per candidate shrinks, and published numbers are not comparable unless both are stated.165166 Second, benchmark results in this area are unusually sensitive to how the evaluation set was built: shared tasks have found substantial biases arising from the construction of document pairs rather than from the methods being compared.167168169 The levels behave differently across these tasks, and a feature set chosen on a closed-set benchmark should not be assumed to transfer to a verification problem.

The three tolls

Reading the experiments together, derivation depth charges three separate tolls, and conflating them is the source of most of the confusion.

A resource toll. Above D1 every feature requires an artefact built by someone else: a lexicon, a tagset, a treebank, a lexicon of hedges, an act inventory. That artefact was built for a purpose, on a variety of the language, at a date. It is the largest uncontrolled variable in most published stylometry, and unlike a model it usually has no error rate at all.

A sample toll. Higher levels count larger units, and larger units are rarer. This toll is arithmetic and unavoidable, and it can be computed in advance from a pilot corpus before any classifier is trained.1817

A transfer toll. The deeper the derivation, the more of the pipeline was tuned on material that is not the material under study, and the more the measurement moves when the register moves. The transfer experiment shows this most clearly for discourse features, which lose 39.3 points, and least for morphological ones, which lose 14.6.

What derivation depth buys against these three tolls is interpretation. A character 4-gram that discriminates two authors tells you nothing about why. A passive-to-active ratio tells you something a linguist can argue with, a court can be asked to weigh, and a subsequent study can try to replicate.17017160172 That is not a small purchase. It is simply a different one from accuracy, and the two are routinely reported as though they were the same.

13What follows for practice

Six practical consequences follow from the measurements above. They are stated as recommendations because that is the useful form, but each is traceable to a specific number in the article.

Report the pipeline, not just the features. A feature list is not a method. The tokeniser, the normalisation form, the tagger and its training corpus, the lexicon and its version are all parameters of the measurement. Changing only the tokeniser changed 10.7 to 14.3 per cent of the attribution decisions here, at essentially unchanged aggregate accuracy, which is the worst case for reproducibility: the summary statistic looks stable while the individual answers move.173174168

Compute the sample requirement before the experiment. The rate of the feature in a pilot text and the effect size worth detecting are enough to give the text requirement. If a study intends to compare hedging between two writers and has 20,000 words of each, the arithmetic says it cannot, and no classifier will repair that. The same calculation explains why short-message attribution is dominated by character-level features.56166165

Match the register before matching anything else. The transfer experiment cost every level between 14.6 and 39.3 points except the pragmatic one, whose within-genre performance was already close to its across-genre performance. Where register cannot be matched, that limitation belongs in the result, not in a footnote about future work.11668175176

Do not count the same evidence twice. Character n-grams, suffix rates and word-class rates overlap heavily: word class is 45.8 per cent predictable from the last three characters alone. Combining feature sets from adjacent levels and treating the agreement between them as corroboration inflates confidence, and in a forensic setting that is the failure mode that matters most.172177178

Prefer the shallowest derivation that answers the question. If the question is whether two documents share an author, character and function-word features are denser, more transportable and easier to audit. If the question is what the difference consists of, or if the answer must be explained to a non-specialist, the deeper feature is worth its tolls. The choice is between accuracy and accountability, and it should be made explicitly.179170

State what could not be measured. Two of the twenty-seven families in the ledger have no countable observation at all in this material. That is a fact about the data, and it is more useful in a report than an estimate manufactured by running an unvalidated classifier over unlabelled text.

Treat feature selection as part of the analysis. With twenty-seven families, several distance measures and a free choice of sample size, a determined analyst can search a large space of pipelines and report the one that worked.180181 The discipline that protects against this is the same one used here: fix the corpora and the protocol before looking, report every level rather than the best one, and state the sample requirement in advance. Casework protocols in forensic authorship analysis have converged on similar disciplines.179182177

Distinguish the person from the population. Age, gender, region and education all leave measurable traces in exactly the features used for attribution.183184162164 In an attribution problem those traces are a resource when the candidates differ on them and a hazard when they do not: a classifier can separate two candidates on a demographic difference and be scored as having identified a person. Applied work that reaches a public conclusion about a named individual, from historical letters to contested modern documents, carries this hazard directly.185186187

Two currents worth watching

The first is adversarial. Every level in this article can be attacked, and the attacks are asymmetric: imitation and obfuscation degrade lexical and character features sharply, while automated translation round-trips damage syntactic ones.188189190 A defence built on a single level is a defence against a single attack. This is now a mainstream concern rather than a theoretical one, given the scale at which authorship can be inferred from ordinary online text.191

The second is machine-generated text. Detection of generated prose is stylometry with the author replaced by a decoder, and it inherits the whole apparatus described here.192193194 It also inherits the confounds in an especially sharp form: detectors trained on stylistic surface features misclassify non-native writing at high rates,195 and the theoretical limits on reliable detection are becoming clearer.196197 The lesson from the transfer experiment applies unchanged. A feature that separates two populations in one register is not thereby a property of either population.

What the levels are for

The seven-level taxonomy is not wrong and this article does not propose replacing it. It is an accurate description of what features are about. It is simply not a description of what they cost, and the two are read as though they were the same, with the top of the ladder taken to be the sophisticated end. The measurements here do not support that reading. The character level was the strongest performer on the common task, the syntactic level carried the most author-related variance, the semantic and discourse levels transferred worst, and the pragmatic level supplied the fewest observations by three orders of magnitude.

The productive question is the one in the title. Before a number can be a fact about a writer, everything beneath it has to be true: that the bytes decoded, that the boundaries fell where the writer put them, that the word class is the class this token has here, that the parse is the parse, that the lexicon knew the word, that somebody judged the act correctly. Each of those is a claim about a machine or a person that is not the author. Counting how many such claims a feature rests on will not tell you whether to use it. It will tell you what you are agreeing to when you do.
