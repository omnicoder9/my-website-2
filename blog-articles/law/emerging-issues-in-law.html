1The question

Every list of emerging issues in law reads like a list of technologies. Artificial intelligence in practice. Automated decisions. Digital evidence. Online courts. Remote hearings. New kinds of provider, new kinds of firm, new kinds of owner. Arranged that way, the list suggests that the profession's problem is novelty, and that the answer is to learn the technology.

That reading does not survive contact with what courts and regulators actually do. When a judge confronts a brief containing five cases that do not exist, the judge does not reach for anything about artificial intelligence. The judge reaches for Rule 11, a certification requirement whose operative language was settled in 1983 for a world in which a signature meant a person had read the paper.1 When the Divisional Court in London confronted the same problem in 2025, it reached for the Hamid jurisdiction, an inherent power developed in 2012 to discipline immigration practitioners who abused out-of-hours applications.2,3 When a Virginia court decided whether a breach post-mortem was privileged, it applied the work-product doctrine as codified in 1970.4,5 The technology is new. The instrument is old, and it was written about something else.

So the question worth asking about each item on the list is not what the technology does. It is narrower and more answerable: what rule is actually doing the work? For each act somebody now performs, name the single provision a tribunal or regulator would point to today, and then ask how far that provision is from the facts it is being made to cover.

This article codes thirty-four such acts. Each pairs an act with the instrument that governs it, on two axes. The first is purchase: how specific the governing text is, running from a provision whose own terms name these facts down to nothing but a general duty of competence. The second is stretch: the signed distance in years between the year the operative text was settled and the year the facts arrived at scale. Stretch is positive when a rule is being reached forward across time to cover something its drafters had not seen, and negative when the rule was written after the problem was already familiar.

The two axes turn out to be strongly related, and in the direction that ought to be obvious and is almost never said out loud. Across the thirty-four acts, the mean purchase of an instrument that predates its facts is 1.90 on a six-point scale. The mean purchase of an instrument written after the facts arrived is 4.08. The correlation between stretch and purchase is −0.45. The difference between a rule that grips and a rule that is being stretched is not the subject matter, the jurisdiction or the sophistication of the drafter. It is almost entirely whether the drafter had already seen the problem.

That sounds close to a tautology and is not one. It would be perfectly possible for a well-drafted general principle to grip new facts firmly, and lawyers routinely claim that this is what the common law is for. The claim is testable, and on this sample it mostly fails. What the general principles produce, when reached forward, is not grip but a decade of case-by-case extension in which nobody can say in advance what the answer is.

Three further things fall out of the coding, and they are the reason for the article rather than the table. The instruments that fit best are disproportionately the ones that are not yet in force. The instruments that fit best and are in force include two that fit perfectly and answer no, which is a different situation from a gap and is routinely confused with one. And the party who bears the loss when a stretched rule fails is, in twenty-one of the thirty-four acts, somebody who is not a lawyer and had no say in the stretching.

2What the apparatus was built out of

The regulatory machinery that all of this is being asked to absorb was built around a single design decision: put a licensed individual at every point where a legal effect is produced, and attach the sanction to that individual. Everything else in professional regulation is downstream of that choice.

In the United States the modern form begins with the 1908 Canons of Professional Ethics, thirty-two aspirational statements addressed to lawyers as persons.6 Canon 33, added in 1928, is the ancestor of the rule that still forbids nonlawyer ownership; it was written about partnerships and letterheads, not capital markets.7 The 1969 Model Code turned the canons into disciplinary rules, and DR 3-102 restated the fee-sharing prohibition in a form a bar counsel could enforce.8 The Kutak Commission's 1983 Model Rules rewrote the whole apparatus into the two-part structure that governs today: black-letter rules, each addressed to "a lawyer", with explanatory comments.9 Read the rules straight through and the grammatical subject almost never changes. A lawyer shall. A lawyer shall not. The entity appears only as a thing a lawyer belongs to.10

England and Wales took a different route to a similar place. The Solicitors Act 1974 consolidated a regime of individual admission and a roll kept by the Law Society.11 What broke the pattern there was the Clementi review of 2004, which found that regulation organised around professional titles could not cope with businesses that combined titles, and recommended regulating entities instead.12 The Legal Services Act 2007 implemented that: authorised persons, licensed bodies, alternative business structures, and a regulatory objectives clause against which every regulator must justify itself.13 The consequence is that England and Wales spent the 2010s with a vocabulary for regulating a firm as such, and the United States, in forty-eight states, did not.

Parsons read the profession as an institution mediating between an impersonal legal order and the individual client, and treated the personal relationship as constitutive rather than incidental.14 Hazard, writing in 1991 about where legal ethics was heading, put the difficulty plainly: rules addressed to individuals become unstable once the economic unit doing the work is not an individual.15 Both were describing the design decision, not complaining about it.

Sociologically, the individual-licence design was never only about competence. Abbott's account of the professions describes jurisdictional control as the defining resource, held by claims about what only members can properly do.16 Kritzer, writing in 1999, argued that the resulting monopoly was already dissolving under routinisation and would keep dissolving.17 Rhode's work documented the other side of the same fact: that the monopoly's price is a very large volume of unmet legal need.18 Recent survey work in the United States estimates that low-income Americans receive no or inadequate legal help for the large majority of their substantial civil problems.19,20 The randomised evidence on what representation actually buys is thinner and more equivocal than the advocacy on either side suggests, which is itself worth knowing.21 In England and Wales the Legal Services Board's annual assessment tracks the same gap under a different methodology.22 That figure is the reason the regulatory reform movement exists, and it is worth holding in view: the profession's rules are not merely a cost borne by lawyers.

Two features of the design matter for everything that follows. First, the sanction apparatus is calibrated to a person who can be trained, warned, suspended and struck off. It has no other instrument. Confronted with something that cannot be trained or struck off, it does what it can: it reattaches the duty to the nearest available person. Formal Opinion 512 does exactly this, and says so with unusual candour, noting that it creates no new rules and simply applies the existing ones.23

Second, the design assumes a competent person looks at the work before it takes effect. Every one of the emerging issues in the list is, at bottom, an arrangement in which that look happens later, more thinly, or not at all. That is why the same handful of provisions keep being reached for, and why they keep coming up short in the same way.

3The purchase ladder

To compare thirty-four situations you need a scale that does not smuggle in the answer. The scale used here measures one thing only: how specific the operative instrument is to the facts of the act.

The coding rule is stated once and applied without exception. The rung is the most specific operative instrument of general application, in that act's reference system, that a tribunal or regulator could point to today. Three parts of that sentence carry weight.

Operative excludes instruments that have been adopted but have not commenced. An adopted-but-dormant provision gets its own rung, P4, rather than being scored as though it were in force. Two acts in the set sit there, and both are European.

Of general application excludes chambers standing orders, single-court practice notes and the rule of one state among fifty. Dozens of United States federal judges have issued standing orders about disclosure of generative tools. They are real and they bind, but a litigant cannot know the answer from them without knowing the judge, and a rule you can only find by knowing the judge is not the same kind of object as Rule 11. Such instruments are recorded in the plate note and do not set the rung.

In that act's reference system matters because three systems are in play: the United States federal courts together with the ABA Model Rules, England and Wales, and the European Union. Each act is coded in one system, named on its plate. This is not a claim that the systems are comparable in general. It is a device for keeping the comparison honest: an act is scored against the instruments its own system actually has.

One consequence of the rule is worth stating before it causes confusion. A provision that squarely covers an act and forbids it scores P5. The ladder measures fit, not permission. Model Rule 5.4 is a perfect fit for the question whether outside capital may enter a law firm: it names fee-sharing with nonlawyers and nonlawyer ownership, in terms.10 It fits, and the answer is no. Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 43 is a perfect fit for the question whether a felony plea may be taken by video: it requires the defendant's presence, in terms.24 It fits, and the answer is no. Both are commonly described as gaps in the law. They are not gaps. They are decisions, taken deliberately, that the profession is now reluctant to defend on the merits and therefore prefers to describe as oversights. Distinguishing foreclosure from absence is one of the things the ladder is for.

The second axis, stretch, is a date arithmetic. Each act carries two years: the year the operative text was settled, and the year the facts arrived at scale in that system. The second is a judgement, and it is the softest number in the article. The rule used is the year of the first widely reported instance in that system, or the year the practice became routine, whichever is better documented; the plate note gives the anchor. Getting one of these wrong by two or three years moves nothing. Getting one wrong by twenty would, and the sensitivity of the headline finding to that is addressed in the closing section.

Every plate carries a small gauge that puts both axes in one figure. The strip along the top is the ladder, P5 at the left to P0 at the right, with the occupied cell filled. Below it is the span. The solid post with a filled head marks the year the text was settled; the dashed post with a hollow head marks the year the facts arrived, to the right of the instrument where the text is being reached forward and to the left where it was written afterwards. The distance between them is the stretch, on a logarithmic scale so that a hundred and forty-five years and twenty-seven years fit the same box. The bar between them is solid where the instrument's own text covers the ground, hatched where it is covered only by extension, and an open dashed arrow where nothing covers it. Length carries the stretch; the solid-hatched-open signature carries the rung. They are separate quantities and the gauge is drawn so that neither can be read off the other.

A third column records incidence: who absorbs the loss when the instrument fails to do its job. Not who is answerable, which is often obvious and usually the lawyer, but who is actually worse off. The four values are the licensed individual, the entity, a third party, and nobody identified. The last is not a throwaway. Two acts in the set produce real losses that fall on no named person, and both of them concern money.

4Signing

The first group covers acts that put a lawyer's name on something a machine produced. This is where the profession's response to generative systems has been fastest, loudest, and least effective, and the pattern is instructive because the underlying instrument is so well understood.

The scale of the problem is now documented rather than anecdotal. A public database maintained at HEC Paris tracks decisions in which a court or tribunal has found, or clearly implied, that a party relied on hallucinated material. As of 12 September 2026 it records 2,039 such decisions, of which 1,396 are from the United States, 217 from Canada, 110 from Australia and 69 from the United Kingdom.25 The composition matters more than the total. In 1,173 of them the party using the tool was a self-represented litigant; in 811 it was a practising lawyer; in 32 it was a judge. Fabricated material appears in 1,689; misrepresented authority in 853; false quotations in 549. Case law is the subject in 1,834, but 234 involve a legal norm and 146 involve exhibits or submissions, which is a different and worse problem.

The database's inclusion rule is deliberately strict: it lists only decisions where a court or tribunal explicitly found, or clearly implied, reliance on hallucinated material, which means allegations alone do not count and the true population is larger.26 The sanctions recorded in it have escalated in a recognisable pattern. Mata, the first widely reported case, drew a joint penalty of five thousand dollars in June 2023.27 The Second Circuit referred an attorney to its grievance panel in Park v. Kim the following January.28 A Colorado attorney was suspended in November 2023, the first suspension tied to an AI filing.29 California's first published appellate opinion on the subject imposed a ten-thousand-dollar sanction and a State Bar referral where twenty-one of twenty-three quotations were fabricated.30 By March 2026 a Sixth Circuit panel had imposed the stiffest sanction available to it, fifteen thousand dollars per attorney plus the opponent's costs, on two lawyers whose consolidated appeals contained more than two dozen fake or misrepresented citations.31 The escalation is real and it is a response to the same rule, applied harder.

Against this, the instrument being used is a signature requirement. Rule 11(b) provides that by presenting a paper the signer certifies that its legal contentions are warranted, to the best of the signer's knowledge after an inquiry reasonable under the circumstances.1 The certification is universal in form. It is a claim about the whole document. Sanctions under it are episodic in fact: they reach the filings somebody happened to check.

The gap between a universal certification and an episodic check is not rhetorical, and it can be quantified exactly. Take a brief with forty citations, two of them fabricated, and suppose counsel verifies k of them chosen at random.

To be ninety-five per cent confident of catching at least one fabrication you must read thirty-one of the forty. To be ninety-five per cent confident of catching both you must read thirty-nine. There is no interior point at which sampling delivers the thing the rule asks for, because the rule asks for a statement about all forty and a sample supports a statement about the average.

The interesting result is what happens when you ask what a clean sample tells you about the citations you did not check, because the answer depends entirely on a fact about the world that the rule does not distinguish. Under an independent-fault model, in which each citation has some fixed probability of being fabricated, the answer is nothing at all: the per-citation risk after thirty clean checks is identical to the risk before any. Under a brief-level contamination model, in which a brief is either verified or it is not, ten clean citations drive the expected number of remaining fabrications from 0.30 to below 0.05. Sampling is either worthless or highly efficient, and which one it is turns on whether failure is per-citation or per-document. Rule 11 speaks to neither.

Empirically the contamination model looks closer to the truth. The reported cases are overwhelmingly documents in which nobody checked anything, rather than documents in which a careful lawyer missed one. Which suggests that the honest reform is not a better sampling rule but a disclosure rule about process, and that is precisely the reform the profession has declined to adopt at the level of a rule of general application, leaving it to individual judges.

England and Wales reached the same place by a different road. In Ayinde the Divisional Court found the threshold for contempt met in respect of a barrister who put five non-existent authorities into grounds for judicial review, and declined to initiate proceedings, referring the matter to the regulators instead.3 The companion case involved a witness statement citing forty-five authorities, eighteen of which did not exist, on a claim for GBP 89.4 million. The court sent its judgment to the Bar Council and the Law Society and invited them to consider urgent further steps. What is notable is the vehicle: an inherent jurisdiction named after a 2012 immigration case, which the court described as the power to regulate its own procedures and enforce the duties lawyers owe it.2 That is a general power of last resort being asked to do routine work, which is what a P1 rung looks like from the inside.

Regulators have supplied guidance rather than rules. ABA Formal Opinion 512 maps six existing Model Rules onto generative tools and expressly creates nothing new; how much verification is required is "fact-specific".23 California and Florida issued their own versions, differing in detail.38,39 The judiciary of England and Wales issued guidance to judges, refreshed in April 2025, and the Solicitors Regulation Authority published a risk outlook rather than a rule.40,41 Guidance is what a regulator produces when it has concluded that the existing rule is adequate and the problem is compliance. That conclusion is defensible. It also guarantees that the answer to "what must I do" remains unavailable in advance, which is the defining property of this rung.

The United States entry is State v. Loomis, in which the Wisconsin Supreme Court held that a sentencing court may consider a proprietary risk score provided it is not determinative and provided the presentence report carries a written caution.42 The remedy is a warning label. It is worth asking what a warning label can do, because the underlying problem is not a matter of emphasis.

ProPublica's 2016 analysis reported that a widely used instrument produced higher false-positive rates for Black defendants; the vendor replied that the instrument was calibrated, with equal predictive value across groups.43,53 Both were right, and Chouldechova showed why in an identity that requires no data at all.44 For a binary classifier, the false-positive rate is fixed once you fix the base rate, the positive predictive value and the miss rate. Hold predictive value at 0.62 and the miss rate at 0.28 across two groups whose two-year re-arrest rates are 0.51 and 0.39, and the false-positive rates are forced to 0.4593 and 0.2821. The gap is 17.72 percentage points, and no adjustment to the instrument can remove it while the base rates differ. An exhaustive sweep of a 49 × 50 grid of predictive-value and miss-rate pairs finds no combination that equalises both. To equalise false-positive rates you must let predictive value differ by 11.94 points, which is the same trade-off wearing different clothes. Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan proved the general version.54,55

A cautionary instruction does not engage with any of this. It tells the sentencer that the score has limitations. It does not tell the sentencer which limitation applies to the defendant in front of them, and it cannot, because the answer is a property of the population rather than of the individual. Citron's account of technological due process identified the structural point two decades ago: procedural protections designed for individualised decisions do not transfer to decisions produced by a rule embedded in software.56,57

None of this is news to the field that studies it. Surden's survey of machine learning in law drew the distinction between systems that model legal reasoning and systems that find statistical regularities in legal outcomes, and warned that the second is routinely described in the vocabulary of the first.58 Ashley's account of legal analytics made the same point at book length, with the historical detail of forty years of attempts.59 The gap is not between what is known and what is possible. It is between what is known and what a sentencing statute has been asked to notice.

The European entries are the opposite case. Article 22 of the General Data Protection Regulation names automated individual decision-making, in terms, and its lineage runs back to article 15 of the 1995 Directive and before that to national scoring statutes.45,60 The Court of Justice interpreted it for the first time in 2023, holding that where a lender draws strongly on a credit reference agency's score, the agency itself makes the article 22 decision.46 Whether the accompanying transparency duties amount to a right to an explanation has been contested since 2017 and remains so.47 But the fit is not contested. This is the only act in the set whose instrument is more than a quarter of a century older than its facts and scores P5, and the reason is simply that the drafters in 1995 were looking straight at automated scoring.

The two remaining European entries fit better still and are not yet operative. The AI Act's annex III names recruitment, creditworthiness, education and essential services, and the chapter III obligations that attach to them were to apply from 2 August 2026.48 Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published on 24 July 2026 and in force three days later, moved that date to 2 December 2027, and the annex I date to 2 August 2028.49 The prohibitions in article 5, the general-purpose model duties and the article 50 transparency obligations were not deferred.50 The revised Product Liability Directive is in the same position: software is a product, a defect can lie in a post-market update, the burden of proof is relaxed where technical complexity makes causation excessively difficult, and none of it operates in national law until Member States transpose by 9 December 2026.51

Set against that, the United States position on harm from a machine-assisted judgement is the lowest rung in the article. A client's action lies against the lawyer, under a standard of care defined as the competence and diligence ordinarily exercised by lawyers in similar circumstances.52 The standard is set by prevailing practice, at precisely the moment when prevailing practice is moving fastest. The tool's maker is generally out of reach: the service-product line and the economic loss rule see to that.61,62 There is no reported decision establishing what ordinary care with a generative tool requires, which means the standard will be set retrospectively, by whichever case gets there first.

The last act in the group is the only one in the article where two instruments of general application point in opposite directions at the same object. How large is that population? The honest answer is that it depends almost entirely on one operational parameter that no instrument in either system specifies: how widely a preservation notice is cast. Simulating a corpus of two hundred custodians producing five records each per month, with a thirty-six-month deletion deadline and sixty matters a year each naming on average three custodians, leaves 63.0 per cent of the corpus sitting past its deletion date and under an active hold, and inflates the corpus 170 per cent above what timely deletion would produce. Halve the scope repeatedly and the zone falls to 28.1 per cent; widen it and it rises to 68.5 per cent. A sixteen-fold change in a parameter chosen by in-house counsel, with every rule in both systems held constant, moves the answer by a factor of 2.4.

That is worth dwelling on, because it is the general shape of a P1 conflict. The law does not resolve it. The law delegates it, silently, to whoever decides how wide to cast the net, and then holds the organisation to both duties.

The remaining acts in the group are better served. Cross-border transfer has a provision squarely on point in the adequacy decision of July 2023, which is the third attempt at the same problem after Safe Harbour and Privacy Shield, each annulled in turn.66,67 A finding of adequacy about a foreign legal order is a strange kind of instrument: it fits the facts exactly and depends for its continued validity on a legislature over which the finding has no purchase.74,75

There is a quieter problem underneath both, which no proposed rule addresses. A certification under Rule 902(13) attests that a record was produced by a system producing an accurate result. It does not attest to when the system's clock was last set.

Simulating adjacent event pairs across two unsynchronised devices makes the size of this visible. A real-time clock drifting two seconds a day for a month gives a sixty-second offset bound. At a ninety-second median gap between messages, 7.29 per cent of adjacent cross-device pairs are recorded in the wrong order, which is roughly thirty-six inversions in a five-hundred-message thread. Tighten the offset to fifteen seconds and it falls to 0.56 per cent; widen the gap to five minutes and it falls to 0.83 per cent at the same offset. Pairs separated by more than twice the offset bound are always safe. None of this is exotic; it is why distributed systems use logical rather than physical clocks for ordering, and why the Network Time Protocol exists.98,99 It is also why forensic practice treats timestamp normalisation as a distinct step.100,101 The certification route was designed to save the cost of calling a custodian. It saves that cost by not asking the question.

The room is the other half of the group, and it is the sharpest comparative contrast in the article. England and Wales made pandemic practice permanent. Section 198 of the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022 inserted sections 85A and 85B into the Courts Act 2003, giving courts and tribunals power to direct that images or sounds of proceedings be transmitted to people not taking part, and creating a specific offence, punishable as a summary offence and as a contempt, of recording what is transmitted.84,83,85,86 The Judicial Review and Courts Act 2022 created an Online Procedure Rule Committee, and on 23 June 2026 that committee made the first Online Procedure Rules, in force 7 September 2026, applying at first only to possession proceedings, with the Civil Procedure Rules disapplied where they bite.90,89,91 Nine years after the reform programme began, there is a procedural code written for a screen rather than adapted from paper.102,103

The scholarship anticipated most of this. Katsh and Rabinovich-Einy traced online dispute resolution from private platforms into public courts and argued that the move changes what a dispute is, not merely where it is heard.104 Sourdin's work on judicial technology separates supportive, replacement and disruptive technologies and notes that courts adopt them in that order regardless of which they need.105 Morison and Harkens asked what remains of adjudication when the decision is computed rather than reached.106 Legg and Bell surveyed the same ground from the profession's side.107 Barton and Bibas made the strongest version of the practical case: for the very large number of people who currently get no hearing at all, a thinner hearing is not the alternative to a fuller one.108,109

The United States federal position went the other way. Section 15002 of the CARES Act authorised video and telephone conferencing for a listed set of criminal proceedings on emergency findings, and that authority lapsed on 10 May 2023 with the end of the national emergency.87 The Judicial Conference expanded remote audio access for civil and bankruptcy proceedings in September 2023 and expressly did not extend it to criminal matters.88 Rule 43 requires presence; rules 5(f), 10(c) and 43(b)(2) permit video for particular pre-trial steps with consent; nothing general survived.24 The rule fits the question exactly and answers no, which is a P5 placement and a very different situation from a gap.

Whether that answer is right is a contested empirical question rather than a doctrinal one. Studies of video bail hearings and of remote immigration adjudication have found effects on outcomes and on participation that are hard to attribute to the technology alone.110,111,112,113 What is not contested is that remote hearings are shorter. The interesting question is what that buys.

Modelling a hearing list as a single-server queue shows that a hearing running thirty minutes instead of forty-five is a real gain and an unstable one. Ineffective hearings have to be relisted, so the effective service time is the nominal time divided by one minus the ineffective rate. Against an in-person ineffective rate of ten per cent, the whole advantage of the shorter remote hearing disappears once remote hearings go ineffective forty per cent of the time, four times as often. Meanwhile the queue itself is governed by utilisation: holding the hearing constant at forty-five minutes, moving from 0.90 to 0.98 utilisation multiplies the mean wait by 5.44, from 0.68 to 3.67 court-days.114 The list is not long because hearings are long. Shortening the hearing is a real intervention on the wrong variable, unless the ineffective rate is held down, and the ineffective rate is exactly what no rule in either system addresses.

Document review is a search problem with a recall target, and recall is expensive at the top. Model a hundred-thousand-document corpus with two per cent responsive and a ranked review whose separation between responsive and non-responsive documents is moderate. Reaching seventy-five per cent recall requires reading 21.6 per cent of the corpus. Reaching ninety-five per cent requires 56.5 per cent, which is 2.62 times the effort. Reaching ninety-nine per cent requires 80.0 per cent. The marginal price of one more responsive document climbs from about 51 documents read between 75 and 80 per cent recall to about 420 between 98 and 99. Blair and Maron established the empirical version of this in 1985, when lawyers who believed they had retrieved seventy-five per cent of relevant documents had in fact retrieved about twenty.132 The courts absorbed technology-assisted review from 2012 onwards.133,134,135,136 What no instrument does is specify a recall target, which means the reasonableness of an inquiry is settled after the fact by whoever is complaining about it.

Litigation funding in England and Wales is the clearest case in the article of a general instrument reached onto a market it was not aimed at. Two structural facts about scale sit behind the merger and globalisation questions, and both are arithmetic rather than doctrine.

The first is that conflict checking scales worse than conflicts do. Simulate two firms merging, one with 400 clients and one with 250, each client carrying on average three adverse parties drawn from a market with a realistic long tail. The merger requires 100,000 client-against-client checks and creates 155 direct adversities, each of which, under the imputation rule, disqualifies the entire merged firm absent informed consent from both sides.155 Scale both firms up together and conflicts grow roughly as the number of clients to the power 1.17, while checks grow as the square. The number of checks performed per conflict found rises from 263 at a hundred clients a side to 2,639 at sixteen hundred. The administrative cost of the imputation rule is superlinear in firm size, which is a straightforward argument for the screening arrangements large firms have been asking for and an equally straightforward explanation of why clients dislike them.156

The second is what happens to a fee when the work gets shorter.

Take a ten-hour task billed at 400 an hour against a cost of 150 a lawyer-hour, and a fixed fee set fifteen per cent under the historic hourly bill. If automation removes thirty per cent of the hours, the hourly firm's revenue per matter falls thirty per cent and it needs 42.9 per cent more matters simply to stand still; at a fifty per cent saving it needs twice the volume. The fixed-fee firm's margin moves the other way. The crossover is exact and early: at a saving of fifteen per cent, precisely the discount built into the fee, the fixed-fee firm begins earning more per matter than the hourly firm, and the client's bill does not move at all.157

Nothing in any of the three systems addresses this. Model Rule 1.5(a) tests whether a fee is reasonable by reference to time, novelty, skill and customary charges, after the fact.147 Formal Opinion 93-379 forbids billing hours not worked, and says nothing about who owns a productivity gain under a fixed or capped fee.37 Opinion 512 repeats the point without extending it.23 This is the only act in the set for which no regulator in any of the three systems has issued so much as guidance, and it is also the one that will determine what the profession looks like in a decade.148,158,159,160,161 Reasonableness is an ethical test. Who keeps the saving is a distributive question, and an ethical test cannot answer it.

10What the ledger shows

Thirty-four acts, six rungs, three systems. Reading the coded matrix as a whole produces four results, one of which reframes the rest.

Purchase is low. Eighteen of the thirty-four acts sit at P2 or below, meaning that the most specific thing anyone can point to is either a formal opinion applying an unamended rule or a line of cases extending one. The mean rung is 2.82. Eleven acts have a provision on point, and two of those provisions answer no.

Purchase tracks the calendar, not the subject. This is the finding that reframes the others.

Split the acts by the sign of the stretch. Twenty instruments predate their facts and are being reached forward; their mean rung is 1.90. Thirteen were written after the facts had already arrived; their mean rung is 4.08. The gap is 2.18 rungs on a six-point scale, and the distributions barely overlap: only three forward-reaching instruments score P5, and no catch-up instrument scores below P2. The correlation between stretch and rung is −0.45. Group the acts by subject instead and the pattern is much weaker; the subject groups' mean rungs run from 1.86 to 3.86, and the range is driven by which groups happen to contain recently drafted instruments.

The lesson is unglamorous and, on this evidence, robust. What determines whether the law grips a new situation is not the elegance of the general principle, the seniority of the court or the sophistication of the regulator. It is whether somebody wrote a provision after watching the problem happen. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e) was amended in 2015, twelve years after Zubulake, and it is the least contested instrument in the group.72 Rule 902(14) arrived in 2017, two decades after electronic records became routine, and nobody argues about it.76 The provisions that generate litigation, formal opinions, working groups and law review articles are the ones being reached backwards across thirty or forty years.

The best-fitting instruments are disproportionately the ones not yet in force.

All four European acts score P4 or P5, the highest of the three systems, and two of the four are P4: adopted for exactly these facts and not operative. The AI Act's high-risk chapter now applies from 2 December 2027, and the revised Product Liability Directive requires transposition by 9 December 2026.49,51 The United States mean is 2.56 across twenty-five acts, with seven at P4 or P5. England and Wales sit at 2.80 across five. Reading the European column as evidence that codification works therefore requires a caveat that is easy to drop: half of it has not yet been tested against a single case.

Two federal United States instruments are in a milder version of the same position. Proposed Rule 707 and the drafted Rule 901(c) both address their facts squarely, and both were held back in the first half of 2026, the first returned for revision and the second kept in reserve.94,80 The rulemaking process is doing what it was designed to do. It was designed on the assumption that the facts would still be recognisable at the end of it.

The loss usually lands on somebody outside the profession.

In twenty-one of the thirty-four acts the party who absorbs the failure is a client, an opponent or the public. In five it is the lawyer, in six the firm, and in two nobody identifiable. The correlation between how far up the incidence ladder a loss falls and the rung of the governing instrument is −0.30: where the lawyer personally absorbs the loss, purchase is lower, mean rung 1.80, and where a third party absorbs it, purchase is higher, mean 3.33. That is not the direction a cynical account would predict, and the explanation is prosaic rather than flattering. The acts where the lawyer is personally on the hook are concentrated in the signing group, which is the newest problem; the acts where third parties bear the loss include the evidence and procedure rules, which are the oldest and best-tended. Purchase follows the age of the problem, which is the same finding as before, seen from a different column.

Together the rung and incidence codes separate 90.6 per cent of the 561 pairs of acts, against 85.4 per cent for the subject-matter grouping, and they separate 37 pairs that the subject grouping does not. Fourteen of the twenty-four possible rung-and-incidence profiles are occupied. The two axes are crosscutting rather than a re-description of the topic list, which is the minimum a taxonomy has to earn.

11What this predicts

A coding scheme is worth something only if it says what happens next. Four predictions follow from the ledger, stated so they can fail.

The signing problem will be solved by a disclosure rule, not by better verification. The certification structure cannot support what is being asked of it, for the reason the sampling arithmetic shows: a universal claim about a document is not supportable by a sample of the document. The cheap answer is to change what is certified. A rule requiring disclosure of the process used, rather than a warranty about the output, is verifiable, is already being imposed by individual judges, and converts an unbounded duty into a bounded one. Expect it to arrive as a rule of general application within two or three rulemaking cycles, and expect it to be criticised for not going far enough.

The evidence rules will move before the practice rules. Proposed Rule 707 was returned for revision rather than abandoned, and the drafted Rule 901(c) is held in reserve explicitly against a change in circumstances.94,80 Both address acts currently sitting at P1 with a governing text from 1975. The rulemaking machinery is slow and it finishes. Nothing comparable is under way on Rule 5.4 or Rule 1.5.

The pricing question will be settled by clients, not by regulators. It is the only act in the article at P0 with no guidance from any regulator in any system, and the arithmetic gives it an early crossover. When the saving exceeds the discount embedded in a fixed fee, the provider's interest flips from billing hours to removing them, and the constraint becomes what a buyer will pay rather than what a rule permits. Watch corporate legal departments and litigation funders, not bar associations.

Foreclosure will be relitigated as though it were absence. The two P5 acts that answer no, remote felony pleas and nonlawyer ownership, will keep being described in professional debate as gaps in the rules. They are not gaps. Each is a decision that was taken, defended on the record, and is now unpopular with a portion of the profession that would rather not argue the merits again. The tell is the vocabulary: proposals to "modernise" or "clarify" a provision that is perfectly clear.

There is a broader point underneath all four, and it is the reason this article codes acts rather than technologies. A profession organised around a licensed individual has exactly one move when work migrates away from that individual, which is to reattach the duty to the nearest person who still holds a licence. That move is cheap, it is fast, and it preserves the apparatus. It also produces the P1 and P2 placements that make up more than half of this set: duties that are formally in force, substantively undetermined, and settled retrospectively in the cases of whoever gets caught. Hart's distinction between the rule and its open texture covers the ordinary case where a rule's edges are uncertain.162,163,164 What the ledger shows is something more specific: a class of situations in which the edges are not merely uncertain but are the whole of the rule's contact with the facts.

12How the numbers were made, and how to break them

Every number in this article comes from one of two places: the coded matrix of thirty-four acts, or nine deterministic experiments run at build time with the output embedded verbatim below. Nothing is quoted from a source that was not read, and nothing is estimated where it could be computed.

The soft parts, in order of softness.

The fact year. This is the weakest column, and the headline finding depends on it. It records the year the fact pattern arrived at scale in that system, using the first widely reported instance or the year the practice became routine, whichever is better documented. Reasonable people will move several of these by a few years. The finding survives that: the forward-reaching and catch-up populations are separated by a mean of 44 years of stretch, so shifting individual entries by three or five years does not reclassify them. Three entries are genuinely arguable in sign. Utah's sandbox is coded at zero, and the Arizona and Product Liability entries at −1, so a small error in any of them could flip it. Dropping all three moves the gap between the two populations from 2.18 rungs to 2.10, and the correlation from −0.454 to −0.425. The finding is not carried by the borderline cases.

The rung of a contested placement. The rule is mechanical, but two placements deserve challenge. Garfield.Law is coded P2 on the ground that an authorisation with bespoke conditions is not an instrument of general application. Someone could argue for P5 on the ground that the conditions are operative and squarely on point. And the deepfake act is coded P1 rather than P0 on the ground that Rule 901(a) is being applied, not merely invoked. Both moves are defensible; neither changes any aggregate by more than a few hundredths.

The sample. Thirty-four acts is not a census. They were selected to cover a brief listing fourteen emerging issues, decomposed into the specific acts each issue is made of, and they over-represent the United States, which supplies twenty-five of the thirty-four. A set drawn from Canadian, Australian or Indian materials would look different, and the hallucination database suggests Canada and Australia would supply plenty of material.25

The models. Each of the nine experiments is a model, and every model is wrong in ways its author chose. The citation-sampling result assumes random selection, which is generous: real verification is not random, and lawyers check the citations they doubt. The review-effort curve assumes a binormal score distribution, which is the standard idealisation and understates the tail. The queue is M/G/1 with a squared coefficient of variation of 0.6, which suppresses the heavy tail a real list has. The conflicts simulation draws adverse parties from a fixed long-tailed universe, so its sub-quadratic growth in conflicts partly reflects saturation against that universe. The retention model's headline is deliberately reported as a sensitivity rather than a point estimate, because the point estimate is not meaningful. The fairness identity is not a model at all: it is algebra, and it holds for any classifier whatsoever.44 The two sampling results are exact rather than approximate, computed from the hypergeometric distribution in the tradition of exact binomial inference rather than from a normal approximation.165

Two experiments were rebuilt during the build and the earlier versions discarded. The conflicts simulation originally drew clients with replacement, so the firms it reported were smaller than the firms it claimed; the fix was to draw distinct clients and the direct-conflict count rose from 122 to 155. The retention model originally applied each hold to a random share of the whole corpus, which saturated at every plausible parameter and reported a meaningless 73.8 per cent; rebuilding it so that holds name custodians produced the sensitivity curve in the article, which is a weaker claim and a true one.

The most useful way to break this article would be to code a second, larger set of acts from a system not represented here, apply the same rule, and see whether the calendar finding survives. If it does, the practical implication is uncomfortable for the way the profession argues about reform: the best predictor of whether a rule will work is not how carefully it was drafted but how late.
