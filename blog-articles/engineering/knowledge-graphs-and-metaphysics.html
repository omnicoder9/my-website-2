KGE-100The claim, and three ways to misread it

A knowledge engineer sits down to record something entirely ordinary: that pump P101 is part of cooling system C1. There is no philosophical ambition in the task. There is a maintenance database on one screen, an equipment register on another, and a deadline. The engineer types one line.

By the time that line is written, positions have been taken on whether parthood composes, whether it holds at a time or eternally, whether the pump that comes back from overhaul is the pump that went, whether the identifier in the maintenance system denotes the same object as the identifier in the register, and whether the absence of a contradicting record counts as evidence of anything. None of these questions was asked. All of them were answered, because the formalism does not admit of an abstention. A triple either carries a temporal index or it does not. A property is either declared transitive or it is not. The engineer's silence on each point is not neutrality; it is the acceptance of a default, and the defaults were chosen by people who were solving a different problem.

That is the phenomenon this article is about, and the thesis is a modest one, deliberately stated so as to be falsifiable:

Knowledge graphs are one place where metaphysical and ontological assumptions become computationally concrete, and GraphRAG is one way of exploiting that structured ontology for machine reasoning.

The claim has been formulated with some care, and the care is worth unpacking, because there are three adjacent claims it is not making and each of them is false.

It is not the claim that a knowledge graph is a metaphysics. A graph is a set of assertions in a formalism. Metaphysics is an inquiry into what there is. The graph does not conduct the inquiry; it records a position in it, usually without noticing, and often incoherently. A graph can be internally inconsistent in ways no metaphysical position could be, and it can be silent where the metaphysics is not. The right relation is narrower and more interesting: the graph is a place where the position becomes concrete, in the specific sense that it becomes something a machine executes and something an audit can catch.

It is not the claim that getting the metaphysics right makes the system work. It does not. The empirical record on graph-augmented retrieval is mixed and in places unflattering. A recent benchmark study finds that graph retrieval-augmented generation frequently underperforms ordinary retrieval on real tasks, matching it on simple fact retrieval while costing an order of magnitude more in tokens, and pulling ahead only where the question demands that several facts be composed.1 An earlier systematic evaluation reports graph methods scoring materially below vanilla retrieval on natural questions and worse still on time-sensitive ones.2 Any account that predicts a clean win from better ontology is contradicted by measurement.

And it is not the claim that GraphRAG is the payoff of ontology. GraphRAG is one architecture, of recent vintage and uncertain half-life, for using graph structure during retrieval and generation.3 The thesis names it because it is the clearest current case of a system whose behaviour visibly depends on ontological choices made upstream, not because it is the destination.

Two neighbouring inquiries should also be set aside, not because they are unimportant but because conflating them with this one guarantees confusion. The question of whether a technology ought to exist, and whose interests its arrangements serve, is a live and urgent one;4 it is downstream of the question of what the thing is. And the question of how engineers know what they know, in Vincenti's sense, is an epistemology rather than an ontology.5 Both intersect the present question. Neither is it.

What remains, when those are set aside, is a claim about where a certain kind of philosophical decision becomes tractable. The argument of this article is that the transition from prose to graph is a compiler with no error output: it accepts every input, silently supplies the defaults, and produces something that runs. The purpose of what follows is to put the error output back.

The method is inherited. A companion article set out an ontology of engineering as thirty provisional axioms, each tested against verdicts that competent practice renders without hesitation — a stopped clock is a clock; a dormant backup channel is a component; a counterfeit bolt with correct metallurgy is not an airworthy part.6 Those axioms are treated here as a specification, and this article asks the engineer's question about them: what happens when you try to build it? Fifteen of the axioms are followed through the encoding, each to the point where the formalism forces a decision, and the decisions are collected in a ledger that turns out to make an argument of its own.

KGE-110What “computationally concrete” means

The thesis leans on the phrase computationally concrete, and the phrase needs a definition sharp enough to be checked.

Start with Quine, because the modern discussion of ontological commitment starts there. Quine's criterion is that a theory is committed to those entities that must lie in the range of its bound variables for the theory to be true.78 The criterion is deliberately syntactic: it does not ask what the theory's author believed, but what the theory, taken literally and regimented into logical notation, quantifies over. This is a useful criterion precisely because it is mechanical. It gives a procedure.

A knowledge graph is a regimented theory of exactly the kind Quine's criterion was built for. It is already in logical notation — that is what RDF is9 — and it is already committed to whatever its vocabulary quantifies over. Ask what a plant's graph is committed to and the answer is readable off the schema: it quantifies over pumps and over systems, and if it carries a Function class then it quantifies over functions, which is a live metaphysical position that most modellers would be startled to find they hold. The graph is not a description of a commitment. It is the commitment, in the technical sense, and it is machine-readable.

Gruber's definition is the bridge from Quine to engineering: an ontology is an explicit specification of a conceptualisation.10 The operative word is explicit. Guarino sharpened it, distinguishing the conceptualisation from the logical theory that approximates it and noting that a theory may be a poor approximation while remaining perfectly consistent.1112 The gap between what a modeller means and what the axioms entail is the working space of this article.

So: a commitment is computationally concrete when it satisfies three conditions. It must be forced — the formalism offers no way to remain silent, only a default. It must be executed — some query, reasoner or retriever behaves differently depending on which way it went. And it must be auditable — a reader of the artefact can determine which way it went, without asking the author.

All three matter. A commitment that is forced but never executed is harmless bookkeeping. One that is executed but not auditable is the ordinary condition of software, and the reason so much of it is untrustworthy. It is the conjunction that is interesting: a philosophical position that changes what a machine does, and which can be read off the machine.

The conditions are not new, even if the setting is. McCarthy and Hayes argued in 1969 that an agent which acts in the world needs a representation adequate to the world, and that supplying one raises philosophical problems that cannot be deferred to the engineering.13 Hayes's naive physics programme proposed to meet them by formalising commonsense ontology directly — liquids, containment, support — and the programme's difficulties are the same ones this article is measuring, on a smaller scale.14 What has changed is that the artefact is now built routinely, at scale, by people who are not doing philosophy and would not describe themselves as taking a position.

Carnap's distinction supplies the necessary deflation.15 Questions asked inside a framework — is P101 a component of C1? — have determinate answers by the framework's own rules. Questions asked about the framework — are there really such things as functions? — are, for Carnap, practical questions about which framework to adopt. One need not accept Carnap's deflationism to accept its methodological point, and the point is the one this article needs: the choice of framework is a real choice with real consequences even if it is not a discovery about the world. Choosing to quantify over functions is a decision the modeller makes and the system then lives with. That the decision may not be answerable to a fact does not make it arbitrary, and it certainly does not make it cheap.

KGE-200The stack, and the four joints

It helps to lay out the levels, because the interesting things happen between them rather than within them.

The levels are familiar and the ordering is not controversial. Metaphysics asks what kinds of thing there are. Ontology engineering settles how a particular domain shall be carved.16 Knowledge representation fixes what a formalism is able to say.1718 A knowledge graph asserts which entities and relations actually obtain.19 GraphRAG exploits that structure at query time.320 What is worth attention is the four joints, because each is a place where something is discarded and nothing records the discarding.

Joint 1, metaphysics to ontology. A live philosophical dispute is settled by fiat. There are at least four serious theories of function in print — etiological, systemic, proper-function, and the intention-and-capacity hybrids — and they disagree about cases.21222324 An engineering ontology must pick one, and the picking is not usually recorded as a philosophical decision. It is recorded as a class definition.

Joint 2, ontology to representation. The chosen conceptualisation meets a formalism that cannot express all of it. Description logics are decidable because they are restricted, and the restrictions are not incidental: binary relations only, no default negation, no modal operators, no temporal indices except by encoding.25 Whatever the ontology says about counterfactual capability, about what a component would do, survives this joint only in flattened form.

Joint 3, representation to graph. Assertions are made, by somebody, from some source, at some time. The formalism's basic unit — the triple — has three slots and none of them is who says. Provenance and time are afterthoughts in the data model and are paid for accordingly, as the fourth experiment below measures.

Joint 4, graph to retrieval. A retriever decides which edges are worth walking. That decision is a claim about which relations carry explanatory weight, and it is usually made by a heuristic rather than by the ontology — which, as the eighth experiment shows, is the difference between one candidate explanation and a hundred and one.

Constraint runs downward and evidence runs upward. What the metaphysics permits bounds what the ontology can say; what the ontology says bounds what the graph can assert; what the graph asserts bounds what retrieval can find. But the diagnostic direction is the reverse. When a retrieval system returns another tenant's billing service inside the change-impact scope of a cooling system, the fault is not in the retriever. It is four levels up, in a decision about parthood that nobody remembers making.

KGE-300Method: commitments, a ladder, and a lab

Three devices structure what follows, and each is chosen to make disagreement productive rather than merely possible.

The first is the commitment plate. Fifteen of them, one per decision point, each recording the question at issue, what in the pipeline forces an answer, the silent default, the query that then returns a wrong answer, the position taken here, and the evidence for it. Each plate shows two encodings side by side: the tempting one and the faithful one. The plates are numbered C01 to C15 and each carries the axiom it inherits from the companion article.6 The layout is deliberate. The upper register is prose about the world; the lower is machine-readable notation; the rule between them is the joint at which one becomes the other, and it is the joint where the argument lives. The split is borrowed: Kroes and Meijers made the irreducibility of an artefact's physical description to its intentional one the organising problem of a decade of work, and the plates are that thesis drawn as a layout.2627

The second is the expressiveness ladder, a seven-rung scale recording the minimum machinery each commitment demands. Rung 0 is a bare edge with no semantics beyond its existence. Rung 1 is RDFS: subclass, subproperty, domain, range. Rung 2 is OWL 2 EL, which buys existential restrictions and property chains at polynomial cost.28 Rung 3 is the rule-shaped and query-rewriting fragments, RL and QL. Rung 4 is OWL 2 DL in full, whose consistency problem is N2ExpTime-complete.29 Rung 5 is rules and first-order logic — SWRL, Datalog — where decidability is bought back only by restriction.30 Rung 6 is outside any decidable logic: procedure, statistics, or human judgement.

The ladder is not a complexity measure and does not pretend to be. It is a record of what you would have to adopt to state the commitment faithfully, and its purpose is to be totalled. The total is in the ledger at the end, and it makes an argument that no individual plate makes.

The third device is the lab: eight deterministic experiments, standard library only, no randomness and no network, run at build time with their output reproduced verbatim below. Every figure quoted in this article comes from that output. The experiments are small on purpose. None of them is a benchmark and none is evidence about how large systems behave; they are demonstrations that a particular consequence follows from a particular encoding decision, on a graph small enough that the reader can check the arithmetic by hand. Where a claim needs evidence about real systems, the article cites measurements from people who made them.12

KGE-400Commitments I: parts, boundaries, and structure

Begin where the knowledge engineer began: with parthood, which looks like the safest relation in the vocabulary and is not.

The trouble is that part of in English covers several relations that behave differently. Winston, Chaffin and Herrmann distinguished six — component-integral object, member-collection, portion-mass, stuff-object, feature-activity, place-area — on the grounds that they differ in transitivity, in functional significance, and in separability.31 Artale and colleagues carried the analysis into knowledge representation and showed that the differences are not academic: they determine which inferences are sound.3233 Simons's study of classical mereology is explicit that the extensional theory is insensitive to arrangement, so that a disassembled engine and an assembled one have exactly the same parts.3435

The formalism, meanwhile, offers one construct: owl:TransitiveProperty. The temptation is obvious and the first experiment prices it.

Sixteen asserted edges, all of them written on purpose and none of them false. Ten record functional integration — the pump is part of the cooling system — and six record platform containment: the controller executes in a virtual machine, which is in a rack, which is in a data centre. Declare one transitive partOf over the lot and the closure runs to 41 pairs, of which 17 do not appear under a non-transitive componentOf, giving a spurious share of 41.46%. The controller acquires six wholes instead of two. And a change-impact query — find everything that shares a whole with a plant item, which is how blast radius is normally computed — drags in five off-site objects including a second tenant's billing service, which is inside the scope of a cooling-system change by an unbroken chain of true assertions.

The instructive thing is that industrial practice worked this out long before anyone tried to put it in a graph. IEC 81346 structures technical systems by aspect: a function aspect, a product aspect, a location aspect, and since the 2022 second edition a type aspect, each with its own structure over the same objects and its own reference designation.3637 The standard's entire architecture is a refusal to have one parthood relation. An engineer writing =K1-P101+DC2 is stating three different structural facts in three different algebras and the notation keeps them apart. The knowledge graph, offered the same job, collapses them into one predicate and declares it transitive.

The second commitment follows immediately, because parthood edges are written once and the world does not hold still. A pump on the bench for overhaul is not currently integrated into anything, and is nonetheless the system's pump, in a sense the maintenance organisation would defend under challenge. If the edge is deleted the certified configuration silently shrinks; if it is kept, the graph asserts a functional integration that does not obtain.

The interesting evidence here is not philosophical but documentary. The Industrial Ontologies Foundry maintains a BFO-aligned mid-level ontology for manufacturing,3839 and in release 202401 it added two new process classes, gain of role and loss of role, with the stated rationale that the top-level ontology it builds on provides no way to express when a bearer started or stopped bearing a role or for how long the role was held.40 That is a gap in a standardised top-level ontology41 being patched, in production, by promoting role-change to a first-class occurrent. It is difficult to think of a cleaner illustration of a metaphysical question becoming an engineering ticket.

It is worth asking what would make a decomposition correct rather than merely available. Simon's answer, that complex systems are nearly decomposable because interactions cluster into subsystems with weak coupling between them, explains why hierarchical decomposition works at all and why it is not arbitrary despite several decompositions being possible.42 Bechtel and Richardson analysed decomposition and localisation as research strategies with characteristic failure modes.43 The mechanistic tradition supplies the sharpest criterion available: Machamer, Darden and Craver treat organisation as a first-class constituent rather than an afterthought,44 and Craver's mutual manipulability test — roughly, that x is a component of the mechanism for Φ when interventions on x change Φ and interventions on Φ change x — is the closest thing in the literature to a workable test for componenthood.45 It is also a test no graph can apply to itself: it quantifies over interventions that have not been performed.

The third commitment concerns the boundary, and it is the one most often made invisible by tooling. Every graph of a plant has a root, and the root was chosen. Whether the power feed is part of the cooling system or an interface to it is not a fact about the plant; it is a fact about the purpose for which the system of interest was drawn. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 makes this normative for architecture description, requiring that descriptions be relative to stakeholder concerns and that the viewpoint be declared.4647 The graph, by contrast, tends to record the boundary implicitly, as whatever the extraction happened to reach, with the consequence that two entirely correct graphs of one plant will not merge.

The fourth is the one classical mereology gets most obviously wrong. A crate containing every part of an engine has the same parts as the engine. If the ontology is extensional they are the same object, which is absurd, and practice knows it is absurd: the whole apparatus of assembly states, torque records and joint inspections exists to track the difference. Fine's theory of rigid and variable embodiment supplies the repair by pairing a matter with a principle of arrangement, so that the object is the pair rather than the matter.48 It maps unusually well onto engineering structure and it is expensive: every joint is an object, every connection is an assertion, and none of it is derivable from the parts list.

KGE-410Commitments II: identity, absence, types, roles

Four commitments in this group concern not what there is but how the graph individuates and quantifies. They are the ones most likely to be regarded as mere data-engineering hygiene, and they are the ones where the philosophy is least avoidable.

Take identity first. RDF makes no unique name assumption: two identifiers may denote one thing, and nothing in the data says otherwise.9 It also makes no unique difference assumption: two identifiers are not distinct until something asserts that they are. Both halves matter, and the second is routinely forgotten.

The third experiment takes five identifiers for two pumps, drawn from an enterprise system, a maintenance system and a process historian, resolves them with three owl:sameAs assertions, and asks the question a safety case asks: are there two independent pumps? The merge itself required ten pairwise identity judgements, one per pair, each of which is a question about the world rather than about the data — and the count grows quadratically, to 4,950 judgements at a hundred identifiers and 499,500 at a thousand.4950 Having done the work, the graph contains exactly two pumps and exactly zero of the one owl:differentFrom assertion the redundancy requirement needs. The requirement is not entailed. The data is correct, the resolution is correct, and the claim does not follow.

This is a small result with an uncomfortable generalisation. A great many availability and redundancy claims are computed by counting rows, and counting rows presupposes a unique name assumption that the knowledge representation explicitly disclaims. The count is right by accident, in the sense that the convention that makes it right is nowhere written down.

Absence is the same problem viewed from the other side, and it is worse because the failure is silent in the safe direction only until it is not. Reiter's closed-world assumption — what is not provable is false — is the semantics of every relational database and every SQL report.51 OWL's semantics are open: a missing triple asserts nothing, and a query asking which items lack a record is not entailed for any item, because for every item there is a model in which the record exists.52

The second experiment sets seven items requiring lubrication against three recorded lubrication events. Under closed-world semantics the audit flags four items. Under open-world entailment it flags none. To make the entailment agree with the audit, the graph needs a closure axiom per item — seven of them, against three records, which is 2.33 axioms for every record of data being closed over. The closure costs more than the data.

Which is why nobody writes it. What production systems do instead is run SPARQL, whose FILTER NOT EXISTS is closed-world, over a store whose knowledge representation is open-world, and report the answer as though the two semantics agreed.53 SHACL is the honest form of the same manoeuvre: it validates a snapshot against shapes without touching the entailment regime, so that a violation reports that this dataset does not contain the record rather than that the world lacks it.54 The distinction sounds pedantic and is the whole difference between a data-quality finding and a compliance finding.

The third commitment in this group is metamodelling, and it arrives the first time a catalogue entry needs both instances and properties of its own. Pump model 4B classifies units, so it looks like a class; it was authored by a design office, superseded on a date, and approved under a standard, so it looks like an individual. OWL 2 permits punning, letting the same name be read as a class in one axiom and an individual in another, and it is important to be clear about what punning does: the two readings do not interact.55 Nothing said about the individual constrains the class. The construct exists because the need is universal and a semantics for metamodelling is not, and it resolves the pressure by declining to resolve the question.

The position taken here is that the pressure is a symptom of a conflation. The design specification, the kind it specifies, and the units built to it are three things: an information artefact, a class, and a set of individuals.5657 Separating them costs one extra node and removes the need for punning entirely.

The fourth is the distinction between a kind and a role, and it is the most common structural defect in real engineering taxonomies. A hierarchy that contains both CentrifugalPump and DutyPump has mixed what a thing is with what it is currently doing. Guarino and Welty's OntoClean supplies the test: rigidity. A rigid property is one that every instance must have in every circumstance in which it exists — nothing that is a pump could fail to be a pump. An anti-rigid property is one that every instance could lack: any duty pump could be reassigned to standby without ceasing to exist.5859 An anti-rigid property cannot subsume a rigid one, and a hierarchy that violates this will misbehave on reassignment.

The distinction is not an artefact of formal ontology. Lakoff's survey of categorisation showed that natural categories are structured by prototypes, graded membership and family resemblance rather than by necessary and sufficient conditions,60 which is part of why an engineering taxonomy drifts: the terms it borrows from practice were not built to the specification a subsumption hierarchy assumes.

What is worth noting is where this discipline lives. There is no OWL construct for anti-rigid. The metaproperties are annotations applied by a methodology and checked by a person or a bespoke tool; the reasoner neither knows nor cares. A commitment that practice has known how to get right for a quarter of a century is, in the standard stack, unstateable — rung 6 on the ladder, outside the logic entirely.

KGE-420Commitments III: time, change, and persistence

An RDF triple is tenseless. It says that a relation holds, not that it holds now, and there is no syntactic slot for a time. Since a great deal of what an engineering graph records is exactly the kind of thing that stops being true, this is not a minor gap.

There are two respectable repairs and they are metaphysically opposed.61 The three-dimensionalist keeps enduring objects and indexes changeable relations to intervals, typically by reifying the holding of the relation as an object in its own right — the fluent pattern.62 The four-dimensionalist admits temporal parts and lets the relation hold timelessly between them: it is not that the pump is failed now, but that the February 2026 stage of the pump is failed, full stop.63 Both work. Both are expensive. Choosing between them is a metaphysical decision that will be made, in practice, by whoever writes the first schema, and it will be very difficult to reverse.

Here again the IOF record is instructive. Release 202401 imported Allen's interval algebra whole,64 with property-chain assertions and SWRL rules to make its relations inferable, plus mappings to OWL-Time so that intervals could carry calendar values.4065 Release 202502 then conceded, in its own release notes, that the representation does not yet fully support distinguishing duration from other temporal characteristics, and that an interim adjustment had been made specifically to avoid overstating the formal semantics. A production ontology declining to claim more than it can support is a good sign about the ontology and a clear signal about the difficulty.

Persistence is the harder case, and it is the one where practice has the firmest verdicts and the formalisms the least to offer. The philosophical apparatus is well developed: Wiggins's account makes identity relative to a sortal, so that the question is it the same? is incomplete until the same what? is answered,66 and Lowe's work on individuation treats criteria of identity as a general category.67 Both are directly usable and neither is implementable: the sortal-relative question has no slot in a triple.

The fifth experiment assembles twelve cases that competent practice adjudicates without hesitation, and runs three criteria over them. The mereological criterion asks whether a majority of the parts is retained. The historical criterion asks whether there is unbroken causal continuity. The designation criterion asks whether the controlling record still carries the same identifier. The cases are the ones that actually come up: overhaul, progressive full rebuild, part swap, relocation, a fork built from spares, the Theseus case where the original parts are reassembled into a second unit, a counterfeit bolt of correct metallurgy fitted under the certified part number, recertification, a firmware replacement, a service rewritten in another language, a cloned virtual machine, and a unit whose register entry was destroyed in a system migration.

Mereology gets 7 of 12. Continuity gets 10. Designation gets 10 — it fails on the counterfeit, where the record does designate and practice says the bolt is not the part, and on the lost register, where the record does not designate and practice says the pump is the same pump. The conjunction of continuity and designation gets 11 of 12. The majority vote over all three gets only 9.

That ordering is the finding, and it is not the one intuition suggests. Identity here is not what most of the evidence indicates; it is what two independent conditions jointly require, and a criterion that tallies evidence does worse than one that conjoins conditions. The three criteria disagree on half the suite, which is a fair measure of how contested the territory is even among practitioners who would each defend their verdict. And the conjunction still fails on the lost register — which is the right result, since it shows that the record is defeasible evidence of identity rather than constitutive of it. A pump does not stop being itself because a database migration went wrong.

KGE-430Commitments IV: function, capability, and role

Function is where the engineering ontology earns its name and where the philosophical literature is deepest, which makes the encoding decisions unusually well documented and unusually contested.

Four families of theory are on offer. Wright's etiological account makes a thing's function a matter of why it is there: an item has the function of doing F if its doing F explains its presence.21 Cummins's systemic account makes a function a capacity that contributes to a containing system's capacity, relative to an analysis somebody gives.22 Millikan grounds proper functions in reproductive history.23 Preston argues that no single account covers the phenomena and defends a pluralism, precisely because things get used for what they were not made for.68 Vermaas and Houkes showed that etiological accounts transfer badly to technical artefacts, since a novel prototype has a function on its first day, before any reproductive history exists;69 their ICE theory grounds ascription jointly in intentions, causal capacities, and the transmission of use plans.24 Chandrasekaran and Josephson catalogued the several senses in which devices are said to have functions and showed that each supports a different kind of reasoning.70 Gero's function-behaviour-structure schema, and its situated successor, model designing as transformations among expected behaviour, structure, and derived behaviour, with function ascribed to expected behaviour.7172 Kitamura and Mizoguchi built a functional ontology intended to make the knowledge reusable across domains.73 Borgo and colleagues gave the whole territory a formal treatment inside a foundational ontology.747576

An engineering ontology must choose. What is remarkable is that at least one has chosen in public, with a changelog entry. IOF Core's 202401 release moved the term artifact function into Core, renamed it designed function, and redefined it: rather than a function of some material artefact, it became a function prescribed by some design specification.40 Read that again as philosophy. The grounding of function was moved from the object to the specification. Wright's question — in virtue of what does this thing have this function? — received an answer, and the answer shipped as a version bump in a manufacturing ontology.

The next distinction is the one every failure report presupposes. A pump with a spalled bearing has the function of circulating coolant and cannot circulate coolant. If the graph carries a single hasFunction predicate, the modeller must either delete the edge — in which case nothing is broken, since a thing with no function cannot fail to discharge it — or keep it, in which case the graph asserts a capability the machine demonstrably lacks. Neither is acceptable, and the resolution is simply that these are two predicates: what the thing is for, and what it can currently do.776 Defectiveness is the case where the first holds and the second does not. This is the entire content of the word, and no formalism supplies it for free.

Then the brick. A brick used as a doorstop has a designed function it is not discharging and an assigned function nobody designed. Preston's pluralism exists to handle exactly this, and it is not an edge case in industry: plant is routinely operated outside its original envelope, and whether a given use is sanctioned is precisely what a deviation report records. Collapsing designed function, assigned function, capability, use and affordance into one predicate does not simplify the model. It destroys the distinctions on which liability and certification turn.787980 The analytic literature on artefact kinds bears on which of these predicates is even a candidate: Dipert's hierarchy makes an object an artefact when an agent intends its modification to be recognised as such;81 Houkes and Meijers set out the hard problem of how the intentional and structural descriptions can be jointly true of one object without either reducing to the other;82 and the volume edited by Margolis and Laurence collects the state of the debate.83 Simondon, writing decades earlier, described technical objects as developing by concretisation, in which separately functioning parts converge on parts that discharge several functions at once84 — which is, incidentally, a direct argument against any encoding that assumes one component bears one function.

Finally, modality, which is where description logic runs out most completely. A standby pump that has never run is a functional component of a redundant pair, and the safety case counts it. Nothing it has ever done supports that count. The claim is counterfactual: under conditions that have not obtained, it would circulate coolant. Engineering claims are overwhelmingly of this form — rated capacities, design margins, failure modes, proof-test intervals — and OWL has no modal operators.25 Every dispositional predicate in an engineering graph is a flattened modality, and the flattening loses the conditions under which the disposition would manifest unless they are separately reified. A capability asserted without its conditions is not a weaker claim than the modal one; it is a different and less useful claim.

KGE-440Commitments V: requirement and satisfaction

The last commitment is the one with the sharpest result behind it, and it is a result from requirements engineering rather than from philosophy.

Zave and Jackson showed that requirements are statements about the environment, that specifications are statements about phenomena shared between the environment and the machine, and that the two are connected only by domain knowledge — so that correctness is not a property of the specification but an entailment: the specification together with the domain assumptions entails the requirement.85 Jackson's problem frames and van Lamsweerde's goal-oriented method develop the separation into method.8687 Parnas's criterion for module decomposition belongs to the same family: a module is not a step in the processing but a design decision hidden behind an interface, which makes componenthood a matter of what is concealed rather than what is contained.88

Now consider what a knowledge graph normally does with this. It writes :CoolingSystem-C1 :satisfies :Req-R14 and moves on. The edge is a two-place fact, believed because it was written. It cannot say why satisfaction holds. It cannot notice when a domain assumption has lapsed — when the ambient temperature that the whole argument presupposed stops being below forty degrees. It cannot distinguish a claim that was verified by test from one that was asserted by a project manager under schedule pressure.

Satisfaction is an entailment, and if the graph is to carry engineering claims at all then the entailment and its premises must be in the graph: requirement, specification, domain assumptions, the argument, and its status.89 This is more expensive than an edge by roughly the factor the fourth experiment measures, and it is the difference between a graph that records compliance and a graph that can lose it.

KGE-500What the formalisms force

Stand back from the individual commitments and look at the tools.

The choice that matters most is not RDF against property graphs, though that is the one usually debated. It is whether the qualifiers on a relation are inside the logic or outside it, and the fourth experiment prices the alternatives on a single fact: that pump P101 was part of cooling system C1 between two dates, according to a named configuration record, asserted by a named organisation, with a stated confidence.

A plain triple carries none of the five qualifiers. RDF 1.1 standard reification carries all five at ten statements.9 The W3C n-ary relation pattern, which makes the relation itself an entity, costs eight.90 A named graph costs seven and makes the graph the unit of provenance. RDF 1.2's triple terms, now at Candidate Recommendation, cost seven and give the occurrence an identity of its own.9192 The singleton-property pattern and RDF-star were proposed to reduce exactly this overhead and were the direct ancestors of the 1.2 work.939495 Faithful RDF encodings span seven to ten statements, a spread of 1.43×, and every one of them costs at least seven times a bare triple.

Wikidata's design is instructive here because it faced the problem at scale and chose. Its statements carry qualifiers and references as first-class structure rather than as edge attributes, precisely so that a claim can record who made it and under what conditions it holds;96 the study of how best to reify that model in RDF found the choice materially affects query performance and expressivity.95 Schema.org went the other way, keeping the vocabulary deliberately shallow on the grounds that adoption matters more than precision, and it was right for its purpose.97 The purposes differ and so should the encodings; what is not defensible is choosing without noticing that a choice was available.

A labelled property graph edge costs one, and carries all five qualifiers as edge properties. This looks like a decisive win and is not, for a reason that has nothing to do with performance. The five properties are not statements. Nothing can be said about them, no reasoner can act on them, and no query can ask which of them is disputed or who asserted them. The saving is exactly the size of the part of the fact that has been moved outside the logic. Both standardised graph query languages preserve this: neither GQL98 nor SQL/PGQ99 extends the property-graph model with hyperedges or with relationships between relationships, so the binary edge is not an implementation limitation that a later release might lift but a feature of the standardised data model.100101102 That GQL is the first new ISO database query language since SQL in 1987, and that it arrived in 2024 with the binary edge intact, is a fair measure of how settled the commitment is.

The second forced choice is expressiveness against decidability, and here the tradition is unusually explicit about what it has given up. Description logics are the decidable fragments of first-order logic, obtained by restriction: binary predicates only, no free variables, careful control of quantifier nesting.10325 SROIQ, the logic underlying OWL 2 DL, is the outcome of a deliberate campaign to push expressiveness as far as decidability allows.104 The price is visible: satisfiability in SROIQ is N2ExpTime-complete, which Kazakov established by showing it strictly harder than its predecessor.29 The profiles exist because that price is unaffordable for most applications, and each profile is a different bargain: EL for large terminologies at polynomial cost, QL for query rewriting into SQL, RL for rule engines.28 Choosing a profile is choosing which of your commitments you are willing to state.

And a great many engineering commitments are not statable in any of them. Modality is not. Default reasoning is not. Anti-rigidity is not. The three-place relation x is a component of y during interval t is not, except by reifying it into an object, which is what the fluent pattern does and what it costs.62 Rules recover some of this ground: SWRL adds Horn-like implications on top of OWL, at the price of undecidability unless restricted to DL-safe rules whose variables bind only to named individuals.30105 The restriction is the point. Every step up the ladder is a decision to give something up.

The third forced choice is the shape of the schema itself, and this is where the fifteen commitments assemble into something usable. The companion article's eight strata — artefact, system, component, structure, behaviour, function, requirement, purpose — map onto a schema directly.6 What the encoding exercise adds is the relation set, which has to be larger and more differentiated than the intuitive one, and partitioned by the algebra each family obeys.

SysML v2 is worth noting alongside this, because it arrives at a comparable partition from the modelling side rather than the ontology side: its metamodel separates parts, ports, connections, actions, states, requirements and constraints as distinct kinds of element with distinct composition rules.106 Two communities that do not much talk to each other have converged on a similar carving, which is weak evidence that the partition is in the subject matter rather than in the tooling.

Six families, and the partition is not cosmetic. Mereological relations compose within a family and not across it; that is the content of the first experiment. Causal relations compose but are not symmetric and support counterfactual queries the others do not. Functional relations are intention-dependent and therefore carry an agent and a specification. Normative relations carry a status and an argument, not merely a truth value. Historical relations are the ones that make identity through change determinable. Structural relations are the ones that classical mereology throws away. A single undifferentiated relatedTo — which is what unconstrained extraction produces — collapses all six, and the eighth experiment measures what that costs at query time.

KGE-600An ontology changelog, read as metaphysics

The strongest evidence for the thesis is not an argument. It is a release history.

The Industrial Ontologies Foundry, a unit of the Open Applications Group, maintains a suite of manufacturing ontologies built on Basic Formal Ontology.38107 Its releases are public, versioned, and accompanied by notes written for engineers. Read those notes as philosophy and they resolve into a sequence of positions adopted, tested and revised.40108

The first release admitted event and state as primitive terms with provisional formalisations, explicitly to solicit feedback — which is to say, it left a question about basic categories open and said so. Release 202401 imported Allen's interval algebra with OWL property chains and SWRL rules, settling how change would be represented. The same release introduced gain of role and loss of role as processes, on the stated ground that the top-level ontology cannot express when a bearer starts or stops bearing a role; that is a metaphysical gap identified, named, and patched with two new occurrent classes. And the same release redefined function as prescribed by a design specification rather than possessed by an artefact.

Release 202502 recorded a known issue: the representation cannot yet distinguish duration from other temporal characteristics, and an interim adjustment was made to avoid overstating the formal semantics. Release 202601 split isMadeOfAtSomeTime and isMadeOfAtAllTime out of parthood in order to distinguish material composition from part-whole structure — which is the constitution-is-not-identity debate, arriving in a manufacturing ontology as two new object properties.109 Release 202602 added denoter and classifier to Core: an account of what a name is and what a category is, shipped in May 2026.

The same pattern appears wherever an engineering domain has been seriously formalised. The maintenance ontologies built in and alongside the IOF suite had to decide what a maintenance activity is, what makes an item the item whose history is being recorded, and how a functional failure differs from a physical one — and found that answering those questions was the precondition for the data quality they were built to improve.110111

Nothing in that sequence is presented as philosophy and all of it is. The notes are written in the register of a changelog because that is what it is: a record of an engineering organisation discovering, by building, that it could not proceed without taking positions on constitution, on roles, on the grounding of function, and on the representation of time — and taking them, and versioning them, and in one case declining to take one because the semantics were not yet good enough to claim.

The thesis says that knowledge graphs are a place where metaphysical assumptions become computationally concrete. This is what that looks like from the inside. It looks like a release note.

KGE-650Four top-level ontologies, all conformant

A natural objection at this point is that the problem is solved, or nearly so, because the field has standardised. It has, and the shape of the standardisation makes the opposite point.

ISO/IEC 21838 is a multipart standard for top-level ontologies. Part 1 specifies what it takes to be one.112 Part 2 standardises Basic Formal Ontology.41 Part 3 standardises DOLCE, the Descriptive Ontology for Linguistic and Cognitive Engineering.113114 Part 4 standardises TUpper, built out of the Process Specification Language among other modules.115116 Part 5, standardising the Unified Foundational Ontology, reached Draft International Standard and entered its enquiry ballot in June 2026.117118

These are not the only candidates — SUMO has been in the field since 2001 and has a substantial user base of its own119 — but they are the ones the standard has certified.

So there are four — shortly, on current progress, five — top-level ontologies, each conformant to the same requirements standard, each axiomatised in OWL 2 and Common Logic, each a legitimate foundation for a domain ontology. And they disagree.

BFO is realist and three-dimensionalist: it draws a hard line between continuants and occurrents, holds that universals are in the world, and is built to describe entities rather than conceptualisations of them.107120 DOLCE is explicitly descriptive and cognitively biased, designed to capture the categories underlying natural language and human commonsense rather than to carve nature at its joints.114121 UFO grew out of conceptual modelling and takes qualities, modes and relators seriously in a way the others do not, supplying the semantics behind OntoUML.122 TUpper is a modular union of independently developed theories rather than a single harmonised carving.115

These are not stylistic differences. Whether functions are real properties of objects or projections of an analyst's interest is a question on which BFO and DOLCE take recognisably different lines, and the answer changes what an engineering ontology built on each can say. A standard that certifies four mutually incompatible answers as conformant is not a standard that has settled the question. It is a standard that has correctly declined to.

The practical consequence is the one this article keeps returning to. A domain ontology inherits its metaphysics from its top-level choice, that choice is usually made on grounds of tooling and community rather than doctrine,123 and the inherited commitments then propagate into every downstream graph and every query run against it. The IOF chose BFO and therefore inherited a role apparatus that could not say when a role began, which it then had to extend.40 That is the mechanism, and it operates whether or not anyone is watching.

KGE-700GraphRAG, and what it actually exploits

The second half of the thesis concerns retrieval, and it needs the same care as the first.

The motivation for retrieval is the well-documented tendency of language models to produce fluent, confident and unfounded output, a failure mode with a substantial taxonomy of its own.124125 Retrieval-augmented generation grounds a language model's output in documents fetched at inference time.126 The retrieval step is normally similarity in an embedding space, sometimes with a lexical component.127128 The known limitation is that similarity is a poor proxy for relevance when the answer is distributed across documents that do not resemble the question.

GraphRAG, as introduced by Edge and colleagues, builds an LLM-derived knowledge graph over a corpus, partitions it into a hierarchy of communities using Leiden,129130 summarises each community, and answers global questions by a map-reduce over community summaries while answering local questions by retrieving around specific entities.3 The variants multiply: LightRAG's dual-level retrieval,131 RAPTOR's recursive summarisation tree,132 HippoRAG's associative memory analogy,133 G-Retriever's subgraph construction,134 and the reasoning-over-graphs family that has the model walk paths explicitly.135136 LazyGraphRAG defers the expensive indexing until query time to cut cost.137 The surveys are already substantial.20138

What is the ontological content of any of this? It is easy to overstate. Most GraphRAG systems do not use a formal ontology at all. They extract entities and relations with a language model, in open vocabulary, and the resulting graph has no schema in the OWL sense. Calling that a knowledge graph in the same breath as a BFO-aligned industrial ontology stretches the term past usefulness.19139

But something real is going on, and the sixth and eighth experiments isolate it.

The sixth experiment poses a question a plant engineer would ask — why can Plant A no longer satisfy requirement R14? — against a twelve-document corpus. Six documents form the causal chain that constitutes the answer, running from the requirement through the cooling duty, the circulation function, the withdrawn pump, the vibration signature, and finally to a blocked grease line. Four documents are lexical distractors: they say Plant A, requirement, R14 and satisfy in almost the question's own words and contain no explanatory content. Two are ordinary corpus noise.

A TF-IDF cosine retriever puts all four distractors in the top four. Lexical recall of the chain is 0% at k=3 and 33.3% at k=6. The document containing the answer ranks tenth of twelve. A typed traversal from the requirement node reaches the entire chain at six hops, at 75% precision.

The point of the experiment is not that traversal beats similarity; similarity has been given an easy corpus to fail on. The point is the shape of the failure. The answer document shares no content word with the question. It says grease line blocked; the question says requirement. The gap between them is not lexical, and it is not semantic either — a better embedding does not know that lubrication is upstream of a requirement, because nothing in the language of either document says so. The gap is structural. It is exactly six typed edges, and the only way to cross it is to have the edges.

The eighth experiment then asks what it costs to cross it. Between the requirement and the root cause there are, in an untyped graph that also carries sixteen perfectly reasonable co-mention edges, 101 simple paths within a six-hop budget. In the typed graph, restricted to the seven relations that carry explanatory load, there is exactly one. That is a 101× reduction in candidate explanations, and the ratio worsens as the budget grows: 236 to 1 at eight hops.

Two details in that result matter more than the headline. First, only 23.3% of the edges carry explanatory load; the rest are true, extracted in good faith, and generate every one of the spurious candidates. Second, 50 of the 101 untyped paths are shorter than the true explanation. A shortest-path heuristic over an untyped graph does not merely have more work to do. It actively prefers a wrong answer.

This is the mechanism the thesis names. GraphRAG's advantage, where it has one, comes from being able to walk relations rather than resemblances, and the value of walking a relation depends entirely on the relation having a type that means something. The type system is the ontology. Where there is no ontology there is no type system, and the traversal is walking co-occurrence, which is similarity by another route.

The seventh experiment closes the loop by showing what happens when nothing constrains the vocabulary.

Four underlying relations, extracted without a schema, come back as twenty-four surface forms — six per relation. Of 536 extracted edges, 22.2% are stated in the inverse direction: caused_by alongside causes, has_part alongside part_of. A query written against any single form recovers 33.02% of the edges. Repairing this after the fact needs twenty mapping rules, of which six require a decision about which direction the canonical relation runs — a decision about the relation, not about the words.

Which is where the ontology earns its keep, and it is not where the promotional literature usually puts it. The ontology is not adding meaning on the way out. It is constraining what may be written on the way in. Its function is to stop an extractor from inventing two dozen near-synonyms and calling the result structure. That is a humbler role than the one usually claimed for formal ontology, and it is the one the measurements support.

KGE-750What the evidence does and does not show

The eight experiments below are demonstrations, not benchmarks. They show that specific consequences follow from specific encoding decisions on graphs small enough to verify by hand. They show nothing about how large systems behave, and any reader who takes 101× as a performance claim has misread them. For that question the published record is the evidence, and it is more equivocal than the enthusiasm around graph retrieval would suggest.

The most careful recent study builds a benchmark specifically to isolate when graph structure helps, on the grounds that existing benchmarks conflate retrieval difficulty with reasoning difficulty and are built on corpora too sparse in conceptual hierarchy to test the claim at all.1 Its findings run in both directions. On simple fact retrieval, ordinary retrieval matches or beats graph methods, and the paper's explanation is that graph processing introduces redundant context that degrades answer quality on questions that never needed it. On complex reasoning, contextual summarisation and creative generation, graph methods pull clearly ahead. On retrieval quality the same split appears: reranked ordinary retrieval achieves 83.2% evidence recall on level-one questions, while on the harder levels HippoRAG reaches 87.9% to 90.9%.

The cost side is stark. On the same corpus, vanilla retrieval averaged 879 tokens per prompt; Microsoft GraphRAG's local search averaged 38,707 — some forty-four times more — and its global search averaged over three hundred thousand.1 An independent systematic evaluation reports graph retrieval scoring 13.4% below vanilla retrieval on natural questions and 16.6% below on questions needing current knowledge, and a unified-framework analysis finds a 4.5% gain in reasoning depth on multi-hop questions bought with 2.3× the latency.2140

Three cautions follow, and they bear directly on the thesis.

The confound is unresolved. Graph methods retrieve more context and impose more structure. When they win, the published evaluations generally cannot separate which of the two did the work. It is entirely possible that a substantial part of the advantage is simply more retrieved text, which would be an argument for larger context windows rather than for ontology.

Extraction error is not modelled. Every experiment here assumes the graph is right. A graph built by a language model over a technical corpus will contain relations that are wrong, missing, or subtly misdirected,141138 and a typed traversal over a graph with a wrong edge produces a confident, well-formed, structurally coherent, wrong explanation. The failure mode of structured retrieval is not vagueness. It is plausible specificity, which is worse.

The engineering case is not the benchmark case. Every published GraphRAG evaluation the author is aware of runs on narrative or biomedical text. An engineering corpus differs in ways that plausibly favour the graph — a stable vocabulary, real reference designations, existing schemas in ISO 15926 and IEC 81346 form, and causal chains that genuinely span many documents14236 — and in ways that plausibly disfavour it, chiefly that the consequences of a confident wrong answer are larger. Nobody has run the experiment. Until someone does, the honest position is that the mechanism is demonstrable and the magnitude is unknown.

KGE-900Objections and replies

The deflationary objection: this is just data modelling

The objection says that everything above is ordinary schema design dressed in philosophical vocabulary, and that calling a decision about transitivity metaphysics adds nothing to calling it a modelling choice.

Half of this is right and the half that is right is important. These decisions are made by data modellers, on schedules, without reading Simons or Fine, and they are made competently a great deal of the time. But the objection proves too much. If the decisions are merely conventional, then it should not be possible to get them wrong except relative to a stipulation — and it plainly is. A change-impact scope that includes another tenant's billing service is not a different convention; it is a mistake, and it is a mistake because parthood does not work that way. The measure of whether a question is substantive is whether competent practitioners converge, under challenge, on an answer they will defend. On these questions they do, which is why the conformance suite in the companion article works at all.6 The vocabulary is not a decoration. It is the vocabulary in which the mistake can be diagnosed rather than merely noticed.

The Carnapian objection: these are practical questions

A sharper version grants the substance and denies the metaphysics. Following Carnap, one might hold that questions internal to a framework have answers by the framework's rules and questions about which framework to adopt are practical, not factual.15 Choosing to quantify over functions is choosing a language, not discovering an entity.

This article does not need to refute that, and does not try. Its claim is compatible with full-blooded Carnapian deflationism, because its claim is about where the choice becomes concrete rather than about whether the choice tracks a fact. Even for the strict Carnapian, the framework choice has consequences that propagate, that can be evaluated for adequacy against practice, and that are extremely expensive to reverse once a decade of data has been written in the framework's terms.123 That is enough for everything argued here. The thesis is about the concreteness of the commitment, not its truthmaker.

The bitter-lesson objection: scale will dissolve this

The most serious objection is Sutton's.143 The historical pattern in artificial intelligence is that methods leveraging computation defeat methods leveraging human-encoded knowledge, and hand-built ontologies are the paradigm case of the losing side. Cyc is the cautionary tale.144 A sufficiently large model may simply learn the structure that ontologies encode, making the encoding effort a detour.

The objection has a data-centric sibling worth stating alongside it: that for many problems, more data with a simpler model beats less data with a richer one, and that effort spent on representation is usually better spent on corpus.145 Both versions have a strong historical record.

Three replies, in increasing order of force. First, the objection is about where structure comes from, not whether it is needed — and the seventh experiment shows that a model asked to extract structure without constraint produces twenty-four names for four relations, a fifth of them pointing backwards. Whatever the model has learned, it does not by default produce a consistent vocabulary, and consistency is what makes traversal possible. Second, the ontology's role in the argument above is not to supply knowledge but to constrain writing. That is a much smaller claim than Cyc's, and it does not compete with scale. Third, and most important: the audit requirement does not go away. When an engineering system asserts that a plant satisfies a requirement, somebody must be able to determine on what grounds. A model that has internalised the structure without exposing it satisfies the reasoning requirement and fails the accountability one, and in certified domains the second is not optional.146147 There is a further point, made sharply by Bender and Koller, that form alone does not fix reference,148 and by Mitchell, that the abstraction and analogy-making which structure supports remain conspicuously weak in systems trained on form.149 Neither argument is decisive, and both cut against the assumption that scale dissolves the representational question by itself.

The pragmatic objection: nobody will pay for this

The costs measured above are real. Seven to ten statements for one qualified fact; quadratic identity judgements; a closure axiom per item audited; role changes reified as processes. Multiply by a plant's worth of equipment and the encoding becomes the project.

The reply is that the cost is not optional, only deferrable. Every commitment listed here is discharged whether or not it is made explicitly: the default is a position, not an abstention. The choice is between paying at modelling time, with the decision recorded, and paying at query time, when a compliance report returns four fewer items than it should and nobody can say why. What the plates do offer is triage. Fifteen commitments do not all matter in every domain: a static asset register can live with tenseless triples, and a graph that never crosses organisational boundaries can live without explicit boundaries. The ledger is a checklist for deciding which ones are load-bearing in a given system, not a mandate to encode all of them.

The formalist objection: the ladder is unfair

Placing ten of fifteen commitments at rung 5 or above looks like an indictment of OWL, and one might object that this compares a formalism against requirements it never claimed to meet. Description logics were designed for decidable subsumption reasoning over terminologies, and they do that well.25

Correct, and the ladder is not an indictment. It is a measurement of fit between a formalism and a domain, and the finding is precisely that the fit is poor — which is useful to know, and which is not the formalism's fault. The constructive reading is that engineering knowledge graphs should expect to run a rule layer and a validation layer alongside the ontology rather than inside it, and should say so, rather than pretending that the OWL fragment is the whole model. SHACL's separation of validation from entailment is the right architecture for exactly this reason.54105 What is not defensible is the common practice of writing the OWL, running a closed-world query engine over it, and describing the result as an inference.

The eliminativist objection: there are no systems

Finally, the position that there are no composite objects at all — no pumps, only simples arranged pumpwise.150 If van Inwagen is right, the entire exercise mislocates the problem.

The reply is the one the companion article gives at greater length.6 Whatever the correct fundamental ontology, engineering practice quantifies over systems, requires their identity conditions, and enforces those conditions through certification regimes with legal force. An account that cannot state what a certification authority does is not a revisionary insight about engineering. It is a failure to describe it. The question here is which individuation an engineering system must implement, and that question is untouched by whether the individuated things are fundamental.

KGE-950Conclusion

Return to the engineer with the deadline and the two screens.

The line they typed was not a philosophical statement and they were not wrong to type it. Most of the time the defaults are survivable, and a system that waited on the resolution of the mereology literature would never ship. What the ledger below establishes is narrower and more actionable: that fifteen decisions were taken by that line and its neighbours, that none of the fifteen can be stated in a bare graph or in RDFS, that two thirds of them sit beyond OWL 2 DL, and that each of them changes what some query returns.

The thesis holds, in the form in which it was stated. Knowledge graphs are a place where metaphysical and ontological assumptions become computationally concrete — forced by the formalism, executed by the machinery, and auditable by a reader — and GraphRAG is one way of exploiting the resulting structure. The evidence for the first half is a release history in which a production manufacturing ontology adopts, revises and versions positions on constitution, roles, time and the grounding of function, in public, as changelog entries. The evidence for the second half is that the value of traversing a graph is entirely a function of its edges having types that mean something, which is a way of saying that the ontology is what makes the traversal worth doing.

Neither half licenses optimism about outcomes. Graph retrieval underperforms ordinary retrieval on easy questions, costs a great deal more, and has not been evaluated on the engineering corpora where its advantages would be largest. The mechanism is demonstrable; the magnitude is unmeasured; the failure mode is plausible specificity rather than vagueness, which is the worse failure mode to have in a domain where the answers get acted on.

What the exercise does license is a change in where the effort goes. The usual model of ontology work is that it adds meaning to data on the way out, at query time, for the benefit of a reasoner. The measurements here point the other way. The ontology's load-bearing contribution is at the point of writing: it stops an extractor emitting six names for one relation, it forces the question of whether this parthood composes, it makes somebody decide whether the record designates or merely records. Those are constraints on inscription, not enrichments of retrieval, and they are worth their cost for a reason that has nothing to do with retrieval quality. They are what makes the artefact answerable to a reader who was not there when it was written.

Which is, in the end, the same standard the rest of engineering already meets. A drawing carries a revision, a tolerance and a signature because somebody will have to determine, years later and without access to the draughtsman, what was claimed and on what authority. A knowledge graph that records only that P101 is part of C1 has met a lower standard than a hand-drawn general arrangement from 1957. That is a solvable problem, the solutions are priced in the plates below, and the price is the honest one: what it costs to say what you actually meant.

Read the distribution. Not one of the fifteen is statable in a bare graph or in RDFS — which is to say, not one of them survives the encoding that most production knowledge graphs actually use. 10 of 15 require rules or lie outside any decidable logic. The five at rung 6 — absence, rigidity, persistence, assigned function, and counterfactual capability — are not hard to state in OWL. The constructive reading is not that the stack is inadequate. It is that an engineering knowledge graph is properly a layered artefact: an OWL terminology for what OWL does well, a rule layer for the three-place and temporally indexed relations, a validation layer that makes closed-world claims about datasets without pretending they are claims about the world, and an explicit record of the commitments that none of these can hold. The last of those is the one nobody builds, and it is the cheapest.
