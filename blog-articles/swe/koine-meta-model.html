Every organisation that takes software engineering seriously eventually tries to write down what it consists of. The attempt produces a skills matrix, a ticket taxonomy, a technology radar, an architecture repository with a folder for each kind of thing. The tree fills, and the filling is the failure: the more complete the list becomes, the more of it is wrong, because the things it lists were never leaves in the first place. This article proposes a different object. Instead of a list of what software engineering contains, it proposes a language in which any of it can be said, and it argues that the second is the only form in which the first could ever have been finished.

Abstract

Attempts to describe software engineering exhaustively fail for a structural reason: a task, a system or a technology is a conjunction of independent choices, and the number of distinct conjunctions grows as the product of the vocabularies while any enumerating taxonomy must grow with it. This article proposes Koine, a general semantic meta-model of software engineering built on the opposite principle: exhaustiveness of the representation language rather than of the list of things represented. Koine has eight layers: borrowed foundational commitments (endurant, perdurant, type, role, relator, disposition); fifteen core primitives; a vocabulary of thirty-nine relations in nine families, each with declared formal properties; thirteen orthogonal facets held as extensible controlled vocabularies; a task frame with a strict type/instance split and recursive decomposition; a compositional task grammar; a knowledge layer; and views defined as projections of one graph. The article locates Koine against UML, SysML v2, the architecture description languages, ArchiMate 3.2, ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022, SPEM 2.0, Essence 1.2, SWEBOK V4 and the UFO-grounded SEON network, gives it a formalisation in OWL 2, SHACL, SKOS and PROV-O, and tests its central claims in a runnable laboratory of eight deterministic experiments. On the fixtures used, eight facets holding 73 terms express 36,578,304 fully specified tasks; a single-parent tree keeps 24.1% of what is true of twenty ordinary engineering entities; the two perdurant primitives, Task and Process, have the highest relational valence of the fifteen; and twelve competency questions are all answerable against the graph while its taxonomy-only shadow answers one.

The organising question: what must a vocabulary be able to say, if the things that have not yet been thought of are to be sayable in it without changing the vocabulary?

1The problem with the exhaustive list

There are two reasons a list of software-engineering things cannot be completed, and they are different reasons. The first is combinatorial. A task such as optimise the customer-transaction query in the production PostgreSQL database so that p95 latency falls below 100 ms is not one thing; it is the conjunction of an action (optimise), a target (a query), a purpose (performance), a technology (PostgreSQL), an environment (production) and a constraint (p95 < 100 ms). Each of those choices was made from a vocabulary that could have supplied a different word. If a description scheme wants a name for every such task, the number of names it needs is the product of the sizes of the vocabularies, and the product is large long before the vocabularies are. The laboratory in §15 holds eight facets with 73 terms between them, none of them exotic; the number of fully specified tasks those terms can express is 36,578,304, and if a task may leave facets unfilled it is 88,451,999. A nested path taxonomy that gave each fully specified task its own node would need 40,056,170 nodes, which is 548,715 nodes for every term in the vocabulary that generated them. Adding one more technology to the technology facet adds 3,048,192 expressible tasks; the compositional scheme records the addition as one line.

The second reason is that the things being listed are not leaves. Kafka is a messaging platform. It is also an event-streaming platform, a distributed system, an open-source project, an Apache project and a durable commit log, and each of those is a defensible answer to the question of what kind of thing it is. A tree gives each node one parent. Whatever parent is chosen, the other facts are not stored anywhere, and every query that would have used them returns nothing. Across the twenty ordinary entities in the laboratory's fixture, from Kafka and PostgreSQL to a latency requirement and the Adapter pattern, there are 83 defensible is-a assertions, a mean of 4.15 per entity. A single-parent tree keeps 20 of them (24.1%) and discards 63 (75.9%). There are 1,811,939,328,000 distinct ways of choosing which 20 to keep, each as defensible as the others, which is the precise sense in which the single hierarchy is arbitrary: not that it is wrong, but that any of 1,811,939,328,000 others would have been exactly as right. The industrial-structuring standard IEC 81346-1 acknowledges the point in its own way by refusing to have one structure at all: it gives a technical system a function aspect, a product aspect, a location aspect and, since the 2022 edition, a type aspect, each a separate tree over the same objects [1].

Brooks distinguished the essential complexity of software, which no representation can remove, from the accidental complexity that a better representation removes [2]. The conjunction is essential: an engineering task really is an action on a target for a purpose under constraints with a technology. What the list adds is accidental: the demand that this conjunction be located at a single address in a single tree whose branching order somebody chose. The remedy is not a bigger list but a grammar. Chomsky's observation that a finite vocabulary and a finite set of rules give a speaker an unbounded set of sentences [3] is the observation that generative capacity, not size, is what makes a vocabulary adequate to a world that keeps producing new things to say; the principle of compositionality, that the meaning of a complex expression is fixed by the meanings of its parts and the way they are combined, is what makes those sentences understood by someone who has never heard them [4].

This article proposes a meta-model built on that principle and gives it a name: Koine. Koine Greek was the common dialect that let speakers of Attic, Ionic, Doric and the rest understand one another across the Hellenistic world without any of those dialects being replaced. That is the relation a semantic meta-model should have to UML, ArchiMate, the architecture description languages, SPEM, Essence and the requirements and quality standards: a shared tongue underneath them, into which each can be translated, rather than a competitor to any of them. The word is also a small act of expectation management. A koine is not a perfect language; it is a working one, adequate to trade.

Two things Koine is not. It is not a body of knowledge. SWEBOK V4 maps software-engineering knowledge into eighteen knowledge areas, three of them new in the 2024 edition (software architecture, software engineering operations and software security), and it is a map of what an engineer should know rather than of what exists [5]. It is not a vocabulary in the dictionary sense either. ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765 defines thousands of terms for systems and software engineering, and defines them one at a time, so that component, requirement and task sit in alphabetical order with nothing in the standard saying how a task bears on a requirement or a component on a system [6]. Koine's claim is that the relations are the point. A vocabulary that cannot say what bears on what has no way of being exhaustive, because the sentences are where the world is.

2What the existing languages already say

A proposal for a common tongue has to begin by taking the dialects seriously. Each of the languages, standards and frameworks below is the answer to a question somebody asked, and the questions are different; the survey's purpose is to show that none of them asked the question this article asks, while several of them supply parts of the answer.

Modelling languages: UML and SysML v2

UML 2.5.1 gives a modelling language: classes, interfaces, components, activities, states, sequences, deployments, packaged as metaclasses whose abstract syntax is itself defined in the Meta Object Facility [7,8]. Its question is how do I model this system? A payment platform can be drawn as a class diagram, a component diagram, a sequence diagram and a deployment diagram, and the four are guaranteed to share a vocabulary because they share a metamodel. What UML does not ask is what a component is such that a component in one model and a component in another are the same kind of thing, nor how a component bears on a requirement, a task or a technology; requirements are not in UML at all except through the SysML profile. SysML v2, with its Kernel Modeling Language, moves closer to the semantic question: OMG adopted the three specifications formally as of 30 June 2025 after two years of finalisation, and KerML gives the language a formal semantics stated in first-order logic with a four-dimensional treatment of temporal and spatial extent [9–11]. A SysML v2 part def, action def and requirement def are definitions with usages, which is the type/instance split Koine also insists on; but SysML v2 is a language for modelling systems, not a vocabulary for describing the engineering of systems, and it has no first-class notion of a task performed by an actor on an artefact for a purpose.

Architecture description languages and architecture description

The ADLs of the 1990s (Wright, Darwin, Acme, later AADL) formalised architecture as components, connectors and configurations, with Allen and Garlan giving connectors a process-algebraic semantics so that a configuration could be checked for deadlock [12]; Medvidovic and Taylor's classification framework fixed the vocabulary the field has used since [13], and AADL carried it into safety-critical embedded systems with a standardised component model [14]. Perry and Wolf had already stated the deeper claim: an architecture is elements plus form plus rationale, and the rationale is what most notations leave out [15]. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010, in its 2022 second edition, does not prescribe a notation at all. It standardises the concepts an architecture description is made of: an entity of interest, stakeholders with concerns, viewpoints that frame concerns, views governed by viewpoints, view components, correspondences between views and, new in the second edition, stakeholder perspectives and architecture aspects [16]. That is a small ontology of description rather than of systems, and it is the origin of Koine's L7. Kruchten's 4+1 model is the same idea in concrete form, four views and the scenarios that tie them together [17]; Zachman's framework had crossed the interrogatives (what, how, where, who, when, why) with perspectives fifteen years before that [18]; TOGAF and the C4 model are the enterprise and the practitioner ends of the same tradition [19,20].

ArchiMate

ArchiMate 3.2 is the most ambitious of the cross-layer languages. It deliberately spans business, application and technology layers and adds motivation elements (stakeholder, driver, goal, requirement, constraint, principle), strategy elements (resource, capability, course of action) and implementation-and-migration elements (work package, deliverable, plateau, gap), with a generic metamodel of relationships that every layer instantiates [21]. It comes closest to Koine's breadth. What it lacks is a foundation that says what its elements are: when Azevedo and colleagues gave ArchiMate's resource and capability a grounding in the Unified Foundational Ontology they found that the language had been using both words for several different kinds of thing, and the well-founded proposal that resulted is the one Koine's Resource and Capability plates adopt [22]. ArchiMate also has no task: its work package is a project-management unit of implementation, not an engineering action with a target and a validation.

Process and method engineering: SPEM, SEMDM, Essence

SPEM 2.0 answered a different question again: how to describe a development method so that it can be tailored and enacted. Its central move, which Koine takes over whole, is the separation of method content from process: a Task Definition, Role Definition and Work Product Definition are reusable knowledge, and a Task Use, Role Use and Work Product Use are their occurrences in a particular process [23]. ISO/IEC 24744 standardised a metamodel for development methodologies on a powertype pattern designed to hold both the method and its enactment in one model [24], and the ontological analysis of that metamodel by Ruy and colleagues found exactly the kinds of ambiguity a foundation exists to remove [25]. Essence, the SEMAT kernel adopted by OMG in 2014 and now at version 1.2, is the most philosophically self-aware of the process standards: it names seven alphas (opportunity, stakeholders, requirements, software system, team, work, way of working) as the things every endeavour has to progress regardless of method, gives each a state machine of health, and defines a language in which practices are composed into methods [26,27]. The alphas are a genuine upper vocabulary for the endeavour; they say nothing about technologies, artefacts as such, data, events or environments, which is where a meta-model of engineering rather than of endeavours has to go further.

Lifecycle, requirements and quality standards

ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 and 15288 define the processes of the software and system lifecycles, each process a set of interrelated activities that transform inputs into outputs [28,29]. ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 defines what a requirement is and how it is elicited, analysed and verified [30]. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 fixes the product-quality model at nine characteristics: functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, interaction capability, reliability, security, maintainability, flexibility and safety [31]. Bass, Clements and Kazman's treatment of quality attributes as the drivers of architectural decisions is the practitioner's bridge between that model and design [32]. These are controlled vocabularies of high quality and narrow scope, and Koine's facets (§8) are built to hold them rather than to replace them.

Ontologies of software engineering

The ontology community has been here before, with results that are instructive in both directions. Calero, Ruiz and Piattini's 2006 collection surveyed ontologies for software engineering and software technology [33]; Happel and Seedorf classified their applications by whether the ontology is used at development time or run time and whether it models the system or the process [34]; Wongthongtham and colleagues built a software-engineering ontology for multi-site development [35]; the Software Ontology models software as a resource for reproducibility in biomedical data analysis [36]; and there are two networks called SEON, the Zurich pyramid of ontologies for software evolution [37] and the Espírito Santo Software Engineering Ontology Network, which is the more directly relevant because it is grounded in a foundational ontology, UFO, with core ontologies for software and software process and domain ontologies for requirements, design, coding and testing [38]. Henderson-Sellers' analysis of how metamodels and ontologies can be made compatible, with a foundational ontology at the metamodel's level of abstraction and domain ontologies at the model's, is the layering Koine adopts [39]; Silva Parreiras, Staab and Winter showed how the two technical spaces can be married in tooling [40], and OMG's Ontology Definition Metamodel gives OWL a home inside MOF so that the marriage has a standard form [41].

Identity and linking

Finally, the supply-chain and lifecycle-integration standards of the last five years supply something none of the modelling languages had: identity. SPDX and CycloneDX give a software bill of materials a standard shape [42,43]; Package-URL, standardised as ECMA-427 in December 2025, names a package independently of its ecosystem with seven components, scheme:type/namespace/name@version?qualifiers#subpath [44,45]; the Software Hash Identifier, ISO/IEC 18670:2025, names a source artefact intrinsically by a Merkle-DAG hash of its content, so that anyone holding the bytes can verify the name without a registry [46]; and OSLC Core 3.0 applies linked-data principles to lifecycle tools so that a requirement in one tool and a change request in another can point at each other [47]. Koine's Artifact and Technology plates lean on these for their identity criteria.

3Taxonomy, ontology, meta-model: which of these is Koine?

The three words are used interchangeably in engineering conversation and mean different things, and the proposal cannot be evaluated until it is clear which of them it is. A taxonomy is a classification: a set of categories arranged by a subsumption relation, usually a tree. It answers what kind of thing is this? with a path. An ontology, in the sense the knowledge-representation community settled on with Gruber's definition, is an explicit specification of a conceptualisation [48]; the definition Guarino, Oberle and Staab analyse and refine, a formal, explicit specification of a shared conceptualisation, adds that the formality is what lets a machine check it and the sharing is what makes it worth having [49], and Uschold and Gruninger laid out the methodology by which such a thing is built and evaluated [50]. An ontology answers what exists, and what are its relations? A metamodel is a model of models: it fixes the constructs from which models in a language are built, and a model conforms to it in Bézivin's sense, the way a sentence conforms to a grammar [51].

Koine is an ontology with a metamodel's job. The distinction that makes this coherent is Atkinson and Kühne's separation of linguistic from ontological instantiation [52,53]. In the MOF four-level architecture, an element of a model at M1 is a linguistic instance of a metaclass at M2 (PaymentService is an instance of Class), and that relation says nothing about what a payment service is. Ontological instantiation is orthogonal: payment-service is an instance of Microservice, which specialises System, which is a Functional Complex, and each of those relations is a claim about the world. A UML metamodel lives on the first axis; an ontology lives on the second; and, as Figure 2 shows, the whole of Koine is one M1 model in MOF's terms (an OWL 2 ontology is a model like any other) while spanning four levels of its own, from foundational categories through the core vocabulary to domain types and finally to instances. Henderson-Sellers drew the same map from the other side: a foundational ontology sits at the metamodel's level of abstraction and a domain ontology at the model's, and the pairs are linked by semantic mappings rather than being the same thing under two names [39].

A representation language carries ontological commitments whether or not it declares them. Quine's criterion is the sharp form of the point: a theory is committed to those entities that its bound variables must range over for the theory to be true, so that to be is to be the value of a variable [54]. A ticket system whose schema has an assignee field and a component field is committed to persons and components and to a relation between a task and each; it merely has not said so. Koine's first job is to make such commitments explicit and to make them the same across the dialects. Wand and Weber gave the engineering discipline its instrument for judging whether a modelling grammar's commitments fit the world: taking Bunge's ontology as the reference [55], they defined construct deficit (something in the world with no construct), construct overload (one construct for several things), construct redundancy (several constructs for one thing) and construct excess (a construct that stands for nothing), and proposed that a grammar be evaluated by the presence and absence of each [56,57]. The evaluation in §16 uses their vocabulary.

Two older lines of work should be named, because Koine is nearer to them than to anything drawn in boxes. Chen's entity-relationship model of 1976 put entities, relationships and the roles entities play in relationships on an equal footing, and its enduring lesson is that the relationships carry the information [58]. Telos, Mylopoulos and colleagues' language for representing knowledge about information systems, treated classes, metaclasses and attributes uniformly as propositions, so that a model could describe its own vocabulary and time could be attached to every assertion [59]. Sowa's synthesis of the logical and philosophical foundations of knowledge representation is the textbook that connects them to the description-logic tradition on which OWL 2 rests [60,61]. Koine is a descendant of the entity-relationship model with the relations given formal properties, of Telos with the type/instance discipline made strict, and of the description-logic ontologies with a grammar of tasks on top.

4Ten design commitments

Koine's architecture follows from ten commitments. They are stated here as rules with the reason for each, because a meta-model whose rules cannot be argued with is a taxonomy wearing a costume.

1. Expressiveness over enumeration

The meta-model must be able to construct new things from existing primitives. Its measure of adequacy is not how many things it lists but whether the next thing an engineer needs to describe can be described without a change to the vocabulary. This is the compositionality principle applied to a description scheme [4]: the meaning of migrate the PostgreSQL database to Aurora with minimal downtime should be recoverable from the meanings of migrate, database, PostgreSQL, Aurora and minimal downtime and from the rule that composes them, by a reader who has never seen that sentence. Enumeration is reserved for the things that genuinely are finite: the primitives, the relation families and the grammar rules.

2. Dimensions, not hierarchies

Classification is by orthogonal facets, each an independent vocabulary, and a thing is described by choosing a term from each facet that applies rather than by locating it in a nesting of them all. This is Ranganathan's faceted classification, introduced with the Colon Classification in 1933 and systematised in the Prolegomena, whose fundamental categories (personality, matter, energy, space, time) were designed so that a subject could be synthesised at the point of need rather than pre-coordinated in a schedule [62]; Broughton's case that facet analysis is the basis of every workable retrieval method makes the same argument for the digital catalogue [63]. In IEC 81346-1 the same principle appears as aspects, with the standard's own notation of = for function, - for product and + for location [1]. The laboratory's second experiment is the arithmetic of the commitment.

3. Relations are first-class

The relationships carry more of the information than the categories, so the relation vocabulary is designed with the same care as the entity vocabulary, and each relation has declared formal properties (transitivity, symmetry, acyclicity, whether it is context-indexed). The result is a knowledge graph in the sense Hogan and colleagues survey, a graph of data intended to accumulate and convey knowledge, with an explicit schema [64], and it is the entity-relationship model with its relationships given properties [58].

4. Type and instance are always both present

Every primitive has a type level and an instance level and the meta-model never lets one stand in for the other. A task type (optimise a database query) is reusable knowledge; a task instance (optimise the customer-transaction query in production PostgreSQL X to bring p95 below 100 ms) is an occurrence. SPEM 2.0's Task Definition and Task Use are this distinction in a standard [23], and Kühne's analysis of the type/instance relation is the reason the distinction cannot be collapsed without losing the ability to say which knowledge is general [53].

5. Roles are separate from their players

A technology is recorded as what it is, and the role it plays in a given task is recorded separately and per task. Kafka is a messaging platform; in this task it is the event broker, in that one the commit log. Steimann's survey of role representation in object-oriented and conceptual modelling assembled the features that make a role unlike a class: a player can take a role up and lay it down without ceasing to exist, and a role depends on a relationship (there is no event broker without something to broker for), which is why a role cannot be a subclass of its player [65]; Masolo and colleagues gave social roles a formal description in DOLCE on exactly those properties [66]. The same holds for people: the release manager is a role, the person is its player, and the two have different identity criteria.

6. Perspectives are projections

The same entity is viewed as a technology, an artefact and a system without any of them being declared its one true category. A view is a query over the graph for a stakeholder concern, not a copy of part of it: the architecture view, the technology view, the requirements view and the security view are projections of one graph and may overlap. This is ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010's viewpoint and view [16] and Kruchten's 4+1 [17] made into a rule about representation rather than about documents.

7. Open world, closed vocabularies

Absence of a statement is not a statement of absence: a system with no recorded dependency on Kafka may still depend on it. This is the open-world assumption OWL 2 inherits from description logic [67], and it is the right default for a description of an engineering practice that nobody has finished describing. Against that, each facet is a controlled vocabulary with a governed extension procedure, held as a SKOS concept scheme in which a new term is a new concept with a preferred label, alternative labels and a broader term rather than an untyped string [68]. Openness is about what may be true; control is about what may be said.

8. Decomposition is recursive

A task may contain tasks, a system systems, an artefact artefacts, and the containment relation is the same relation at every depth. This removes the need to decide in advance whether a unit of work is a project, an epic, a story, a task or a sub-task; those are depths, not kinds. The relation is parthood in Simons' sense, transitive and antisymmetric within a kind, with the well-known caveat that parthood across kinds (a component's part of a system, a task's part of a process) is a family of relations rather than one [69].

9. The foundation is borrowed

Koine does not invent its own answer to what an object, an event, a role or a disposition is. It borrows those answers from the foundational ontologies that have spent twenty years on them: primarily UFO, with stated alignments to BFO and DOLCE [70–72]. The special issue in which those three were laid side by side with GFO, GUM, TUpper and YAMATO is the evidence that the foundational choices are contested but tractable [73], and ISO/IEC 21838 has since certified four of them as conformant top-level ontologies, with a fifth in ballot, which is a stronger statement of maturity than any single research programme could make [74].

10. Identity and provenance are in the vocabulary

Every primitive has an identity criterion stated on its plate, and where a standard exists for the identifier the meta-model uses it: the Software Hash Identifier for source artefacts [46], the Package-URL for packages [44], and PROV-O's wasGeneratedBy, wasDerivedFrom and wasAttributedTo for how any artefact came to be [75]. A meta-model that cannot say whether two records name the same thing cannot be checked, and a vocabulary that cannot be checked is not a specification.

Two disciplines from ontology engineering are applied throughout rather than listed as commitments. Ontology design patterns, in Gangemi and Presutti's sense of reusable modelling solutions for recurring problems, are used for the role, the situation and the type/instance split rather than modelling each afresh [76]. OntoClean's meta-properties (rigidity, identity, unity, dependence) are used as the test of whether a primitive is a kind or a role: Task is rigid, since a task cannot stop being a task and survive; event broker is anti-rigid, since Kafka can stop being one and remain Kafka [77].

Together the ten commitments fix an architecture of eight layers, each expressed in the vocabulary of the layers beneath it. A change at L1 or L2 is a change to the language and should be rare and argued; a change at L3 through L6 is ordinary content and should be routine.

5L0: foundational commitments borrowed, not invented

The bottom layer says what kinds of thing there are at all. Koine takes the distinctions from the Unified Foundational Ontology, whose 2022 formalisation is the reference [70] and whose origins lie in Guizzardi's ontological foundations for structural conceptual models [78], and states for each how it maps to Basic Formal Ontology [71,79] and DOLCE [72], so that a Koine graph can be re-grounded in either without loss. The mapping matters institutionally as well as intellectually: ISO/IEC 21838 now certifies BFO (part 2, 2021), DOLCE (part 3, 2023) and TUpper (part 4, 2023) as conformant top-level ontologies against the requirements of part 1, and UFO is part 5, registered as a draft international standard in April 2026 with its ballot initiated on 8 June 2026 [74,80–83]. A meta-model grounded in one of these inherits a formal semantics that has been audited by more than its authors.

Endurants and perdurants

The first cut is between things that persist through time and are wholly present at each moment (endurants: a system, a component, an artefact, a person, a database) and things that happen and are extended in time (perdurants: a task, a process, an event, a deployment). BFO calls them continuants and occurrents; DOLCE calls them endurants and perdurants; UFO uses the latter names and subdivides endurants into substantials (objects that exist in their own right) and moments (aspects that depend on a bearer: qualities, dispositions, relators). The cut is not a taxonomic nicety. It fixes which relations a primitive can enter: an endurant participates in a perdurant, a perdurant has temporal parts, and the relation between a task and the artefact it produces is a participation relation with a direction, not a containment. The laboratory's first experiment shows the consequence: the two structured perdurant primitives, Task and Process, are the only two that can enter all nine relation families, because a happening is the thing that agents perform, that produces and consumes, that satisfies or violates, that precedes and follows, and that is validated.

Universals and individuals; kinds, roles and phases

Every endurant primitive comes as a universal (a type) and as individuals (instances), and UFO's theory of types is what lets Koine keep the two apart with discipline rather than convention. A kind supplies an identity criterion and is rigid: every instance of System is necessarily a System. A role is anti-rigid and relationally dependent: event broker, release manager, persistent store are roles, and an individual plays them in the context of a relator, which in Koine is always a task or a process. A phase is anti-rigid but not relationally dependent: a service in production and the same service decommissioned. Technology is the one primitive that is deliberately both a universal and an individual, because Kafka is a class of installations and a thing with a licence, a release history and a project governance; OWL 2 punning is the formal device that permits this without leaving the decidable fragment (§13).

Relators, qualities and dispositions

A relator is a moment that connects two or more endurants and founds a material relation: an employment connects a person to an organisation, a deployment connects an artefact to an environment, a satisfaction connects a system to a requirement with the evidence that grounds it. Koine's evidence-bearing relations (satisfies, violates, validatedBy) are relators in this sense, which is why they can carry provenance. A quality is a measurable aspect of an individual (the latency of a service, the size of a table) and a disposition is a capacity that is realised in processes of a type (the capability of a platform to process payments), which is BFO's disposition and function and UFO's function; Azevedo and colleagues' grounding of ArchiMate's capability and resource in exactly these categories is adopted whole [22].

Information objects and artefacts

Data is an information object, content that can be carried by many carriers and copied without loss of identity, which DOLCE distinguishes from the information realisation that carries it and the BFO family models as an information content entity. An artefact is the carrier with a design behind it: Kroes and Meijers' dual nature of technical artefacts, a physical or informational structure together with a function that a designer intended it to have, is the correct account of a source file, a container image or a schema migration, each of which is both a structure of bytes and a thing for something [84]. The two are different primitives in Koine because their identity criteria differ: two files with different bytes are two artefacts, and they may carry the same data.

Intentional states: requirements and constraints

A requirement is not an object and not a happening; it is a proposition that a stakeholder intends to be made true of the entity of interest. Jureta, Mylopoulos and Faulkner's revisiting of the core ontology of requirements engineering gives the categories Koine uses, distinguishing goals, softgoals, quality constraints, functional goals and domain assumptions, and grounding the requirements problem as one of finding a specification that, with the domain assumptions, satisfies the goals [85]. A constraint is the same kind of thing with a different origin: it prescribes means rather than ends and is imposed from outside the problem, by a regulator, a budget, an existing platform or a schedule. SWEBOK V4's requirements chapter, which files technology constraints and quality-of-service constraints as their own categories alongside functional and non-functional requirements, is evidence that the practitioner community has arrived at the same split [5].

Situations

The last foundational category is the situation: a configuration of individuals and their moments at a time, which is what an environment is. Production is not an object and not an event; it is the set of hosts, policies and connections that obtain in a region under a change regime, and a task occurs in it. UFO's situations are what the Environment primitive instantiates, and 42010's environment, the context that determines the setting and circumstances of a system's influences, is its architectural counterpart [16].

6L1: the fifteen primitives, as plates

The core of Koine is fifteen kinds of thing. The number is not sacred; it is what fell out of asking, for each engineering account anyone has to give (of a system, a ticket, an incident, a migration, a hiring decision, a compliance audit), which words could not be reduced to the others without losing a relation somebody needs. The fifteen fall into six families, and the families carry the article's colour code: structural (System, Component, Artifact, Technology), informational (Data, Concept), behavioural (Process, Task, Event), normative (Requirement, Constraint), contextual (Actor, Resource, Environment) and dispositional (Capability).

Each primitive is given as a plate with a fixed set of fields. The foundational category says what kind of thing it is at L0. The identity criterion says when two records name the same individual, which is the question every integration eventually has to answer and every taxonomy quietly avoids. Type and instance shows the split of commitment 4 for that primitive. What it displaces names the taxonomy node or spreadsheet column the primitive replaces, because a proposal that does not say what it removes is not a proposal. The payment example runs the same small commerce platform through every plate, so that the plates can be read as one description. Beneath the fields, the plate lists its counterparts in six reference standards, with a dash where there is none, and the ledger in §18 counts them.

The gauge on each plate is a rosette of the nine relation families of §7. A filled spoke means the primitive can be the domain or a member of the enumerated range of at least one relation in that family; a hollow spoke means it cannot. The number in the middle is the primitive's valence, the count of families it can enter, and the rosettes are derived from the relation table in the laboratory rather than drawn by hand. Total valence across the fifteen is 95, a mean of 6.33, ranging from 5 to 9; the highest, 9 of 9, belongs to Process, Task, and the taxonomic and mereological families are the only two that reach all fifteen, which is to say that every kind of thing can be typed and every kind of thing can be part of something.

Three observations from the plates. First, the valence gauge is not a measure of importance; it is a measure of how much can be said with a primitive, and the fact that Task and Process score highest is the reason the task is the unit of composition in §9 and §10: a task is the thing that connects an actor to an artefact to a requirement to a technology to a time. Second, the identity criteria are heterogeneous on purpose. Content identity for artefacts, allocation identity for resources, proposition-plus-owner identity for requirements and accountability identity for actors are four different answers because the things are four different kinds, and a scheme that gave them one answer would be committing OntoClean's identity error [77]. Third, the counterparts are uneven, and the unevenness is information: every one of the six reference standards has a word for System, while only ArchiMate and SPEM have anything for Technology and neither treats it as a kind that is also an individual, which is why technology decisions are the least well described part of most architecture repositories.

The table is the contract. Domains and ranges name primitives, and the admissibility matrix below is computed from it: a primitive can enter a family if it is the domain, or a member of an enumerated range, of at least one relation in that family. One exception is stated in the laboratory's source: a range of any (as on appliesTo) is a permission to be talked about, not a capacity of the target, so it does not confer membership; typing is the one genuinely universal relation, and the taxonomic family is credited to every primitive on that ground.

Four formal properties do most of the work. Transitivity is declared for isA, specialises, derivedFrom, precedes and for partOf within a kind; it is what lets a query walk from a sub-task to the epic's requirements. Symmetry is declared for integratesWith, alternativeTo and variantOf, so that a fact recorded once holds in both directions. Acyclicity is declared for dependsOn within a build and for supersedes, which is what makes a dependency graph a DAG and a version line a line. And context indexing is declared for playsRole and alternativeTo: Kafka plays the event-broker role in task T5, and is an alternative to RabbitMQ for that role, not in general. A context-indexed relation is a ternary fact, and a binary graph cannot state it without reification. The two standard devices are the qualified statement of Wikidata, in which a claim carries qualifiers such as the point in time or the applicable context [87], and the triple term of RDF 1.2, which reached W3C Candidate Recommendation on 7 April 2026 and lets a triple stand in the object position of another so that a statement can be made about a statement without a blank node [88]. Koine uses the latter where the store supports it and a relator individual (a RoleAssignment with a task, a technology and a role) where it does not; the two are interconvertible.

The context-indexed playsRole is where commitment 5 lives, and the laboratory's fourth experiment shows what it records that a technology list cannot. Twelve technologies were observed in 44 (technology, role) pairs over 36 distinct roles, a density of 10.2% of the 432 pairs the two vocabularies permit; 7 of the roles are played by more than one technology and those shared roles account for 15 of the pairs (34.1%). Configuration store is played by PostgreSQL, Kubernetes and SQLite; full-text index by PostgreSQL and Elasticsearch; runtime platform by Kubernetes and Docker. Those are the substitutability facts on which every replace-this-with-that decision turns, and a vocabulary in which PostgreSQL-as-configuration-store is one node cannot state any of them. The experiment also records, deliberately, that at this density the separated vocabulary of technologies plus roles is 4 nodes larger than the conflated pair list, and that the pair list only overtakes it past 11.1% density: the argument for separation was never about node count.

Two relations deserve a word each. satisfies and violates are evidence-bearing: a claim that payment-service satisfies REQ-3 is only as good as the validatedBy that points at the test run, and the meta-model treats a satisfaction claim without evidence as a claim, not a fact. identifiedBy is the relation to an identifier, and the plate for Artifact says why it is intrinsic where it can be: a SWHID is computed from the bytes it names by a Merkle DAG over the content, so anyone who has the artefact can verify the identifier without consulting a registry, which ISO/IEC 18670 made the first international standard for content-based software identification in April 2025 [46].

Three facets carry standard vocabularies and inherit their governance. The quality facet is ISO/IEC 25010:2023 exactly, including the 2023 renamings (usability became interaction capability, portability became flexibility) and the new safety characteristic [31]. The lifecycle facet takes the process names of ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 and, where an organisation prefers them, the knowledge-area names of SWEBOK V4 [5,28]; the Ecma Common Lifecycle Enumeration approved in December 2025 is a candidate source for the lifecycle states of a component as distinct from the phases of an endeavour [45,89]. The technique facet holds the design patterns of Gamma and colleagues [90], the strategic and tactical patterns of domain-driven design [91] and the architectural rules of approaches such as Clean Architecture [92], and it holds them as Concepts, which is where the layer-6 knowledge relation will find them. This is one of the more consequential consequences of the design: architectural styles, patterns and methods become objects in the meta-model, related to the tasks that use them and the systems that exhibit them, rather than the skeleton of the meta-model itself. Clean Architecture is a architectural approach; its dependency rule constrains component dependencies; a use case is a application capability; and hexagonal, layered, event-driven and serverless architectures are described in exactly the same way in the same graph.

Facets are extended, not redesigned. Adding a term to a facet is a SKOS operation: a new concept with a preferred label, alternative labels for the synonyms the organisation already uses, and a broader concept if one exists [68]. The laboratory's grammar experiment records 6 such extension terms (two new target kinds and four new technologies) introduced by ordinary task expressions without a change to the grammar, which is the open-world commitment doing its work. What a facet does not permit is an untyped string: a term that is not in the scheme is an extension request, and the request has a place to go.

9L4: the task frame, type and instance

A task in Koine is not a sentence that says do X. It is a structured object with named slots, most of which are relations to other primitives, and the structure is what makes a task queryable, comparable and decomposable. The frame has nineteen slots; a task instance fills the ones it needs and leaves the rest open, under the open-world commitment, and a task type fills the ones that are true of every instance.

The frame's most important property is the one it inherits from commitment 4. Introduce idempotency is a task type: it carries the knowledge that any such task needs (idempotency, key design, storage of processed keys, concurrency), the artefact kinds it typically produces, the risks it typically runs and the validation it typically needs, and it is written once. T4 is an instance: it has a target, a context, a date, a team and a result. SPEM 2.0 drew this line as Task Definition against Task Use [23]; Essence draws it as an activity described in a practice against the work that enacts it [26]. The laboratory's fifth experiment measures what the line is worth on a fixture of 10 task types and 36 instances: attaching knowledge at the type takes 46 assertions, attaching it at every instance would take 163, so the split saves 117 assertions, 71.8% of the instance-level cost, and it saves them before the knowledge has changed once. Two types (Migrate database engine, Decompose monolith) have a single instance so far and are no less reusable for it.

The second property is recursion. partOf is the same relation between T4 and T3 as between T3 and T0 and would be the same between T0 and a programme above it, so the frame does not need, and does not have, a slot that says whether a unit of work is an epic, a story, a task or a sub-task. Those words name depths in a decomposition, and depths are a property of the tree, not of the node. Any two teams that decompose the same work to different depths have described the same thing; a level-labelled scheme would have forced one of them to be wrong. The frame also makes the decomposition inherit what it should: a requirement satisfied by a leaf is satisfied by every ancestor whose sub-tasks together satisfy it, which is the second competency question of §16 and needs the transitive closure of partOf to answer.

10L5: the task grammar

The frame says what a task is. The grammar says how one is written, and it is the layer that turns the eight facets from a classification into a generative device. A task expression is an action followed by slots; a composite task expression is a sequence or a parallel group of task expressions; and that is the whole language.

task-expr   ::= simple | composite
composite   ::= ( "SEQ" | "PAR" ) "(" task-expr ( ";" task-expr )* ")"
simple      ::= ACTION slot*
slot        ::= key "=" value
key         ::= "on" | "for" | "in" | "with" | "under" | "using" | "from" | "to" | "at" | "by"
value       ::= term | "'" free-text "'"

ACTION      ::= create | modify | remove | analyse | design | test | debug | deploy
              | migrate | optimise | monitor | secure | integrate | document

well-formedness
  every ACTION requires  on              (something is acted upon)
  migrate   requires     from, to        (a migration has a source and a destination)
  integrate requires     with            (an integration has a counterpart)
  optimise  requires     for             (an optimisation has a quality or purpose)
  deploy    requires     to              (a deployment has a destination environment)
  secure    requires     under           (a hardening has a constraint or standard)
  monitor   requires     for             (monitoring has a quality in view)
  test      requires     by              (a test has a method)

slot semantics (the relation each slot asserts)
  on    → modifies / target     for  → purpose or quality      in    → context (Environment or System)
  with  → uses (Technology)     using → uses (Concept)           under → constrainedBy
  from  → source (V family)      to   → destination             at    → layer         by → validatedBy

The grammar is small because the facets are doing the work. An action is a term from the action facet; the value of on is a term from the target facet or an individual; the value of with is a technology; the value of for is a purpose or a quality attribute; and the well-formedness rules are the only place the grammar knows anything about the world. The two task sentences that motivated the design fall out immediately:

optimise  on=query  for=performance-efficiency  with=PostgreSQL  under='p95 < 100 ms'
migrate   on=database  from=PostgreSQL  to=Aurora  for=modernisation  under='minimal downtime'

Neither needed a category called PostgreSQL query optimisation or PostgreSQL-to-Aurora migration; both are expressions in a language of 14 verbs and ten slot kinds. The laboratory's sixth experiment runs a corpus of 24 such expressions through a checker of 14 rules: 20 are well-formed, 4 are rejected with a named reason (a migration with no source, an optimisation with no purpose, an integration with no counterpart, and the unknown verb refactor, which the action facet does not yet contain and which is therefore an extension request rather than an error in the grammar), and the well-formed ones fill a mean of 3.5 of the 10 slot kinds. The composite expression is where the design earns its keep. The ticket that opened the argument, migrate a Java/Spring monolith using PostgreSQL to Kubernetes, split it into microservices, introduce Kafka, maintain 99.99% availability and achieve zero-downtime deployment, is not a new node called Java-Spring-PostgreSQL-to-Kubernetes-Kafka-microservice-migration. It is:

SEQ( migrate   on=system  from='current infrastructure'  to=Kubernetes  under='availability >= 99.99%' ;
     PAR( modify    on=system  for=flexibility  using=microservices ;
          integrate on=system  with=Kafka  using=event-driven-architecture ) ;
     deploy    on=system  to=Kubernetes  under='zero downtime' )

Composition has semantics, not just syntax. SEQ asserts precedes between consecutive children; PAR asserts that its children may overlap and share preconditions; and each leaf becomes a task instance whose slots become relations in the graph. The parse of the ticket is therefore also a partial task frame for T0 with four sub-tasks, and a query can walk it. What the grammar deliberately does not do is enumerate composites: there is no list of the kinds of migration, because the kinds of migration are the set of well-formed expressions whose action is migrate, and that set is open.

Two connections outside the ontology literature. SysML v2's action def and action usage, with succession and parallel flow between usages, are the nearest thing in a standard modelling language to a task grammar, and a Koine composite maps to a SysML v2 action tree almost slot for slot [9]. And the tool-calling interfaces of language-model agents, which describe a tool by a JSON Schema over its parameters, are the nearest thing in current practice to a task frame with the semantics left out; Anthropic's own account of why its platform added tool-use examples on top of schemas puts the gap in one sentence, that JSON schemas define what is structurally valid but cannot express usage patterns [93,94]. A schema says which slots exist; a grammar with well-formedness rules says which combinations mean something; and an OpenAPI description of a service, which also stops at the schema, has the same limit for the same reason [95]. The grammar of this section is what a tool description would look like if it knew that a migration has a source.

11L6: knowledge, skill and tool

If the meta-model is to serve education, staffing, job design and planning as well as description, it needs a layer that says what a task takes. Koine adds three relations, all in the evaluative family, all attached at the task type by default: requiresKnowledge to a Concept, requiresSkill to a Concept drawn from a competency framework, and requiresTool to a Technology in the role of tool. With them, the type Implement Kafka consumer reads:

TASK-TYPE  Implement Kafka consumer
    requiresKnowledge  distributed systems; messaging; serialisation; concurrency; offset management; idempotency
    requiresSkill      SFIA PROG (programming/software development) at level 3+; SFIA ARCH where the consumer crosses a boundary
    requiresTool       Kafka; a build tool in the role of build tool; Docker in the role of test runtime
    produces           Kafka consumer  [Component]; source code; configuration; tests  [Artifact]
    validatedBy        integration tests against a broker  [Task type]

The knowledge vocabulary is SWEBOK V4's eighteen knowledge areas and their topics, which is what a body of knowledge is for [5]; the skill vocabulary is SFIA 9, the seven-level framework whose four-letter skill codes and level descriptors are already the lingua franca of digital job descriptions in the organisations that use it [96]. Both are held as facets, which means both can be swapped for an organisation's own framework without touching the relations. What the layer buys is that a job description, a training plan, a hiring rubric and a project plan become queries against the same graph rather than four documents that drift apart: the competency of a team is the union of the skills its members possess; the knowledge an epic requires is the union over the transitive closure of its sub-tasks (competency question 5 in §16); and a gap is a set difference. The laboratory's fifth experiment found that on its fixture of 10 task types, 6 of the 40 distinct knowledge items were required by more than one type, which is the beginning of an answer to which knowledge is foundational: the items that recur are the curriculum.

12L7: views are projections

The last layer exists so that nobody has to answer the question the taxonomy could not: is PostgreSQL a technology, an artefact or a system? It is all three. As a Technology it is a kind of DBMS with a release line and a licence; as an Artifact, a particular version is a package with a Package-URL and a checksum; as a System, a running installation has hosts, a configuration and a set of capabilities in production. The graph holds all three as three instantiation relations of one individual and its versions, and a view selects which of them a reader sees.

A view in Koine is a query, not a copy. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 defines a viewpoint as the conventions that frame a set of stakeholder concerns and a view as the description that results from applying them, and it requires the correspondences between views to be recorded [16]. Koine implements the viewpoint as a set of relation families and a starting entity, the view as the sub-graph the query returns, and the correspondences as the nodes two views share, which fall out of the queries rather than being maintained by hand. The technology view of the payment platform is the sub-graph reachable over the dependency family; the requirements view is reachable over the realisation family; the data view over the flow family; the work view over the agentive and chronological families; the knowledge view over the evaluative family. The architecture view, the security view, the DevOps view, the business view and the lifecycle view are further sets of families with a starting concern, and a new stakeholder gets a new view without a new graph.

The laboratory's seventh experiment makes the point quantitative on the payment graph of 67 statements over 46 nodes and 21 relation types. Six views between them cover all but 6 of the 67 edges (the 6 left over are typing edges, which belong to a type view every other view presupposes), and they overlap: the mean pairwise Jaccard similarity of their node sets is 0.15, the highest pair is Data and Structure at 0.37, 39.1% of the nodes appear in at least two views and none in all six. The largest single view, Structure, carries 25.4% of the edges. A view that carried all of them would not be a view; it would be the graph, and a view that overlapped with no other would be a silo. The numbers describe a set of projections that share their edges and divide their concerns, which is what 42010 asked for and what a folder per view could never have delivered.

In SPARQL the technology view of a system is a short query [97]; in GQL, the ISO graph query language published in April 2024 and the first new ISO database language since SQL, it is a path pattern [98]. The SPARQL form is given here because it makes the view-as-query claim concrete:

PREFIX koine: <https://koine.example/core#>
SELECT ?tech ?role ?platform WHERE {
  ?sys  a koine:System ;  koine:name "commerce-platform" .
  ?comp koine:partOf ?sys ;  koine:uses ?tech .
  OPTIONAL { ?comp koine:deployedOn ?platform . ?platform a koine:Technology }
  OPTIONAL { << ?tech koine:playsRole ?role >> koine:inTask ?task . ?task koine:partOf* ?epic }
}

The triple-term pattern in the last line is SPARQL 1.2 syntax over RDF 1.2 [88]; under SPARQL 1.1 the same view is written against a RoleAssignment relator with three properties, and the two forms return the same rows.

Kruchten's scenarios, the +1 of 4+1, have a natural home here too: a scenario is a task instance whose trace through the graph touches every view, and it is the correspondence check that 42010 wants, run as a query rather than read from a document [17]. Zachman's grid is the same graph indexed by interrogative and perspective [18]. Conway's observation that the structure of a system mirrors the communication structure of the organisation that built it becomes checkable: it is a correspondence between the structural view and the work view, and a graph that holds both can measure it [99].

13Formalisation: OWL 2, SHACL, SKOS, PROV-O and identity

A meta-model that is only prose is a taxonomy that has not yet been drawn. Koine's normative form is a small stack of W3C and ISO technologies, each chosen for one job.

OWL 2 DL for the core

The fifteen primitives, the relation vocabulary with its formal properties and the foundational alignments are an OWL 2 ontology in the DL profile, so that consistency and classification remain decidable and a standard reasoner can check that partOf is transitive within a kind, that dependsOn is acyclic in a build and that no individual is asserted to be both an endurant and a perdurant [61,67]. OWL 2's punning is what lets Technology be both a class and an individual: the same IRI denotes the class of Kafka installations and the individual Kafka with its licence and release history, and the reasoner treats the two uses as distinct entities that happen to share a name, which is precisely the metaphysics the Technology plate asks for [100]. The core is expressed in RDF 1.1 and, where the store supports triple terms, in RDF 1.2 for the context-indexed relations [88,101]; OMG's Ontology Definition Metamodel is the bridge by which the same core can be loaded into UML tooling as a profile [41].

SHACL for the frame

The open-world core cannot say that a migration must have a source, because in an open world the source may simply be unrecorded. The task frame's well-formedness rules are closed-world constraints and belong in SHACL, whose shapes validate a graph against expectations without changing what the graph entails [102]. A shape per action does the job the grammar checker did in the laboratory:

koine:MigrateShape a sh:NodeShape ;
    sh:targetClass koine:Task ;
    sh:property [ sh:path koine:action ; sh:hasValue koine:migrate ] ;
    sh:property [ sh:path koine:source ;      sh:minCount 1 ; sh:message "a migration has a source" ] ;
    sh:property [ sh:path koine:destination ; sh:minCount 1 ; sh:message "a migration has a destination" ] ;
    sh:property [ sh:path koine:target ;      sh:minCount 1 ; sh:class koine:Entity ] .

koine:SatisfactionEvidenceShape a sh:NodeShape ;
    sh:targetSubjectsOf koine:satisfies ;
    sh:property [ sh:path koine:validatedBy ; sh:minCount 1 ;
                  sh:severity sh:Warning ; sh:message "satisfaction claimed without evidence" ] .

The pairing is the formal expression of commitment 7. OWL says what follows from what is recorded; SHACL says what a well-formed record contains; and the same graph can be inconsistent under neither while being incomplete under the second, which is the normal condition of an engineering record that is still being written.

SKOS for the facets

Each facet is a skos:ConceptScheme; each term a skos:Concept with a skos:prefLabel, any number of skos:altLabels and, where the facet has internal structure, skos:broader [68]. Two things follow. Synonymy is handled where it belongs: interaction capability and the older usability are one concept with two labels, so a requirement tagged under either is found by both. And the extension procedure is a data operation with an audit trail, not a schema migration. The quality facet is loaded from ISO/IEC 25010:2023 [31]; the lifecycle facet from ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 [28]; the knowledge and skill facets from SWEBOK V4 and SFIA 9 [5,96]; the architecture-style, technique and concern facets from the organisation's own registers with the design-pattern and DDD literature as seed [90,91].

PROV-O and identity

Provenance is PROV-O: an Artifact prov:wasGeneratedBy the Task that produced it, prov:wasDerivedFrom its inputs and prov:wasAttributedTo the Actor responsible, and a satisfaction claim prov:wasGeneratedBy the validation run that grounds it [75]. Identity is the plate's criterion made machine-readable: a source Artifact carries a SWHID, which ISO/IEC 18670 computes intrinsically from content so that two copies anywhere in the world agree on the name [46]; a package-level Technology carries a Package-URL under ECMA-427, whose seven components identify it independently of registry or ecosystem [44]; and a deployed System's bill of materials, in SPDX or CycloneDX form, is the list of Artifacts and Technologies it is composed of, with each of those identified in the same two ways [42,43]. Where lifecycle tools already hold the records, OSLC Core 3.0's linked-data resources are the join: a requirement in one tool and the change request in another are both IRIs, and a Koine graph can reference both without copying either [47].

What is not formalised

Two things are left informal on purpose. The objective of a task is a proposition in natural language, because the meta-model describes engineering rather than performing it and a formal specification of every objective would be a specification language, not an ontology. And the rationale that Perry and Wolf identified as the missing third element of architecture is held as a Concept linked by appliesTo and describedBy to the decision it justifies, which records that a rationale exists and where it is written, without pretending to formalise why anyone believed it [15].

Correspondence is not identity, and three mismatches are worth stating so that nobody maps naively. UML's Constraint is an OCL expression on a model element, a different sense of the word from a regulatory constraint on a system, and the two should not be merged. ArchiMate's Work Package is a project-management unit with a start and an end, not a task with a target and a validation; a Koine task maps to a work package only at the coarsest grain. And 42010's Concern is broader than a requirement, since a concern may be an interest (cost, evolvability) that never becomes a proposition to be satisfied; a requirement is a concern that has been made precise, and the mapping is one way.

The correspondence rules of 42010 ask that an architecture description record how its views relate. Koine's answer, for the standards themselves, is the mapping table above held in the graph as alternativeTo and specialises statements between the Koine primitive and the standard's construct, each a Concept in the concern facet of the standard in question. That makes the correspondence queryable: the question which of my ArchiMate elements have no counterpart in the SPEM description of the same project? is a set difference over one graph rather than a comparison of two documents.

def tokenize(s):
    toks, i, n = [], 0, len(s)
    while i < n:
        c = s[i]
        if c.isspace():
            i += 1
        elif c in "();":
            toks.append(c); i += 1
        else:
            j = i
            while j < n and not s[j].isspace() and s[j] not in "();":
                if s[j] == "'":
                    j = s.index("'", j + 1) + 1
                else:
                    j += 1
            toks.append(s[i:j]); i = j
    return toks


def parse_simple(tok_words):
    """Return (ast, problems, extensions)."""
    problems, extensions = [], []
    action = tok_words[0]
    slots = OrderedDict()
    for w in tok_words[1:]:
        if "=" not in w:
            problems.append(f"malformed slot '{w}'"); continue
        k, v = w.split("=", 1)
        if k not in SLOTS:
            problems.append(f"unknown slot '{k}'"); continue
        slots[k] = v.strip("'")
    if action not in ACTIONS:
        problems.append(f"unknown action '{action}'")
    else:
        for req in REQUIRED.get(action, []):
            if req not in slots:
                problems.append(f"missing required slot '{req}' for '{action}'")
    if "on" in slots and slots["on"] not in TARGETS:
        extensions.append(("target", slots["on"]))
    if "with" in slots and slots["with"].replace("-", " ") not in KNOWN_TECH and slots["with"] not in KNOWN_TECH:
        extensions.append(("technology", slots["with"]))
    return {"action": action, "slots": slots}, problems, extensions


def parse_composite(tokens):
    """Recursive descent over SEQ( a ; b ) / PAR( a ; b ) / simple."""
    pos = [0]

    def expr():
        t = tokens[pos[0]]
        if t in ("SEQ", "PAR"):
            pos[0] += 1
            assert tokens[pos[0]] == "("; pos[0] += 1
            children = [expr()]
            while tokens[pos[0]] == ";":
                pos[0] += 1
                children.append(expr())
            assert tokens[pos[0]] == ")"; pos[0] += 1
            return {"op": t, "children": children}
        words = []
        while pos[0] < len(tokens) and tokens[pos[0]] not in (";", ")"):
            words.append(tokens[pos[0]]); pos[0] += 1
        ast, problems, ext = parse_simple(words)
        ast["problems"] = problems
        return ast
    return expr()


def render(ast, depth=0, lines=None):
    lines = [] if lines is None else lines
    pad = "  " * depth
    if "op" in ast:
        lines.append(f"{pad}{ast['op']}")
        for c in ast["children"]:
            render(c, depth + 1, lines)
    else:
        slots = " ".join(f"{k}={v}" for k, v in ast["slots"].items())
        lines.append(f"{pad}{ast['action'].upper()}  {slots}")
    return lines

def index(triples):
    fwd, bwd = defaultdict(list), defaultdict(list)
    for s, r, o in triples:
        fwd[(s, r)].append(o)
        bwd[(o, r)].append(s)
    return fwd, bwd


def closure(fwd, start, rel):
    seen, frontier, hops = set(), [start], 0
    while frontier:
        nxt = []
        for n in frontier:
            for o in fwd.get((n, rel), []):
                if o not in seen:
                    seen.add(o); nxt.append(o)
        frontier = nxt
        if nxt:
            hops += 1
    return seen, hops

16Evaluation: competency questions and what the numbers show

Grüninger and Fox proposed that an ontology be evaluated by competency questions: the questions it must be able to answer, stated before it is built and tested against it afterwards [103]; Noy and McGuinness made the practice the first step of ontology development for a generation of practitioners [104]. The eighth experiment states twelve for the payment graph and runs each against two encodings of the same facts: the full Koine graph, and the shadow of it that keeps only the taxonomic and mereological relations, which is what a taxonomy with containment can express.

The result is 12 of 12 against 1 of 12. The one question the shadow answers is the twelfth, which things are messaging platforms?, because that question is a taxonomy lookup. Every other question is a question about a relation that is not subsumption or parthood: what breaks if a technology is replaced (dependency and variation), who is working on a requirement (agentive and realisation), what an event sets in motion (flow), what must finish before a deployment (chronological), what knowledge an epic needs at every depth (evaluative over the transitive closure of parthood). 10 of the 12 need two or more hops and the deepest needs 3; between them they exercise all 9 relation families. The point is not that a taxonomy is useless but that its competence ends exactly where the questions engineers ask begin, and that the remaining eleven questions were answered by a graph of 67 statements, none of which required a category invented for the occasion.

Beyond competency questions, three evaluations are applied to the primitives themselves. The first is OntoClean: each of the fifteen is checked for rigidity, and each is rigid, which is what qualifies it as a kind rather than a role [77]. Roles (event broker, release manager, persistent store) are anti-rigid and are correctly excluded from the primitives and held as Concepts related by playsRole; phases (in production, decommissioned) are anti-rigid and are held as states of an Environment or System rather than as kinds. The identity criteria on the plates are the OntoClean identity check made explicit, and they are heterogeneous, which is the correct result: an ontology in which artefacts and requirements shared an identity criterion would be committing the error the method was built to catch.

The second is Wand and Weber's construct analysis [56]. Construct deficit is the dash in the correspondence table, and the table shows where each existing language has one: 5 for UML, 5 for SysML v2, 0 for ArchiMate, 9 for ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010, 7 for SPEM and Essence, 4 for SEON. Construct overload is what Azevedo and colleagues found in ArchiMate's resource and capability and what the Requirement/Constraint split repairs where a single requirement construct carried both [22]. Construct redundancy is the risk Koine runs in the other direction, with System and Component two words for functional complexes; the plates defend the split on identity criteria (a boundary and a purpose against an interface and a role within a whole) and accept that a critic who rejects the distinction can merge them without loss to the relations. Construct excess is the charge that a primitive stands for nothing; the plates' payment examples are the answer, since every primitive is instantiated by the same small platform.

The third is definitional discipline. ISO 704 states that an intensional definition, one that gives the superordinate concept and the delimiting characteristics, is preferable to other kinds because it reveals the concept's place in a system of concepts [105]; every plate's foundational category and identity criterion together form an intensional definition of that form, and the ledger records the superordinate for each. The FAIR principles, findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable, are the test the formalisation of §13 is built to pass: IRIs for every individual, standard vocabularies for the facets, and identifiers from ISO/IEC 18670 and ECMA-427 that resolve without a registry [106].

What the evaluation does not show is coverage of real engineering records at scale. The fixtures are small and were written by the author of the meta-model, which is the usual condition of a proposal and the usual weakness of one. The honest statement of the evaluation is that the structural claims hold on fixtures a reader can check by hand, and that the next test, a corpus of tickets, architecture decision records and bills of materials from organisations that did not write the vocabulary, is the one that would decide whether Koine is a common tongue or another dialect. §17 says how that test should be run.

17Limitations and open problems

Reasoning cost

OWL 2 DL is decidable but not cheap: the W3C's own profiles document puts reasoning in the full language at N2ExpTime-complete [107], and a graph that records every task, artefact and event in a large organisation is not a graph one classifies with a tableau reasoner on demand [61]. The practical design keeps the core small and puts the volume in the instance graph, where SHACL validation is linear in the data and SPARQL or GQL queries are indexed. Reasoning is for the vocabulary; querying is for the world.

Governance and drift

A vocabulary with a governed extension procedure is a vocabulary somebody has to govern. The facets will accumulate synonyms, the relation table will be asked for a fortieth relation, and every decision will be a small metaphysical decision with consequences for every query already written. The Industrial Ontologies Foundry's release notes are the best public record of what this looks like in practice: releases in 2024 through 2026 that imported an interval algebra, introduced gain of role and loss of role as process classes because the foundation had no way to say when a bearer starts or stops playing a role, redefined an artefact's function as prescribed by a design specification rather than possessed by the artefact, and added a distinction between what a thing is made of and what it is a part of [108]. Koine will need the same kind of record, and the same willingness to change a definition when a use case shows it wrong.

Tacit knowledge and the objective slot

The meta-model describes tasks; it does not know how to do them. The objective of a task is a proposition in natural language, the rationale of a decision is a pointer to a document, and the judgement that makes an experienced engineer choose the outbox pattern over dual writes lives in neither. A knowledge layer that records requiresKnowledge idempotency records that the knowledge is needed, not what it is, and the distinction between a body of knowledge and the possession of it is one no ontology closes.

The boundary of software engineering

Koine's primitives were chosen for software engineering and its scope is the entity of interest of the masthead. But nothing in the fifteen is specific to software: an Actor performs a Task on an Artifact for a Requirement under a Constraint in an Environment using a Technology in any engineering discipline, and the systems-engineering standards that sit above the software ones (ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288, SysML v2) suggest the vocabulary generalises upward [9,29]. Whether it generalises is an empirical question; that it was not designed to is a limitation stated here so that nobody mistakes the payment example for a claim about bridges.

Organisations and people

The Actor primitive is thin. It holds accountability and skill, and it holds teams as members of organisations, but it holds nothing of the communication structure that Conway showed to be predictive of system structure, nor of incentive, tenure or trust [99]. A meta-model that wants to explain why a system came to have the shape it has, rather than to describe the shape, will need a social layer that this one does not attempt.

Identity of tasks and events

Artefacts have content identity and requirements have proposition-plus-owner identity, but the identity of a task instance is the weakest criterion on the plates: objective plus target plus context. Two tickets in two systems describing the same work will be two task instances until somebody asserts variantOf or merges them, and the merge judgement is not mechanisable from the frame alone. Event identity is sharper (participants plus time) but depends on a clock the graph does not own.

The thirteen facets are a choice

Eight facets appear in the laboratory and thirteen in §8, and a reader could defend eleven or sixteen. The number is not a claim; the orthogonality is. What matters is that no facet can be derived from another, that each has a governed vocabulary, and that adding one is a data operation. A facet that turns out to be derivable (if system layer were always fixed by target, for example) should be removed, and the test for that is the co-occurrence statistics of a real corpus, which the fixtures cannot supply.

What would falsify it

The proposal makes three testable claims. That a corpus of real task records can be parsed into the grammar of §10 at a high rate without new grammar rules, with the residue being facet extensions rather than new slot kinds; a low rate, or a residue of new slot kinds, would falsify the compositional bet. That the fifteen primitives are sufficient for the entities in a corpus of architecture descriptions, bills of materials and requirements documents from several organisations, in the sense that every entity is an instance of one of them and no relation anyone needs falls outside the nine families; an entity or relation that will not fit would be a construct deficit and would have to be added at L1 or L2, which is the change the design is built to avoid. And that the competency questions organisations actually ask of their engineering records are answerable over the relation vocabulary; a recurring question that needs a fortieth relation is evidence that the vocabulary is incomplete, and a recurring question that needs a hierarchy is evidence that the article is wrong. The laboratory tests the shape of these claims on fixtures. The claims themselves need a corpus.

Read down the valence column and the L0 distinction shows through: the two perdurants, Task and Process, are the only primitives at nine, because a happening is what an actor performs, what produces and consumes, what satisfies and violates, what precedes and follows, and what is validated; the structural trio of System, Component and Artifact sit at eight; and Event, which is a perdurant but has no internal structure at its own granularity, sits at five with the three contextual primitives and Constraint. The argument of the article can now be stated in one paragraph. A description of software engineering cannot be completed as a list because the things listed are conjunctions of independent choices, and because each of them is several kinds of thing at once. A description can be completed as a language, in the sense that matters: a finite vocabulary of kinds, a finite vocabulary of relations with declared properties, a finite set of facets with governed extension, a task frame that keeps types apart from instances and lets decomposition recurse, and a grammar that composes tasks from the facets. In that language the ticket migrate a Java/Spring monolith on PostgreSQL to Kubernetes, split it into microservices, introduce Kafka, keep 99.99% availability and deploy with zero downtime is an expression of four leaves reusing four of fourteen verbs and seven of ten slots, and it needed nothing that was not already there. The trick the article set out to describe is exactly that: exhaustiveness of the representation language, not exhaustiveness of the list of things represented.

What Koine borrows should be acknowledged as plainly as what it proposes. Its foundation is UFO's, with BFO and DOLCE as alternative grounds and ISO/IEC 21838 as the certificate that the ground is solid [70,74]. Its type/instance discipline is SPEM's [23]. Its views are 42010's [16]. Its facets are Ranganathan's [62]. Its motivation and strategy elements are ArchiMate's as grounded by Azevedo and colleagues [21,22]. Its identity criteria are the supply-chain community's [44,46]. Its formal clothing is the W3C's [67,68,75,102]. What is new is the arrangement: that all of these can be held in one graph underneath the languages they came from, that the task is the natural unit of composition because it is the primitive of highest valence, and that a grammar over facets is the device that makes the arrangement generative rather than descriptive.

The research question that remains is the one §3 raised and §17 made testable: whether there is a sufficiently general upper ontology of software engineering from which UML, ArchiMate, the ADLs, SPEM, Essence, the requirements and quality standards and the bodies of knowledge can be related, mapped or derived. Koine is a candidate answer with its assumptions on the table, its relations in a checkable form and its arithmetic in a program a reader can run. Whether it is a common tongue or one more dialect will be decided by the corpus, not by the proposal, and the proposal has been written so that the corpus can decide.
