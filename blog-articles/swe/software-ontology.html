SOFTWARE ENGINEERING · APPLIED ONTOLOGY

Where Was the Word Bound?

The proposal is familiar: describe a system in a technology-independent ontology, then derive requirements, architecture, tests and code from that one description. The proposal is also forty years old. What has never been characterised carefully is the obstacle — which is not that we lack names for the things in a software system, but that we have one name for six different things and no convention for saying which one we meant.

Section 1

The question that is actually being asked

Here is the proposal, in the form it usually arrives in. Describe a software system once, in a vocabulary that is semantically explicit and tied to no particular technology. Then derive everything else from that one description: a requirements model, an architecture model, a domain model, an implementation plan, a test suite, documentation, and in the limit the source code itself. The description is the single source of truth; the artefacts are projections. UML, SysML, C4, ArchiMate and the entity-relationship diagram stop being rivals and become views.

It is a good proposal. It is also not a new one. Some version of it has been put forward in every decade since the 1970s, by people who were not naive about software and who had working prototypes. Feigenbaum argued in 1977 that the power of a system lies in the knowledge it holds rather than in the sophistication of its inference.52 Greenspan, Mylopoulos and Borgida built a requirements modelling language on that premise in 1982, and Telos generalised it into a knowledge base for information systems by 1990.53,54 Balzer, Cheatham and Green sketched the whole pipeline — informal description, formal specification, mechanised derivation, maintained at the specification level — as the shape software development would take in the 1990s.55 The Object Management Group put a technology-independent semantic representation of existing systems into a formal standard, and a metamodel explicitly designed to bridge modelling languages and formal ontologies.56,57 Brazilian researchers have spent two decades assembling a layered network of software engineering ontologies grounded in a foundational ontology, and it is still being extended.58,59

So the interesting question is not whether the idea is worth pursuing. It is why forty years of serious work has produced a large literature, several standards, no shortage of ontologies, and almost no evidence that any of them changed an engineering outcome. The systematic review of ontology-based systems engineering is unusually candid about this: the field has grown, the proposed ontologies are often broad and lightly formalised, and very little research evaluates the actual benefit.60 The systematic review of ontologies in requirements engineering reports the same shape — many approaches, little comparative evidence.61

The reframing this article proposes

The request build an ontology of software contains an assumption: that the hard part is deciding what kinds of thing exist in a software system. Is a database a component? Is a Kubernetes pod? Is a REST endpoint an interface or a service? These questions feel like the crux, and a great deal of effort has gone into answering them with foundational distinctions.

They are not the crux. Software engineering is not short of names for the things in a system; it has an international vocabulary standard running to thousands of entries.6 The difficulty is that the discipline's words are each bound — fixed to a meaning by some authority, at some particular level of description — and then reused, by the same engineers, in the same documents, at every other level, with no mark to say that the binding changed. The word component is bound by the C4 model to a grouping of functionality that is explicitly not separately deployable, and bound by Clean Architecture to exactly the unit of deployment.27,28 Neither is wrong. They are bound at different levels, and a sentence containing the word does not say which.

That is the problem an ontology of software would actually have to solve, and it is not a taxonomy problem. A taxonomy answers what kinds of thing are there. The question that has to be answered before that one is useful is: where was this word bound, what has to be true for a use one level down to preserve that binding, and what kind of thing could establish that it did?

Brooks drew the distinction that matters here in 1987, when he separated the essential difficulty of software — constructing the conceptual object itself — from the accidental difficulty of representing it.62 An ontology is a representation. It cannot remove essential difficulty. What it can do, and what none of the existing ones do systematically, is make visible exactly where the conceptual object is being asserted, exactly where that assertion is carried downward, and exactly where nothing carries it at all. Naur's argument that a program is the residue of a theory held by the people who built it is the same observation from the other side: what survives in the artefacts is not the theory, and the gap between them is where maintenance costs live.63

The distinction the discipline is missing is an old one. Frege separated the sense of an expression from the thing it refers to, precisely because two expressions can pick out the same object while presenting it differently, and the difference is not in the object.64 What software engineering has is the harder case: one expression, several senses, several referents, and no marker to say which is in force. Uschold and Gruninger observed thirty years ago that the principal use of an ontology is to establish a shared understanding among parties who will otherwise use the same terms differently — which presupposes knowing that they do.65

There is also a reason to think the bindings are not arbitrary. Conway's observation that a system's structure mirrors the communication structure of the organisation that produced it applies to vocabulary as much as to code: a word is bound where the group that needed it worked, and it is reused by the next group because the word was already there.66 The stepwise- refinement account of development, which is the received story about how the levels relate, assumes each step preserves the meaning of the one above; it does not say what would show that it had.67

And the honest baseline is that the notations meant to carry these distinctions are not much used. Petre's interview study across dozens of practising developers found that most used no UML at all, and that of those who did, most used a handful of diagram types selectively and informally.68 Any proposal that adds semantic precision to a modelling practice has to reckon with how little of that practice exists.

What this article does

It characterises the problem rather than proposing a solution, and it does so by measurement. Twenty-nine words that engineers use to describe software systems are catalogued. For each one the article records every sense that a standard, a specification or a canonical text actually defines; the level of description at which each sense sits; which level the term's own defining authority binds it to; and the weakest mechanism that could establish that a downstream artefact preserved the claim made with it. Nine experiments then measure what the resulting structure implies.

The findings that matter can be stated up front. The level of description is a sufficient statistic for the sense: name the level and the sense is determined, for every one of the twenty-nine terms. Six levels are required to achieve that, and no coarser division works. Merging the architecture level into the technology level — the merge that UML and C4 both effectively perform — collides eleven of the twenty-nine. A complete, conforming set of requirements for one modest service leaves several thousand admissible designs, and four of ten design dimensions untouched by any requirement at all. And of the twenty-nine terms, the number whose primary claim can be established by reasoning over the models alone is zero.

Section 2

What an ontology is, and four things it is not

The word ontology carries a philosophical sense and an engineering sense, and the engineering sense is narrower than the philosophical one in a way that matters here. Quine's formulation of the philosophical question — what is there, and what does a theory commit us to — is about the furniture of the world.69 The engineering sense, which is the one in play, is due to Gruber: an ontology is an explicit specification of a conceptualisation.70 Studer, Benjamins and Fensel sharpened it into the formulation most often quoted — a formal, explicit specification of a shared conceptualisation — where formal means machine-interpretable and shared means agreed by a community.71

Guarino's contribution was to insist that the specification and the conceptualisation are different objects, and that a given specification only approximates the conceptualisation it is meant to capture72; the space of models a logical theory admits is generally larger than the space of states of affairs the conceptualisation intends.73 That gap is the reason foundational ontologies exist at all. Its practical form in software engineering is the gap between a diagram that type-checks and a diagram that means what the architect meant.

Gruber's design criteria — clarity, coherence, extendibility, minimal encoding bias, minimal ontological commitment — are worth restating because the last one is routinely violated by software modelling languages.74 Minimal ontological commitment means committing to as little as will serve the intended uses, so that parties who disagree about the rest can still share the vocabulary. A language that fixes component to one level of description has made a large commitment, and every community that needs the word at a different level must either fight it or quietly redefine it. Both happen.

Four things an ontology is not

It is not a metamodel. A metamodel says what constructs a modelling language offers and how they may be combined; an ontology says what the constructs are about. The two are close enough that the OMG published a standard whose entire purpose is to bridge them, mapping between UML-style and entity-relationship-style modelling on one side and formal ontology languages and logic on the other.57 Model-driven architecture assumes the metamodel layer;75,76 Bézivin's account of models as the unifying concept of software engineering is explicitly about the metamodel relation, not the ontological one.77 The distinction is not pedantic: a metamodel constrains what you may write, an ontology constrains what you may mean, and the mismatch between the two is where most ontological analysis of modelling languages has found its findings.78

It is not a schema. Noy and Klein made the point precisely: ontology evolution is not schema evolution, because a schema governs data whose only consumer is the application, whereas an ontology is shared and its changes are visible to independent parties who have committed to it.79 A change to a column type is a migration; a change to the meaning of service is a renegotiation.

It is not a knowledge graph. The ontology supplies the vocabulary and the constraints; the graph is an instance-level description of one particular system. Contemporary surveys of knowledge graphs treat the ontological layer as the schema-and-semantics component of a larger architecture, not as the graph itself.80 Confusing the two is the most common error in proposals of this shape: describing this banking system is an act of modelling, and a modelling act does not by itself yield a reusable vocabulary.

It is not an executable specification. This is the load-bearing distinction in what follows, and it has a long pedigree. Swartout and Balzer argued in 1982 that specification and implementation are inevitably intertwined, because implementation decisions feed back into what can be specified at all.81 Rich and Waters listed the myths of automatic programming and put this one first: that the specification determines the program.82 Deductive synthesis does derive programs from specifications, but only where the specification is complete and formal, and the derivation is a search whose cost is the real subject of the field.83,84 An ontology states what must be true. It does not state how to bring it about, and the distance between those two is not a gap in the notation.

The languages, and the commitments they smuggle

Because the specification has to be machine-interpretable, the choice of ontology language is itself an ontological commitment. OWL 2 is the standard vehicle, and its profiles exist because full expressivity and tractable reasoning are not simultaneously available: each profile trades constructs for a decidability or complexity guarantee.85,86 The underlying description logics come with well-characterised limits, and a modeller who wants a construct the profile omits must either change profile or change the model.87 Constraint checking under a closed-world assumption — the kind engineers actually want when they ask whether every requirement is realised — is a different activity from description-logic inference, which is why SHACL exists alongside OWL rather than inside it.88

Above the language sits the foundational ontology. The Unified Foundational Ontology, built over two decades from formal ontology, cognitive science and philosophical logic, supplies the categories most relevant here: the distinction between a kind and a role, between an intrinsic quality and a relator, between an endurant and a perdurant.89,11 Its lightweight OWL implementation is designed for exactly the knowledge-graph use this article's proposal implies.90 ISO/IEC 21838 now certifies conformant top-level ontologies as separate parts — requirements, then Basic Formal Ontology, then DOLCE, then TUpper — with UFO at draft international standard stage, its public enquiry having closed on 31 August 2026.91,92,93,94,95 Four mutually incompatible top-level ontologies are certified as conformant by the same standard. That is not a failure of the standard. It is a standards-level acknowledgement that the top of the stack is contested, and any software ontology that imports one of them inherits the contest.

How ontologies actually get built

There is a methodological literature, and its existence is itself evidence about the problem. Grüninger and Fox proposed evaluating an ontology against competency questions — the queries it must be able to answer — which converts ontology design from a metaphysical exercise into a requirements exercise.96 METHONTOLOGY formalised the lifecycle;97 the NeOn methodology generalised it to networked ontologies built by reusing and re-engineering existing ones, which is exactly the architecture SEON adopts;98 and tooling now exists to scan a finished ontology for recurring modelling pitfalls.99 Ontology design patterns formalise the recurring fragments and the round trip between a reference ontology and the patterns extracted from it.100,101

Foundational ontologies come with their own discipline. OntoClean supplies meta-properties — rigidity, identity, unity, dependence — against which a proposed subsumption can be tested and often rejected, and it is the source of the observation that most published taxonomies contain subsumptions that fail those tests.102 DOLCE was built as a descriptive ontology of the categories underlying natural language and human commonsense, explicitly not of the world as science finds it;103,104 Basic Formal Ontology takes the opposite, realist stance, and says so.105,106,107 That the same ISO standard certifies both tells you how far the top of the stack is from settled.

The vehicle for all of it is the Semantic Web stack, whose original programme was machine-processable meaning on the open web,108 whose data model is a graph of triples,109 and whose actual two-decade record — broad adoption in some domains, near-zero in others — has been reviewed candidly from inside the field.110 Sowa's synthesis remains the clearest statement of what a knowledge representation is being asked to do at all.111

The terminology standards, which are a different thing again

Parallel to the ontology literature runs a terminology literature that engineers almost never read, and it has already solved part of this problem for other disciplines. ISO 704 sets out principles for terminology work and states plainly that intensional definitions — those that give the superordinate concept and the delimiting characteristics — are preferable, because they place the concept in a system rather than merely gesturing at instances.112,113 Software engineering has a vocabulary standard in that tradition, maintained as a searchable database, and it is the artefact against which any rung-marking convention would have to be applied.6,114

There are also working examples of a domain ontology built to a standard and maintained as a record of its own decisions. The Industrial Ontologies Foundry publishes dated releases whose notes read as a log of metaphysical choices — when a role begins and ends, whether material composition is a kind of parthood — rather than as a changelog.115 The OMG has a standard for capturing a business vocabulary and its rules in a form derived from natural language, which is the closest thing the modelling world has to a terminology standard.116 Neither is a software ontology. Both are the right shape.

A precedent worth naming
Wand and Weber's work in the early 1990s applied Bunge's ontology as a yardstick against which to evaluate information systems analysis and design grammars, using Bunge's categories of thing, property, state and event117, asking not is this ontology correct but what can this notation fail to express, and what does it let you express that has no ontological counterpart.118,78 That is the posture this article takes. The interesting measurements are about the fit between a vocabulary and the descriptions people need to make, not about the vocabulary's internal elegance.
Section 3

What has already been built

The proposal is well populated. It is worth being specific about what exists, because the gaps in the map are informative.

Requirements

Lin, Fox and Bilgiç published a formal requirement ontology for engineering design in 1996, axiomatised in first-order logic, addressing exactly the concerns that recur in every later proposal: shared communication across concurrently working engineers, traceability, completeness and consistency.119 Jureta, Mylopoulos and Faulkner revisited the foundations more than a decade later and argued that the received ontology of requirements engineering — essentially Zave and Jackson's — was incomplete, and proposed a core ontology that separates goals, softgoals, quality constraints, plans and domain assumptions as distinct categories.19 The systematic review covering the literature through 2015 found a large body of ontology-driven work spread across elicitation, analysis, specification, validation and management, with OWL as the dominant language.61 The requirements subnetwork of SEON follows the same architecture, with a reference ontology and specialised ontologies for goal-oriented, runtime and process aspects.59

Architecture

ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 is the standard that defines what an architecture description is, and its 2022 second edition is instructive for this article's argument: it was retitled to cover software, systems and enterprises together, it generalised system of interest to entity of interest, and it renamed architecture model to view component.2 Those are ontological moves. They concede that the thing being described is whatever a stakeholder has chosen to bound, and that a model is a part of a view rather than a thing in its own right.

The most careful empirical work in this space is de Graaf and colleagues' study of ontology engineering for architecture documentation, which built a software ontology into a semantic wiki and evaluated retrieval against conventional file-based documentation in controlled industrial settings.120,121 It is worth being precise about what it measured: the efficiency and effectiveness with which professionals could find architectural knowledge. It did not measure whether the resulting systems were better. That is the strongest evidence the field has, and it is evidence about documentation retrieval.

The vocabulary of architectural decisions is a separate and more successful strand. Kruchten's ontology of architectural design decisions and Jansen and Bosch's reframing of architecture as a set of decisions both shift the unit of record from the structure to the choice that produced it.36,37 Perry and Wolf's foundational formulation — architecture as elements, form and rationale — had already put rationale in the definition.46

Software artefacts, and reverse engineering

OMG's Knowledge Discovery Metamodel is the closest thing the industry has to a technology-independent semantic representation of software as it actually exists, intended for modernisation, analysis and assurance, and built to be populated from existing code rather than written by hand.56 That direction of travel — from artefacts up, rather than from requirements down — is the one with the fewest unsolved problems, for reasons this article will make precise: the artefacts are formal at both ends. Oberle, Grimm and Staab's ontology of software addresses the categories themselves, and the collected work on ontology-driven software development surveys the broader programme.122,123,124 Happel and Seedorf's taxonomy of applications remains a useful map of the space, and Ruiz and Hilera's survey of uses in software engineering and technology predates most of the current enthusiasm.125,126

Model-driven engineering, which is the closest previous attempt

If the proposal has a direct ancestor it is model-driven engineering, and its record is the best-documented evidence available about what happens when an industry tries to make a model the source of truth. The programme was clearly stated: raise the level of abstraction, capture the system in platform-independent models, and generate the platform-specific ones by transformation.75,127,128 The tooling for deterministic lowering was already mature — generative programming and partial evaluation had worked out how to turn a specification plus a configuration into code long before.129,130

What happened is documented by people who went and looked. Selic's account of the pragmatics identified the gap between what the technology could do and what an organisation could absorb early.131 Hutchinson and colleagues interviewed and surveyed practitioners across industries and found adoption patterns dominated by organisational and social factors rather than technical ones, with success concentrated where a company controlled its own narrow domain.132,133 Petre's finding that most developers use no UML is the same result from the other end.68

The lesson for this article is specific. Model-driven engineering did not fail because its transformations were wrong. It ran into the problem that the model has to be maintained by people who must agree on what its elements mean, and the evidence above suggests that agreement is expensive precisely where the vocabulary is ambiguous. That is a measurable claim, and Section 9 measures it.

Where the evidence actually is

One controlled experiment in this whole literature measures an engineering outcome rather than a retrieval outcome. Mäder and Egyed gave developers maintenance tasks on real systems with and without requirements traceability and found those with traceability performed measurably better on both speed and correctness.134 That result is about traceability, not ontology, and the traceability research community's own roadmap is candid that capture and maintenance remain the bottleneck.135 It is nonetheless the closest thing the field has to evidence that making cross-level relations explicit pays, and it is worth noticing that what it measured is exactly the structure this article is about.

The architecture literature proper supplies the vocabulary that the ontologies then try to formalise: Shaw and Garlan's styles, the views-and-beyond documentation approach, and the standard textbook treatment of architecture as multiple simultaneous structures.136,137,138 Where data is concerned the lineage is older still: the relational model and the entity-relationship model are both, in this article's terms, technology-rung formalisations of world-rung concepts, and the gap between the two was visible from the start.139,9

Systems engineering

The systems-engineering version of the problem is larger and older, and its state-of-the-art review is the most useful single document for calibrating expectations: ontologies have been applied across most systems-engineering knowledge areas, many of the proposed ontologies are broad and lightly formalised, and evaluation of real benefit is scarce.60 SysML v2 and its underlying Kernel Modeling Language are the most significant recent development, formally adopted by OMG on 30 June 2025 with the specifications editorially updated in March 2026 for submission to ISO; KerML's semantics are specified in first-order logic with four-dimensional semantics of temporal and spatial extent.140,141 That is a modelling language with an explicit formal semantics — much closer to the proposal than SysML v1 was.

What the map shows

Two things. First, coverage is uneven and the unevenness is not random: the artefacts cluster at the levels where the things being described are formal objects. Second, where two artefacts overlap, they do not agree. UML's component, C4's component and Clean Architecture's component occupy overlapping but distinct positions.7,27,28 ArchiMate's service and Kubernetes' Service share a word and almost nothing else.23,30 The problem is not a missing ontology. It is that the ontologies that exist are each locally coherent and mutually incompatible, in a pattern that turns out to be highly structured.

Section 4

The ladder: six levels of description

The structure is a ladder of six rungs. Each rung is a level of description, and what distinguishes a rung from the one below it is not granularity but what the description is answerable to.

The top two rungs come from a distinction that requirements engineering settled thirty years ago and that the rest of the discipline has never fully absorbed. Jackson's formulation separates the machine from the world it is installed in, and locates the requirement in the world.13 Zave and Jackson made the consequence explicit: a requirement is a statement in the optative mood about the environment, a specification is a statement about the phenomena shared between environment and machine, and a domain assumption is an indicative statement about the environment that the machine relies on but does not enforce.3 Gunter, Gunter, Jackson and Zave turned it into a reference model with a proof obligation of exactly the shape this article is concerned with: the specification, together with the domain assumptions, must entail the requirement.14

That obligation is the template for every rung transition. A claim at one rung, together with assumptions, is supposed to entail or realise a claim at the rung above. The interesting question at every transition is the same three-part one: what is the claim, what are the assumptions, and what could establish that the entailment holds.

The six rungs

W — world. The domain as it is, whether or not the system exists. Balances, customers, regulators, the shape of a working day. Answerable to observation. Nothing in the system can make a world-rung statement true; the system can only rely on it or try to change it.

R — requirement. What the system must bring about in that domain, including its quality attributes and the constraints imposed from outside. Governed by ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 for the process and the properties a good requirement has, and by ISO/IEC 25010 for the quality model itself.4,25 Answerable to stakeholder assent now, and to the running system later.

A — architecture. How responsibility is divided so that the requirement can be met: elements, their relationships, the principles of their design and evolution, and the decisions that produced them.2,5 Kruchten's insistence that an architecture is described through several simultaneous views rather than one is a statement about this rung's internal structure.142 Answerable upward to the requirement and downward to the artefacts.

T — technology. The languages, frameworks, schemas and interface descriptions in which the division is expressed: classes, modules, tables, message schemas, API documents.12,32 Answerable to compilation, type checking and schema validation. This rung is formal at both ends, which is why it is the rung where tooling actually works.

C — configuration. The values and manifests that bind the built thing to one place: environments, replica counts, secrets, feature flags, released versions. The twelve-factor formulation of configuration as everything that varies between deployments is the clearest statement of what this rung contains, and Kubernetes object definitions are its most widely used concrete vocabulary.15,47,34 Answerable to the deployment system accepting the declaration.

X — execution. What the system actually does when it runs: processes, requests, transactions, faults, latencies. Answerable to measurement, and to nothing else. The fault–error–failure vocabulary of dependable computing is anchored here, with failure defined as observed deviation of delivered service from the correct service.51

Why rungs, and not layers

A layer is a partition of one description. Two elements in different layers of an architecture are the same kind of thing at different positions in a permitted-use relation.20 A rung is different: two elements on different rungs are not the same kind of thing at different granularity, and the relation between them is not containment. It is a claim, and the claim carries an obligation.

The standards world has an established precedent for this move. IEC 81346 structures technical systems by aspect — function, product, location, and in its 2022 second edition a type aspect — each with its own independent structure over the same objects, so that the same physical thing has a function designation, a product designation and a location designation that need not nest.24 It is, in effect, a standardised refusal to have one parthood relation. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010's architecture viewpoints are the software equivalent.2

Two clarifications are needed, because the ladder is easy to misread.

It is not a process. Nothing here says that engineers do W, then R, then A, then T. Swartout and Balzer's argument against exactly that reading is the classical one, and it has never been refuted.81 Lehman's laws describe the consequence for systems embedded in the world: an E-type program must be continually adapted or it becomes progressively less satisfactory, because the world-rung statements it relies on go stale.143

Nor is it refinement. Stepwise refinement decomposes a task into subtasks in the same conceptual vocabulary; moving down a rung changes the vocabulary and the judge.67 The distinction shows up as the frame problem in procedure specifications: stating what an operation changes leaves open what it leaves alone, and the omission is not recoverable from the specification itself.144 Requirements engineering has been explicit about this since its roadmap papers, which treat the relationship between the environment and the machine as the discipline's organising problem rather than as a preliminary.145,146

It is also not a claim about abstraction. A Kubernetes manifest is not more concrete than an architecture diagram in the sense of containing more detail about the same thing; it is a different kind of statement about a different thing, answerable to a different judge. The philosophical literature on what a program is has circled this point for decades without settling it: programs have been argued to be abstract artefacts, dual entities with both a syntactic and a semantic nature, and things whose correctness is only ever relative to a model.147,148,149,150,151 The ladder does not resolve that debate. It only insists that a description says which of those things it is about.

Section 5

The census: twenty-nine words

Twenty-nine words follow. They were selected on one criterion: the word is used, in ordinary professional practice, to describe a software system, and at least two authorities of general application define it at different rungs. Words used only within one community (a single framework's jargon, a single vendor's product vocabulary) are excluded, because the whole point is words whose ambiguity crosses community boundaries.

How to read a plate

Each plate carries the term, its ontological sort, every sense the survey found, the rung at which each sense sits, a note, and a binding post.

The sort is the ontological category the term's primary sense falls into, in the vocabulary of foundational ontology: a kind, a role, a relator, a quality, an event or process, a situation, an information object, a disposition, a collective.89,11 It is recorded because it is the answer a conventional ontology would give, and because one of the measurements below asks how much that answer buys.

The senses are listed from the highest rung down. A sense is included only if an authority of general application — an international standard, an OMG or W3C specification, a canonical text, or documentation that the industry treats as normative — defines the term that way. Senses attested by more authorities are weighted more heavily in the entropy measurement; the sources are cited on each sense.

The primary binding is the rung at which the term's own defining authority puts it. Where more than one authority has a claim, the primary binding is the one whose scope is the term itself rather than a passing use of it.

The check recorded on the binding post is the weakest mechanism that could establish that an artefact one or more rungs down preserves the claim made with the term in its primary sense. Four values are available: logic over the models alone; static analysis of artefacts; dynamic execution, test or observation; and human judgement, meaning no mechanism at all. The binding post draws this as a probe lead dropping from the primary terminal to the rung where the mechanism bites, with the span between them hatched. That span is the lag.

The coding rule that does the work
The check is the weakest sufficient mechanism, not the strongest available one. Anything can be checked by a person, so human judgement is recorded only where nothing weaker will do. Anything checkable by logic is also checkable by running the system, so dynamic is recorded only where no static or deductive route exists. Coded this way, the distribution is a statement about the vocabulary, not about the state of tooling.

collective (whole with parts)  ·  bound at the requirement rung  ·  human judgement only

  • WThe system-of-interest, or entity of interest: the whole whose life cycle is under consideration, drawn to include operators and environment1,2
  • RThe scope fixed by the requirements: what the machine will do and what is left to the environment3,4
  • AThe architectural subject: elements, their relationships, and the principles of their design and evolution2,5
  • XThe deployed, running system as it is observed6

P02Actor

role (played by an agent)  ·  bound at the world rung  ·  human judgement only

  • WAn external person, organisation or system that interacts with the system; a stakeholder class7,4
  • XA concurrent computational entity that reacts to messages (the actor model)8

A full homonym: the runtime sense shares nothing with the world sense but the word. An ontology should refuse to let one term carry both.

Section 6

The rung is the sense

The census makes one structural fact available for measurement, and it is the central finding of the article.

Within a single term, no two senses sit on the same rung. Across all twenty-nine terms and all ninety catalogued senses, there is not one case where naming the rung fails to pick out exactly one sense. The rung is a sufficient statistic for the sense.

That would be an uninteresting artefact of construction if the ladder had been built to make it true — with enough rungs, any set of senses separates. So the sharper question is how coarse the ladder can be made before it stops holding. An exhaustive search over every contiguous partition of the six rungs into fewer bands answers it.

The full six-rung ladder is the only division that leaves no term ambiguous. Merging any single adjacent pair breaks at least three terms, and the cost of each merge is informative:

  • Merging world into requirement collides three terms: system, requirement, capability. This is the merge Zave and Jackson spent a paper arguing against, and the three casualties are exactly the words whose world-rung and requirement-rung senses their distinction separates.3
  • Merging requirement into architecture collides six, adding constraint, quality attribute and service. This is the merge that produces an architecture document in which nobody can tell which statements are obligations and which are decisions.
  • Merging architecture into technology collides eleven — more than a third of the vocabulary. This is the most expensive merge on the ladder, and it is the one that UML and C4 both effectively perform, because both define a component in terms that reach across the boundary.7,27
  • Merging technology into configuration collides six, and merging configuration into execution collides six more, including component, service and resource — the three words that a platform engineer and an architect are most likely to be using simultaneously about different things.

What the rung is worth against the alternative

A conventional ontology would identify a term by its ontological sort: this is a kind, that is a relator, the other is a perdurant. How much does that buy, compared with recording the rungs the word reaches?

Over the 406 unordered pairs of terms, the set of rungs a word reaches separates 96.8% of them; the ontological sort separates 94.1%. Adding the checkability mechanism to the rung profile reaches 98.0%, occupying 22 distinct cells of a possible 256. The primary binding rung on its own separates only 74.9%, which is the expected result and worth stating: it is not the level a word is bound at that identifies it, but the pattern of levels it reaches.

The margin over ontological sort is small, and it should not be oversold. The point is not that sort is useless — it is the right answer to a different question — but that the cheaper, more local coding does at least as much discriminating work, and unlike sort it is a property an author can record at the point of writing without committing to a foundational ontology.

The cost of leaving it unsaid

How much information is lost when a term appears without its rung? Weighting each sense by the number of independent authorities that define it, the mean Shannon entropy across the twenty-nine terms is 1.525 bits per occurrence.

A rung label drawn from six values costs 2.585 bits. So the annotation costs about 1.06 bits more than the ambiguity it removes — an unusually favourable ratio for a disambiguation scheme, and the reason this is a tractable problem rather than an intractable one. A document containing a thousand term occurrences carries roughly 1,525 bits of unresolved sense ambiguity; annotating every occurrence with a rung removes all of it at a cost of 2,585 bits. Nothing in the discipline's notation currently spends those bits.

Eight terms carry between 1.9 and 2.0 bits each — transaction, system, service, process, entity, constraint, event, dependency. These are, without exception, the words that appear in the first paragraph of a system description.

Section 7

What no ontology can decide

Suppose the ontology is built and the knowledge graph populated, every requirement is stated, every quality attribute is quantified, every constraint is formalised, and the whole thing is consistent. How much of the system has been determined?

Far less than the proposal assumes, and the shortfall is structural rather than a matter of insufficient detail. The reason was stated plainly by Rich and Waters in 1988, in their list of the myths of automatic programming: the myth that a specification determines a program, and the related myth that specifications can be complete and unambiguous.82 Manna and Waldinger's deductive synthesis derives programs from specifications, and the derivation is a proof search whose branching is exactly the space of implementations the specification admits.83 Four decades of program synthesis research have refined the search without shrinking the space.84

Counting the residue

Take one service: durable, authenticated, auditable, with a defined interface. Enumerate the decisions a designer must make that the semantic model does not make for them — authentication protocol, session state location, durability substrate, consistency protocol, delivery semantics, failure isolation boundary, audit sink, schema evolution policy, key management, retry placement.16,43,39,48 Ten dimensions; 408,240 distinct admissible designs; 18.64 bits to name one.

Now add five demanding quality requirements: a latency bound, tamper-evident auditability, a prohibition on credentials leaving the host, a recovery point objective of zero, and single sign-on with the existing estate. Each is a proposition the ontology can carry, and each eliminates options.

They remove 98.1% of the space. What remains is 7,776 designs, all of them fully conforming. Four of the ten dimensions — session state location, failure isolation boundary, schema evolution policy, retry placement — are untouched by any of the five requirements, and those four alone carry 81 designs. To reach a single design you would have to supply at least 13 further bits of decision, and those bits are not requirements. They are engineering judgement.

The example is a toy, and the specific counts are illustrative rather than empirical. What is not a toy is the shape: adding requirements attacks the space multiplicatively along the dimensions they touch and leaves the untouched dimensions exactly as they were. Since the number of dimensions a requirement touches is bounded by what the stakeholder can think to say, and the number of dimensions grows with the system, the residue grows with the system too.

The other residue: what the words themselves leave open

Underdetermination of the design is the structural residue. There is a second one, closer to this article's subject, which is that the requirement statement itself is ambiguous in ways the author cannot see. Berry and Kamsties' treatment distinguishes the ambiguity a reader notices from the ambiguity a reader resolves silently and wrongly, and it is the second that survives review.152 Gervasi and Zowghi's work on reasoning about inconsistency in natural-language requirements is the closest the field has come to mechanising the detection, and it works by translating to a logic first — which is to say, by fixing the rung.153 Boehm's early account of verification and validation already separated building the product right from building the right product, which is the same boundary drawn between two adjacent rungs.154

Why this is the ontology's best argument, not its worst

The instinct on seeing 7,776 conforming designs is to regard it as a failure of the specification. It is not. Every one of those designs satisfies every stated requirement; the choice among them is an engineering decision that nobody stated because nobody was asked. Left implicit, that decision is made by whoever writes the code first, is never recorded, and becomes an assumption that the next engineer cannot distinguish from a requirement.

This is precisely what the decision-oriented view of architecture is for. Kruchten's ontology of architectural design decisions and Jansen and Bosch's account of architecture as the set of decisions taken exist to make that residue an object of record.36,37 A software ontology that exposed the residue — here are the dimensions your requirements do not constrain, and here is who decided them and when — would be doing something no current artefact does, and would be more valuable than one that pretended to eliminate it.

The deeper limit
Smith's argument about the limits of correctness applies to every rung transition in the ladder: a proof establishes a relation between a model and an artefact, never between an artefact and the world, and the gap between the model and the world is bridged by a judgement that no formalism covers.151 The world rung is where that judgement lives, and it is the one rung no mechanism reaches.
Section 8

What a mechanism can establish

The binding posts record, for each term, the weakest mechanism that could establish that an artefact one or more rungs down preserves the claim. Collected, they produce a ledger.

Read it as a picture of where verification is available and where it is not. The lag — the distance between where a word is bound and where a mechanism can test it — averages 1.90 rungs across the twenty-one terms that have a mechanism at all.

Three results deserve to be stated separately.

Logic over the models alone establishes nothing. Zero of twenty-nine. This is the finding most damaging to the proposal in its strong form, and it deserves care, because it is not a claim that logic is useless. Consistency checking over a model is real and valuable: Alloy finds counterexamples to structural claims;155 Heitmeyer, Jeffords and Labaw's consistency checking of requirements specifications is a working technique;156 SHACL validates a graph against shapes.88 What logic over the models cannot establish is that a lower artefact preserved the claim, because the lower artefact is not in the model. The moment you ask whether TransferService.java realises the capability TransferFunds, you have left the model and are doing either static analysis of code or observation of behaviour.

Ten terms are checkable statically, and they are the ones everyone already tools. Component, module, interface, API, dependency, layer, resource, deployment unit, configuration, version. What they share is that both ends of the claim are formal artefacts. Murphy, Notkin and Sullivan's software reflexion models are the canonical demonstration: state the intended architecture, extract the actual dependencies, and compute the difference mechanically.35 Notice that the technique works precisely because the layer rule and the source code are both syntactic objects. Contract conformance in the behavioural sense — Liskov and Wing's substitutability, Meyer's design by contract — requires more than signature matching, and the gap is where the static check stops being sufficient.31,22,21

Four terms bound at the requirement rung are checkable only by running the system. Requirement, constraint, capability, quality attribute. Their lag is four rungs — the widest on the ledger. A quality attribute is stated at the requirement rung and settled at the execution rung, with nothing in between able to close it. Bass, Clements and Kazman's tactics are heuristics for making the eventual measurement come out right, not derivations that guarantee it.5 ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119's definition of a test case as inputs, conditions and expected results is the mechanism, and its limit is that no mechanism establishes that the test checks the requirement it names.50

What the formal-methods record actually shows

It is worth being accurate about the deductive option, because the article's zero is easy to misread as dismissal. Formal methods work, at a cost, on artefacts that are formal at both ends. The state-of-the-art surveys thirty years apart tell a consistent story: the techniques are sound, the tooling has improved enormously, and the constraint on use is the cost of writing and maintaining the formal object rather than the cost of the proof.157,158 Hoare's verifying-compiler grand challenge was framed in exactly those terms.159 seL4 proved functional correctness of an operating-system kernel against an abstract specification;160 Amazon Web Services reported using TLA+ on production systems and finding design defects that testing had not, which is the clearest industrial evidence available.161,162

Two caveats matter here. First, every one of those results establishes a relation between an artefact and a model, never between an artefact and the world. Second, even a proved translation can be undermined by the tool that performs it: random differential testing of production C compilers found hundreds of defects in compilers that were not formally verified, and notably found far fewer in the one that was.163,164 The moral is not that proof is unavailable. It is that proof is available exactly where this article's ledger says static and deductive checks live, and nowhere above them.

A smaller point, on the terms themselves: even at the technology rung, naming is not a solved problem. Deissenboeck and Pizka's work on concise and consistent naming takes the position that an identifier is a mapping from a concept to a name, that the mapping should be bijective, and that most codebases violate this in both directions.165 That is the same finding as this article's, one rung lower and restricted to a single program.

The eight with no mechanism at all

System, actor, entity, assumption, environment, role, decision, test. Six of the eight are bound at the world or requirement rung. These are not hard cases that better tooling would fix. Whether the table accounts represents the customer's account, whether the boundary you drew around the system is the right boundary, whether the assumption you relied on holds in the world — these are judgements, and their being judgements is exactly Smith's point about correctness being model-relative.151

That test appears in this list is worth dwelling on. A test's execution is checkable; whether a test verifies the requirement it is attached to is not. The traceability link from requirement to test case is an assertion by a person, and no amount of coverage tooling converts it into a derivation.

The one place semantic preservation has been achieved

Between two formal levels, with enormous effort, it can be done. CompCert is a C compiler whose semantic preservation is proved: the generated assembly behaves as the source program's semantics prescribe.164 It is the existence proof the whole proposal wants. It is also a compiler between two languages with complete formal semantics, which is a description of exactly one rung transition on this ladder — and not one of the ones the proposal is interested in. The transitions people want automated (world to requirement, requirement to architecture) have no formal object at the upper end, and no amount of work on the lower end supplies one.

Section 9

What the graph buys, and what an unmarked edge costs

The traceability graph is the part of the proposal with the clearest practical payoff. Select a requirement, traverse to the capabilities that realise it, the components that implement them, the code, the tests, the deployment; change the requirement and read off what is affected. Gotel and Finkelstein's analysis of the traceability problem established that the difficulty is not recording links but sustaining them, and Ramesh and Jarke's reference models were derived from what practitioners actually needed to trace rather than from what tools happened to support.166,167

The question this article can answer is a narrower one: what does the ambiguity measured above do to such a graph?

Model a traceability graph over the six rungs, with edges from each rung to the one below. A fraction of those edges are labelled with a term whose rung is not marked — an edge that says depends on or realises or is a component of without saying at which rung. A reader resolving one of those edges will sometimes follow it to the wrong place: not a random place, but the element the same word denotes one rung further down.

At one edge in ten unmarked, the computed impact set is 94.0% precise and 88.5% complete. The failure is asymmetric, and in the dangerous direction. A misread edge skips a rung, so the analysis misses affected elements more often than it invents them: 11.5% missed against 6.0% spurious. An impact analysis that over-reports wastes effort; one that under-reports ships a change with a consequence nobody saw.

At three edges in ten — not an implausible rate for a hand-maintained architecture document — recall falls to 64.0%. The graph is still useful, but it is a hint generator rather than a safety net, and it is important that a tool built on it says so.

What it costs to keep consistent

The second practical question is what the ongoing obligation looks like. Two kinds of check arise on a cross-rung model. Completeness: every element at a rung has at least one realiser below it. Non-interference: no two elements make contradictory claims about the same element below.

The first is linear in model size and cheap. The second is the one that can blow up, and the measurement shows that what makes it blow up is not size.

With realisers distributed uniformly, the non-interference obligation grows with an exponent of 1.04 on model size — essentially linear — over a fortyfold increase in elements. Holding size fixed and increasing only the concentration of realisers multiplies it 153-fold, from 1.67 to 255 checks per element. Concentration is the shared database, the god service, the common base class, the single configuration object that everything reads: the elements that many upstream claims converge on.

This is a restatement, in the ontology's own terms, of what Parnas said about modular decomposition in 1972 and what Stevens, Myers and Constantine graded as coupling in 1974.29,33 The cost of keeping a cross-rung model consistent is a property of where the model converges, not of how large it is — which also means that a system whose architecture has one central element will be expensive to describe ontologically for exactly the same reason it is expensive to change.

The agreement ceiling

One more measurement bears on whether any of this can be populated at scale. Suppose two competent modellers independently assign a rung to each occurrence of a term in a corpus, with the surrounding context settling the sense some fraction of the time and each modeller otherwise choosing among the term's attested senses.

With no contextual help at all, Cohen's kappa is 0.225 — barely above chance. To reach 0.80, the conventional threshold for substantial agreement, the context must settle the sense in about 86% of occurrences. That is a demanding figure, and it is the honest answer to the question of why ontology population has never scaled: the vocabulary's own sense distribution supplies almost no agreement, so nearly all of it has to come from context that the notation does not currently carry.

Section 10

Where the language model actually sits

Everything above is true whether or not language models exist. But the reason the proposal is being revisited now is that a language model appears to remove the cost that killed it before: somebody has to write the semantic description, and nobody ever did.

The systematic review of large language models in software engineering covers 395 papers across the lifecycle and is the right starting point for calibration.168 Fan and colleagues' survey is the complementary statement of open problems.169 The picture that emerges from both is consistent with the structure this article has described: models are extremely good at transitions between representations and unreliable as authorities about which representation is in force.

The four jobs the evidence supports

Interpretation, completion, transformation and explanation. The clearest positive result for the proposal is Endres, Fakhoury, Chakraborty and Lahiri's work on translating informal natural language intent into formal method postconditions: the generated postconditions are generally correct, non-trivial, and discriminative enough to separate 64 real historical bugs in industrial-scale Java projects from their fixed versions.170 That is exactly a rung-crossing translation — requirement-rung prose into a technology-rung assertion — and it works because the far end is a mechanically checkable object. Ontology learning from text is an active benchmarked task on the same principle.171

The benchmark lineage matters for reading any of this. Functional-correctness evaluation of generated code began with HumanEval and the pass@k metric;172 the field's current reference point for realistic work is a benchmark of real repository issues rather than isolated functions;173 and the survey literature on code generation specifically has grown large enough to need its own systematic treatment.174 Every one of those is a measurement at the technology rung. None of them measures whether a generated artefact preserves a claim made at the architecture rung or above.

The studies that do try are less encouraging. A controlled evaluation of models' grasp of cohesion and coupling — two of the oldest design concepts in the discipline — found the grasp degrades markedly once contextual noise is introduced and once the task moves from verification to open-ended generation.175 An experience report on using a model for UML modelling tasks found competent syntax and unreliable semantics, with the reliability falling as the model was asked for things further from the training distribution.176 Both are measurements of the architecture rung, and both find the same shape.

The three failures, and why they are the same failure

Terminology. Ejaz, Peña and Herbold asked state-of-the-art models to judge whether definitions drawn from ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765 — the systems and software engineering vocabulary — were correct, mixing genuine definitions with systematically falsified ones, both by substituting key terms and by removing critical information.177,6 Most models detected the falsifications with high accuracy and also rejected many correct definitions, which the authors characterise as a systematic rejection bias rather than genuine discriminative understanding; explicit reasoning did not consistently help and sometimes hurt. This is the terminology problem in its purest experimental form. A system that cannot reliably tell a correct definition from a corrupted one is not a candidate for deciding which sense of component a document meant.

Reference. Spracklen and colleagues generated 576,000 code samples across sixteen models and two languages and measured how often the generated code referred to packages that do not exist: at least 5.2% of generated packages for commercial models and 21.7% for open-source ones, with 205,474 distinct hallucinated package names, creating a supply-chain attack surface for anyone who registers them.178 A 2026 replication on five frontier models released between October 2025 and March 2026 found the range compressed roughly elevenfold, to between 4.62% and 6.10%, and — more interestingly for this argument — 127 package names hallucinated identically by all five models.179 The rate fell; the phenomenon did not change character. A fluent reference to a non-existent artefact is precisely a claim asserted at one rung with nothing at the rung below to bind it to.

Adequacy of the check. Liu, Xia, Wang and Zhang extended the test cases of a standard code-generation benchmark by roughly eighty times and found that pass@k fell by up to 19.3 to 28.9%, and that test insufficiency had been producing mis-ranking of models.180 The lesson is not about models. It is that the mechanism everyone was using to establish that a lower artefact satisfied an upper claim was too weak to do so, and nobody noticed until the mechanism was strengthened.

All three are the same failure in different clothes: fluent output at one rung, with no obligation discharged against the rung it claims to serve. This is why the answer is not a better model. The obligation has to be discharged by something whose competence is preservation, not fluency — a type checker, a schema validator, a reflexion model, a test, a measurement.35,50

The division of labour the structure implies

A model should be trusted to propose a binding and never to certify one. That is a stronger constraint than it sounds. It rules out the architecture in which a model reads a requirements document, emits a knowledge graph, and the graph is taken as the system's semantic state. It permits the architecture in which the model emits a candidate graph, every rung-crossing edge in it carries an explicit rung on both ends, and each edge is either discharged by a mechanism or flagged as resting on human judgement — which, per the ledger above, will be true of eight terms in twenty-nine and of every claim whose upper end is at the world rung.

Section 12

What the problem is

The problem is not that software engineering lacks an ontology. It is that the discipline's vocabulary is a set of words each bound at a level of description, reused at every other level without marking the change, and that almost nothing in the notation records the binding or the obligation it creates.

Stated that way, the research programme changes shape. It is no longer write down the kinds of thing that exist in software, which is a task with no completion criterion and four incompatible top-level answers already certified by the same standard.91,92,93,94 It becomes four narrower questions, each of which admits evidence.

At which rung was this word bound? The measurement says the sense is recoverable from the rung and from nothing cheaper: six values, 2.585 bits, and the ambiguity it removes is 1.525 bits. It also says six is the minimum — every coarser contiguous division collides at least three terms, and the architecture-technology merge collides eleven. A notation that recorded one rung per term occurrence would eliminate, by construction, all the sense ambiguity this survey found. No current notation does.

What has to be true for a use one rung down to preserve it? The template already exists: Gunter, Gunter, Jackson and Zave's obligation that specification plus domain assumptions entails requirement is the general form, stated for one transition.14 What does not exist is the same obligation stated for the other four transitions, per relation rather than per term. An ontology whose relations each carried a preservation obligation would be a different kind of artefact from any in the survey above.

What kind of thing can discharge that obligation? Logic, static analysis, measurement, or a person. The census answer is stark: never the first, in ten cases the second, in eleven the third, and in eight there is no mechanism. Recording the answer per relation would tell a team, before it starts, which of its claims can ever be verified and which are permanently matters of judgement. That is a modest deliverable and nothing currently provides it.

What is left undecided when every obligation is discharged? Several thousand conforming designs for one service, with four of ten dimensions untouched by any requirement. The residue is engineering judgement and it does not shrink to zero. The ontology's job is to expose it and attribute it, not to pretend it away — which is what the decision-oriented strand of architecture research has been saying since 2004.36,37

What would count as progress

Three things, in order of tractability.

First, a rung-marked vocabulary. Not a new ontology: an annotation convention over the existing one, with the six rungs as its only new primitive, applied to the terms in ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765 and to the elements of UML, SysML v2, ArchiMate and C4.6,7,140,23,27 The measurements above suggest this is cheap and high-yield, and the agreement study suggests it would have to be authored rather than inferred, since the context required to reach usable inter-annotator agreement is not present in current documents.

Second, obligation-carrying relations. Every cross-rung relation in an architecture description should state what it claims and what would refute it. The reflexion-model literature shows this is achievable for the architecture-to-technology transition;35 the requirements literature shows the form of the obligation for the world-to-requirement one.14 The middle transitions are where the work is.

Third, and most valuable, evidence. The single most conspicuous absence in a literature going back to 1982 is a controlled study showing that any of this changes an engineering outcome. The one careful industrial evaluation in the survey measured knowledge retrieval from documentation.120 The systematic reviews of both the requirements and the systems-engineering literatures report the same gap.61,60 Before the field builds another ontology it could measure whether marking the rung reduces defects at a rung boundary, which is a study anyone with two teams and a quarter could run.

A closing distinction

There is a version of this proposal that is not worth pursuing, and a version that is. The version that is not worth pursuing is the one in which the ontology is the source of truth and everything else is generated: it founders on underdetermination, which is structural, and on the absence of a formal object at the world rung, which is permanent. The version worth pursuing is narrower and duller and probably achievable: a convention that says which level of description a statement is answerable to, a relation vocabulary that says what a downstream use is claiming, and an honest register of which of those claims a machine can check.

The first version promises to eliminate engineering judgement. The second promises to say exactly where it was exercised, by whom, and on what evidence. The first has been promised for forty years. The second has never been tried.
