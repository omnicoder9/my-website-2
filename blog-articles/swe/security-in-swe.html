§ 1Two kinds of wrong

Ordinary engineering asks whether a system does what it is supposed to do. Security engineering asks whether it can be made to do anything else. The first question is universally quantified over the inputs you thought of; the second is existentially quantified over the inputs someone else will think of. That difference is not a matter of degree. It changes what counts as evidence, what counts as done, and who gets a vote in the design review.

A functional bug is discovered by a user who is trying to succeed. A security bug is discovered by an adversary who is trying to make you fail, who has read your source code, who has a budget, and who is not obliged to use your product in any order you anticipated. Test suites are a poor instrument here because tests encode the developer’s model of the system, and the vulnerability is precisely the gap between that model and the machine. Every control described in this article exists to shrink a specific gap of that kind.

The second thing worth saying at the outset is that security is not a property a system has. It is a relation between a system, an asset, and an adversary with particular capabilities. “Is this secure?” has no truth value until you say secure against whom, protecting what, for how long. James Mickens made the point more memorably than anyone: the defences that stop a bored teenager and the defences that stop a nation-state intelligence service are not the same defences, and pretending otherwise wastes money at one end and lives at the other.1 Ross Anderson made the same point in the register of economics: most security failures are not failures of mathematics but failures of incentive, where the party able to fix a problem is not the party that suffers from it.2

The fifteen controls that follow are the working vocabulary of the field. Each is presented the same way: the invariant it is supposed to enforce, what an attacker gains when it is missing, and at least one case where its absence became public. They are not independent. Least privilege determines the value of an authentication bypass; output encoding is meaningless if the template engine can be reached by a route that skips it; audit logging is the control that tells you whether any of the others worked. Treat the set as a system, because attackers do.

On the digest in the masthead The hexadecimal string at the top of this page is a real SHA-256 of the article’s rendered text, computed when the file was built. It demonstrates integrity and nothing else: change one character and the digest changes, but a digest with no key and no signature is a claim anyone can recompute — including someone who altered the text and recomputed it to match. Integrity without authenticity is a common and expensive confusion; §14 is about the difference.

§ 2A short history, in four eras

The discipline is roughly sixty years old and has changed its mind about where the boundary lies four times.

Confinement, 1965–1985

The first era assumed the enemy was inside the machine room. Time-sharing systems put mutually distrustful users on one computer, which made isolation an engineering requirement rather than a philosophical one. The 1970 Ware report, produced by a Defense Science Board task force, is the first systematic statement of the problem: once a computer serves users at different clearance levels, the hardware and operating system become part of the security perimeter.3 James Anderson’s 1972 planning study supplied the concept that still underwrites every access-control system: a reference monitor that is always invoked, tamper-proof, and small enough to be verified.4 Bell and LaPadula turned military classification into a formal model with provable properties.5 By 1985 the Orange Book had converted the era’s consensus into a graded evaluation regime, and with it the assumption that security is something a product is certified to possess before deployment rather than something maintained afterwards.6

In 1975, Saltzer and Schroeder distilled the era’s experience into eight design principles — economy of mechanism, fail-safe defaults, complete mediation, open design, separation of privilege, least privilege, least common mechanism, psychological acceptability.7 Half of the controls in this article are those principles wearing modern clothes. The same decade produced public-key cryptography,8,9 the first protocol for authenticating parties over a hostile network,10 and Morris and Thompson’s account of why Unix passwords are salted and slow — an argument from attacker economics published in 1979 that most of the industry then ignored for thirty years.11 Thompson’s Turing Award lecture closed the era with the observation that you cannot trust code you did not write yourself, and possibly not even then.12

Connection, 1986–2001

Networking dissolved the machine room. The 1988 Morris worm — propagating through a buffer overflow in fingerd, a debug mode in sendmail, and weak passwords — demonstrated that a vulnerability in one program was now a property of the whole network, and produced the first coordinated response organisation.13 Kerberos brought Needham–Schroeder into production,14 SSL brought cryptography to commerce, and a Phrack article in 1996 turned stack smashing from folklore into a repeatable technique with a tutorial.15 By 1999 the field had a shared vocabulary for vulnerabilities (CVE)16 and a shared vocabulary for threats: Kohnfelder and Garg’s STRIDE, written as an internal Microsoft memo.17 Schneier’s attack trees arrived the same year.18

Process, 2002–2016

After Code Red and Nimda, Bill Gates halted feature work across Microsoft with the Trustworthy Computing memo, and the resulting Security Development Lifecycle became the template for how a large organisation embeds security into engineering rather than bolting it on at the end.19,20 OWASP published the first Top 10 in 2003, giving developers a prioritised list in their own language.21 The era’s lesson was that security is a property of the development process, not of a final audit. Its counter-lesson arrived as Stuxnet, which used stolen code-signing certificates and four zero-days to cross an air gap and damage centrifuges — proof that the sufficiently motivated adversary is a real engineering constraint, not a thought experiment.22 Then Heartbleed showed that critical infrastructure could rest on an unfunded volunteer project,23 and Mirai showed that shipping devices with default credentials produces a botnet large enough to take out a DNS provider.24

Supply chain, 2017–present

The current era began when attackers noticed that compromising one build system is cheaper than compromising ten thousand targets. Equifax lost 145 million records to an unpatched Struts deserialisation flaw that had a fix available for two months.25,26 SolarWinds put malicious code into a signed, legitimately distributed update by compromising the build pipeline itself.27 Log4Shell demonstrated that a logging library — the most boring dependency imaginable — could be a remote code execution primitive reachable from any string an application logged.28,29 The XZ Utils backdoor of 2024 was social engineering played over three years against an exhausted maintainer.30,31 In September 2025 a self-replicating worm on npm harvested developer credentials and used them to republish the maintainers’ own packages, compromising more than five hundred of them.32,33 The response has been regulatory as much as technical: software bills of materials, secure-by-design commitments, and the EU Cyber Resilience Act placing obligations on vendors rather than operators.34,35,36,37

Part I

Deciding what to build against

control · modelThreat modelling

model
Invariant
Every trust boundary in the design has been named, and every element that crosses one has an identified threat and a decision recorded against it.
Without it
You defend the attacks you happen to imagine, in the order you happen to imagine them, and discover the boundary you forgot when someone else crosses it.
Case
The 2015 Jeep Cherokee work: the head unit was assumed to be on a separate bus from the drivetrain. It was bridged, and a cellular-reachable service became a path to the CAN bus.38

Threat modelling is the practice of reasoning about a design before it exists, and it is the only control in this article that costs nothing but attention. Adam Shostack reduced it to four questions that a team can actually hold in their heads: what are we building, what can go wrong, what are we going to do about it, and did we do a good job.39 The Threat Modeling Manifesto adds the observation that the value is in the conversation, not the artefact — a whiteboard photograph that changed a design beats a forty-page document filed and forgotten.40

The classic technique starts with a data-flow diagram, because threats live at boundaries and a DFD is a drawing of boundaries. Four element types — external entities, processes, data stores, data flows — are enough. Then STRIDE, from Kohnfelder and Garg’s 1999 memo, supplies the prompt list: spoofing attacks authentication, tampering attacks integrity, repudiation attacks accountability, information disclosure attacks confidentiality, denial of service attacks availability, elevation of privilege attacks authorization.17 The mnemonic is not a taxonomy of attacks; it is a taxonomy of the properties you are trying to preserve, which is why it survives changes in technology. Applied per element rather than to the system as a whole, it produces a bounded, checkable list.

Other frameworks address other questions. Attack trees, from Schneier, model an adversary’s goal as a root and refine it into alternatives, which is the right shape when you need to compare defensive options by cost.18 PASTA is a seven-stage, risk-centric process that produces business-legible output for organisations that must justify spending.41 LINDDUN does for privacy what STRIDE does for security, enumerating linkability, identifiability, non-repudiation, detectability, disclosure, unawareness, and non-compliance — a distinct axis, because a system can be perfectly confidential and still be a surveillance instrument.42 MITRE ATT&CK inverts the perspective: rather than enumerate what could go wrong in your design, it catalogues what real adversary groups have actually done, mapped to tactics and techniques, which makes it the natural bridge between a threat model and the detections in §17.43

Doing it without ceremony

The failure mode of threat modelling is ritual: a mandatory template completed after the design is frozen. The practices that survive contact with a delivery schedule share three properties. They are triggered by a diagram change rather than a calendar. They produce a small number of decisions, each with an owner and a test. And they record the assumptions explicitly, because an assumption is a threat model’s load-bearing structure — “this queue is only reachable from inside the VPC” is a fine reason not to authenticate a consumer, right up until a peering change makes it false and nobody re-reads the document.

A practical form: for each boundary crossing, write one line of the shape “X can Y because Z, and we prevent it by W”, and make W something a test can assert. If W is “code review will catch it”, the control is a hope. Insecure design has been a distinct category in the OWASP Top 10 since 2021 precisely because a large class of vulnerabilities cannot be found by scanning the implementation: they are correct implementations of a design that never had the property you wanted.21

control · riskRisk assessment

risk
Invariant
Remediation effort is ordered by expected loss — likelihood of exploitation against this system, times impact on this business — not by the severity number attached to the finding.
Without it
Teams patch what is loudest. The backlog grows faster than it drains, everything becomes “critical”, and the one flaw under active exploitation waits behind four hundred that are not.
Case
Equifax: a CVSS 10.0 in Apache Struts, publicly patched in March 2017, still exploitable in May because the scan that would have found it did not cover the affected host.25

Risk assessment is the arithmetic that connects a technical finding to a business decision, and it is where security engineering most often loses credibility. NIST SP 800-30 gives the canonical structure — identify threat sources and events, identify vulnerabilities and predisposing conditions, determine likelihood and impact, communicate results — and SP 800-37 wraps it in a lifecycle for systems that must be authorised to operate.44,45 The structure is sound. The difficulty is entirely in the inputs.

Three instruments dominate practice, and they answer different questions. CVSS scores the intrinsic characteristics of a weakness: attack vector, complexity, privileges required, impact on confidentiality, integrity and availability. Version 4.0 finally separated the base metrics from threat and environmental metrics clearly enough that the base score can be read as what it always was — a severity rating in the abstract, with no knowledge of your deployment.46 EPSS estimates the probability that a vulnerability will be exploited in the wild in the next thirty days, built from observed exploitation data rather than expert judgement.47,48 The CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue records exploitation that has actually been observed, and for US federal agencies it carries remediation deadlines.49 Severity, probability, observation: use all three and the ordering changes dramatically.

Two structural facts now complicate this. First, volume: published CVEs have grown to the point where the National Vulnerability Database can no longer enrich them all. In April 2026 NIST moved to a risk-based enrichment model, prioritising vulnerabilities in the KEV catalogue, in software used by the federal government, and in software designated critical under Executive Order 14028; submissions had grown 263% between 2020 and 2025, and NIST enriched roughly 42,000 CVEs in 2025 while still falling behind.50 Everything else remains listed but unenriched. Any vulnerability-management process that silently assumed NVD would supply a CVSS vector and product mapping for every finding now has a gap where its inputs used to be, and the compensating work — deciding severity from the advisory text and your own deployment knowledge — lands on engineering teams.

Second, calibration. Scores are ordinal labels, and adding them up does not produce a quantity. The FAIR taxonomy and the quantitative school associated with Hubbard and Seiersen argue for expressing risk as a probability distribution over loss in currency, elicited from calibrated estimators, precisely so that it can be compared with the cost of the fix and with every other investment the business is considering.51,52 The objection that “we don’t have the data” is weaker than it looks: a 90% confidence interval that is honestly wide is more useful than a five-point scale that hides its own uncertainty. It also makes the awkward conversations tractable. Some risks are worth accepting; a risk register that cannot represent acceptance is a wish list.

Anti-pattern DREAD — damage, reproducibility, exploitability, affected users, discoverability — is still taught in places. Its numbers are unanchored, so two analysts rating the same issue reliably disagree, and averaging the five values destroys what little signal each carried. Microsoft dropped it from SDL guidance years ago. If you need an ordinal scale, use CVSS with the threat and environmental metrics actually filled in; if you need a decision, use expected loss.
Part II

The shape of the system

control · privLeast privilege

priv
Invariant
Every principal — human, service, process, build job — holds exactly the permissions its current task requires, for exactly as long as the task runs.
Without it
Any single compromise inherits the union of everything that principal was ever allowed to do, which in practice is everything the team could not be bothered to scope.
Case
Capital One, 2019: a server-side request forgery reached the EC2 instance metadata service, retrieved credentials for a role with broad S3 read access, and roughly 100 million applications were copied.53,54

Saltzer and Schroeder stated it in 1975: every program and every user should operate using the least set of privileges necessary to complete the job.7 Fifty years later it remains the control with the largest gap between universal agreement and actual practice, because privilege accumulates through a thousand individually reasonable decisions and is removed only by deliberate effort that no feature ticket ever funds.

The underlying distinction is between ambient authority and explicit authority. In an ambient system, a process’s permissions are attributes of the process: if the code can name a resource, it can act on it. In a capability system, going back to Dennis and Van Horn in 1966, the reference is the permission — you cannot act on what you were not handed.55 Norman Hardy’s “confused deputy” note is the canonical demonstration of why this matters: a privileged program tricked into using its authority on behalf of a caller who lacks it.56 Every SSRF, every path traversal into a service account’s home directory, and every “the CI job could push to production” incident is a confused deputy.57

Where the levers are

  • Process level. Linux capabilities split root into ~40 distinct privileges, so a program that needs to bind port 80 can hold CAP_NET_BIND_SERVICE and nothing else.59 seccomp filters restrict which syscalls a process may issue at all, which is the difference between a container escape being possible and being unreachable.60
  • Orchestration level. Kubernetes RBAC is deny-by-default, but the defaults teams copy from tutorials frequently are not: cluster-admin bound to a service account is the modern equivalent of running everything as root.61
  • Cloud IAM. Wildcards are where least privilege goes to die. s3:* on Resource: "*" is a decision to make every future bucket part of this application’s blast radius. Permission-boundary and service-control-policy mechanisms exist so that a delegated administrator cannot grant more than they were given.
  • Time. Standing access is the largest single contributor to blast radius. Just-in-time elevation — request, approve, expire — converts a permanent capability into an auditable event, and the audit trail is often more valuable than the restriction.

Two cautions. Least privilege applied without measurement produces breakage and then blanket exceptions, so derive policies from observed access rather than from imagination: most cloud providers can generate a policy from CloudTrail-style access logs, and the honest starting point is “what did this service actually call last quarter”. And privilege is not only about data access. The privilege to deploy, to change a DNS record, to modify a CI workflow, or to approve a merge is frequently more dangerous than the privilege to read a table, because it converts into all the others.

control · depthDefence in depth

depth
Invariant
No single control failure produces a total compromise; each layer’s failure mode is independent of the others, and at least one layer is a detective control rather than a preventive one.
Without it
The system has a hard shell and a soft interior, and the first bug that gets through the shell is worth as much as every bug behind it combined.
Case
Target, 2013: network credentials from an HVAC vendor reached the payment environment because the network was flat where it should have been segmented.62

Defence in depth is the cheapest idea in security and the easiest to fake. The genuine version requires that the layers fail for different reasons. Three web application firewalls in series are one layer wearing three hats; a WAF, a parameterised query, and a database account with no DROP privilege are three layers, because the input that defeats the first has no bearing on the second or third.

James Reason’s model of organisational accidents is the standard mental image: barriers with holes, an incident occurring when the holes line up, and the holes moving over time.63 Its usefulness here is the emphasis on independence. Correlated failure is the enemy: a shared library, a shared credential, a shared assumption about where a request came from. When every layer trusts the same header, you have one layer.

The modern articulation of the idea is zero trust, formalised in NIST SP 800-207: no implicit trust from network location, per-request authentication and authorization, and continuous evaluation of device and identity posture.64 Google’s BeyondCorp moved employee access off the privileged corporate network entirely,65 and BeyondProd extended the same reasoning to service-to-service traffic: mutual TLS with per-workload identity, so that a compromised service cannot impersonate a peer merely by being inside the perimeter.66 This is not a product category. It is the claim that the network is a transport, not a credential.

Depth also applies temporally, and this is the part most often missing. Prevention, detection, response, and recovery are four layers on the time axis. A system that can be restored from an immutable backup within four hours has a fundamentally different risk profile from an identical system that cannot, and the difference has nothing to do with how hard it is to break in. Critical-infrastructure incidents make this vivid: in the 2015 Ukrainian grid attack, operators who had lost remote control restored power by driving to substations and switching them manually — a recovery layer that existed only because the plant had not been fully modernised.67

control · defaultSecure defaults

default
Invariant
The out-of-the-box configuration is the safe configuration; insecurity requires an explicit, logged, deliberate action by someone who can be asked why.
Without it
Security becomes a tax on attention. The distribution of deployed configurations is the distribution of how much time each operator had, which is to say: the default.
Case
Mirai enrolled hundreds of thousands of devices using a table of about sixty default username/password pairs shipped by their manufacturers.24,68

Saltzer and Schroeder called it fail-safe defaults: base access decisions on permission rather than exclusion, so that a mistake in the mechanism fails by refusing access rather than granting it.7 The modern version is broader and blunter — whatever the default is, that is what most of your users will run, forever — and it has become a policy position. CISA’s secure-by-design guidance, co-sealed by agencies in seven countries, asks vendors to take ownership of customer security outcomes and to eliminate whole classes of defect rather than documenting workarounds; the accompanying pledge asks for measurable commitments including default MFA and the elimination of default passwords.36,69 The EU Cyber Resilience Act converts similar expectations into legal obligations for products with digital elements sold in the single market,37 and sector regulators have moved the same way — the FDA now expects a cybersecurity bill of materials and a plan for postmarket vulnerability handling as part of a premarket submission for a connected medical device.70

Concrete examples of the default being the control:

  • TLS 1.3 did not merely deprecate RC4, static RSA key exchange, renegotiation and compression — it removed them from the protocol, so a misconfiguration cannot re-enable them.71
  • Amazon S3 enabled Block Public Access and disabled ACLs for all new buckets in 2023, after a decade in which “public S3 bucket” was a reliable source of breach headlines.72
  • Modern web frameworks auto-escape template output, set SameSite cookies, and enable CSRF tokens without being asked. The insecure path still exists — it is spelled raw or dangerouslySetInnerHTML — and that visibility is the point.
  • Memory safety is a default at the language level. Android’s public data is the clearest large-scale evidence: as new code shifted to Rust, Java and Kotlin, memory-safety bugs fell from 76% of Android vulnerabilities in 2019 to 24% by 2024, without rewriting the existing C++.73,74 Because vulnerability density decays with code age, changing what new code is written in is most of the win — the reasoning behind the US national cyber director’s memory-safety report and the joint agency roadmap guidance.75,76

The counterweight is psychological acceptability, the eighth Saltzer–Schroeder principle. A default so restrictive that everyone disables it has negative value, because it teaches users that security controls are obstacles and trains them to click through. Cormac Herley’s argument — that users’ rejection of security advice is often economically rational, since the advice’s costs are certain and its benefits are speculative — deserves more attention than it gets from people who write security advice.77 The same applies to developers: an API whose safe path is harder than its unsafe path will be used unsafely, and the fault is the API’s.78,79 Empirically, developers reach for whatever the first search result showed them, which means the security properties of your ecosystem’s most-copied snippet are a real engineering concern.80

Part III

The request path

control · authnAuthentication

authn
Invariant
Every request is associated with a principal whose identity was established by evidence an attacker cannot obtain remotely, replay, or be tricked into forwarding.
Without it
Every other control is enforcing policy against a name the attacker chose.
Case
August 2022: a phishing campaign harvested credentials and one-time codes from employees at dozens of companies in real time. At Cloudflare the attack failed at the same step it succeeded elsewhere — the hardware security keys would not sign for the attacker’s origin.81

Authentication answers “who is this?” and nothing else. Conflating it with authorization is the oldest architectural error in the field, and it produces systems where being logged in is quietly equivalent to being permitted. Keep the two mechanisms and the two failure modes separate; the code that decides whether the caller is Alice should not be the code that decides whether Alice may delete this invoice.

The authoritative modern reference is NIST SP 800-63, finalised as Revision 4 in 2025 after roughly four years and some six thousand public comments.82 Its volume on authentication is the document to quote in design reviews, because it settled several arguments with evidence.83 Composition rules — one uppercase, one digit, one symbol — are out; they push users toward predictable transformations without materially raising guessing cost. Mandatory periodic rotation is out, except on evidence of compromise, because it produces incremented suffixes. In their place: a minimum length with a generous maximum, screening candidate passwords against known-breached lists, and support for password managers rather than defences against them. Bonneau and colleagues had established the analytical frame a decade earlier — every replacement for passwords wins on some dimensions and loses on others, and deployability is usually the binding constraint.84

Why WebAuthn is categorically different

In a WebAuthn ceremony the relying party sends a challenge; the authenticator signs over that challenge together with the origin the browser is talking to, using a key pair generated for that origin and never exported.86,87 A phishing site at a different origin cannot obtain a signature its target will accept, because the authenticator will not produce one — not because the user was careful. The private key never leaves the authenticator, so there is no shared secret in the relying party’s database to steal; a breach of the server yields public keys. Passkeys extend this with credentials synchronised across a user’s devices through a platform provider, which trades some key-custody assurance for the recovery story that kept hardware-only FIDO out of consumer deployments.88 Usability studies found FIDO2 authentication faster and preferred by users once enrolled — the enrolment and recovery flows, not the ceremony, were the barrier.89

Push-based approval deserves a specific warning. Sending a “did you just log in?” prompt to a phone is phishing-resistant only if the user reliably declines unexpected prompts. In September 2022 an attacker with valid credentials sent repeated prompts to an Uber contractor for over an hour, then messaged them on WhatsApp claiming to be IT; the contractor accepted.90 Number matching and context display reduce but do not eliminate this. The structural fix is an authenticator that cannot be socially engineered because it evaluates the origin, not the user.

Machines, federation, and the tokens in between

Service-to-service authentication is the larger surface in most architectures. Mutual TLS with short-lived, automatically rotated certificates gives each workload a cryptographic identity, and SPIFFE standardises what that identity looks like so it can be issued consistently across platforms.91 For human sign-in across organisational boundaries, OpenID Connect adds an identity layer on top of OAuth 2.0.92 The distinction matters more than the acronyms suggest: OAuth 2.0 is a delegated authorization framework — it issues a token that says what an application may do on a user’s behalf, not who the user is.93 Using an OAuth access token as proof of identity, a mistake repeated in a great many integrations, produces authentication that can be defeated by getting the user to authorise any application at all.

control · authzAuthorization

authz
Invariant
Every access to every object is checked against a policy on the server, on the path the request actually takes — Saltzer and Schroeder’s complete mediation, restated.
Without it
Object identifiers become capabilities. Changing a number in a URL becomes an exploit, and it is undetectable in logs because every request is well-formed and authenticated.
Case
Broken access control has ranked first in the OWASP Top 10 in both 2021 and 2025; in the 2025 data it appeared in 3.73% of tested applications across the largest dataset the project has assembled.21

Authorization is where most real breaches live, and it resists tooling in a way injection does not. A scanner can recognise string concatenation into a SQL statement; it cannot know that order 8812 belongs to a different customer. That semantic gap is why broken object level authorization sits at the top of the OWASP API Security list as well.94

Role-based access control, standardised in the NIST/ANSI model, introduces roles as an indirection between users and permissions, with hierarchies and separation-of-duty constraints.95 It scales beautifully with headcount and badly with exceptions: role explosion is what happens when every combination of region, department and seniority becomes its own role. Attribute-based access control evaluates a policy over attributes of subject, resource, action and environment, which is more expressive but harder to reason about in reverse.96 Relationship-based access control, popularised by Google’s Zanzibar, stores tuples such as doc:readme#viewer@user:alice and answers queries by graph reachability, which is the natural model for sharing, folders and nested groups; Zanzibar’s hard problem was not expressiveness but returning consistent answers globally at low latency.97

Architecturally, the useful separation is XACML’s: a policy enforcement point sits on the request path and asks a policy decision point, which evaluates policy against information from policy information points.98 Open Policy Agent is the widely deployed general-purpose implementation, with policies in Rego evaluated as a sidecar or library;99 Cedar takes a deliberately restricted language design so that policies can be analysed automatically — you can ask whether one policy set is strictly more permissive than another, which is exactly the question you want answered before a change ships.100

The failure modes worth memorising

  • Client-side enforcement. Hiding the delete button is not a control. If the endpoint answers, the button was decoration.
  • Checks in the wrong layer. A check in a controller that a background job, an admin console, and a GraphQL resolver all bypass. Enforce as close to the data as you can — ideally in a repository layer that cannot be addressed without a subject.
  • Missing function-level checks. The endpoint /api/admin/users that verifies a session but never verifies the role.
  • Confused deputy by header. Trusting X-Forwarded-For, an internal X-User-Id, or the mere fact that a request arrived on the internal interface. Anything a proxy can set, a request that reaches the service directly can set too.
  • Fail-open on error. If the policy service times out and the code returns allow, an attacker’s cheapest exploit is a denial-of-service against the policy service. OWASP added mishandling of exceptional conditions as a distinct Top 10 category in 2025 for exactly this family.21

A structural defence worth more than any amount of review discipline: make the unauthorised query impossible to express. If every data access goes through a repository whose methods require a subject, and there is no way to construct a query without one, then “forgot the check” becomes a compile error rather than an incident. This is the same move that parameterised queries make against injection, applied to a different class of bug.

control · inputInput validation

input
Invariant
Untrusted input is parsed into a typed value at the boundary, and every downstream interpreter receives structure rather than a string it must re-parse.
Without it
Data and code share a channel. Somewhere downstream, a parser decides that part of your user’s name is an instruction.
Case
Log4Shell: a string logged by any application — a user agent, a username, a chat message — triggered a JNDI lookup and remote class loading. The vulnerable operation was writing a log line.28,101

The phrase “input validation” suggests checking that a string looks acceptable. The stronger and more useful formulation is Alexis King’s: parse, don’t validate.102 Validation returns a boolean and throws away what it learned; parsing returns a value of a narrower type that carries the proof forward. Once EmailAddress, OrderId and PositiveQuantity exist as types, the check happens once at the edge and the type system enforces it everywhere else. A function that takes a String cannot be sure; a function that takes an OrderId cannot be wrong.

The rules that hold across languages and sinks:

  • Allowlist, not denylist. Enumerate what is acceptable. Denylists lose to encoding, case, Unicode normalisation, and the next syntax feature the interpreter adds.
  • Canonicalise before deciding, and only once. Path traversal and filter bypasses are almost always disagreements about which form of a string is the “real” one. Decode to a canonical form, then check, then never decode again.
  • Validate on the server. Client-side validation is a user-experience feature.
  • Bound everything. Length, depth, count, size, rate. Unbounded nesting in a JSON or XML parser is a denial of service; XML external entities turn a parser into a file-reading and SSRF primitive.
  • Never deserialise untrusted data into arbitrary types. Java, .NET, PHP and Python object deserialisation reconstruct objects and call methods during construction; a suitable gadget chain in the classpath is a remote code execution.104 Use data formats that describe data, and schemas that constrain them.

Two less obvious cases. First, the validator itself can be the vulnerability: catastrophic backtracking in a regular expression turns a 50-character input into seconds of CPU, and Davis and colleagues found such patterns at ecosystem scale in npm and PyPI.105 Cloudflare took down its global proxy for around thirty minutes in July 2019 with a single regex deployed to its WAF — a security control that became an availability incident.106 Use linear-time engines or bound the work. Second, server-side request forgery is an input-validation failure about destinations: an application fetches a URL supplied by a user and thereby lends the attacker its network position, which is why SSRF was folded into broken access control in the 2025 Top 10 rather than left as its own category.21 Validate the resolved IP, not the hostname, and re-validate after redirects.

The same shape now appears in machine-learning systems. When an application concatenates untrusted text into a prompt for a model that can call tools, the model is an interpreter and the text is grammar; retrieved documents, emails, and web pages are all untrusted input. The controls that work are the familiar ones — keep the untrusted content structurally distinguishable, give the tool-calling identity minimal privilege, and require confirmation for consequential actions — not a denylist of dangerous phrases.

control · encodeOutput encoding

encode
Invariant
Every value written into an output document is escaped for the exact grammar of the position it lands in, by a mechanism that knows that position.
Without it
Attacker-controlled text executes in your users’ sessions, with your origin’s cookies, storage and permissions.
Case
The Samy worm, October 2005: a stored cross-site scripting payload in a MySpace profile added the author as a friend and copied itself to the viewer’s profile, reaching a million accounts in about twenty hours.107

Input validation and output encoding are often taught as one topic, which obscures the most important fact about the second: the correct transformation depends on the destination, and the destination is not known at input time. The same username can be safe in an HTML text node, dangerous in an attribute, and catastrophic inside a <script> block. Encoding therefore belongs at the sink, applied by something that knows what the sink is.108

Modern template engines auto-escape by default and track context, which handles the overwhelming majority of cases. What remains is the deliberate escape hatch — |safe, html_safe, dangerouslySetInnerHTML, v-html — and DOM-based XSS, where no server-side template is involved at all: the payload never reaches the server, arriving in a fragment identifier and flowing through client-side JavaScript into innerHTML. Christoph Kern’s account of Google’s approach is the strongest available argument for solving this with types rather than vigilance: define SafeHtml, SafeUrl, TrustedResourceUrl such that they can only be constructed through contextually-correct builders, then make the dangerous DOM sinks accept only those types.109 The reviewer’s job shrinks from “is every string escaped” to “is every unchecked conversion justified”, and there are few of those.

Content Security Policy is the second layer, and the research on it is worth knowing before deploying it. Weichselbaum and colleagues analysed CSPs across the web and found host allowlists largely ineffective — most real policies included at least one endpoint that permitted bypass, typically a JSONP endpoint or an unrestricted CDN path.110 The recommendation that followed is a strict, nonce- or hash-based policy with strict-dynamic, which does not depend on enumerating hosts.111 Trusted Types goes further, making the browser itself refuse to assign a plain string to a dangerous sink, which turns Kern’s type discipline into a runtime guarantee enforced by the platform.112 None of these replaces encoding; they bound the damage when encoding is missed.

Finally, note the contexts that are not HTML. A value interpolated into a CSV file can begin with = and become a formula in a spreadsheet. A value written into a log line can contain newlines and forge a log entry — a direct attack on the control described in §17. A value echoed into a terminal can contain escape sequences. “Output encoding” means every output, and the discipline is the same each time: know the grammar of the destination, and escape for it there.

Part IV

Data, keys, and the things that keep them apart

control · cryptEncryption

crypt
Invariant
Data at rest and in transit is unintelligible and unmodifiable to anyone without the key, using authenticated encryption with a nonce that is never reused under a given key.
Without it
Every layer below your application — disk, backup, network, log aggregator, cloud provider technician — is a place where the plaintext exists.
Case
Adobe, 2013: around 150 million credentials encrypted with a block cipher in ECB mode under one key, with unencrypted password hints alongside. Identical passwords produced identical ciphertext, and the hints did the rest.113

The engineering advice on cryptography is unusually simple and unusually often ignored: use a well-reviewed library at the highest level of abstraction it offers, choose an authenticated mode, and spend your creativity elsewhere. Nearly all deployed cryptographic failures are integration failures — wrong mode, reused nonce, unauthenticated ciphertext, key in the wrong place — rather than breaks of the primitive.

Modes matter more than algorithms. AES is a block cipher; a mode turns it into something that can encrypt a message.114 ECB encrypts each block independently, so equal plaintext blocks produce equal ciphertext blocks and structure survives encryption — the reason it is unsuitable for anything but a single random block.115 CBC provides confidentiality but no integrity, and the resulting padding oracles are a well-documented family of practical attacks going back to Vaudenay in 2002 and resurfacing as POODLE in 2014.116,117 The correct default is authenticated encryption with associated data: AES-GCM118 or ChaCha20-Poly1305,119 both exposed through the AEAD interface that RFC 5116 standardised.120 AEAD gives you one operation that provides confidentiality and integrity together, and lets you bind context — a record identifier, a tenant, a version — into the associated data so that a valid ciphertext cannot be moved to a different row.

Nonce discipline is a correctness requirement, not a nicety. Repeating a nonce under the same key in GCM does not degrade security gradually; it leaks the XOR of the plaintexts and, worse, allows recovery of the authentication subkey, which forfeits integrity for every message under that key. Use a counter where you can guarantee uniqueness, random 96-bit nonces only under a documented message limit, or a nonce-misuse-resistant mode.

In transit. TLS 1.3 is the modern baseline. It removed static RSA key exchange (so every session has forward secrecy), removed renegotiation, compression, and the weak ciphersuites, and cut the handshake to one round trip.71 Certificate validation is the part applications still get wrong: disabling verification “temporarily” for a staging environment, or accepting any hostname. For service-to-service traffic prefer mutual TLS with short-lived certificates; for user data that must survive server compromise, end-to-end encryption with a ratcheting protocol such as Signal’s Double Ratchet gives forward secrecy and post-compromise recovery at the cost of a much harder key-distribution and recovery story.121

What encryption does not do. Encryption at rest defends against a stolen disk, a discarded drive, and a misdirected backup. It does not defend against an application bug, because the application holds the key and will happily decrypt for whoever asks. Treating “encrypted at rest” as an answer to “what if someone exploits the API” is a category error common in compliance conversations. Field-level or client-side encryption, where the key never reaches the service processing the data, is a genuinely different control — and costs you searching, sorting and indexing on those fields.

The migration already scheduled. In August 2024 NIST published the first post-quantum standards: ML-KEM for key establishment, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA for signatures.122,123,124 The draft transition report proposes deprecating 112-bit-security classical public-key algorithms — RSA-2048, ECC P-256 — after 2030 and disallowing them after 2035, and NSA’s CNSA 2.0 sets an earlier schedule for national security systems.125,126 The engineering consequence today is not to swap algorithms but to acquire crypto-agility: know where every algorithm and key length lives, negotiate rather than hard-code, and version your ciphertext formats. The “harvest now, decrypt later” argument means data with a long confidentiality lifetime — health records, sealed legal material, long-lived credentials — should move first.

control · hashHashing

hash
Invariant
The right function for the job: a fast collision-resistant hash for integrity, a keyed MAC for authenticity, and a slow memory-hard function with a per-record salt for passwords.
Without it
A stolen credential table becomes a plaintext password list at GPU speed, or a “verified” artifact turns out to have a twin.
Case
LinkedIn, 2012: 6.5 million unsalted SHA-1 password hashes published; a large fraction were cracked within days, and the company moved to salted hashing afterwards.127

Three distinct jobs get the same word. Conflating them is the source of most of the field’s recurring mistakes, so it is worth stating the separation plainly: digest for integrity, MAC for integrity plus authenticity, password hash for making guessing expensive.

Integrity. SHA-256 or SHA-512 for general use, SHA-3 where a different construction is wanted as a hedge, BLAKE3 where throughput dominates.128,129 MD5 and SHA-1 are broken for collision resistance and must not be used where an adversary can influence both inputs. This is not theoretical: Wang and Yu’s 2004–2005 work made MD5 collisions practical;130 the Flame malware used a chosen-prefix MD5 collision against Microsoft’s terminal-server licensing certificate authority to forge a Windows Update signature, which is as complete a demonstration of the consequence as one could construct;131 the SHAttered team produced the first SHA-1 collision in 2017;132 and by 2020 chosen-prefix SHA-1 collisions were within reach of a modest budget.133 A digest still detects accidental corruption when computed with a broken function, but nobody should be relying on that distinction in new code.

Authenticity. A bare digest proves only that someone computed a digest. If an attacker can change the data, they can change the digest. HMAC binds a key into the construction, so only a holder of the key can produce a valid tag — and it is provably secure under weaker assumptions than the underlying hash, which is why HMAC-SHA1 remained acceptable long after SHA-1 collisions did not.134 Where you need to derive several keys from one secret, HKDF is the standard extract-then-expand construction, and its info parameter gives you domain separation for free.135 Compare tags in constant time: a byte-by-byte comparison that returns early leaks the position of the first mismatch, and a few thousand requests turn that into a forged tag.

Passwords. A per-user random salt defeats precomputation and makes identical passwords hash differently.11 A deliberately slow, hardware-hostile function does the rest. bcrypt was designed in 1999 to be adaptable as hardware improved;136 scrypt introduced memory-hardness to defeat custom silicon;137 Argon2, the Password Hashing Competition winner, is specified in RFC 9106, which recommends Argon2id and gives concrete parameter sets to start from.138 PBKDF2 remains acceptable where a FIPS-validated primitive is required, at a high iteration count.139 Two refinements are worth the effort: a pepper, a secret key held in a KMS and mixed in via HMAC before hashing, so that a database dump alone is not enough; and a version tag in the stored record, so that parameters can be raised over time and records upgraded on next successful login.

Naiakshina and colleagues gave developers the task of storing passwords and observed what actually happens: participants who were not explicitly told security was part of the task overwhelmingly stored them insecurely, and several who tried to do it well produced something worse than a library default.140 The engineering conclusion is not “train harder”. It is that password storage should be a one-line call to a well-chosen library, and that no application should ever write its own.

Never log the input In 2018 Twitter disclosed that a bug had written unhashed passwords to an internal log before the hashing step; in 2019 Facebook disclosed the same class of problem, affecting a substantially larger population of internal records.141,142 Neither was an attacker; both were logging placed on the wrong side of a transformation. Redaction at the logging boundary is a design decision, not an operational one — see §17.

control · signDigital signatures

sign
Invariant
A verifier can establish, without secrets of its own, that a specific artifact was produced by a specific private key — and can state which key it expected and why.
Without it
“It came from the official domain” is the whole of your integrity story, and a compromised mirror, a hijacked account, or a poisoned build server ends it.
Case
SolarWinds: the malicious update was correctly signed. Signing was working; the build system that fed it was not.27

Signatures answer authenticity and non-repudiation, the two properties a hash cannot. RSA-PSS remains widely deployed and is standardised alongside ECDSA in FIPS 186-5;143 Ed25519 is the modern default for new designs — fast, small keys, deterministic, and with no per-signature randomness to get wrong.144,145 That last property is not academic. ECDSA leaks the private key outright if the per-signature nonce repeats: Sony’s PlayStation 3 used a constant nonce and its master signing key was recovered and published;146 a 2013 flaw in Android’s SecureRandom produced repeated nonces and drained Bitcoin wallets that had signed twice.147

The harder problem is never the mathematics. It is which key should I have expected? Public key infrastructure answers that with certificate chains and a set of trusted roots, and its failure mode is that any one of hundreds of certificate authorities can vouch for any name. In 2011 DigiNotar was compromised and issued fraudulent certificates, including for Google domains, used to intercept traffic in Iran; the company did not survive the investigation.148 Certificate Transparency was the structural response: every issued certificate must be recorded in an append-only, publicly auditable log, so that misissuance becomes detectable even when it is not preventable.149 Note the pattern — the fix was not stronger prevention but mandatory, verifiable evidence. It recurs in §17.

This is why the supply-chain toolchain has moved beyond signing artifacts. in-toto defines a layout of steps and the evidence each must produce, so a verifier can check that the whole chain was followed rather than just the last link.150 SLSA turns that into a levelled framework with concrete requirements for build provenance, hermeticity and isolation.151 Sigstore removes the key-custody problem that killed earlier signing efforts: sign with an ephemeral key bound to an OIDC identity, record the signature in a public transparency log, and verify against the log rather than a long-lived key you had to store somewhere.152 Combined with an SBOM — the minimum elements were defined by NTIA following Executive Order 14028 — a verifier can answer both “what is in this?” and “where did it come from?”.35,34

Two implementation traps. First, verify the whole structure, not a field inside it: the JWT alg confusion of 2015 let an attacker change the header to none, or to a symmetric algorithm keyed with the server’s public key, because libraries asked the token which algorithm to use.153,154 Pin the expected algorithm and key at the verifier. Second, signature verification that logs a warning and proceeds is not verification. The academic surveys of open-source supply-chain attacks are worth reading precisely because they show how ordinary the entry points are — typosquats, account takeovers, malicious maintainer handoffs — and how rarely cryptography was the thing that failed.155,156

control · keysKey management

keys
Invariant
Every key has a generator, a custodian, a scope, a lifetime, and a rehearsed path to replacement — and the highest-value keys never exist in the memory of a general-purpose computer.
Without it
The cryptography is decorative. Keys accumulate in configuration files, images and terraform state, and the answer to “can we rotate?” is discovered during the incident.
Case
Debian, 2008: a patch to silence a memory-checker warning removed most entropy from OpenSSL’s RNG, so keys generated for about two years came from a set of roughly 32,000 possibilities.157,158

NIST SP 800-57 is the reference, and its framing is the useful one: a key has a lifecycle — generation, distribution, storage, use, rotation, archival, destruction — and each stage has requirements.159 Most organisations have a policy for the first stage and improvise the rest.

Generation. Use the operating system’s CSPRNG (getrandom, /dev/urandom, BCryptGenRandom) or a library that does. The Debian episode is the canonical failure, and Heninger and colleagues later scanned the internet and found large numbers of devices sharing keys or primes because they had generated them at first boot with no entropy — a systematic failure of embedded key generation rather than a single bug.160

Storage and hierarchy. Hardware security modules and cloud KMS services exist so that a key can be used without being readable: the application sends data to the module and receives a result, and even a full compromise of the application does not yield the key.161 Envelope encryption (Fig. 11) makes this affordable at volume — the module wraps and unwraps data keys, and bulk cryptography happens locally under a key that only ever existed for that object.162

Rotation. Rotate on a schedule that you have rehearsed, not one you have documented. The property that matters is that rotation is routine, because the moment you actually need it — a suspected compromise — is the worst moment to discover that three services hard-coded the key ID. Design for two active keys at all times: sign or encrypt with the current one, accept both, promote and retire on a clock. The same pattern works for JWT signing keys (publish a JWKS with a key ID in each token header), for database credentials, and for TLS certificates.

Ceremony. For the small number of keys whose compromise is unrecoverable, the practice is procedural rather than technical: split knowledge, dual control, tamper-evident storage, witnesses, and a script. ICANN’s DNSSEC root key signing ceremonies are the public exemplar — scheduled, filmed, audited, and boring by design.163 Most organisations need one such key at most, but they should know which one it is.

control · secretSecrets management

secret
Invariant
No long-lived credential exists in source, images, CI configuration or developer machines; workloads obtain short-lived credentials by proving an identity the platform can attest.
Without it
Every repository clone, container layer, log line and stack trace is a potential distribution channel for a credential nobody can revoke without a change-management ticket.
Case
The September 2025 npm worm harvested tokens from developer machines and CI runners with a legitimate secret-scanning tool, then used them to republish the maintainers’ own packages — over five hundred were compromised.32,33,164

Key management is about cryptographic material. Secrets management is about everything else a program needs to prove it is allowed to do something: database passwords, API tokens, webhook signing keys, cloud credentials. The distinction matters because the failure mode is different — secrets are rarely broken, they are copied.

The scale is not in doubt. GitHub reported detecting 39 million leaked secrets across repositories in 2024, and has pushed push protection as a default on public repositories precisely because detection after the fact is a losing race.165 The incident record is consistent: Uber’s 2016 breach involved credentials in a private repository, and the FTC’s expanded settlement made the storage practice part of the finding;166 Codecov’s bash uploader was modified to exfiltrate environment variables from customers’ CI runs, which is to say from every secret those runs held;167 CircleCI’s January 2023 incident led it to ask every customer to rotate every secret stored in the platform.168

What good looks like, roughly in order of value:

  1. Eliminate the secret. Workload identity is the strongest available answer: the platform attests what the workload is, and the resource provider exchanges that attestation for a short-lived credential. Cloud instance roles, Kubernetes projected service account tokens, and OIDC federation from a CI provider all have this shape, and SPIFFE/SPIRE generalises it across platforms.91
  2. Shorten the lifetime. Where a secret must exist, make it dynamic: a vault that generates a database credential valid for an hour turns theft into a race the attacker usually loses.169
  3. Centralise custody with an audit trail. One system that issues, stores, rotates and logs, rather than a spreadsheet, a chat channel and three .env files. Note that Kubernetes Secret objects are base64-encoded, not encrypted, unless encryption at rest is explicitly configured for the API server — a default that surprises people.
  4. Detect leakage continuously. Pre-commit hooks and server-side push protection, plus scanning of build logs and images. Assume detection is the last line, not the first.
  5. Rehearse revocation. The measure of a secrets programme is how long it takes to invalidate every copy of one credential and confirm nothing broke. If nobody knows, the answer is “longer than the incident”.

One cultural note. Secrets end up in repositories because the alternative was slower at 6pm on a Friday. Any programme that only punishes the commit, without making the correct path the fast path, will keep producing the same finding. This is the developer-facing version of psychological acceptability, and it is the reason trusted publishing succeeded where “please rotate your tokens” did not.

Part V

Evidence

control · auditAudit logging

audit
Invariant
Every security-relevant decision produces a record that identifies actor, action, object, time and outcome; the records are append-only, tamper-evident, and something is watching them.
Without it
You cannot answer the only three questions that matter after an incident: what did they touch, when did it start, and is it over.
Case
Target had the alerts. The malware was detected by the monitoring the company had paid for, and the notifications were not acted upon.62

Audit logging is the control that makes the other fourteen falsifiable. It is also the one most likely to be treated as an operational concern and therefore designed by nobody. OWASP renamed its category in 2025 from “logging and monitoring failures” to “logging and alerting failures” to make the point explicit: excellent logs with no alerting have minimal value for identifying incidents.21

Audit logs are not application logs. They have a different audience (investigators, auditors, sometimes courts), a different retention period, a different schema, and different integrity requirements. NIST SP 800-92 remains the reference for the operational side — generation, transmission, storage, disposal, and the resourcing that log management actually requires.170 A workable schema is boring by design: timestamp (UTC, from a synchronised source), actor (with the authentication method used), action, object, outcome, source, and a correlation_id that survives across services. Emit it as structured data; OCSF is a reasonable common schema if you want detections to be portable across tools.171

What to log. Authentication attempts and their outcomes; authorization denials (a spike is an attack signature); privilege changes; access to sensitive records; administrative actions; key and secret operations; configuration changes; and the export or bulk read of data. What not to log is equally load-bearing: credentials, tokens, session identifiers, full card numbers, health data, and anything else that turns your log aggregator into the highest-value target in the estate. Redaction belongs in the logging library, applied to typed fields, not in a regex on the collector — a lesson that Twitter and Facebook both learned publicly.141,142 Regulatory regimes push in both directions here: PCI DSS requires detailed logging of access to cardholder data, while the GDPR’s minimisation and storage-limitation principles constrain what may be retained and for how long.172,173

Tamper evidence. An attacker who reaches your servers will edit your logs; a log an operator can silently rewrite is not evidence of anything. The construction is a hash chain: each record includes the hash of the previous, so altering record n invalidates every hash after it. Publishing the head periodically — to a separate account, a WORM store, or a transparency log — anchors the chain to a time before the intrusion. Crosby and Wallach’s work gives the efficient version with Merkle trees, allowing logarithmic-size proofs of inclusion and consistency rather than replaying the whole log.174,175 Certificate Transparency is this idea deployed at internet scale.149 If the log must resist an attacker who can also write to it, chain with a keyed MAC whose key lives in a KMS, so recomputing the chain requires a capability the intruder does not have.

Alerting, and the cost of noise. Detection engineering has matured into a discipline with version-controlled, reviewed, tested rules; Sigma provides a vendor-neutral format so a detection can be written once and translated per backend.176 Map rules to ATT&CK techniques so coverage gaps are visible rather than assumed.43 And treat alert fatigue as a security property: a queue nobody can clear is functionally the same as no monitoring, which is the Target lesson restated. The useful metric is not alert volume but time-to-detect measured against your own red-team exercises.

One structural note: log injection is real. If a user-supplied value containing a newline is written raw into a line-oriented log, an attacker can forge entries — the output-encoding problem of §11 aimed at the audit trail itself. Structured logging with proper field encoding removes the class.

§ 18A small implementation

The following is a complete, dependency-free tamper-evident audit log: a keyed hash chain, Merkle inclusion proofs, constant-time verification, and a password record for contrast. It is small enough to read in one sitting and exercises four controls at once — hashing, key management, audit logging, and the constant-time comparison discipline that shows up wherever a secret is checked.

# attest.py — standard library only
import hashlib, hmac, json, os, secrets
from dataclasses import dataclass, field

DOMAIN_ENTRY = b"audit-log/entry/v1"
DOMAIN_NODE  = b"audit-log/node/v1"

def _mac(key: bytes, domain: bytes, *parts: bytes) -> bytes:
    """Domain-separated keyed hash. Separation stops a value valid in one
    position from being replayed in another."""
    m = hmac.new(key, domain, hashlib.sha256)
    for p in parts:
        m.update(len(p).to_bytes(8, "big"))   # length prefix: no ambiguity
        m.update(p)
    return m.digest()

@dataclass
class AuditLog:
    key: bytes                        # lives in a KMS/HSM in production
    entries: list[dict] = field(default_factory=list)
    chain:   list[bytes] = field(default_factory=list)

    @property
    def head(self) -> bytes:
        return self.chain[-1] if self.chain else b"\x00" * 32

    def append(self, actor, action, resource, **ctx) -> int:
        entry = {"seq": len(self.entries), "actor": actor,
                 "action": action, "resource": resource, "ctx": ctx}
        payload = json.dumps(entry, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":")).encode()
        self.entries.append(entry)
        self.chain.append(_mac(self.key, DOMAIN_ENTRY, self.head, payload))
        return entry["seq"]

    def verify(self):
        """Recompute the chain. Returns (ok, first_bad_index)."""
        prev = b"\x00" * 32
        for i, entry in enumerate(self.entries):
            payload = json.dumps(entry, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":")).encode()
            if not hmac.compare_digest(_mac(self.key, DOMAIN_ENTRY, prev, payload),
                                       self.chain[i]):
                return False, i
            prev = self.chain[i]
        return True, None

    def _levels(self):
        level, levels = [_mac(self.key, DOMAIN_NODE, c) for c in self.chain], []
        levels.append(level)
        while len(level) > 1:
            nxt = []
            for i in range(0, len(level), 2):
                left = level[i]
                right = level[i + 1] if i + 1 < len(level) else left
                nxt.append(_mac(self.key, DOMAIN_NODE, left, right))
            level = nxt; levels.append(level)
        return levels

    def root(self) -> bytes:
        return self._levels()[-1][0] if self.chain else b"\x00" * 32

    def inclusion_proof(self, seq: int):
        proof, idx = [], seq
        for level in self._levels()[:-1]:
            sib = idx ^ 1
            sib = sib if sib < len(level) else idx
            proof.append(("R" if idx % 2 == 0 else "L", level[sib]))
            idx //= 2
        return proof

def verify_inclusion(key, leaf_hash, proof, root) -> bool:
    node = _mac(key, DOMAIN_NODE, leaf_hash)
    for side, sibling in proof:
        node = (_mac(key, DOMAIN_NODE, node, sibling) if side == "R"
                else _mac(key, DOMAIN_NODE, sibling, node))
    return hmac.compare_digest(node, root)

# --- password records, for contrast: slow by design, salted, versioned
def hash_password(password: str, iterations: int = 600_000) -> str:
    salt = secrets.token_bytes(16)
    dk = hashlib.pbkdf2_hmac("sha256", password.encode(), salt, iterations)
    return "pbkdf2_sha256$%d$%s$%s" % (iterations, salt.hex(), dk.hex())

def check_password(password: str, record: str) -> bool:
    scheme, iters, salt_hex, dk_hex = record.split("$")
    if scheme != "pbkdf2_sha256": return False
    dk = hashlib.pbkdf2_hmac("sha256", password.encode(),
                             bytes.fromhex(salt_hex), int(iters))
    return hmac.compare_digest(dk, bytes.fromhex(dk_hex))   # constant time

Digests differ per run because the demo generates a fresh key; set AUDIT_KEY to a hex string for reproducible output. Four details are doing real work: the length prefix in _mac (without it, ("ab","c") and ("a","bc") hash identically, so an attacker can move bytes across field boundaries); the domain separation constants (a leaf hash can never be replayed as an internal node); the canonical JSON serialisation (verification must reproduce the exact bytes that were signed); and hmac.compare_digest everywhere a tag is checked.

What this small module does not do is as instructive as what it does. It keeps everything in memory, so it has no durability story. It holds the key in the same process that writes the log, so an attacker with code execution can forge entries going forward — only the past is protected. Real deployments push the head to a separate trust domain on a timer, and that periodic anchoring is what converts “the operator says so” into “the arithmetic says so”.

§ 19Making it routine

None of the fifteen controls is difficult in isolation. What is difficult is having all of them, everywhere, on the day someone looks. That is an organisational problem, and the field has converged on a fairly consistent answer.

Attach controls to the pipeline, not to the calendar. NIST’s Secure Software Development Framework is the vendor-neutral statement of what a development process should include: prepare the organisation, protect the software, produce well-secured software, respond to vulnerabilities — each with practices and tasks that map onto existing engineering activity rather than replacing it.177 It became load-bearing in the US after Executive Order 14028 required federal software suppliers to attest to conformance.34 The Microsoft SDL remains the most complete published account of what this looks like inside one large organisation.20

Choose gates that can fail the build. A finding that produces a ticket is a suggestion; a finding that fails a merge is a control. The pragmatic split: block on the things with low false-positive rates and unambiguous fixes (secrets in a diff, a dependency on the KEV list, a missing signature on a base image, a disabled TLS verification flag), and route everything else to triage with an owner and a clock. Static analysis, dependency scanning, container scanning, and infrastructure-as-code checks all belong in the pipeline; the question is only which ones stop the line.

Verify against a standard rather than a habit. OWASP ASVS 5.0, released in May 2025, provides roughly 350 testable requirements across seventeen chapters with three assurance levels, which is precisely the artefact needed to turn “is this secure” into a set of claims a test can check.178 SAMM measures the maturity of the process that produced the software rather than the software itself.179 ISO/IEC 27001 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 sit above both, at the level of management systems and organisational governance.180,181

Practise the response. Incident response capability decays without exercise, in exactly the way that backup restoration does. Game days and adversary simulations are the security equivalent of chaos engineering’s premise: the way to know whether a system tolerates a failure is to cause the failure deliberately, on a Tuesday, with the people who would have to handle it.182

Keep the economics in view. Anderson’s 2001 argument — that security fails where the party who can fix a problem is not the party who bears its cost — explains more of the industry’s history than any technical account.2 It explains default passwords on consumer routers, unmaintained transitive dependencies, and the long gap between a patch being available and being applied. It also explains why the current policy direction — secure-by-design commitments, SBOM requirements, and product liability under the Cyber Resilience Act — is aimed at moving cost back to the party with the ability to act.36,37 Inside an organisation the same logic applies at smaller scale: if the security team owns the risk and the product team owns the schedule, the schedule wins. Ownership has to sit in the same place as the decision.

A closing observation on human factors. Adams and Sasse argued in 1999 that users are not the enemy, and that security mechanisms which ignore how people actually work will be circumvented by people who are trying to do their jobs.78 The same is true of developers.79 Every control in this article has a version that engineers will route around and a version they will adopt without noticing, and the difference is almost always whether the safe path is also the easy one. That is a design problem, which means it is ours.

§ 21Where to go next

Books. Ross Anderson’s Security Engineering (3rd ed.) is the field’s reference work and the only one that treats banking, nuclear command and control, biometrics and DRM as parts of a single subject.183 Bishop’s Computer Security: Art and Science is the rigorous academic treatment, strongest on policy models and formal foundations.184 Google’s Building Secure and Reliable Systems is the best available account of how the controls here behave at scale and where they conflict with reliability.185 Shostack for threat modelling;39 Hubbard and Seiersen if you have to argue about numbers.52

Standards and specifications, in reading order. Saltzer and Schroeder first — it is twenty pages and half of this article is a gloss on it.7 Then ASVS 5.0 as a checklist you can test against,178 SSDF for the process,177 SP 800-63-4 for anything touching identity,82 SP 800-57 before you design a key hierarchy,159 and SLSA before you touch a build pipeline.151 The OWASP Cheat Sheet Series is the fastest route from “what is the correct way to do X” to a defensible answer,186 and MASVS covers mobile, where the threat model differs because the client is in the attacker’s hands.187

Living sources. The KEV catalogue and EPSS scores, wired into your triage rather than read as news.49,48 CWE for shared vocabulary when writing findings.188 ATT&CK for mapping detections.43 OpenSSF Scorecard for a quick read on a dependency’s hygiene.189 The annual Verizon DBIR for a check on whether your threat model matches what is actually happening to organisations like yours.190

Two papers to read even if you read nothing else. Anderson’s economics paper, because it explains why the technically obvious fix does not happen.2 And Thompson’s Turing lecture, because forty years on, the supply-chain era is simply his argument arriving at scale: the code you did not write is running with your permissions, and the only durable answers are provenance, reproducibility, and the willingness to check.12

Speculative attacks such as Spectre and Meltdown are a reminder that the boundary can move beneath you: in 2018 the abstraction that a process cannot read another’s memory turned out to be violable through the microarchitecture, and every cloud tenant’s threat model changed in a week.191,192 That is the normal condition of this field. The controls in this article are the ones that have survived several such revisions, which is the strongest recommendation any of them can carry.
