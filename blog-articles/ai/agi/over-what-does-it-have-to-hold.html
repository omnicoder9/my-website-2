Part I

The frame

What a capability claim asserts, and the five ranges it can assert it over.

1.1A list of ten things

Any serious attempt to say what a general intelligence would have to be able to do converges on roughly the same list. Perception, language, knowledge, memory, world models, causal understanding, abstraction, metacognition, creativity, social intelligence. The list is old. It is close to the inventory Newell assembled when he argued that a science of cognition could not be built by accumulating experimental phenomena one at a time1–5, close to the functional decomposition that cognitive architectures have used to organise themselves ever since6–12, and close to the criteria proposed for judging whether any such architecture is a theory of anything13. Forty years of architectures have kept it more or less intact14–16. Recent attempts to define levels of machine general intelligence reproduce it again, with the same headings and the same silences17–22.

The list is not wrong. It is under-specified in a way that matters more than any disagreement about its contents. Every entry names a behaviour, and no entry says what the behaviour has to be invariant to. That omission is not a detail of exposition. It is the entire content of the claim, because the same observable behaviour is produced by systems with wildly different capabilities, and the difference between them shows up nowhere except in the range.

This article takes the list as given, in the form set out above, and asks of each entry the question the entry does not answer: over what does it have to hold? The answer, in each case, turns out to be the whole of the empirical content, and the nine experiments in the companion lab exist to show what happens numerically when the question is left unasked.

1.2What a capability claim asserts

Consider two systems that produce identical outputs on every item of a test. On the evidence available from the test, they are indistinguishable. Yet we routinely want to say that one of them has a capability and the other does not, and we are not being confused when we say it. What could the difference consist in?

Not in the behaviour on the test, by construction. Not in the internal mechanism either, or at least not directly: a capability ascription is not a claim about implementation, and two implementations of the same capability may share nothing. The difference has to lie in what the systems would do under conditions the test did not create. That is: the difference is a difference in the range of circumstances over which the behaviour is preserved.

This is not a new kind of claim, only an unfamiliar place to make it. Woodward's account of causal explanation turns on exactly this notion: a generalisation counts as explanatory to the degree that it is invariant under a specified range of interventions, and generalisations that hold only over a narrow range are correspondingly weaker explanations, not different in kind but different in extent23–25. The proposal here is that capability ascriptions have the same logical form. A capability is an invariance: a behaviour, plus the set of transformations under which the behaviour survives. Naming the behaviour without naming the set names nothing that can be measured, and in practice names whichever range the available test happened to vary.

The consequence is uncomfortable. It means that most of the disagreements about whether a system "really" has a capability are not disagreements about the system at all. They are disagreements about the range, conducted by parties who have not said which range they mean.

1.3Five ranges

Ranges do not form a tidy algebra, but for the capabilities on the list they cluster into five families, and the clustering is stable enough to be worth naming. This article uses them throughout: as the gauge on every plate, as the palette, and as the columns of the closing ledger.

Population is the range over other items of the same kind. It includes items no data pipeline would produce: the photograph nobody takes, the sentence nobody writes, the configuration that occurs in the world at a rate far below its rate in any corpus. It is the range that dataset bias makes narrow without anyone deciding to narrow it26–29.

Composition is the range over novel combinations of familiar parts. It is the range Fodor and Pylyshyn were pointing at, and the one on which the systematicity argument has been fought ever since30–33. Its distinctive feature is that it is unbounded from a finite basis, so no test can sample it and every test must instead be designed to separate hypotheses about it.

Horizon is the range over steps, turns and elapsed time. It is the range that turn-level evaluation systematically cannot see, and it is the one on which claims about memory, dialogue and agency all quietly depend.

Medium is the range over surface: notation, modality, format, phrasing, layout. It is the range over which meaning is by definition preserved, which is what makes it the sharpest diagnostic available, and it is also the range that a fixed rendering pipeline collapses to a point.

Intervention is the range over ways the world could be made different. It is Pearl's second and third rungs, and it is the only range on this list that cannot in principle be reached by collecting more observations34–37.

Most entries on the capability list load two or three of these. A few load one. None load none, and the plates in this article record which, so that the totals at the end are a finding rather than an assertion.

1.4The confound and the tell

If a capability is an invariance, then failing to have it does not look like failing. It looks like succeeding, on the tested range, by some other means. The literature has a name for the general phenomenon — shortcut learning38,39 — and a growing catalogue of instances: hypothesis-only cues that carry a majority of a natural-language inference benchmark40, lexical-overlap heuristics that survive the introduction of adversarial premises41, background texture standing in for object identity42, and distractor sentences that move reading-comprehension answers a human reader would not move43.

The important structural point is that these are not errors of care. A shortcut is whatever the tested range fails to separate from the target, and something always fails to separate, because a test is finite and a capability is not. What varies is whether the designers know which separations their test does and does not make.

Each plate in this article therefore carries two fields that do the working. The confound names what passes the test without the capability. The tell names the observation that would distinguish them from outside a system one did not build — the behavioural signature that the confound cannot produce. Neither field is a criticism of anyone's benchmark; both are attempts to say, for each capability in turn, what a measurement would have to vary before its result meant what its name says.

A capability is a behaviour together with a range. The behaviour is what a test measures; the range is what the claim was about.

One clarification before the survey. Nothing here requires that ranges be the only thing we care about in a mind. It requires only that they are what can be measured from outside, and therefore that they are what capability talk has to cash out into if it is to do any work in engineering or evaluation. Section 13.3 states what would refute even that weaker claim.

Part II

Perception

Vision, hearing, speech, spatial perception, sensor fusion — and why the joint matters more than the sensors.

2.1Vision

Vision is the recovery of a scene from measurements that under-determine it. Marr's formulation of the problem as the computation of a sequence of representations from an image remains the cleanest statement of what makes it hard44, and the inverse-problem framing makes the under-determination explicit: infinitely many scenes produce any given image, and perception is the selection of one45. Gibson's alternative — that the optic array already specifies the environment, and that perception is the pickup of invariants in it, not the inversion of a projection — disagrees about the mechanism while agreeing about the criterion46. Both theories say that seeing is whatever is stable across the transformations the world performs on the light.

That is a range claim, and it is unusually explicit. The classical accounts of object recognition state it as such: recognition is the achievement of a representation invariant to pose, illumination, scale and clutter while remaining selective for identity47,48. Held and Hein's kittens established, sixty years ago, that the invariance is not free: the animals that could not produce the transformations themselves did not acquire the perception49, a result that the sensorimotor tradition later made central50.

Measured against that criterion, the standard evaluation is narrow in a specific and documented way. Systems that reach human-level accuracy on curated photographs lose a large fraction of it on the same objects in unusual poses51, on backgrounds and viewpoints controlled to remove capture bias52, under corruptions that do not trouble a person53, and under perturbations too small to see54. The texture-versus-shape result is the sharpest of these, because it identifies the confound rather than merely demonstrating fragility: the systems had been reading a statistic that co-varies with the label in photographs and comes apart from it under style transfer42. None of this shows that the systems cannot see. It shows that the claim "sees" had never been indexed to a range, and that the range it was tacitly indexed to was the one a camera and a crawler jointly produce.

2.2Hearing and speech

Auditory scene analysis poses the same problem in time: a single pressure waveform is the sum of every source, and hearing is the decomposition55. The cocktail-party effect names the range directly — the capability is the one that survives competing talkers, and it was defined by the condition under which it fails56.

Speech adds a second range that is easy to miss. A benchmark built from read speech measures the recovery of words from acoustics. But speech is a form of action57, and the properties that make it action — prosody that marks what is being done with the utterance, disfluency, self-repair, the sub-second timing of turn transfer — are precisely what read corpora remove58. A system can be excellent at transcription and have no purchase at all on the range in which prosody covaries with intent while the words are held fixed. The motor theory's long argument about whether perceiving speech is perceiving gestures is, from this angle, an argument about which range the capability is indexed to59.

Perception is also not modality-separable in the way the list's headings suggest. McGurk's demonstration that a seen articulation changes a heard syllable shows that the auditory judgement is not a function of the audio60, which means that any test holding vision fixed is measuring something narrower than hearing.

2.3Spatial perception

Spatial perception is the capability the list names most confidently and specifies least. There are at least two representations in play — egocentric, in which locations are coded relative to the perceiver, and allocentric, in which they are coded relative to the world — and the interesting capability is the transformation between them61,62. A system with only the first can execute a learned route perfectly and cannot take a shortcut; a system with the second can, and the shortcut is the observation that separates them. This is Tolman's original argument, and it was an argument about range: the rats' behaviour on a novel path was the evidence that what they had acquired was a map rather than a chain of responses63.

The engineering literature has the same distinction under a different name. A localisation system evaluated along the trajectories from which its map was built is being tested inside its own support; the loop-closure case, in which the system must recognise that it has returned to somewhere it has been by another route, is the one that separates a map from an odometric memory64,65. The claim "the system perceives space" is empty until it says whether it covers the second case.

2.4Sensor fusion

Fusion is where the range question becomes arithmetic, and where the answer can be computed exactly rather than argued. The textbook result is that under independent errors, inverse-variance weighting is optimal, and human multisensory integration approximates it well enough that the approximation has become a standard finding66,67. The result is genuine. Its scope is narrower than its fame.

The lab's ninth experiment holds the two sensors fixed — identical error distributions, identical variances — and varies only the coupling between their errors, over three joint distributions with the same marginals.

The robotics literature knows this and has instruments for it — covariance intersection exists precisely because the correlation is usually unknown, and it buys consistency at the price of tightness68,69. Multisensory perception in humans looks the same: whether to integrate two cues at all is itself an inference about whether they share a cause, and the integration breaks down, correctly, when they do not70. Predictive-processing accounts make the same structure the general case rather than a special one71,72.

Part III

Language

Syntax, semantics, pragmatics, dialogue, reading comprehension, communication.

3.1Syntax

Syntactic competence is the clearest case in the whole list of a capability whose range is infinite by construction. A grammar is a finite object that licenses an unbounded set of structures, and the standard argument for hierarchical structure is precisely an argument about behaviour under transformations that leave linear statistics unchanged73,74. Chomsky's distinction between competence and performance is a distinction between a range and a sample of it75, and the recursive-generation claim in the language-faculty literature is a claim about which range a system covers76.

Evaluation here is comparatively mature, and it is mature exactly because it was designed around ranges rather than around aggregate accuracy. Agreement across intervening material tests whether the dependency tracked is structural or positional77; nonce-word sentences remove lexical co-occurrence as an explanation78; minimal pairs isolate one grammatical contrast at a time79; and studies of inductive bias ask what a learner generalises to when the training data are consistent with both a hierarchical and a linear rule80. Every one of these is a range instrument rather than a difficulty instrument. They are not harder tests; they are tests that vary something the ordinary test holds fixed.

3.2Semantics

The semantic capability is invariance in the medium and covariance with the truth conditions: the same content, however expressed, licenses the same inferences, and different content licenses different ones. Compositionality is the standard statement of why the range is unbounded81,82.

Two long-running disputes are, on this reading, disputes about range and about determination rather than about behaviour. The symbol-grounding problem asks what fixes the interpretation of internal symbols83, and the Chinese Room argues that no amount of behaviour over any range does84. Bender and Koller's argument that form alone underdetermines meaning is the same argument recast for systems trained on text85, and the replies — that reference is not the only semantic relation, and that internal conceptual role does substantial work — are attempts to widen what could count as determination86,87. This article does not adjudicate that. It notes only that the dispute is not settled by any measurement, which is a diagnostic: when a capability question is invariant to all possible observations, it is a constitutive question wearing empirical clothes.

What is measurable is composition, and here the evidence is specific. Systems that score near ceiling under a random split of the same data fall dramatically under a split that requires a familiar predicate to be applied to a familiar argument in an unseen combination88,89. The literature has decomposed the notion into several distinguishable properties and shown that they do not travel together90,91, and multi-step compositional tasks degrade in ways consistent with pattern matching over sub-computations rather than systematic composition92. Crucially, the difference between these results and the ceiling results is entirely in how the data were divided. The same model, the same items: the split makes the hypotheses separable.

3.3Pragmatics

Pragmatic competence is the recovery of speaker meaning, which is a function of the sentence and of the speaker's state, so the capability is by definition covariant with something the sentence does not contain93,94. The formal treatments make this explicit: a listener model reasons about what a speaker with particular knowledge and goals would have said, and the inference falls out of the counterfactual alternatives the speaker did not choose95,96.

The evaluation consequence is severe. Generalised implicatures are conventionalised enough to be listed97, and a test built from listed cases can be passed by a lexicon. The capability is only exercised where the same sentence must be read differently because the speaker's evident knowledge differs, with the sentence held fixed. This is the range over interventions on the speaker model, and a test that does not vary it is measuring vocabulary.

3.4Dialogue

Dialogue is the horizon range made unavoidable. Conversation is a joint activity in which participants accumulate common ground, and a contribution is accountable to contributions made much earlier98,99. Turn-level evaluation — score each reply for plausibility, average — measures local fluency and is structurally incapable of detecting the failure that matters, which is a commitment abandoned forty turns later.

This is not a hypothetical gap. Consistency across related queries has to be measured deliberately, and when it is, systems that answer each query well contradict themselves across them at rates their per-item scores do not predict100. Extending the measure from related queries to a long single exchange is a harder instrument to build, and the reason to build it is that the capability named "dialogue" is defined over the exchange rather than over the turn.

Repair is the second horizon phenomenon and the more diagnostic one. Human conversation is organised around the detection and correction of trouble at the point it occurs, and the machinery for it is elaborate99. A system that never repairs is not a system with fewer errors; it is a system whose errors are not accountable to anything.

3.5Reading comprehension

Comprehension is the construction of a model of the situation the text describes, not of the text101,102. The distinction is old, well-evidenced in humans, and almost entirely absent from the design of comprehension benchmarks, which pair passages with questions written after the passage was selected and score span overlap.

The measurement consequences are documented. A large fraction of items on a widely used dataset are answerable by lexical matching without reading103; inserting a distracting sentence that changes nothing a person would use drops accuracy sharply43; and analyses of what benchmark datasets actually require find that the required skills are far narrower than the datasets' names104.

The lab's fourth experiment makes the underlying geometry explicit, using an arithmetic word problem rather than a passage because the transformation group can then be enumerated exactly. One abstract problem is rendered under every meaning-preserving transformation available: renaming the entities, reordering the two given clauses, three phrasings, two unit systems, two layouts.

Item-level difficulty is not the variable. Coverage of the group is.

3.6Communication

Communication is not language plus a channel. It is a capability defined over partners, and its range is the set of partners it holds for. Self-play produces codes that are efficient and private; the capability at issue is the one that survives a partner who does not share the code, including the negotiation by which a shared code comes into being98. The evolutionary and developmental literatures make shared intentionality the precondition rather than the by-product108.

The distinction matters for evaluation because a reference game with a fixed partner measures coordination, and coordination with a copy of oneself is the degenerate case. The instrument that separates them is a partner drawn from outside the training population — most sharply, a human one.

Whether language in the relevant sense is primarily a system for communication or primarily a medium of thought is an active question with real evidence on both sides109,110. It matters here for one reason: the two answers imply different ranges. If language is a communication system, the capability is indexed to partners and contexts. If it is a format for reasoning, the capability is indexed to inferential problems. A benchmark that samples neither range consistently is answering the question by accident.

Part IV

Knowledge

Facts, concepts, procedures, episodic knowledge, common sense.

4.1Facts

To know a fact is not to store a sentence. The difference is closure: knowing that p makes the consequences of p available without each of them having to be stored and retrieved separately. Sellars's attack on the given and Brandom's inferentialist development of it both locate the content of a belief in its inferential role rather than in its being present111–113, and the epistemological literature's long argument over what has to be added to true belief is an argument about which further conditions the ascription quantifies over114.

The measurable version of the question is whether the store is closed under the inferences it licenses, and here the evidence is unusually clean. Probing a language model with cloze templates recovers a great deal of factual content115, but the result is sensitive to the phrasing in a way that a knowledge store should not be, and careful re-analysis shows that some of the apparent knowledge is the prompt doing the work116. The reversal result is sharper still: a system trained on a statement in one direction frequently fails to produce it in the other, which is a failure of closure under a single trivial inference rule117. A store with content and without closure is a corpus, not knowledge, and the word "facts" on a capability list does not distinguish them.

4.2Concepts

The psychological literature on concepts converged decades ago on the conclusion that a concept is not a classifier. Prototype and exemplar accounts explain typicality effects well and explain badly why concepts license inferences about instances never seen, why some features are more central than others independent of frequency, and why category membership can be settled by reasons rather than by similarity118–122. The theory-based view exists because the classifier view under-generates.

That is a range claim in disguise. A classifier is invariant over the distribution it was fitted to; a concept has to hold over instances constructed to order, over borderline cases, and over the inferential uses that arise when nothing is being classified at all. Carey's account of conceptual change adds a further range: the capability includes the reorganisation of the conceptual system itself, which no classification metric touches123. Barsalou's perceptual-symbol proposal and the broader grounded-cognition programme relocate where the content lives without changing the criterion124,125.

4.3Procedures

Ryle's distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that is the reason this entry is on the list separately126, and the reason it stays there is that the two dissociate: procedural and declarative knowledge have different acquisition curves, different failure modes and, in humans, different neural substrates127.

The range that matters for procedures is repair. A policy that reproduces a demonstrated trace is invariant over instances whose parameters fall inside the demonstrated range and has no defined behaviour outside it. A procedure that is understood — that has a representation of what each step accomplishes — can be resumed after interruption, interleaved with another, and repaired when a step fails, because failure can be localised to a subgoal. The difference is invisible on success rates within the demonstrated range and total outside it.

4.4Episodic knowledge

Episodic knowledge is knowledge of particulars: what happened, when, and to whom. It is distinguished from semantic knowledge by its indexing rather than by its content128, and the distinguishing capability is the ability to keep two similar occasions apart and to know which one is being recalled.

Contemporary evaluation of this capability is confounded by an architectural accident. A sufficiently long context window makes retrieval unnecessary for anything stated inside it, so a test whose items fit in the window measures the window rather than the capability. The relevant range is across sessions, across interleaved episodes, and across delays during which other episodes have intervened. Source memory — knowing where a remembered particular came from — is part of the capability and is rarely scored at all.

4.5Commonsense knowledge

Common sense is the entry on the list whose range is explicitly the range nobody has written down. That is what makes it common rather than encyclopaedic, and it is why the frame problem is a problem about it: the difficulty is not representing what changes but circumscribing what does not, over a space of situations no enumeration reaches129,130. Default and non-monotonic logics are formal attempts to make the circumscription finite131; CYC was the attempt to pay the enumeration cost directly132; and the state of the problem was well summarised, before the current wave, as one of coverage rather than of representation133. The crowd-sourced resources that followed are all enumerations with the same structural limit134–136.

Evaluation has taken an unusual turn here that is worth naming as a range problem. The Winograd schema was designed so that the answer turns on world knowledge rather than on statistics137, and its successors were built by adversarial filtering: generate candidates and keep the ones current models get wrong138,139. Adversarial filtering is a principled response to shortcut learning, and it has a specific consequence. The resulting benchmark's range is defined relative to the models used to filter it, so it drifts as models change, and a score on it is a comparison to those models rather than a measurement of a domain140–143. This is not a criticism of the datasets. It is an observation about what a score on them can and cannot support.

Part V

Memory

Working, episodic, semantic and procedural memory, long-term storage, and the part that actually fails.

5.1Working memory

Working memory is the capability on the list with the most precisely characterised range, because a capacity limit is a range statement with a number attached. The estimates have moved — from around seven chunks144 to around four items when chunking and rehearsal are controlled145 — and the theoretical accounts differ about what the limit is a limit on146–148, but the field agrees on the form of the claim, and has assembled explicit benchmarks that a model of the system must reproduce149.

The lab's third experiment shows what happens to that form when a capacity limit is reported as an accuracy. Every sequence of six operations over three one-bit variables is enumerated; an agent holds bindings in a buffer of c slots with least-recently-used eviction.

An item-level score is an average over a distribution of demands, most of which fall below the ceiling. It converts a hard limit into a soft number, and the softness is an artefact of the demand distribution, which is a property of the test rather than of the system. The correct instrument for a capacity claim is the load at which the curve has a knee, not the mean above and below it.

5.2Episodic memory

Tulving's characterisation of episodic memory involves not just content indexed to a time but a particular relation to that time — the sense of re-experiencing rather than of looking up150. Whether that relation is required for the engineering capability is a fair question; what is not in question is the functional signature. Human episodic retrieval is reconstructive, which is why it fails in characteristic ways — plausible intrusions, source confusions, blends of similar episodes — and the same machinery is used to construct hypothetical futures151.

The functional signature is a range test. Encoding specificity says that retrieval succeeds to the degree the cue overlaps the encoded context152, so the interesting range for an artificial system is cues that share no surface with the encoding. A store that returns exact matches to exact queries has a different failure profile from a memory: it fails abruptly at the edge of the match rather than gradually across cue similarity, and it never produces a plausible intrusion. Neither profile is better. They are different capabilities, and the word "memory" covers both.

5.3Semantic memory

The requirement on a semantic store is not capacity but revisability under a stream of updates that arrive in no useful order. This is the stability-plasticity dilemma153, and its sharpest empirical form in artificial systems is catastrophic interference: training on a new task destroys performance on an old one, at rates that have no analogue in biological memory154–156. The mitigations — regularising towards parameters important for old tasks157, or maintaining a fast episodic system alongside a slow structured one158,159 — are all attempts to buy range in the horizon dimension.

The evaluation gap here is almost structural. A system evaluated at a single checkpoint is a photograph of something that is supposed to be a film. Nothing in a benchmark score reports what happens to the store when the next fact arrives, and nothing reports whether the arrival of a contradicting fact produces a revision, an overwrite, or a silent inconsistency. Those three outcomes are behaviourally identical at the moment of measurement and completely different capabilities.

5.4Procedural memory

The reason procedural memory appears separately on the list is that it dissociates from declarative memory in humans: amnesic patients acquire and retain skills they cannot report acquiring127,160. For artificial systems the dissociation is not guaranteed, and whether it exists is exactly what a range test would establish.

The relevant range is interference. A single-task evaluation cannot detect the loss of a skill, because there is no second skill to displace it. The measurement that means something is retention of skill A across training on skill B, and the number that means something is the bound on the loss rather than its presence: some interference is correct, since a system that never revises anything is not learning either.

5.5Long-term memory

Long-term memory, taken as a capability of a system rather than as a store, is the claim that the system accumulates. That claim has a horizon range and essentially nothing else, and it is the range that current evaluation practice is least able to address, because the artefact evaluated is static and the capability described is not.

Two features of the range deserve naming. First, what is retained is selected, and the selection ought to reflect what is likely to be needed — Anderson and Schooler's demonstration that human forgetting curves track the statistics of environmental need is the cleanest statement of the criterion161. Second, a deployed system's own outputs re-enter its inputs, so the trajectory is not a passive accumulation but a feedback loop, and nothing about a checkpoint score speaks to its stability.

5.6Retrieval

Retrieval is where memory claims actually fail, and it is the least glamorous entry on the list. Storage is easy to state and easy to advertise. Getting the right thing out at the right moment, cued by something other than the words used to put it in, is the hard part, and it is a range claim over cues, positions and delays.

The empirical shape of the problem in current systems is well documented. Accuracy at locating information inside a long input depends strongly on where in the input it sits, with a pronounced trough in the middle162, and controlled evaluations find that effective context is much shorter than advertised context once the task requires more than matching163. Retrieval augmentation is an architectural response to the same problem164, and differentiable external memories were an earlier one165. What matters for the present argument is that a mean over positions reports a system that works, and the shape over positions reports one that does not. The mean is the number that gets published.

There is one further range that is almost never scored: the case in which the correct answer is that nothing relevant is stored. A retriever that always returns its best match has no representation of absence, and a memory without a representation of absence will confabulate exactly when it is least able to detect that it is doing so.

Part VI

World models

Objects, events, space, time, physics, agents, and social environments — and why model error hides.

6.1What a world model is for

Craik's formulation is still the most useful: an organism that carries a small-scale model of external reality can try out alternatives, conclude which is best, and react to future situations before they arise166. Everything in the modern literature that matters is a specification of that sentence. Mental models in reasoning167, cognitive maps in navigation and beyond63,168,169, learned latent dynamics in reinforcement learning170–175, and the predictive architectures proposed for autonomous machine intelligence176 are all answers to what the model is of and what it is for.

The range that defines the capability follows from Craik directly. A model is worth having to the extent that it supports the evaluation of options not taken, which is exactly the intervention range. A predictor that is accurate on the trajectories the system actually follows and wrong about the ones it does not follow is not a world model; it is a policy with a forecast attached. Recent evaluations that probe whether a generative model's implicit world model is coherent — rather than whether its predictions are accurate — are constructed around exactly this distinction177,178.

The self-concealment property is worth stating on its own, because it makes model error a different problem from other errors. An agent visits only the transitions its own plan selects. A model error that adds an edge is therefore encountered, because the plan proposes the shortcut and the shortcut fails. A model error that removes an edge is not, because the plan never proposes the path. Model accuracy measured on visited states is systematically blind to the second kind, and no amount of on-policy data corrects it.

6.2Objects and events

Objects are the first commitment a world model makes. Infants represent objects as persisting through occlusion within the first months, and the evidence is violation-of-expectation: looking time is longer for outcomes that are impossible than for outcomes that are merely unusual179,180. That methodology is exactly a range instrument. It does not ask whether the system predicts the scene; it asks whether the system's surprise is correctly ordered across a pair of scenes matched on everything except physical possibility, and benchmarks that import it into machine evaluation preserve that structure181.

The confound is specific and easy to miss: a model that continues textures across an occluder predicts the pixels of a persisting object without representing that anything persists, and the two are separated only by cases in which continuation and persistence disagree.

Events are the temporal analogue. Human experience is segmented into events with beginnings, ends and participants, and the segmentation is driven by changes in the predicted situation rather than by changes in the sensory signal182,183. The corresponding confound is motion energy, which coincides with event boundaries in most recorded footage and diverges precisely in the cases where the boundary is marked by a change in goals.

6.3Space and time

The neuroscience of spatial representation gives the clearest existing example of a capability characterised by its range rather than by its task. Place and grid cells were described as a metric for space168,184, and the subsequent reinterpretation generalises the same structure to non-spatial relational knowledge — the map is a map of whatever has relational structure, and space is the case where the structure is obvious169,185.

For evaluation, the operative question is what a spatial claim covers. A learned policy that solves benchmark environments may be a collection of memorised routes; the observation that separates it from a map is a never-traversed shortcut, or a detour forced by a blockage introduced after planning. Evaluations built specifically to ask this of language models find that competence on route descriptions does not imply competence on the map178, and the parallel literature on planning finds the same dissociation between describing a plan and producing one that executes186,187.

Time is the range the list under-specifies most. Order, duration and simultaneity are three different claims, and the pervasive confound is that discourse order stands in for event order, which is correct in most narratives and wrong in exactly the cases that test the capability. Duration is worse: a system that has read a great deal about how long things take can report durations without composing them, and composition is what the capability is.

6.4Physics

Intuitive physics is usually modelled as approximate probabilistic simulation over a noisy representation of the scene, which explains a wide range of human judgements including their characteristic errors188,189. The alternative — that physical prediction is learned pattern completion over sensory sequences — makes very similar predictions on scenes drawn from the training distribution and different ones off it.

That is the whole methodological problem in this area. When the training scenes and the test scenes come from the same simulator, the system can learn the simulator rather than the physics, and the two are indistinguishable while the simulator is also the examiner. The range that separates them is novel shapes, materials, mass ratios and configurations — in particular, regimes where the training simulator's own approximations break down, since a system that has learned the simulator will reproduce its errors and a system that has learned coarse physics will not.

The general form of the diagnostic is worth stating, because it recurs: predictions from a correct but coarse model degrade gracefully and in a characteristic direction, whereas predictions from an interpolator degrade discontinuously at the edge of its support. Relational inductive biases were introduced partly as a way to buy the first behaviour190–192.

6.5Agents and social environments

A world model that contains other agents has to model them as agents, which means modelling them as having goals and information rather than trajectories. Dennett's intentional stance is the specification: predict by attributing beliefs and desires and assuming rationality193,194, and the computational form is inverse planning — invert a planner to recover the goals and beliefs that would make the observed behaviour rational195. Heider and Simmel's result, that people attribute intentions to moving triangles, shows how cheaply the stance is adopted and how little sensory evidence it needs196.

The confound is trajectory extrapolation, which predicts agents about as well as it predicts projectiles, up to the moment their goals change. The tell is a prediction that changes when only the other agent's information changes, with the world state held fixed — which is the same instrument that Section 11.1 uses for theory of mind, and not a coincidence.

Social environments extend this to standing structure: roles, relationships, obligations and institutions, most of which are never stated because everyone inside them takes them for granted. Reading off stated relations is comprehension of a text. Inferring an unstated obligation from a stated role, and revising it when the role changes, is a model of a group.

Part VII

Causal understanding

Cause and effect, intervention, counterfactual — three claims, three ranges, three prices.

7.1Cause and effect

Causal understanding is the family where the range structure has already been worked out formally, which makes it the best available test of the present argument. Pearl's hierarchy separates three kinds of question — associational, interventional and counterfactual — and establishes that they are not merely harder versions of one another but distinct in what data can settle them34,35,197–199. The associational rung asks what is seen given what else is seen; the interventional rung asks what would happen if something were set; the counterfactual rung asks what would have happened in a particular case that in fact went otherwise.

The causal-discovery literature is the study of exactly how much of the second rung can be bought with the first. Constraint-based and score-based methods recover a Markov-equivalence class from observational data, which is to say they recover the graph up to a set of orientations that no amount of further observation will settle200–202; assumptions such as additive noise or non-Gaussianity buy orientation at the cost of being assumptions rather than findings203–207. That trade is the whole subject, and it recurs unnamed whenever a capability list writes "causal understanding" as one entry.

Woodward's manipulationist account supplies the philosophical version of the criterion this article is built on: a relationship is causal to the extent that it would remain invariant under a specified class of interventions23. A capability claim and a causal claim are therefore the same kind of claim, and the range is the content of both.

The observed risk difference is 0.5000, and the interventional effect consistent with that observation ranges over the entire interval from -0.2500 to 0.7500 — width 1.0 on a quantity bounded in [-1, 1]. The methodological reading is the important one. An evaluation that presents a system with observational data and scores it on a causal question is not scoring the causal capability; it is scoring agreement with the evaluator's own untested assumptions about which completion is right. The version of the test that measures something requires the system to act, or at minimum to say which action would settle the question — which is why the intervention range appears in the gauge of every plate in this band.

A correlation constrains an effect to an interval of width one. Anything narrower came from an assumption, and the assumption is the answer.

7.3Counterfactual reasoning

The third rung is not reachable from the second, and the lab shows the gap concretely. After randomisation has pinned the interventional distribution to a point, 26 distinct counterfactual mixtures remain consistent with both arms, and they agree on every interventional quantity while disagreeing about the probability of necessity, which ranges over [0.6667, 1.0000] — a residual width of 0.3333 that no experiment of that design can reduce. Adding monotonicity as an assumption selects a single mixture and returns the point value 0.6667. The assumption does the work; the data cannot.

This matters for evaluation because counterfactual questions are the easiest kind to ask and the hardest kind to score. "What would have happened if X had not occurred" has a determinate answer only relative to a structural model, and a system that produces a fluent answer has produced a fluent answer. Lewis's and Stalnaker's semantics make the dependence on a similarity ordering explicit208,209; the psychological literature shows that human counterfactual selection is systematic but strongly constrained by norms and by mutability210,211. Benchmarks that recognise the structure — by generating items from a known model and scoring against the model's own counterfactuals rather than against annotator intuition — exist and are the correct instrument212–214.

Part VIII

Abstraction

Concept formation, categorisation, hierarchy, general principles — and what a taxonomy costs.

8.1Concept formation

Concept formation is the acquisition of a category that was not supplied. The distinguishing property is not sample efficiency but the shape of what is acquired: human learners generalise from very few examples in a way that respects the hypothesis-space structure rather than the similarity structure, which is what the Bayesian analyses of word learning and number-game generalisation were introduced to explain215–218. Programs-as-concepts models make the same point constructively219,220.

The range that separates formation from fitting is the population range plus a constraint the population range does not usually carry: the learner must be able to extend the new category to instances no generative process it has seen would produce. A category acquired by clustering is defined on the support of the data. A category acquired as a rule is defined everywhere the rule's predicates are defined, which is a strictly larger set, and the difference is only visible outside the support.

8.2Categorization

Categorisation appears separately from concept formation because a system may be given categories and asked to apply them. The literature on how humans do this is a century-old argument between rule-based, prototype and exemplar accounts, with the best-supported answer being that people use several strategies and switch between them221–224.

For evaluation the operative distinction is between accuracy and the structure of errors. Two systems at the same accuracy can have completely different confusion structure, and the confusion structure is where the category representation shows. Human category confusions are graded by typicality and by feature overlap in ways that have been mapped in detail; a system whose confusions are ungraded, or graded by something else, is doing a different thing at the same score. Reporting the score without the structure discards the diagnostic information and keeps the number.

8.3Hierarchical representation

A hierarchy is a bet. It says that features are shared down branches, that the sharing is worth the cost of encoding the branch structure, and that exceptions will be rare enough to be paid for individually. Collins and Quillian's inheritance model made the bet explicit and its reaction-time predictions were partly borne out and partly not225, which is precisely the situation in which counting the cost is informative. Minimum description length gives the accounting226–230.

8.4General principles

The extraction of a general principle from instances is the last entry in this family and the one whose range is most obviously composition and medium. A principle earns the name by transferring to instances that share its structure and share nothing else. The analogy literature is the relevant body of work: Gentner's structure-mapping theory says that analogical transfer maps relations rather than attributes, and the empirical finding that has survived best is that people are poor at spontaneous far transfer while being good at transfer once the correspondence is pointed out231–238.

That asymmetry is a measurement warning. Nearly all evaluation supplies the correspondence — by presenting source and target together, in the same session, in the same format. The capability at issue is retrieval of the relevant principle when nothing signals that a principle is relevant, and this is close to unmeasured. Abstract visual reasoning corpora such as ARC were designed to force structure rather than surface239–242, which is why performance on them tracks so poorly with performance elsewhere.

Part IX

Metacognition

Monitoring, uncertainty, error detection, strategy selection — and when knowing you are wrong is worth nothing.

9.1Monitoring one's own reasoning

Metacognition is knowledge and control of one's own cognitive processes243, and the experimental tradition that measures it well separates two components: resolution, the ability to discriminate one's correct from one's incorrect responses, and calibration, the correspondence between confidence and accuracy in the aggregate244–248. They dissociate. A system can be well calibrated on average and unable to tell any particular right answer from any particular wrong one, by reporting the base rate every time.

The relevant range for monitoring is the intervention range, and this is the one place where that claim needs defending. A monitor that reports on a process it does not influence is a predictor of that process; a monitor is metacognitive to the extent that its report changes what the system then does — abstain, re-derive, seek information, switch method. The self-consistency and self-critique literatures sit exactly on this line, and the finding that intrinsic self-correction without external feedback frequently degrades performance is a finding about a monitor whose report is not informative about the thing it is monitoring249–252.

9.2Uncertainty estimation

The distinction the capability list omits is between uncertainty that more data would reduce and uncertainty that it would not — epistemic and aleatoric253. They call for different actions, which is the operational reason the distinction is not academic: epistemic uncertainty is a reason to gather information, aleatoric uncertainty is a reason to hedge or to decline, and a scalar confidence that conflates them supports neither decision.

Calibration itself is well studied and reliably imperfect: modern networks are systematically overconfident relative to their accuracy254–257, and the failure is worse off-distribution, which is exactly where calibration is load-bearing258,259. Selective prediction formalises the abstention decision260–262, and it is worth noting that a system's stated confidence and its internal signals can come apart, which is a separate measurement problem again250,263,264.

The sharpest number is the last. At λ = 5, three distinct monitors all have a flagging accuracy of exactly 0.7000, and their utilities span 0.8400 — from −0.8000 for the monitor that flags nothing to +0.0400 for the best. Flagging accuracy is not a measure of a monitor. It is a number that a monitor also has.

9.4Strategy selection

Strategy selection is the control half of metacognition: choosing a method rather than executing one. The experiment's routing section prices it. Solver A is right on 95% of easy items and 40% of hard ones, for an overall 0.6750; solver B is uniform-ish at 0.7000; an oracle router reaches 0.8750. A router that identifies item difficulty with accuracy q recovers part of that gap, and the threshold is 0.54 — below it the router is worse than simply always using B. At q = 0.60 it scores 0.7025 against B's 0.7000, a gain of a quarter of a point for a whole extra component.

The general lesson is that a selector is only worth its own error rate, and selectors are evaluated far less carefully than the solvers they select among. Human strategy choice has been modelled as adaptive selection from a repertoire under time and accuracy pressure265–270, and the modelling makes clear that the capability is the fit between strategy and situation rather than possession of the strategies.

Part X

Creativity

Recombination, discovery, design, hypothesis generation — the family whose criterion is external.

10.1Recombination

Boden's distinction between combinatorial, exploratory and transformational creativity is the standard frame, and the third category — changing the space rather than searching it — is the one that resists measurement271–273. Combinatorial creativity is the tractable case: novel juxtaposition of existing elements, valued for the fit of the combination rather than for its improbability.

The measurement problem in this family is structurally different from every other family in the list, and the difference is worth being explicit about. Elsewhere the criterion is internal to the task: the answer is right or it is not. Here the criterion is external and social — an output is creative if a competent community judges it so274–276. That makes reliability the first obstacle, not validity, and it means the range statement has to quantify over judges as well as over items. Divergent-thinking instruments partly finesse this by scoring statistical infrequency277–280, which is a measurable proxy for novelty and no proxy at all for value.

10.2Discovery

Discovery is the case where the value criterion is external and also objective: a hypothesis is a discovery if it is true and was not known. The BACON programs demonstrated that rediscovery of quantitative laws from clean data is a search problem281, and modern symbolic regression makes the same point with better search282–285. Automated experimentation systems close the loop by acting286,287, and this is the substantive point: discovery in a closed data set is interpolation over a fixed sample, whereas discovery in the world requires choosing which measurement to make, which is the intervention range again.

Evaluating discovery has an unavoidable contamination problem. Any known law is in the training corpus of any system trained on text, so rediscovery demonstrates recall unless the target is held out at the corpus level, and corpus-level hold-out for scientific facts is close to impossible to verify. The clean version of the test is prospective: predictions about experiments not yet run288.

10.3Design

Design is creativity under constraint, and the constraint is what makes it measurable. A designed artefact can be checked: it satisfies the specification or it does not, it performs or it does not. Simon's characterisation of design as the science of the artificial — devising courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones — makes the normative criterion explicit289,290, and the ill-structured-problem literature explains why the hard part is usually the formulation rather than the search291–293.

The range for design is composition and intervention jointly: a designer must handle constraint combinations never presented together, and must be able to say what would have to change for a currently impossible specification to become satisfiable. That second ability — diagnosing infeasibility and locating it — is a strong tell, because producing something that fails silently is what a generator does and reporting that the specification is over-constrained requires a model of the specification.

10.4Hypothesis generation

Peirce's abduction — inference to a hypothesis that would explain the observation — is the entry that connects this family to causal understanding294,295. The psychological literature on how children generate and test hypotheses finds systematic strategies rather than random variation296–298, and the computational literature treats generation as search over a structured hypothesis space299–302.

The measurable property is not whether the generated hypothesis is correct but whether the generated set is discriminating: whether some experiment distinguishes its members. A system that produces twenty paraphrases of one hypothesis has generated one hypothesis. A system that produces three mutually incompatible hypotheses and names the observation that separates them has done the thing. The scoring instrument is therefore pairwise discriminability within the generated set, and it is almost never used.

Part XI

Social intelligence

Theory of mind, cooperation, norms, negotiation, communication — and how often belief can be ignored.

11.1Theory of mind

The false-belief task is the canonical instrument: a protagonist's belief is made to diverge from the world, and the question is where the protagonist will look303–305. Second-order versions add a belief about a belief306, and the developmental data are robust enough that the task has become the default import into machine evaluation307–313.

The lab's seventh experiment enumerates the whole space of short scenarios over three locations and a seven-event alphabet, and asks how often the belief question can be answered by reporting the world.

The instrument that survives both is a matched pair — identical surface, one item divergent and one not — scored on getting both right, which is why the minimal discriminating length of 2 in the output matters more than the percentages above it.

11.2Cooperation

Cooperation is a capability with a well-developed formal theory and a badly underspecified range. The theory of repeated games explains when cooperation is sustainable314–316, the shared-intentionality literature argues that human cooperation involves joint goals and joint attention rather than merely aligned incentives108,317,318, and the multi-agent learning literature has constructed environments where the trade-off between individual and collective payoff is explicit319–322.

The range that matters is composition of partners. A policy trained in self-play is invariant over copies of itself and has no defined behaviour against a partner with a different convention, which is the whole content of the ad-hoc-teamwork and zero-shot coordination literatures323–325. A cooperation score against copies of oneself is a measurement of a fixed point, not of a capability.

11.3Norm understanding

Norms are the standing structure that makes social prediction cheap, and the capability has three separable parts that a single entry on a list runs together: inferring the norm from behaviour, applying it to a new case, and knowing when it is suspended. The developmental evidence is that children infer norms from very few observations and enforce them on third parties, which is a strong signal that what is acquired is a rule rather than a habit326–329. The moral-psychology literature adds that norm application is sensitive to context in ways that are systematic rather than noisy330–333.

The tell is suspension. Applying a norm correctly in ordinary cases is compatible with having memorised the ordinary cases. Recognising that a norm does not apply here — and being able to say what feature of the situation cancels it — requires a representation of what the norm is for, and it is the case that reliably separates rule-following from regularity-matching.

11.4Negotiation

Negotiation combines everything in this family under a horizon: it requires a model of the other party's preferences and beliefs, a model of what they believe about yours, and consistency across turns. The bargaining literature supplies the normative structure334–336, and the behavioural literature supplies the systematic departures from it337,338.

The confound here is unusually clean, because it is fluency. Negotiation dialogue is highly formulaic, and a system that produces well-formed negotiation moves can appear competent while conceding value monotonically. The measurement that means something is the outcome against a strategic counterpart, not the plausibility of the transcript, and the range that is almost never tested is the long horizon: consistency of a stated position across many turns, and the ability to detect that the other party's position has changed.

11.5Social communication

The last entry closes the loop with Part III. Communication as a social capability is audience design: choosing what to say given a model of what the listener knows, and revising it when that model is updated. Clark's common-ground account makes this the core of the phenomenon rather than a refinement of it98, and the rational-speech-act framework gives it a computable form in which speaker and listener recursively model one another95,96.

The range is intervention over the listener model, and the instrument follows immediately: hold the message content fixed, vary what the audience is stipulated to know, and check whether the message changes in the right direction. A system that produces the same explanation for an expert and a novice has not failed to communicate; it has demonstrated that its communication does not quantify over audiences.

Measurement

What a score licenses, how long a certifying test has to be, and why emergence is partly an artefact.

12.1What a perfect score leaves open

Everything above has been an application of one idea, and the first experiment states the idea in its bare form. Six binary features, of which four are content and two are surface; the target is the parity of two content features; the training region is constructed so that one surface feature agrees with the label everywhere in it — a shortcut of exactly the kind that appears in real corpora as annotation artefacts and photographic conventions26,38,40,339,340.

The hypothesis class contains 4,524 distinct functions. Sixteen of them are consistent with every single item in the training region — not a sample of it, the whole region — and fifteen of those sixteen read the surface feature. One is the target. A perfect score therefore leaves a version space in which the probability of having the right function, absent any further preference, is one in sixteen.

Four off-distribution items suffice to reduce that space to one. But only 4,096 of the 35,960 possible four-item sets do it: 11.4%. Choosing four items at random from outside the training distribution fails to certify the concept almost nine times in ten. Both halves of that sentence matter, and the second half is the one that gets forgotten. Off-distribution evaluation is necessary and is not by itself sufficient; the items have to be chosen against the hypotheses that survive, which requires knowing what they are.

A perfect score is a constraint on the hypothesis space, not a location in it. What the score licenses is whatever every surviving hypothesis agrees on.

Same domain, same number of concepts, same test length, a factor of four hundred in whether the test does its job. "How many examples should the benchmark contain" is not a question with an answer independent of what is being distinguished.

12.3Construct validity

Psychometrics worked all of this out under different names, and the vocabulary is worth importing wholesale because it is precise. Construct validity is the question of whether an instrument measures the thing it is named after350–354; the argument-based framing treats validity as a claim requiring support rather than a property an instrument has355–359. Reliability bounds validity: an instrument cannot measure a construct more accurately than it measures itself. The machine-learning literature has begun to reconstruct this vocabulary independently360–367, and the reconstruction would be faster if it read the original.

Two specific imports are overdue. Discriminant validity requires that a test of X correlate less with tests of not-X than with other tests of X; almost no capability benchmark reports this, and because general-purpose systems produce correlated scores across nearly everything, the omission is doing real work. And the distinction between a construct and its indicators — between the capability and the tasks used to index it — is exactly the distinction that collapses when a benchmark name becomes a capability name, which happens routinely and passes unremarked.

When the indicator becomes the construct, improvement on the indicator stops being evidence about anything except the indicator.

12.4Emergence

The claim that capabilities appear abruptly with scale368,369 has been substantially qualified by the demonstration that abruptness often follows from the metric rather than from the system: discontinuous scoring rules such as exact-match produce sharp transitions from smooth underlying improvement, and the same runs scored with a continuous rule show no discontinuity370. Scaling laws themselves are smooth in the quantities they are stated over371–373.

The connection to the present argument is direct rather than incidental. A capability defined as a behaviour has no natural notion of partial possession, so its indicator tends to be all-or-nothing, so improvement in it looks like emergence. A capability defined as an invariance has a natural partial ordering — the size of the range over which the behaviour survives — and improvement in that quantity is a widening, which is smooth by construction and considerably more informative. The three-slot working memory in Experiment 3 is the model case: the capacity is a hard threshold, but the range over which the two-slot agent succeeds is a well-defined and continuously varying set.

13.2What the ranges add up to

Read down the ledger and the distribution is informative in a way that was not designed in. Population leads, at 35 of the 49 plates, which is no surprise: almost every capability claim is a claim about items of a kind. Intervention is second at 27, and it is loaded not only in the causal band, where it is definitional, but across metacognition, world models and social intelligence, where it is definitional of nothing and varied by almost no published instrument. Composition follows at 23. The two lowest are medium at 16 and horizon at 15, and they are low for opposite reasons: medium is cheap to vary and therefore often varied by accident rather than by design, while horizon concentrates in a single band — all six memory plates load it — and is otherwise close to unrepresented.

The pattern suggests a reorganisation of the taxonomy that this article does not adopt but should name. If capabilities are individuated by their ranges, then the ten families are not ten kinds of competence; they are ten task domains, cross-cut by five kinds of requirement. A system might be strong on the population range everywhere and weak on the intervention range everywhere — which is a coherent and empirically plausible profile, and one that the family-wise taxonomy cannot express, because it has no cell for it.

The practical version of that observation is a recommendation about reporting. A capability report of the form "perception: 0.87" carries less information than a report of the form "perception, holds over medium up to k renderings, not tested over intervention", and the second is not harder to produce. It is only harder to compress into a headline.

13.3What would refute this

The argument has a definite shape and therefore definite failure conditions. Four are listed, in order of how much damage each would do.

A single-range system. If a system that is demonstrably invariant over one range — say medium — turned out to be reliably invariant over the others without further training, the ranges would not be separable dimensions and the gauge would be measuring one thing five times. The available evidence points the other way: robustness to surface perturbation and robustness to distribution shift come apart empirically53,374, and interventional competence has not been observed to follow from either. But this is an empirical claim, and it could go the other way.

Range-free prediction. If a single scalar score on a fixed benchmark predicted deployed performance across genuinely novel conditions as well as a range-specified report does, the extra apparatus would be waste. This is the strongest version of the objection because it is a prediction about predictive validity, and predictive validity is measurable. The evidence available so far is that benchmark scores transfer poorly to deployment conditions, but "poorly" has not been quantified against the alternative proposed here, because the alternative has not been tried at scale.

Vacuity by regress. If specifying a range required specifying a range over which the range specification holds, the account would regress and mean nothing. The reply is that the regress terminates the way it terminates in metrology: a stated range is a finite, checkable claim, and the standard is not certainty but auditability. If in practice range specifications turned out to be systematically gamed — chosen after the fact to fit the observed competence — the account would be no better than the scores it criticises, and this failure mode is realistic.

The ranges are wrong. Five is a choice. If some capability's crucial variation falls outside all five — and affect, motivation and embodied constraint are candidates — the framework under-describes it. This would not refute the approach so much as require the list to grow, and a framework that can be repaired by adding a column is weakly constrained. That is a real cost and it is worth naming as one.

13.4Against this article

Four objections, with the strongest conceded.

The lab experiments are toys. They are. Six binary features, three one-bit variables, sixteen concepts, a 2×2 table. The defence is that they are not evidence about systems but demonstrations about measurement: the version-space result is a theorem about hypothesis classes, and no amount of scale changes the fact that a consistent score constrains rather than identifies. But the honest reading is narrower than the article's rhetoric occasionally implies. The lab shows that these failures are possible and quantifies them in cases where quantification is exact; it does not show how large they are in any deployed system, and it cannot.

Ranges are unbounded in practice. The intervention range in particular is not a finite set that anyone could enumerate, so "specify the range" is an instruction that cannot be followed exactly. Conceded, with the observation that the same is true of every engineering specification, and that partial specification strictly dominates none. A stated range that turns out to have been too narrow has still made a checkable claim, which is what the current practice does not do.

This is psychometrics with new labels. Largely, yes — and that is the concession that costs most, because if the contribution is a re-derivation of construct validity for an audience that has not read Cronbach, it is a smaller contribution than the length of this article suggests. The specific additions are the five-way decomposition of the range and the argument that intervention is the range most often omitted and most load-bearing. Whether those additions are worth the apparatus is not something the article can settle about itself.

The taxonomy is arbitrary. The ten families come from the community's habit rather than from any principled decomposition, and Section 13.2 argues that the ranges cross-cut them. Adopting the taxonomy while arguing that it is the wrong axis is a tension the article carries rather than resolves. The reason for carrying it is that the list is what gets used, and an argument addressed to a list nobody uses would be addressed to nobody.
