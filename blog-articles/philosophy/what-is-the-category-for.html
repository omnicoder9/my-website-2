2  [P]The question

A producing oil well is a borehole through rock, a multiphase flow problem, a structure under fatigue loading, a corrosion cell, a control loop, a cash flow subject to price risk, a set of contractual obligations, a thing somebody has to be trained to operate safely, and an entry in an organisation chart. Ask which of these it really is and the question does not so much have a wrong answer as fail to have the shape of a question. Every one of the descriptions is accurate. None of them is complete. The well does not choose.

The habit of speaking as though it did is deep enough to be invisible. We say a problem belongs to chemistry, that a company is in an industry, that a patient has a disorder, that a file is in a directory. The grammar puts the thing inside the category, as though the category were a location and the thing had exactly one. Directories are a fair warning about how well this works: every operating system that took the containment metaphor literally eventually grew symbolic links, tags, or both, because the same file needs to be in more than one place and no amount of insisting otherwise changes that.

This article defends three claims and then tries to make them expensive to hold loosely. First, that reality contains phenomena and structures, and that human knowledge organises those phenomena into concepts and those concepts into categories, according to particular purposes, perspectives, methods and institutions. Second, that an actually existing complex entity is generally not inside one category, but participates simultaneously in many conceptual structures. Third — and this is the part that separates the position from the relativism it is often mistaken for — that categories can be human-made and still answerable, because what a category is answerable to is its purpose, and purposes can be failed.

The question that organises everything below is therefore not what is this thing? but: what is this category for, and what does it cost the thing being classified? The first half of that question is asked constantly in engineering and almost never in the places where it matters most. The second half is asked almost nowhere.

Rather than argue this in the abstract, the middle of the article examines eighteen boundaries that real institutions really draw, each with its authority, its stated purpose, a partition it cross-cuts, and a dated record of the times it has moved. That last field does most of the work. A boundary that has moved, by a named body, on a recorded date, for a stated reason, is not a feature of the world that we discovered; and eighteen of them make the general claim harder to wave away than any argument could. Eight computational experiments follow, because several things usually asserted about classification turn out to be calculable, and one of them comes out as a null.

2,1  [P]  ·  IThree levels, and which one the boundary lives on

Start with a distinction that is easy to state and constantly elided in practice. There is the world; there are the concepts with which we describe it; and there are the categories into which we organise those concepts. The three are not the same, and the boundaries usually live at the third level while appearing to live at the first.

A useful way to hold the distinction is to think of each description as a projection. The entity is high-dimensional; a discipline chooses a coordinate system and reads off the components that system can represent. The projection is not a distortion, and it is not the whole thing either.

The world's contribution is not nothing. There are regularities out there that are indifferent to how we describe them, and they constrain description in ways that show up as failure when ignored. Electrons behave the same whether or not anyone has the concept; a bridge falls down under loads that were miscategorised as static; a patient dies of a condition the classification had no code for. Realism about this much is cheap and almost everyone holds it 1 2. The interesting disagreements begin one level up.

The distinction is old and the vocabulary has changed more than the problem. Aristotle's categories are an inventory of the ways a thing can be said to be 3; Locke's separation of real from nominal essence is exactly the observation that the sorting we do and the constitution of the thing sorted need not line up 4; Whewell argued that a classification is an instrument for stating laws and should be judged as one 5; and Mill built an account of natural kinds around the number of properties a grouping lets you infer 6. The twentieth-century revival added the point that some terms have their reference fixed by whatever the stuff turns out to be, rather than by the description speakers associate with them 7 8 — a genuine constraint, and one that covers a much smaller fraction of our vocabulary than its enthusiasts sometimes suggest.

A further choice is made at this level before any category appears: what counts as a thing at all. Describing a domain in terms of enduring objects with properties, rather than in terms of processes with rates, is itself a modelling decision, and biology in particular has a live literature arguing that the object framing distorts what it describes 9. And the phenomena never arrive raw. What reaches the second level has already been through instruments, protocols and formats that embed classifications of their own; Suppes made the point formally in noting that theory is never compared with data but with a model of data 10, and the subsequent work on how data are curated, labelled and made portable has documented it in detail 11 12. There is no floor at which description has not yet begun.

At the second level, phenomena are carved into concepts. This is already selective: a concept picks out some aspects of what is going on and ignores others, and which aspects it picks depends on what the concept is for. The classical positions divide here. Realism holds that at least some concepts correspond to real structures, so that our taxonomies can be more or less accurate to something independent 13. Conceptualism holds that concepts are constructions imposed by minds. Nominalism holds that categories are names for collections and nothing further. Stated baldly, all three are hard to believe, which is a sign that the question has been posed at too coarse a grain.

The position that most working science actually occupies is narrower and more defensible: the world has structure, and our taxonomies carve that structure in several possible ways, not all of them equally good. Quine put the pressure on the notion of similarity that any such carving needs 14; Goodman showed that the predicates we happen to find natural are doing more work than they appear to 15 16; and the subsequent literature on natural kinds has largely been an attempt to keep the constraint without the fantasy of a unique correct partition 17 18 19. Perspectival realism is one name for the resulting settlement: the claims made from a perspective are true or false, and there is no perspective-free inventory 20 21.

At the third level, concepts are gathered into categories and categories into taxonomies, and this is where institutions arrive. Disciplines, departments, professional bodies, standards committees, statistical agencies and funding lines all impose partitions, and those partitions are neither arbitrary nor about the world alone. They are about the world and a purpose and a history. Petroleum engineering is not divided into reservoir, drilling, production and facilities because reservoirs, drill bits, wells and plants belong to four natural kinds. It is divided that way because those divisions correspond to distinct bodies of technique, distinct career paths and distinct budget lines, and because the alternative divisions were not adopted 22.

The arrows run in both directions. Categories do not only record what we have noticed; they determine what we go on to notice, measure and fund.

The reverse arrow matters more than it usually gets credit for. A category, once institutionalised, decides which measurements are routine, which comparisons are possible, which anomalies are visible and which questions have a funder. A phenomenon that falls between two established categories tends not to be studied by either, and its absence from the literature is then read as evidence that there is nothing there. This is why Star and Griesemer's boundary objects — things robust enough to be shared across communities while being understood differently in each — are worth more attention than they usually get 23, and why the history of statistics reads as a history of categories creating their own subject matter 24 25 26.

One consequence deserves stating separately, because it is where this account differs most from the picture it replaces. If categories are purpose-relative, then so are the domains over which our best-confirmed generalisations hold: laws come with a range of application rather than governing everything, and the world they describe is a patchwork rather than a single system 27. That is a claim about science, not a complaint about it.

It also explains a recurring social fact about classification. Things that fall between established categories are not treated as neutral gaps. They are treated as anomalies, and anomalies attract handling that has more to do with the integrity of the scheme than with the thing itself — an observation first made about ritual pollution and transferable, without much strain, to standards committees 28.

Two cautions before going on. The first is that recognising the third level as constructed does not push everything up into it. Borges's imaginary Chinese encyclopaedia, with its animals belonging to the Emperor, embalmed ones, and those that from a long way off look like flies, is funny because those categories fail at a job that other categories do 29 30. The failure is real. The second caution is the reverse: recognising that a category answers to the world does not license the inference that it was read off the world. Both errors are common, and they are usually made by the same person about different categories.

2,1:3  [P:E]  ·  IIWhat a category is answerable to

If categories are not read off the world, what stops any grouping being as good as any other? The honest answer is that nothing does, until a purpose is supplied — and that this is not a weakness of the account but a theorem.

Similarity does not exist until something selects the predicates

The cleanest statement of the problem is Watanabe's, sometimes called the ugly duckling theorem 31. Take any finite set of objects and consider the set of all predicates definable over them. Count how many predicates any two objects agree on, where agreement means that the predicate holds of both or of neither. The count is the same for every pair. A swan and a duckling agree on exactly as many predicates as two swans do. Similarity, over the unrestricted predicate set, is a constant function.

This is easy to verify rather than take on trust, and the first experiment in the lab does so exhaustively for sets of three to nine objects. With n objects there are 2n predicates and every pair of distinct objects agrees on exactly 2n-1 of them: at every size, the number of distinct pairwise similarity values is 1. Restrict the predicate set to three chosen attributes over eight objects and the same calculation yields three distinct similarity values with a variance of 0.4898. Nothing about the objects changed. The entire structure that makes grouping possible was contributed by the restriction.

The moral is not that similarity is fake. It is that a similarity judgement is always relative to a weighting of respects, and the weighting comes from outside the data 32. This is the same shape as the no-free-lunch results in learning theory, where averaged over all possible target functions no algorithm beats any other, and the useful question becomes which inductive bias matches the world you are actually in 33. Classification has a no-free-lunch theorem too, and it is older.

And no clustering rule can have everything you want

The second constraint is Kleinberg's impossibility theorem 34. He asks for three properties of a clustering function that takes a distance function on n points and returns a partition. Scale invariance: multiplying all distances by a constant should not change the output. Richness: every partition should be achievable for some distance function. Consistency: shrinking within-cluster distances and growing between-cluster distances should not change the output. Each is close to undeniable when stated alone. No function has all three.

The lab searches for the violations rather than reciting them, over integer distance functions in Kleinberg's own setting, where d need only be positive and symmetric. Single-linkage stopped at a fixed number of clusters produces only three of the five partitions of a three-element set across all 125 distance functions tested, so two partitions are unreachable at any distances and richness fails. Single-linkage stopped at a fixed distance changes its output when every distance is doubled in 2,886 of 4,096 cases, 70.5 per cent, so scale invariance fails. Single-linkage stopped at a fixed fraction of the diameter changes its output under 26,280 of 50,952 single-step consistency transforms, 51.6 per cent, so consistency fails.

The theorem is often read as bad news about clustering. It is better read as a statement about what a clustering is. Choosing a stopping rule is choosing which of three obviously desirable properties to give up, and that choice cannot be made by looking harder at the data. It is made by knowing what the partition is for. Von Luxburg, Williamson and Guyon put the practical version of this bluntly: the question of what a good clustering is only becomes well posed once the downstream task is specified 35.

Purpose-relative is not arbitrary

Put the two results together and the position is forced. There is no partition of anything that is correct independent of a purpose. It does not follow that partitions cannot be wrong. A category can fail in at least five ways that have nothing to do with metaphysics:

  • It can fail to support the inferences it was adopted to support. A disease category that does not predict response to treatment is not doing the job a diagnostic category exists to do, whatever else is true of it 36.
  • It can be unreliable: two competent people applying it to the same case reach different verdicts, so nothing built on it aggregates 37.
  • It can be a proxy that has come loose from what it was proxying for, which is the charge the 2025 obesity commission levels at body mass index 38.
  • It can cost more than it returns, in the precise sense that describing the exceptions takes more room than describing the members 39.
  • It can be answerable to two purposes at once and serve neither, which is the commonest institutional failure and the hardest to see from inside.

Each of these is a way of being wrong that a purely conventional category can exhibit. That is what keeps this position from collapsing into the claim that anything goes. Anything does not go; it is just that what fails is the fit between a category and a job, rather than between a category and the world considered apart from any job.

2,1:3;5  [P:E;M]  ·  IIIWhere the joints are, and what a joint is worth

The oldest metaphor for good classification is Plato's: carve nature at the joints, like a competent butcher, rather than splintering the bone 40. It is a good metaphor and it repays being taken more literally than usual. A joint is not a place where the animal is really divided. It is a place where a cut is cheap — where the blade meets less resistance, where a slightly misplaced cut gives nearly the same two pieces, and where two butchers working independently will produce comparable results.

That is measurable. Take a population and a continuous variable, and consider only thresholds that actually divide it — say, those putting between 45 and 55 per cent on each side. Two questions follow. How wide is the band of admissible thresholds? And how much of the population changes class when the threshold moves one step?

The lab runs this on two populations with identical means and identical variances, one an equal mixture of two narrow normals and one a single normal. The bimodal population admits a band of thresholds 1.138 standard deviations wide; the unimodal one admits 0.251, a factor of 4.5. At the best available placement, moving the line one step reclassifies 0.0428 per cent of the bimodal population against 1.9789 per cent of the unimodal one, a factor of 46. That is the joint, quantified: not a place where the population is really divided, but a wide interval within which the choice of line barely matters.

The same experiment returns a null worth reporting. At the worst placement inside the admissible band, the bimodal population costs 1.4606 per cent per step against the unimodal population's 1.9945, a ratio of only 1.37. A genuinely jointed population offers no protection to a line drawn in the wrong place. The joint has to be found; its existence does not help anyone who has not found it. This is the difference between a category being well founded and a particular boundary being well drawn, and the two are routinely conflated in both directions.

Reliability is not validity, and can be bought

A second measurement makes the point sharper. Two coders apply the same category to the same population but place the boundary at slightly different points. On the bimodal population, a disagreement of 1.20 standard deviations — a large fraction of the whole range — still yields Cohen's kappa of 0.7652, with the two coders' categories differing in coverage by 11.90 per cent. On the unimodal population the same disagreement gives kappa 0.2499 and a coverage difference of 45.15 per cent.

Read that carefully, because it cuts against a standard research move. High inter-rater agreement is routinely offered as evidence that a category is well founded. What the calculation shows is that kappa is substantially a property of the population's shape rather than of the concept. On a bimodal population, two coders who disagree considerably about what the category means will still agree about almost every case, and their reliability statistic will look excellent. Reliability is necessary and it is not evidence of validity 37.

The IUCN Red List is the clearest case of an institution that understood this and made the trade deliberately. Its quantitative thresholds were introduced in 1994 specifically so that assessors on different continents would reach the same answer, and its designers said in print that the numbers are conventions calibrated against expert judgement rather than discovered discontinuities 41 42. That is a category engineered for reproducibility with the reasoning published, which is a great deal more honest than the usual practice of engineering for reproducibility and then presenting the result as a finding.

Clusters that are real without being sharp

Between the forced boundary and the stipulated one sits the case that covers most of the interesting categories: the homeostatic property cluster 13 43. A set of properties co-occur reliably, because some mechanism keeps them together, without any of them being necessary or sufficient. Species are the standard example, and the reason roughly thirty species concepts have survived is instructive: interbreeding, genetic clustering, morphological gap and ecological niche mostly co-vary, which is why the concept works, and they come apart at exactly the cases specialists argue about 44 45 46. De Queiroz's proposal is that the competing concepts agree on what a species is and differ only over which evidence delimits one 47, which is precisely what a cluster kind would predict.

There is a strong piece of evidence on the realist side of this that deserves more airing than it gets. Folk classifications of plants and animals, built independently in unrelated societies with no shared theory, converge to a remarkable degree at one particular level — roughly the generic rank — and that level corresponds fairly well to what trained taxonomists recognise 48 49. Convergence of that kind is hard to explain if the groupings are cultural all the way down. It is easy to explain if the world contains clusters at a certain grain and human perception is tuned to find them. Notice, though, what the finding does not show: the convergence is at one rank. Above and below it, folk schemes diverge freely, which is precisely what a cluster account predicts and a strong realism does not.

It is also worth noting how the disciplines that cannot avoid the problem handle it. Biological nomenclature is governed by codes that fix names, priority and types while saying nothing whatever about what a species is 50: the naming is stabilised and the concept is left open, which is a deliberate and rather elegant division of labour. Law does the opposite and has to. Hart's rule forbidding vehicles in the park is the standard case: the term has a settled core and an open penumbra, and the hard cases cannot be settled by looking harder at the word 51. Fuller's reply is that they cannot be settled without asking what the rule is for 52, and the subsequent literature has largely conceded the point while disputing how far it reaches 53 54. A judge cannot suspend judgement pending a better taxonomy. The purpose has to do the work, in public, on the record.

Wittgenstein's family resemblance is the same observation reached from the other direction 55, and the psychological literature on graded membership and prototypes is its experimental descendant 56 57 58 59. The important point for present purposes is that cluster categories are real, useful and unsuited to definition by necessary and sufficient conditions — and that a demand for such definitions is itself a purpose-laden choice, imported from contexts, such as law and formal ontology, where sharp edges are worth their cost 60 61.

2,2:4'a  [P:E;M'T]  ·  IVEighteen boundaries and who moved them

What follows are eighteen boundaries drawn by real institutions. Each plate records the line as currently drawn, the body that draws it, the purpose it serves, one coexisting partition of the same domain that does not nest inside it, and the dated occasions on which the line has moved.

The gauge on each plate places it on a six-rung ladder measuring how much of the boundary's placement is fixed by the world rather than chosen. The ladder is not a scale of respectability. A J0 category can be indispensable and a J5 category can be useless for a given job; the rung says how much freedom the classifier had, not how good the result is.

The revision record beneath each gauge is the empirical core of the article. It is one thing to argue that categories are made rather than found, and another to point at the meeting, the ballot and the date. Every plate below carries at least two.

GROUP I

Lines the world nearly draws for you

Boundaries where a discrete invariant does most of the work, and the remaining choice is small but never zero.

B01

An element is a proton count

THE LINE
Two atoms belong to the same chemical element exactly when their nuclei contain the same number of protons. Nothing else about the atom is consulted.
DRAWN BY
IUPAC, following Moseley's 1913 X-ray spectra, which showed that the elements order on an integer and not on atomic weight.
FOR
A chemistry in which composition is conserved through reaction, and in which the periodic table is a consequence of the ordering rather than a list to be memorised.
CROSS-CUT BY
The nuclide. Nuclear physics indexes on the pair (Z, N) and needs the neutron count that the chemical line throws away. Isotope separation is an entire industry conducted strictly inside one chemical class, and it works because the classes cross-cut.
MOVED
  • 1913Atomic number replaces atomic weight as the ordering principle; three known pair inversions stop being anomalies.
  • 1961The mass scale is re-based on carbon-12, harmonising the physical and chemical scales that had diverged.
  • 2009IUPAC begins replacing single atomic-weight values with intervals for elements whose isotopic composition varies measurably with the source of the sample.

This is the strongest case available, and even here the line has a purpose attached. It is drawn where it is because chemistry is the science of the electron shell, and the electron count follows the proton count. A discipline interested in nuclear binding draws it elsewhere and is not making a mistake. 98, 99, 100

B02

One is not a prime number

THE LINE
A prime is an integer greater than 1 divisible only by 1 and itself. The unit is excluded by fiat.
DRAWN BY
No committee. A convention that consolidated through nineteenth- and early twentieth-century practice and is now universal.
FOR
Unique factorisation, statable without an exception clause. If 1 were prime, every integer would have infinitely many factorisations and the fundamental theorem of arithmetic would need a proviso.
CROSS-CUT BY
Ring theory splits what elementary number theory bundles: unit, irreducible and prime become three distinct properties that coincide only in nice rings. On that partition, 1 is a unit, which is a positive classification rather than an exclusion.
MOVED
  • 1800Lists of primes routinely begin with 1 well into the nineteenth century.
  • 1900The exclusion consolidates as unique factorisation takes its modern form and the unit is given its own name.

Mathematics is where categories are supposed to be found rather than made, so it is worth noticing that the most famous definition in arithmetic contains a stipulation whose sole justification is that it makes a theorem come out clean. The mathematics is forced. Where the boundary is drawn around it is not. 6, 14

B03

The kilogram after 20 May 2019

THE LINE
The kilogram is defined by fixing the numerical value of the Planck constant at 6.62607015e-34 when expressed in J s. The platinum-iridium cylinder in Sevres is now an artefact with a measured mass like any other.
DRAWN BY
The General Conference on Weights and Measures, 26th meeting, Resolution 1 (2018), in force 20 May 2019.
FOR
A unit that does not drift, is not stored in one building, and can be realised independently anywhere by anyone with the apparatus.
CROSS-CUT BY
The practical mass scale. Every calibration chain in the world still runs through physical artefacts, so the metrological hierarchy of standards is a different partition of the same quantity, organised by traceability rather than by definition.
MOVED
  • 1889The kilogram becomes the mass of a specific cylinder.
  • 2019The definition moves to a fixed constant. The numerical value of h was chosen so that the new kilogram agreed with the old one to within the uncertainty of the comparison.

Nothing physical changed on 20 May 2019. What changed was which facts count as definitional and which count as measured, and the value of the defining constant was picked precisely so that no measurement would have to change. It is hard to find a cleaner demonstration that a boundary can be entirely conventional in its placement and entirely serious in its consequences. 101, 102

B04

Liquid and gas, above the critical point

THE LINE
Below the critical temperature a liquid and its vapour are separated by a first-order phase transition with a latent heat and a meniscus. Above it there is no transition, no meniscus and no boundary.
DRAWN BY
Classical thermodynamics; IUPAC nomenclature.
FOR
A description of matter in which distinct phases coexist, and in which boiling is an event.
CROSS-CUT BY
The Widom line, a crossover in the supercritical region traced by the maxima of response functions. It marks a change from liquid-like to gas-like behaviour, but its location depends on which response function is tracked, so it is a family of non-coinciding lines rather than one.
MOVED
  • 1869Andrews identifies the critical point of carbon dioxide and shows that the liquid-gas distinction has an endpoint.
  • 2010The Widom line is characterised experimentally as the supercritical crossover, restoring a boundary in the region where the phase boundary had vanished, on different terms.

Liquid and gas is the standard example of a joint in nature, and it is a real one. It also simply stops existing above 647 K and 22 MPa for water. A distinction can be perfectly objective over the range where it applies and have no application at all outside it, which is different from being false. 100, 103

B05

Left hand and right hand

THE LINE
Two molecules that are non-superimposable mirror images are distinct compounds. The distinction is exact and admits no intermediate case.
DRAWN BY
Physics, not a committee. The naming, however, is committee work: the Cahn-Ingold-Prelog rules assign R and S.
FOR
A chemistry that can predict interaction with other chiral things, which includes essentially all of biology.
CROSS-CUT BY
Three naming systems partition the same molecules differently and none nests inside another. R/S is assigned by substituent priority, D/L by configurational relation to glyceraldehyde, and (+)/(-) by the measured sign of optical rotation. A compound can be S, D and (+) at once, and the correspondences do not generalise.
MOVED
  • 1848Pasteur separates the two forms of tartrate by hand and establishes that the difference is real.
  • 1966CIP priority rules are formalised, replacing an inconsistent patchwork of local conventions.
  • 2013IUPAC consolidates the rules again in the preferred-name recommendations.

The distinction is as forced as anything in this article. The labels are not: three systems coexist, they were each built for a different purpose, and chemists carry all three. B06

A species, on any of about thirty definitions

THE LINE
A species is the smallest unit of a certain kind. Which kind is disputed: reproductive isolation, monophyly, ecological niche, genotypic cluster, diagnosability, and more.
DRAWN BY
No single authority. Nomenclature is governed by codes; the concept is not.
FOR
Counting, comparing and protecting biological units, and stating evolutionary theory in terms of the things that evolve.
CROSS-CUT BY
Rank. The Linnaean hierarchy imposes genus, family and order on a branching structure that has no natural ranks, which is why phylogenetic nomenclature abandons ranks entirely and names clades instead. The same organisms, a different partition, no nesting between the two schemes.
MOVED
  • 1942The biological species concept is codified around reproductive isolation.
  • 1997A survey counts twenty-two named species concepts in active use.
  • 2007A unified account proposes that the concepts agree on what a species is, a separately evolving metapopulation lineage, and differ only over which evidence delimits one.
  • 2020The PhyloCode is published with its companion volume, giving rank-free clade names formal standing.

This is the textbook case of a category that is neither arbitrary nor determinate. Interbreeding, genetic clustering and morphological gap mostly co-vary, which is why the concept works at all; they come apart at exactly the cases taxonomists argue about, which is why thirty definitions survive. B07

Planet, dwarf planet, and the neighbourhood

THE LINE
A planet orbits the Sun, is round under its own gravity, and has cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit. A body meeting the first two conditions but not the third is a dwarf planet.
DRAWN BY
The International Astronomical Union, Resolution B5, 24 August 2006.
FOR
A count of major bodies that does not grow without limit as survey telescopes improve. The vote followed the discovery of Eris, which was going to be either the tenth planet or the beginning of a long list.
CROSS-CUT BY
Composition. Rocky, gas giant, ice giant and icy body is a different and older partition, on which Pluto sits comfortably with the Kuiper belt objects and Earth sits with Mars. Neither partition nests inside the other, and the exoplanet literature mostly uses the compositional one because orbital clearing is hard to observe.
MOVED
  • 1801Ceres is announced as a planet and is counted as one for about fifty years.
  • 1930Pluto is discovered and counted as the ninth planet.
  • 2006The IAU adopts the clearing criterion; Pluto, Ceres and Eris become dwarf planets.
  • 2015A quantitative clearing discriminant is published, showing the eight planets separate from the dwarfs by several orders of magnitude rather than marginally.

The 2006 vote is usually told as a story about astronomers being arbitrary. The gap it tracks is real and large. What was arbitrary was not whether there is a discontinuity but that a discontinuity in dynamical dominance, rather than in composition or size, was chosen to carry the word. 112, 113, 114

THE LINE

2,2;6  [P;M]  ·  VOne thing, many files

The second claim was that a complex entity is generally not inside one category but participates in many conceptual structures at once. The plates show this from the side of the categories — every one of them cross-cuts something. This section takes it from the side of the entity, and asks what it costs to pretend otherwise.

Facets, and the price of ordering them

Ranganathan's answer, developed for library classification in the 1930s and still the most useful engineering treatment of the problem, is to stop building one hierarchy 62 63. A faceted scheme describes each item by several independent facets and composes an address from them instead of assigning a location in a tree. The item is then in as many places as it has facets, which is the correct number.

The cost of the alternative is calculable. The lab takes four facets over twenty-four engineered items — function with four values, lifecycle stage with three, location with three, regulatory regime with two, giving a 72-cell address space. Of the six pairs of facet partitions, zero nest inside one another: all six cross-cut, which is what independent facets means. Their common refinement has 24 blocks for 24 items, so together the facets individuate every item exactly, using twelve classes in total.

Now force them into a single tree. Any of the 4! = 24 orderings of the facets produces a hierarchy with the same twenty-four leaves, and the trees differ in every other respect. The number of internal nodes ranges from 23 to 36 depending only on the ordering. More consequentially, which items count as near neighbours changes: two orderings agree on the relation these two items are in the same branch at depth two for a mean of 84.90 per cent of item pairs and as little as 77.17 per cent in the worst case, and the number of item pairs grouped together at that depth ranges from 18 to 41 out of 276.

Nothing about the items changed. The classifier chose which facet to apply first, and that choice — which feels like an implementation detail — determined which items would be found together by anyone browsing the result. Alexander's point about cities is the same one about trees in general: the tree is a structure we can hold in our heads, and the overlap it forbids is where most of the life is 64.

What a single-parent tree cannot say

The sharper version of the cost appears when the underlying structure is already known to be a network. The lab builds a 40-node directed acyclic graph in which 15 nodes, 37.5 per cent, have more than one parent, and computes its transitive closure: 185 ancestor relations. There are 32,768 ways to keep one parent per node and obtain a tree. The best of them preserves 164 of the 185 relations, 88.6 per cent; the worst preserves 93, 50.3 per cent; the mean is 64.8 per cent. Every single-parent tree over this structure is lossy, and the best achievable loss is 11.4 per cent.

This is not a thought experiment. The Gene Ontology has been a directed acyclic graph rather than a tree since its first description, precisely so that a term can have several parents, and it carries distinct relation types because is a and part of are different relations that a single containment hierarchy would conflate 65 66. It remains a DAG in its current release 67. Biomedical ontology learned early that the cost of a tree is paid in inferences you can no longer draw.

Institutions that keep both structures

The most instructive engineering response is ICD-11's, described in full as plate B17. WHO maintains two structures. The Foundation is a network in which an entity may have several parents and categories need not be mutually exclusive: tuberculous meningitis is an infection and a brain disease, and the Foundation says both. A linearization is a subset derived from the Foundation in which every entity has exactly one parent, the categories are mutually exclusive, and the set is jointly exhaustive 68 69 70.

The reason for the second structure is stated plainly and is worth quoting in substance: single parenthood is required in a statistical classification so that a case is not counted twice. That is a purpose, and it generates a tree. Different purposes generate different linearizations — mortality, primary care, research — over the same Foundation, and postcoordination lets a code be assembled from several axes rather than looked up in one place. The 2026 release continues the arrangement 71.

The Foundation is for representing what is known. A linearization is for counting. WHO does not ask which one is the real classification, because the question has no work to do.

The same architecture appears wherever the problem has been taken seriously enough for long enough. IEC 81346 structures technical systems by aspect — function, product, location, and a type aspect added in the 2022 edition — with a separate reference-designation structure over the same objects for each 72. It is, in effect, a standardised refusal to have one parthood relation. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010, in its second edition, makes stakeholder perspectives and architecture aspects first-class elements of an architecture description, so that a system has many views by construction and correspondence rules relate them 73. The International Patent Classification assigns several symbols to one document and marks which is the invention information 74. In each case an institution that started with a tree and met reality has ended up with a network plus a set of purpose-labelled projections.

Psychiatry is running the same argument without having settled it. DSM-5-TR is a categorical system of disorders with thresholds 75; the Research Domain Criteria framework was introduced explicitly as a cross-cutting alternative, organising the same phenomena by dimensional constructs and units of analysis rather than by diagnosis 76. The two are not rival answers to one question so much as two partitions serving different purposes — clinical communication and reimbursement on one side, mechanistic research on the other — and the friction between them is what it looks like when a field has two purposes and has not yet admitted that they need two structures 36. Longino's study of research on aggression and sexuality makes the general case: five well-established approaches to the same behaviour measure variables that cannot be mapped onto one another, so the results cannot be pooled into one account no matter how much of each is accumulated 77.

The representational machinery for all of this is well developed and routinely unused. SKOS models a concept scheme as a poly-hierarchy directly, so broader and narrower are relations rather than containment 78. Top-level ontologies exist precisely to make the commitments explicit that an ad hoc hierarchy leaves implicit 79 80 61. And the oldest answer is still the cleanest: the relational model was motivated in part by the observation that hierarchical databases forced applications to depend on one particular access path through data that had many 81. Every generation of information systems has rediscovered that a tree is a query plan, not an ontology.

Philosophy has an argument for why this keeps happening. Simon's near-decomposability explains why hierarchies are so often approximately right and never exactly 82. Anderson's observation that more is different explains why higher-level categories are not eliminable in favour of lower-level ones 83, and the multiple-realisation argument gives the formal reason 84 85. Marr's three levels made the point operational for one science 86, and the levels literature has since made it general 87 88 89 90. A river is not a fundamental particle and is not thereby less real; it is a pattern at a scale, and the description that picks it out is answerable to what it is for 39 91.

2,3:7  [P:E]  ·  VIWhen a category earns its keep

If categories are made for purposes, there ought to be a way of asking whether a particular one is paying for itself. There is at least one, and it is computable.

A category is a compression claim

Dennett's criterion for a real pattern is that describing the data via the pattern, plus the exceptions to it, is shorter than describing the data directly 39. This is the minimum description length idea used as an ontological test 92 93 94 95, and it makes category membership an empirical matter without making it a metaphysical one.

The lab computes the crossover. A 64 by 64 field of 4,096 cells is described either literally, at one bit per cell, or by a coarse category — the cells inside this rectangle are 1, the rest are 0 — stated in four coordinates at 24 bits, plus an explicit list of exceptions at 12 bits each. The coarse description can afford 339 exceptions, 8.28 per cent of the field, before it stops being shorter than saying it cell by cell. Sweeping the noise rate confirms the crossover between 8 and 9 per cent.

The number itself is an artefact of the encoding; the shape is not. Every category is a bet that the regularity it names will hold often enough to pay for the exceptions, and the bet can be lost. When a clinician says a diagnostic label has become useless because every patient needs individual management anyway, the claim being made is exactly this one, and it is in principle checkable. So is the opposite claim, which is how a category earns promotion: if the exception list keeps shrinking as the mechanism is understood, the category is tracking something.

When the level of description is what is wrong

The last experiment is the one that changed how the rest of the article is framed. Construct a category over six features arranged in a ring: the members are the twelve pairs of features lying within two steps of each other, the non-members are the three pairs that sit opposite. All fifteen pairs are labelled, so nothing is left undecided, and the concept is exact.

Now try to find it from the feature table. No feature is held by every member. Every feature is held by exactly four of the twelve members and one of the three non-members, so no feature carries any marginal information. Every member has exactly two features and so does every non-member, so no count separates them. A prototype rule scoring distance from the feature-frequency centroid achieves a balanced accuracy of exactly 50.0 per cent. An exemplar rule scoring Hamming distance to the nearest known member 96 achieves exactly 50.0 per cent. Both are at chance, not approximately but identically, because the construction is symmetric.

A classical definition does exist: an exhaustive minimum-cover search finds one at four disjuncts of two literals each, and proves that none shorter exists. But it is four times the length of the obvious statement, and it explains nothing. The obvious statement is one clause: the two features lie within two steps of each other around the ring. It is exact, has no exceptions, and is not about the items at all. It is about a relation.

The failure here is not a shortage of evidence. Every fact about every item was available. The failure is that the description was pitched at the wrong level: a summary of items one at a time cannot recover a concept about how items stand to each other. This is the general form of a very common mistake. When a category resists definition, the usual conclusion is that it is vague, or socially constructed, or not a real kind. Sometimes the correct conclusion is that it is a perfectly sharp category at a level of description nobody has moved to yet 83 86 88.

It is also, incidentally, the strongest available argument against reading this article as deflationary. The concept in the experiment is not less real for being invisible to feature-based methods. It is exactly as real as the ring.

2.9  [P.S]  ·  IXWhat follows

The three claims can now be stated in a stronger form than the one they started in.

Reality contains phenomena and structures; our organisation of them into concepts and categories answers to purposes, perspectives, methods and institutions. Not because the world is silent — it constrains description constantly, and the constraint shows up as failure — but because constraint underdetermines partition. Watanabe's result makes this exact: similarity is constant over the unrestricted predicate set, so any grouping at all requires a prior selection of respects. Kleinberg's makes it exact from a second direction: three obviously desirable properties of a clustering cannot be had together, so a stopping rule is a decision rather than a discovery.

A complex entity is not inside one category. The eighteen plates cross-cut in eighteen different directions, and the cost of denying it is measurable: every single-parent tree over a modest 40-node network loses at least 11.4 per cent of its ancestor relations, and the facet ordering alone moves which items count as neighbours by up to 23 per cent of item pairs. The institutions that have run classification systems for longest — WHO, IEC, WIPO, the Gene Ontology consortium — have all converged on the same architecture: keep a network for what is known, generate trees for what is being done, and label each tree with its purpose.

Made is not the same as arbitrary. A category can fail by not supporting its inferences, by being unreliable, by proxying for something it has come loose from, by costing more than it returns, or by serving two purposes and satisfying neither. Those are five ways of being wrong that survive the loss of a unique correct taxonomy, and they are enough. What they have in common is that each is a mismatch between a category and a job, which is why the question worth asking is the one in the title.

What to do differently

Four practical consequences, in descending order of how often they are ignored.

  • Make the purpose part of the artefact. A taxonomy without a stated purpose cannot be evaluated, only preferred. ICD names what each linearization is for; NAICS states its four principles at the front of every revision notice, including the awkward one about time-series continuity 97; the IUCN criteria come with a published account of why the numbers are what they are 41. Most internal taxonomies come with nothing, and are then defended as though they had been discovered.
  • Record the revisions. The single most clarifying field on every plate in this article is the one listing when the line moved and who moved it. A classification with a visible revision history is one that nobody can mistake for a feature of the world; a classification without one invites exactly that mistake. This is cheap to do and almost never done.
  • Do not buy reliability and report it as validity. Kappa is substantially a property of the population's shape. A category can be coded consistently by two people who disagree about what it means, and on a well-separated population it will be. Reliability is a floor, not evidence 37.
  • When a category resists definition, check the level before checking the category. Some concepts are vague. Some are cluster kinds and should not be expected to have necessary and sufficient conditions 13. And some are perfectly sharp relations being described as though they were properties of items, in which case no amount of additional data about the items will help.

None of this is a counsel of despair about knowledge. It is closer to the opposite. The reason the tree keeps failing is that reality is richer than the tree, not poorer; the reason the categories keep moving is that they are being held to account by something. What the eighteen plates record is not a history of error but a history of maintenance — committees meeting, thresholds being revised, definitions being re-based on constants, a proxy being retired when it came loose from what it was proxying for. That is what answerable-to-a-purpose looks like when it is working.

The well is still not going to choose. The useful response is not to insist that one of its descriptions is the real one, nor to conclude that they are all equally good, but to ask of each: what is it for, what does it let us infer, what does it cost, and when did it last move.
