01The question this asks

The genre of the famous scientific blunder is comfortable, and its comfort is the problem. Kelvin got the age of the Earth wrong; Einstein put in a constant and took it out; Pauling proposed a triple helix. Each story arrives shaped as a moral about humility, and each is told as though the interesting quantity were the size of the error. It is not. Almost every claim in this collection was wrong by a wide margin, and the margins tell you nothing you did not already know.

A more useful question is one about time. For any claim that turned out to be false, there is a date on which the settling evidence became publicly available -- printed, indexed, in principle consultable by anyone who cared to look. There is a second date on which the relevant community actually reallocated its belief. The interval between those two dates is not a measure of anyone's intelligence. It is a measure of how long a correct answer can sit in the open without being read.

This article dates eighteen cases three ways. Claimed is when the false proposition was publicly asserted. Checkable is the earliest date at which a determined and competent person could have settled the matter from published material -- not the date the settling work was noticed, but the date it existed and was reachable. Settled is when the field moved. Subtracting gives two intervals. The first, checkable to settled, is the field clock. The second, from the claim to the point where the individual either conceded or died, is the personal clock. The article's thesis question is the first of these: how long did the answer sit unread?

Making the dates explicit does something a narrative cannot. It exposes a quantity the genre systematically hides -- what one might call the arrears on a claim: the gap between when it was asserted and when it had already been answered. Where that number is positive, the claimant was contradicting material already in print at the moment of speaking. Where it is negative, the claim was made in a world where no available evidence could have settled it, and calling it a blunder is a category mistake dressed up as a lesson. Five of the eighteen cases carry positive arrears. Eleven carry negative ones. The genre treats all sixteen identically.

The dating is a judgement in every case, and the judgement is contestable in several. Placing the checkable date for inheritance at 1866 assumes Mendel's paper is the settling document, which is defensible and not obligatory; placing it at 1900 with the rediscoveries would change the number by thirty-four years.[1, 2] Every such choice is stated in the plate it affects. The reader who disagrees with a date can move it and recompute; the point of publishing the ledger and the code together is that this costs nothing.

There is a literature on the question and it is older than one might expect. Barber catalogued the forms scientific resistance takes in 1961.[3] Stent asked in 1972 why some correct discoveries are ignored, proposing that a finding is premature when it cannot be connected to the canonical knowledge of its time -- Mendel being his central case.[4, 5] Swanson went further and showed that some answers are not merely unnoticed but already implicit in disjoint literatures that no one has read together, coining the phrase undiscovered public knowledge.[6, 7] What follows is an attempt to put dates and arithmetic on that tradition rather than to extend it argumentatively.

One finding, stated at the front because the article would otherwise appear to be building to it. The classification developed in the next section was designed on the expectation that stubbornness predicts delay: the more a person's error looked like a commitment held against evidence, the longer the world would take to correct it. That expectation is wrong, and the measurement says so with an unambiguous sign. The rank correlation between the classification and the field clock is strongly negative. The longest delays in this collection belong to cases in which nobody was being stubborn at all.

02A ladder of being wrong

The eighteen cases are sorted by a single criterion: what would have had to change for the claim to have been right. This is not a scale of severity and not a scale of blame. It is a scale of what the error was made of.

The bottom rung, E0, is a slip -- arithmetic, transcription, a misread dial. Correct it and the claim stands. E0 is empty in this collection, and its emptiness is informative rather than accidental. Slips are caught by the ordinary machinery of a working field within weeks, which is why none of them become famous. Every case that survives to be told as a story is made of something more durable than arithmetic. The genre selects against its own least interesting member.

E1 is evidence at hand: the settling material was published, reachable, and in two of the three cases here sitting in the claimant's own institution. E2 is evidence not yet made, where nothing then in existence could have decided the question. E3 is a method overreached -- a sound technique carried past the domain where its assumptions hold, which is the most technically interesting failure mode and the most common one here. E4 is a premise smuggled: an assumption that entered with the framing of the problem and was never labelled as an assumption. E5 is a commitment kept, held against evidence the surrounding community had already accepted.

The ordering has a property worth stating explicitly, because it is what makes the ladder testable rather than decorative. Moving up the rungs, the error becomes progressively less a matter of information and more a matter of what the claimant was unwilling or unable to give up. An E1 is a failure of reading. An E5 is a refusal. If the folk theory of scientific error is right -- if what delays correction is people digging in -- then the field clock should lengthen as the rungs rise.

Read the figure for the diamonds rather than the bars. A diamond marks the checkable date. Where it falls to the left of the start of the bar, the claim was asserted into a world that had already answered it. Five cases have that shape and they are not the cases the genre finds most interesting: the largest arrears in the set belong to von Neumann, who invoked an argument refuted two hundred and eleven years earlier, and to Tesla, whose objections to relativity postdate the eclipse result by thirteen years. The dramatic cases -- Darwin, Kelvin, Lowell -- all have the opposite shape.

Each of the eighteen plates that follow carries a gauge in this form: a claim marker in the rung colour, an outline diamond at the checkable date, a bar at settlement, the field interval hatched, and the personal interval as a line beneath. The gauges use a local time axis, so the printed span differs from plate to plate and the widths are not comparable across plates; the common-axis view above is the one to use for comparison.

03E1 -- the evidence was at hand

Three cases where the material that settles the question was already published and reachable, and in two of them physically present in the building. These are the cases the folk theory handles worst, because there is no stubbornness to point at. There is only a piece of information that failed to travel the last few metres.

P12

The triple helix

Linus Pauling / February 1953

E1
claim
Deoxyribonucleic acid is a three-stranded helix with the phosphate groups packed along the axis and the bases turned outward.
warrant
Two years earlier the same method -- build a physical model, satisfy the bond geometry, let the structure fall out -- had produced the alpha helix, the single most important structural result in protein chemistry.[8]
defeater
Phosphoric acid is a strong acid. At physiological pH the phosphate groups are ionised, so packing three of them along an axis puts a dozen like charges into contact with nothing to neutralise them. The chemistry is stated in Pauling's own textbook, published in 1939.[9]
residue
It set the clock. Watson and Crick understood they had weeks, not years, and worked accordingly.[10]
verdict
E1 — evidence at hand

Pauling and Corey submitted their nucleic-acid structure to the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences at the end of 1952.[11] It was wrong in three separate ways: the wrong number of chains, the wrong side out, and, most damagingly, not a chemically possible molecule. The phosphates at the core were un-ionised in the model, which is to say the model required a strong acid to behave as though it were not one.

The interesting thing is not that Pauling was wrong. It is where the correction was sitting. Not in Franklin's unpublished photographs, not in the King's College data he had been denied, not in any measurement he lacked. It was in The Nature of the Chemical Bond, which he had written.[9] Watson, reading the manuscript, saw it immediately, and his first reaction was disbelief that a chemist of that standing had let it through.[10] Judson's reconstruction and Olby's both land in the same place: the error was elementary, and it was elementary in Pauling's own specialism.[2, 12]

Hager's biography supplies the mechanism.[13] Pauling was working at speed against a competitor he could not see clearly, using a method that had just delivered a triumph. Speed is the wrong instrument for a chemistry check, and the alpha-helix success had made the model-building step feel like the whole of the work rather than the half of it that generates candidates. The correction interval here is fourteen years measured from his own book and about eleven weeks measured from publication, which is as close to instantaneous as this record gets: Watson and Crick's paper appeared in April.[14, 15, 16, 17]

P17

The primary mirror

Perkin-Elmer and NASA / 1981-1990

E1
claim
The Hubble primary mirror has been figured to specification.
warrant
It had been measured, to nanometre precision, by a purpose-built reflective null corrector -- the designated primary instrument, and the most accurate device available for the job.
defeater
Two other instruments, an inverse null corrector and a refractive null corrector, were used during fabrication and both indicated spherical aberration. Their readings disagreed with the primary and were discounted.[18]
residue
A generation of engineers who can recite it. The failure review is still the standard case for why redundant measurement without an escalation path buys nothing.
verdict
E1 — evidence at hand

The mirror was polished beautifully to the wrong shape. The reflective null corrector that guided the figuring had a lens spaced 1.3 mm from where it should have been, traced to an unintended reflection off a metering-rod end cap where non-reflective paint had flaked away.[18] That 1.3 mm propagated to about 2.2 micrometres of surface error at the mirror's edge and roughly 0.4 waves rms of wavefront error -- a $1.5 billion observatory delivering images no better than a good night on the ground.[19, 20, 21]

The 1.3 mm is not the interesting number. The interesting number is two: the count of independent instruments that measured the defect correctly, on the ground, before launch, and were overruled. The Allen Board found that the data revealing the error existed during fabrication and were not recognised or investigated.[18] Perkin-Elmer had bought redundancy in measurement and spent nothing on the willingness to act when the redundant measurements disagreed.

This is the one case in the eighteen where the claimant is an organisation rather than a person, and it is here deliberately. Strip out the individual and the structure of the error is unchanged: a designated authoritative source, dissenting evidence on file, and no mechanism that converts dissent into action. Vaughan's account of Challenger describes the same machinery producing the same result five years earlier at the same agency.[22, 23, 24, 25] The corrective optics flew in December 1993 and worked.[26]

The Hubble arithmetic in the figure is the general form of the E1 problem. Detection probability is the product of two terms -- whether a test fires and whether anyone acts on it -- and the second term is not a small correction to the first. With a hundred independent tests at a firing rate of seventy per cent and an acted-on probability of zero, the chance of catching the flaw is exactly zero. One test with a fifty per cent chance of being acted on gives thirty-five per cent. The programme did not lack tests. Two other null correctors indicated the problem.[18, 19] They were set aside on the grounds that the reflective null corrector was the more accurate instrument, which was true, and which is exactly the reasoning that makes a redundant check useless.

P06

The canals of Mars

Percival Lowell / 1894-1916

E1
claim
Mars is crossed by a network of artificial channels, built by an intelligent civilisation to move water from the poles.
warrant
Schiaparelli had reported linear markings in 1877 and better observers kept seeing them.[27] Lowell built an observatory at altitude specifically to see them better, and did.
defeater
Evans and Maunder, in 1903, showed schoolboys a disc bearing irregular markings and no lines at all, from a distance at the limit of resolution. The boys drew lines.[28]
residue
A working demonstration that an observation can be perfectly honest, perfectly repeatable, and entirely an artefact of the observer.
verdict
E1 — evidence at hand

Lowell mapped about 183 canals in 1895 and about 700 by 1908.[29, 30] He read the growth as progress. It is, on the arithmetic, exactly what you would expect from an artefact.

The experiment in this article's laboratory places features at random on a disc and counts how many fall into apparent straight lines within a stated tolerance. The count grows as roughly the cube of the number of features resolved: a fitted exponent of 3.10. Lowell's catalogue grew 3.8-fold between 1895 and 1908, which on that exponent needs only a 1.54-fold improvement in resolving power. A better telescope pointed through a worse method produces more of the artefact, and the growth reads from the inside as confirmation.

Maunder had said as much in print in 1903, with an experiment rather than an argument.[28] Wallace hammered the habitability case in 1907.[31] Antoniadi, using the larger Meudon refractor in 1909, resolved the linear features into disconnected detail and said so.[32] None of it moved Lowell, and more importantly none of it settled the question: canals appeared in serious astronomical writing for decades and were not finally killed until Mariner 4 returned photographs in 1965.[33, 34, 35, 36] Sixty-two years is the longest field latency in this collection, and it belongs to a case where the decisive experiment had already been done, published in the Monthly Notices, and required no equipment at all.

Lowell is the longest field clock in the collection at sixty-two years, and he is the reason the folk theory has to be abandoned rather than adjusted. Maunder and Evans ran the settling experiment in 1903: schoolboys at a distance, copying a disc with irregular markings, drew canals.[28, 31, 32] The paper was published in a journal any astronomer could read. What kept the canals alive for six further decades was not Lowell's obstinacy, which ended with his death in 1916, but the fact that a decisive result sat in the literature and was not treated as decisive by the people who could have applied it. The laboratory's dot-field model gives the reason the illusion was so stable: with alignments rising as the cube of the resolved feature count, a modest gain in seeing produces a large gain in canals, so every improvement in the instrument appeared to confirm the hypothesis.[34, 35, 37]

04E2 -- the evidence had not been made

One case, and it is the control. If a claim was made in a world that contained nothing capable of settling it, then whatever went wrong is not a defect of the claimant's reasoning, and the interval before correction measures the progress of instruments rather than the movement of minds.

P04

Inheritance without a mechanism

Charles Darwin / 1859-1868

E2
claim
Natural selection acts on heritable variation. (The mechanism of heredity is left open; where Darwin commits, he commits to blending.)
warrant
No theory of particulate inheritance existed in a form anyone could use. Blending was what breeders described and what the phenomena appeared to show.
defeater
Mendel's paper, published in 1866 in the proceedings of the Brunn natural history society.[38] Under particulate inheritance variance is conserved; under blending it is not.
residue
The gap itself became a research programme. Fisher closed it in 1918 by showing that Mendelian inheritance yields exactly the correlations biometry had measured.[39]
verdict
E2 — evidence not yet made

Fleeming Jenkin's 1867 review is usually remembered for a bad argument about a white man on an island. Its good argument is arithmetic.[40] If offspring are the average of their parents, then a novel favourable variant, mating into a normal population, contributes half its deviation to its children, a quarter to its grandchildren, and so on. The laboratory experiment for this article runs the numbers: the deviation is below one per cent of its original value by generation 6.6, and holding the population's variance steady requires half of it to be created fresh every generation.

That is not a gap in Darwin's theory. It is an incompatibility. Selection needs standing variation to work on, and the inheritance model Darwin leaned on destroys half of it per generation. Under particulate inheritance the same variant, with a selective advantage of one per cent, goes from a single copy in ten thousand individuals to near-fixation in 1,458 generations; under blending it is gone to a part in a million by generation twenty.

Darwin felt the force of it and reached for the wrong repair, raising the assumed rate at which new variation appears and edging toward use-inheritance in later editions and in Variation.[41, 42, 43] Bulmer has since argued the swamping objection was less lethal than the standard account holds, because Jenkin's own model of variation was too narrow.[44] What is not in dispute is that the answer existed, in print, from 1866, and did not reach the people who needed it.[1] Hardy and Weinberg supplied the conservation law in 1908.[45, 46] Fisher supplied the reconciliation in 1918.[39, 47, 48, 49] This is the only case in the eighteen where, at the moment the claim was made, no one on Earth could have settled it -- and it still took fifty-two years from the moment someone could.

The figure is the reason Darwin's difficulty was real rather than a failure of nerve. Under blending, offspring variance is halved every generation, so a novel favourable variant is diluted out of existence long before selection can act on it. Jenkin made the objection in 1867 and it was sound.[40, 43, 44] The laboratory quantifies what the theory needed: to hold variance steady against blending, fifty per cent of it must be manufactured fresh in each generation, which is to say that Darwin needed a mechanism producing variation at a rate no one had any reason to believe in. Under particulate inheritance the same beneficial allele, starting at a frequency of five in a hundred thousand with a selection coefficient of one per cent, reaches fixation in 1,458 generations. The variance does not leak, so the arithmetic works.

Darwin's own experiments were with snapdragons rather than peas, and they produced ratios he recorded without recognising what they were.[41, 50] Whether the checkable date should be 1866 or 1900 is the single most consequential dating decision in this ledger: Mendel's paper existed and was reachable from 1866, and by the criterion used throughout -- published and consultable, not noticed -- that is the date. It gives a field clock of fifty-two years, the second longest here. On the alternative dating it falls to eighteen. Both numbers are stated so the reader can choose.

05E3 -- a method carried past its domain

The largest group, and the one that should worry a working scientist most. In every case here the technique was sound, the execution was competent, and the error was in the extension: an assumption that held inside the tested regime was carried outside it without being re-examined. None of these are failures of rigour. Several are failures caused by rigour, in the sense that a well-validated method carries an authority that makes its boundary conditions harder to see.

P03

The age of the Earth

William Thomson, Lord Kelvin / 1862-1897

E3
claim
The Earth is between twenty and forty million years old, on thermodynamic grounds that geology is not free to ignore.
warrant
Fourier's heat equation, applied to a cooling solid, with a measured surface temperature gradient as the observable.[51, 52] The method is correct and the arithmetic is correct.
defeater
John Perry, in Nature in 1895, showed that a convecting interior decouples the surface gradient from the age entirely.[53]
residue
The calculation is still the textbook derivation for the thermal structure of oceanic lithosphere. It was never a bad method; it was a method applied to the wrong planet.
verdict
E3 — a method overreached

Everyone knows why Kelvin was wrong: he did not know about radioactivity. Everyone is mistaken. England, Molnar and Richter checked, and introducing the known distribution of radiogenic heating into Kelvin's calculation does not invalidate its conclusion.[54, 55] The laboratory experiment here reproduces that: at Kelvin's own inputs, radiogenic heating contributes 0.88 per cent of the surface gradient and moves the inferred age by 1.8 per cent, from 27.3 to 27.8 million years. It is not the correction that mattered. It is not within an order of magnitude of being the correction that mattered.

What mattered was Perry's, and Perry published it in 1895, before radioactivity was discovered.[53, 56, 57] Kelvin's model has the Earth cooling by conduction, so the surface gradient falls forever as the inverse square root of time and can therefore be inverted to give an age. Put a stirred interior beneath a conductive lid and the gradient asymptotes instead: the laboratory computation puts the saturation at about 46 million years, past which 100 million years and 4,500 million years predict the same measurement to within one per cent. Perry did not hand Kelvin a bigger number. He showed that the number was not measurable that way.

Kelvin replied, in the same volume, without engaging the substance.[58, 59, 60, 61] Perry's argument then sat unread for the better part of a century while radiometric dating supplied the right answer by another route.[62, 63, 64] England and his co-authors make the cost explicit: a working demonstration that the Earth's interior moves was available in 1895 and was overlooked, and it would have been a powerful argument for mobilism during the decades when continental drift was being dismissed.

This plate carries the correction that most needs making, because the received version of the story is itself an error of exactly the kind the article is about. Everyone knows that Kelvin was wrong because he did not know about radioactivity. He was not. England, Molnar and Richter put the known distribution of radiogenic heating into the calculation and found it changes the answer by under two per cent -- from 27.3 to 27.8 million years.[54, 55, 60, 65] What defeats the calculation is convection, and John Perry published that in Nature in 1895, before radioactivity was discovered.[53, 56, 57] Perry did not give Kelvin a larger number. He showed that the number was not obtainable from the measurement, which is a stronger and more uncomfortable result: past forty-six million years the surface gradient is within one per cent of its asymptote, and a hundred million years and four and a half billion are indistinguishable at the surface.

P07

The sixth place of decimals

Albert Michelson / 1894

E3
claim
The grand underlying principles of physical science are firmly established; future discoveries are to be looked for in the sixth place of decimals.
warrant
It was, in 1894, an accurate description of what the previous fifty years of physics had looked like. Michelson's own field, precision metrology, was exactly the discipline of the sixth decimal.
defeater
X-rays in 1895, radioactivity in 1896, the electron in 1897, the quantum in 1900.[66, 67, 68, 69]
residue
A permanent cautionary tale, and one that is routinely misattributed to Kelvin, who appears never to have said it.[70]
verdict
E3 — a method overreached

The remark comes from the dedication of the Ryerson Physical Laboratory in 1894.[71] Michelson hedged it -- he attributed the sentiment to 'an eminent physicist' -- and Badash's survey of the period found the attitude was less widespread among physicists than the anecdote suggests.[70] It is also, persistently, put in Kelvin's mouth, which is a small independent demonstration of how error propagates once a story is too good to check.

The error is a clean E3: an accurate extrapolation from a mature technique, carried past the domain where the technique's assumptions hold. Michelson's own 1887 experiment with Morley was already the anomaly that would break the framework, and he did not read it that way.[72, 73] The sixth decimal was where the new physics was hiding; the mistake was in thinking that was a statement about magnitude rather than about where the seams are.

The correction interval is the shortest of any E3 case in the set: ten years from Rontgen to special relativity. It is short because nobody was defending the claim. It was a mood rather than a position, and moods do not resist evidence.

P09

The fine-structure constant as an integer

Arthur Eddington / 1929-1946

E3
claim
The reciprocal of the fine-structure constant is exactly 136, derivable a priori from the number of independent components of a certain 16-element object. Later, exactly 137.
warrant
Eddington had used exactly this style of argument to real effect elsewhere, and the value was, at 1929 precision, not obviously inconsistent with an integer.[74]
defeater
Measurement. The modern value is 137.035999, and by the late 1930s the precision was sufficient to exclude any integer.[75]
residue
Kilmister's reconstruction argues there is a defensible programme buried in Fundamental Theory. Nobody has extracted it.[76]
verdict
E3 — a method overreached

Eddington derived 136 by counting: sixteen by seventeen over two.[77] When measurement drifted toward 137 he added one, and defended the addition. Cambridge gave him the nickname Adding-One Arthur and it has outlived the theory.[78]

The laboratory experiment here asks how hard the target actually was. A deliberately small grammar -- three atoms, two operations, no free parameters -- generates 22,736 distinct values between 1 and 1000, of which 305 fall in the fifteen-unit window around 137. At the five-figure precision available by the late 1930s that grammar produces no hits at all, which is worth stating against my own case: a claim pinned to five figures would have been a hard claim.

Eddington's was not pinned to five figures. It was pinned to an integer, and six of the sixteen integers in that window fall out of elementary counting arguments already. Then came the amendment. Admit one free additive constant, as the move from 136 to 137 did, and coverage of the interval goes to sixteen out of sixteen. At that point no measurement to integer precision can refute the programme, which is a different condition from being wrong.[79, 80] The look-elsewhere effect is now standard practice in particle physics precisely because this failure mode is not rare.[81, 82] Fundamental Theory was published posthumously in 1946.[83, 84]

The Eddington figure is offered against the case it was built to make. The search space is dense enough that agreement to one per cent is worth nothing -- sixty-five expressions hit that band, and six of the sixteen integers in the neighbourhood are reachable from a handful of elementary counting forms, with 136 reachable two different ways. Allow one free additive integer and the coverage is complete: every value in the range becomes derivable. But the enumeration also declines to convict. At the precision available when Eddington was writing, no expression in the space matches, which means the coincidence he was pursuing was not the cheap kind. The honest conclusion is that his method could not have distinguished a real relation from an artefact of a large search, and that this is a criticism of the method rather than a demonstration that the target was empty.[76, 78]

P11

Moonshine

Ernest Rutherford / 11 September 1933

E3
claim
Anyone who expects a source of power from the transformation of the atom is talking moonshine.
warrant
Entirely correct about the technique in front of him. Accelerator-driven transmutation consumed vastly more energy than it liberated, and Rutherford had the numbers.
defeater
Szilard conceived the neutron chain reaction the following day, reportedly while crossing Southampton Row, and patented it in 1934.[85, 86]
residue
The remark is now the standard illustration of extrapolating a constraint from a method to a phenomenon.
verdict
E3 — a method overreached

Rutherford's address to the British Association was reported in The Times on 12 September 1933.[87] Szilard read it, was irritated by it, and worked out the following day that a neutron-multiplying reaction would sidestep the objection entirely.[86, 88] The arrears on this plate are zero to within a day, which makes it the tightest case in the collection.

Jenkin's reconstruction is worth registering before the story is told as stupidity.[89] Rutherford was answering a specific question about a specific technique, and about that technique he was right; he also had good reason, in 1933, to want to damp public speculation about atomic power. The error is E3 and nothing worse: a correct energy balance for accelerator bombardment, generalised to a claim about the atom.

The chain of settlement runs Hahn and Strassmann in December 1938, Meitner and Frisch's interpretation in January 1939, and Fermi's pile in December 1942.[90, 91, 92] Rutherford died in 1937 and never saw any of it, which is why the personal clock on this plate stops at four years and carries no information about what he would have concluded.

P14

Vitamin C and cancer

Linus Pauling / 1970-1994

E3
claim
Ascorbic acid in gram quantities prevents and shortens colds, and in high doses substantially prolongs survival in advanced cancer.[93, 94]
warrant
A Nobel-level command of the chemistry of the molecule, plus observational survival data from Cameron's Scottish series.[95]
defeater
Two randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at the Mayo Clinic, in 1979 and 1985, both null.[96, 97]
residue
A partial vindication on the smaller claim: regular supplementation does shorten colds, by around eight to nine per cent.[98]
verdict
E3 — a method overreached

This plate needs its halves kept apart, because the popular version flattens them. On the common cold Pauling was partly right. Cochrane's synthesis finds that regular supplementation shortens episodes by roughly eight to nine per cent, with no effect on incidence in the general population -- a real effect, far smaller than he claimed, and one his critics also got wrong by denying it outright.[98, 99]

On cancer he was wrong, and the manner of the wrongness is the point. Cameron's series compared treated patients against historical controls matched after the fact.[94, 95] Moertel's group ran the randomised version twice, the second time restricted to patients who had received no prior chemotherapy to remove the objection Pauling had raised to the first.[96, 97] Both were null.

Pauling's response was not to revise. It was to reframe the trials as an institutional conspiracy and to attack Moertel's conduct.[13, 100] That is the transition worth marking, and it is why this plate sits at E3 with an E5 tail: the original error is a warrant carried across a boundary -- structural chemistry does not confer authority in clinical epidemiology -- and the fifteen-year sequel is something else.[101, 102] A man who had twice changed the shape of a field on the strength of evidence declined to let evidence change him once.

Pauling's vitamin C plate is the one where the received account is too harsh rather than too kind, and the distinction matters for the ladder. The common cold claim was partly vindicated: the Cochrane review finds no effect on incidence in the general population but a small consistent reduction in duration of about eight per cent in adults.[98, 103] The cancer claim was refuted, and refuted properly, by two randomised trials at the Mayo Clinic.[96, 97] What places this at E3 rather than E5 is that Pauling's method -- reasoning from molecular structure and dose-response to clinical effect -- was the method that had worked spectacularly for him twice before. It was carried into a domain where it does not substitute for a controlled trial, and his response to the trials was to dispute their conduct rather than their result.[13, 93]

06E4 -- a premise entered without being stated

Five cases in which the false step was not an inference but a framing. Something was assumed in the setting-up of the problem, carried through the reasoning untouched because it was never presented as a claim, and inherited by everyone who accepted the framing. These are hard to catch from inside because the premise is not asserted anywhere; it is the shape of the space the argument moves in.

P01

The cogito

Rene Descartes / 1637-1641

E4
claim
I think, therefore I am -- offered as a proposition that cannot be doubted and can therefore serve as the foundation for everything else.
warrant
The method of doubt is genuinely powerful, and the performative point underneath it is genuinely secure: the act of doubting cannot be performed by nothing.[104]
defeater
Gassendi's objections, printed in the same volume as the Meditations in 1641: the argument delivers a thinking, not a thinker.[105]
residue
Nearly four centuries of productive argument, and a permanently useful demonstration that grammar can do metaphysical work unnoticed.
verdict
E4 — a premise smuggled

The step from there is thinking to there is an I that thinks is made in the width of a comma.[106, 107] Nothing in the method of doubt licenses it. What licenses it is a feature of Indo-European syntax: verbs take subjects, so a thought must be somebody's.

Gassendi said so at once, in the fifth set of objections bound into the first edition.[105] Lichtenberg later made the point in four words -- one should say it thinks, as one says it is raining -- and Nietzsche turned it into an indictment, arguing that the 'I' is smuggled in as a grammatical habit and then presented as a discovery.[108, 109] Russell narrowed the surviving claim to the observation that there is thinking now, with no warrant for the continuity of any thinker across time.[110] Kant had separately removed the second half's footing by denying that existence is a predicate.[111]

The plate is here because it is the cleanest instance of the E4 pattern and because its arrears are unusual: the defeater was printed in the same book as the claim, at the author's invitation. Descartes solicited the objections, published them, and replied to them. The premise was named to his face and survived anyway, which suggests that naming a premise is a weaker intervention than one would hope.[112, 113, 114] This is the one case in the eighteen with no settlement date, and it is not clear it should have one.

P05

Atoms as bookkeeping

Ernst Mach / 1883-1916

E4
claim
Atoms are a useful economy of thought, not entities. Physics should confine itself to relations among sensations.
warrant
A principled and historically productive position. Mach's critique of absolute space on exactly these grounds was one of the inputs to general relativity.[115]
defeater
Einstein's 1905 analysis of Brownian motion and Perrin's measurements, which extracted Avogadro's number by thirteen independent routes that agreed.[116, 117]
residue
The demand that theoretical terms earn their keep, which is the useful residue of positivism and outlived the doctrine.
verdict
E4 — a premise smuggled

Mach's objection was not that atoms were improbable. It was that they were the wrong kind of thing to put in a physical theory: unobservable, and therefore a bookkeeping device that should not be mistaken for furniture.[115, 118, 119] The premise doing the work is a criterion for what counts as physical, and it entered with the framing rather than as a claim to be argued.

Perrin's programme is what makes this plate a clean case rather than a philosophical stalemate. He did not argue that atoms exist; he measured Avogadro's number thirteen different ways -- Brownian displacement, rotation, sedimentation equilibrium, viscosity, blackbody radiation, radioactive decay -- and the numbers agreed.[117, 120, 121] An unobservable that thirteen independent instruments concur on has stopped being a bookkeeping device by any criterion worth holding.

The field moved in about five years. Mach did not, and there is a document: his 1910 statement of position, written after Perrin, restating the criterion.[118, 122] The gap between the two clocks on this plate is modest but its shape is the one that recurs -- a field that reallocates quickly and an individual for whom the disputed claim is load-bearing for a larger commitment, and who therefore cannot trade it in isolation.

P10

The cosmological constant

Albert Einstein / 1917-1931

E4
claim
A constant term must be added to the field equations so that the universe comes out static.[123]
warrant
Every astronomer in 1917 believed the universe was static. The Milky Way was the universe, and it was not observed to be flying apart.
defeater
Friedmann's 1922 non-static solutions, followed by Lemaitre in 1927 and Hubble's velocity-distance relation in 1929.[124, 125, 126]
residue
The term is back, and is now how we write down dark energy. It was reinstated in 1998 on evidence Einstein could not have had.[127, 128]
verdict
E4 — a premise smuggled

The premise was not in the physics. General relativity did not want a static universe; Einstein put the constant in to force one, because staticity was what everybody knew.[123, 129] That is the definition of an E4: an assumption imported from the surrounding consensus, installed in the mathematics, and never labelled as an assumption.

Friedmann's solutions arrived in 1922 and Einstein's first response was a note asserting an error in them; he withdrew it the following year, conceding the solutions were correct while continuing to doubt they meant anything.[124, 130, 131] He abandoned the constant in 1931.[132] Nine years from the first correct non-static solution to acceptance, with the acceptance arriving only once the observational case was unavoidable -- and, as O'Raifeartaigh and colleagues discovered, Einstein spent part of the interval drafting a steady-state model of his own before abandoning that too.[133]

One caution about the story everyone tells. The phrase 'greatest blunder' is attested only through Gamow's memoir, and no first-hand source in Einstein's own hand has been produced.[134, 135] It is also, taken at face value, wrong twice over: the constant is a permitted term in the theory, it is now measured to be non-zero, and the actual error was never the constant but the unexamined premise it was installed to satisfy.[136]

P15

The wager, taken at face value

John von Neumann / 1955-1957

E4
claim
Pascal's Wager is sound: the asymmetry between finite cost and infinite reward makes belief the rational choice.
warrant
The dominance structure is genuinely compelling if the decision table has two rows, and von Neumann was dying.[137]
defeater
The many-gods objection, published by Diderot in 1746: the same argument runs for every rival deity and therefore selects none.[138, 139]
residue
The most-taught worked example of what goes wrong when infinite utilities enter a decision problem.
verdict
E4 — a premise smuggled

In 1955 von Neumann was diagnosed with cancer. At Walter Reed he asked for a Catholic priest and, according to those present, justified the request by the wager.[140, 141, 142] Colleagues who had known him as an agnostic for decades doubted the conversion had gone deeper than the calculation, and by his own account it was a calculation.

The calculation does not work, and its author had written the reason. The wager needs a partition containing exactly one god-hypothesis. Admit a second and the laboratory table shows every option carrying identical expected utility: the argument says wager, and cannot say on what.[138, 143, 144] Worse, with a genuinely infinite prize, any practice yielding belief with probability epsilon greater than zero also has infinite expected utility, so a one-in-a-trillion gesture ranks equal with a monastic life.[139, 145, 146]

And underneath both: the continuity axiom in Theory of Games and Economic Behavior requires that for any A better than B better than C there be some probability p making a p-weighted lottery on A and C indifferent to B.[147] An infinite A admits no such p. The wager is not merely defeated by an objection from 1746; it is inconsistent with the axioms of expected utility that von Neumann had set down thirteen years earlier. He is the only person in this collection whose defeater he wrote himself, and the arrears -- 211 years from Diderot -- are the largest in the set.

The wager figure states the objection in its own currency. Set the prize at a trillion and the cost at one, and with a single hypothesis the calculation runs as Pascal intended. Add a second and the table becomes flat: a k-way tie in which the argument still says wager and can no longer say on what. Then the third column, which is the one that finishes it -- a mixed strategy putting a billionth of a unit of probability on belief still carries infinite expected utility, so the argument cannot distinguish a lifetime of devotion from a passing thought.[139, 146] That the axioms ruling out this behaviour appear in von Neumann's own 1944 book is the detail that makes this the strangest plate in the collection.[147]

P16

The population bomb

Paul Ehrlich / 1968-

E4
claim
The battle to feed all of humanity is over; hundreds of millions will starve in the 1970s.[148]
warrant
Population growth was genuinely accelerating, and the extrapolation of yields against mouths was arithmetically correct given the yields assumed.
defeater
Semi-dwarf cereal varieties, already being released in Mexico and South Asia when the book went to press.[149, 150]
residue
Real and lasting attention to resource limits and to the environmental cost of intensification, most of which survives the failed forecast.
verdict
E4 — a premise smuggled

The hidden premise is that food supply is a stock rather than a function of effort. Treat yields as fixed and the arithmetic is unanswerable; treat them as responsive and the whole forecast is contingent on a term Ehrlich did not model. Borlaug's semi-dwarf wheat was already in Indian and Pakistani fields when the book appeared.[149, 150, 151] The arrears are positive: the answer was in the ground.

The 1980 wager with Julian Simon is the part everyone remembers, and it deserves more care than it usually gets.[152, 153] Ehrlich chose five metals, $200 on each; all five fell in real terms and he sent a cheque for $576.07 in 1990.[154] But the bet was a poor instrument for the question. Ritchie's reconstruction of every decade since 1900 finds Simon and Ehrlich would have split the wins roughly evenly, and Emmett and Grabowski reach the same conclusion by a different route.[155, 156, 157, 158] Simon won a ten-year window on a claim both men had framed as a long-run one.

So Ehrlich was wrong, and the famous demonstration that he was wrong does not establish it. What establishes it is the forecast itself: the mass famines of the 1970s did not happen and per-capita food supply rose. He has restated the core position repeatedly in the decades since.[159] The personal clock on this plate is the longest in the collection at fifty-five years and is a floor rather than a total. Connelly's history is the necessary companion volume on what coercive population policy did with the authority these forecasts conferred.[160]

07E5 -- a commitment kept

Four cases of the thing everyone thinks the whole subject is about: a position held after the community had accepted the evidence against it. This is the rung the folk theory treats as the paradigm, and the ledger will show that it is the rung with the shortest field clocks. When a single identifiable person is holding out against a settled field, the field has by definition already settled.

P02

South Sea stock

Isaac Newton / 1720

E5
claim
Implicitly: that South Sea stock at 700 was worth buying with essentially the whole portfolio, having sold the same stock at 350 weeks earlier.
warrant
Newton was an experienced and successful investor, personally active in the London market, and better placed than almost anyone to judge the company's relationship with the public debt.[161]
defeater
His own judgement of April 1720, when he sold. Nothing changed about the company between April and June except the price.
residue
The single most-cited example of a first-rate mind failing at position sizing, and now, thanks to the archive, a well-documented one.
verdict
E5 — a commitment kept

The familiar story -- sold early for a £7,000 profit, bought back at the top, lost £20,000, forbade the words 'South Sea' in his presence -- rests on two anecdotes recorded a generation or more after his death and seven quantitative items.[162, 163, 164, 165] Odlyzko went to the Bank of England ledgers, the London Metropolitan Archives and the accounts of an estate Newton executed, and found the folk tale substantially correct and probably understated.[161, 166, 167, 168]

The corrected numbers are worse for Newton, not better. The April-May liquidation produced a profit nearer £20,000 than the £7,000 usually quoted. He then put the proceeds back into the same security at roughly double the price, converted his remaining government securities into it, and subscribed for more in the Fourth Money Subscription on 24 August, taking the maximum a single investor was allowed -- weeks before the collapse. In mid-September, acting for an estate, he was still buying as the price fell through 520, 405 and 395.

The laboratory reconstruction separates the two errors. Terminal wealth is exactly linear in the fraction re-committed at 700: every pound returned about 25 pence, dividends included. Under log-optimal sizing, committing everything requires about 90 per cent confidence in a further doubling; a coin-flip belief justifies 11 per cent.[169, 170] Being wrong about direction cost him a fraction. Being wrong about size cost him the rest. And the counterfactual that stings is the null one: doing nothing at all for the whole of 1720 was profitable.

The famous quotation, incidentally, is not quite his. The earliest version, from Spence's memorandum book via Lord Radnor, has Newton saying only that he could not calculate the madness of the people.[161, 162] The heavenly bodies were added later by someone with a better ear.

The Newton figure separates two errors that the anecdote merges. The directional call -- that the stock would rise from 700 -- was wrong, and being wrong about direction is survivable at any sensible position size. The sizing was the ruinous part. Terminal wealth is linear in the fraction re-committed, so each pound put back returned about twenty-five pence; committing the whole portfolio converted a bad call into a catastrophe. Under log-optimal sizing a coin-flip belief in a further doubling justifies eleven per cent of the portfolio.[169, 170] Newton committed everything, then subscribed for more. The null strategy -- holding the original position through the entire episode and collecting the dividends -- ends the year in profit.

P08

Relativity rejected

Nikola Tesla / 1919-1943

E5
claim
Relativity is error dressed in mathematics; space is a void and cannot be curved; the electron does not exist; cosmic rays travel at many times the speed of light.[171, 172]
warrant
None that survives 1919. This is the distinguishing feature of the plate.
defeater
The 1919 eclipse expedition, and, for the electron, Thomson's 1897 measurement.[68, 173]
residue
Nothing in physics. In engineering, a great deal that has nothing to do with these claims.
verdict
E5 — a commitment kept

Tesla's polyphase system is one of the most consequential pieces of engineering of the nineteenth century. His statements about theoretical physics from the 1930s are of a different order, and the arrears are what mark them: his objections to relativity postdate the eclipse result by thirteen years, and his rejection of the electron postdates Thomson's measurement by thirty-five.[174, 175] This is the only kind of case where 'he should have known' is straightforwardly true at the moment of speaking.

His stated objection was philosophical: space is a void and a void can have no properties, so curvature is a category error.[171, 172, 176] That is a position, and it is the position of an ether theorist who has not accepted that the ether was removed. He also claimed to have measured cosmic rays at fifty times the speed of light, which was never replicated and which no one has been able to reconstruct from his notes.

Wardenclyffe belongs here as the practical face of the same commitment. The wireless power scheme depended on conduction through the Earth in a way the physics does not support, and it consumed his backing and his reputation.[174, 177] The field's clock on this plate reads zero, because the field had settled the question before he opened it. The personal clock reads eleven years and stops only at his death.

P13

The steady state

Fred Hoyle / 1948-2001

E5
claim
The universe has no beginning. Matter is created continuously at a rate that holds mean density constant as space expands.[178]
warrant
A genuine and elegant theory, developed with Bondi and Gold, making sharp testable predictions -- which is more than the rival could say in 1948.[179]
defeater
The cosmic microwave background, measured by Penzias and Wilson and interpreted by Dicke and colleagues in the companion paper.[180, 181]
residue
Stellar nucleosynthesis. The steady-state model had to make the elements in stars because it had no early universe to make them in, and B2FH is the result.[182, 183]
verdict
E5 — a commitment kept

Two corrections to the version of this story that circulates. First, the steady-state theory was good science: it forbade things, it could be killed by observation, and it was.[178, 179, 184] Second, the claim that Hoyle coined 'big bang' to ridicule his opponents is disputed by the historian who has looked hardest at it. Kragh traces the term to a BBC Third Programme broadcast on 28 March 1949 and argues Hoyle did not intend it derisively -- he used it again in The Nature of the Universe in ways that read as descriptive, and the phrase took nearly twenty years to enter general use.[185, 186, 187, 188] The irony everyone enjoys is partly manufactured.

What is not disputed is the shape of the two clocks. The field took about two years from the microwave background to consensus -- one of the fastest turns in the collection. Hoyle took the remaining thirty-six years of his life and never arrived, developing a quasi-steady-state model with Burbidge and Narlikar into the 1990s.[189]

The residue is the reason this plate is not simply a story about stubbornness. Because the steady state had no hot early universe, Hoyle needed the heavy elements to be built inside stars, and the programme that produced that answer is the foundation of stellar nucleosynthesis.[182, 183] A wrong theory generated a correct and permanent body of work by forcing a question its rival did not have to ask. That is an argument for tolerating wrong theories, and it is a considerably better argument than the one about lone geniuses being vindicated.

P18

New Madrid, 3 December 1990

Iben Browning / 1989-1990

E5
claim
A magnitude 6.5 to 7.0 earthquake will strike the New Madrid seismic zone within about forty-eight hours of 3 December 1990, at odds of about fifty-fifty, triggered by tidal forces.
warrant
Almost none. The tidal-triggering hypothesis had no support, and Browning's climatology credential was self-conferred; his doctorate was in zoology.[190]
defeater
The National Earthquake Prediction Evaluation Council reviewed the claim in October 1990 and found no scientific basis.[190]
residue
A well-documented natural experiment in how a prediction with no technical content acquires the authority of one.
verdict
E5 — a commitment kept

This plate tests whether the word 'genius' is doing any work in the phrase 'geniuses who were wrong'. Browning was not one, and the case is instructive precisely because the mechanism did not need him to be.

The chain is documented in USGS Circular 1083, a 248-page account of the episode.[190] A regional newspaper reported in November 1989 that a scientist who had correctly predicted the Loma Prieta earthquake was forecasting one on the New Madrid fault. He had not predicted Loma Prieta. The credential was misdescribed, the misdescription was repeated, and each repetition became the warrant for the next. Schools closed, insurance sales rose, families left Memphis.[191]

Seismologists were slow to engage in public, partly on the reasonable-sounding ground that rebutting the claim would dignify it -- a judgement that in this case left the field clear for a year. What settled it was the third of December. The underlying science had been settled long before and has been restated since: earth tides show no useful correlation with earthquake occurrence, and prediction at this specificity is not currently possible.[192, 193, 194, 195, 196] The field clock here reads six weeks and the personal clock stops at Browning's death seven months later. The expensive quantity was neither: it was the year of public credence in between, which no clock in this ledger measures.

08The two clocks disagree

The ledger is now complete and it does not say what it was built to say. Seventeen of the eighteen field clocks are closed. Their mean is 14.7 years and their median is 9.0, which means the distribution is dragged by a small number of very long delays rather than being uniformly slow. The personal clocks have a mean of 12.2 years and a median of 9.3 -- close enough to the field figures that a reader glancing at the summary statistics would conclude the two quantities were measuring the same thing.

They are not measuring the same thing. The rank correlation between the rung and the field clock is -0.77, a strong relationship with the opposite sign to the one predicted. The correlation between the rung and the personal clock is -0.10, which is no relationship at all. This is worth stating precisely, because the temptation is to describe it as a reversal and it is not one: the ladder predicts one clock strongly and in the opposite direction, and fails to predict the other in either direction.

The three longest field clocks are Lowell's canals at sixty-two years, Darwin's missing mechanism at fifty-two, and Kelvin's Earth at thirty-six. Two of those are E1 and E2, the two rungs where nobody was holding out against anything. Maunder's experiment was published, correct, and available for sixty-two years before the canals died -- and Lowell, the supposedly stubborn party, had been dead for forty-nine of them. Mendel's paper was in print for thirty-four years before the rediscoveries and fifty-two before the synthesis; Darwin had been dead for thirty-six of those. In neither case can a person's obstinacy be the explanation, because in neither case was the relevant person alive for most of the delay.

Meanwhile the E5 cases -- the commitments kept, the rung the genre is really about -- have field clocks of zero, two, and six weeks. Tesla's is zero because the field had settled relativity thirteen years before he began objecting. Hoyle's is two years from the microwave background to consensus, one of the fastest turns in the collection, while his own clock ran another thirty-six to his death. The widest gaps between the two clocks in the whole ledger belong to Hoyle at 34.6 years, Ehrlich at 28.0 and Tesla at 11.0, and in all three cases the field is the fast one.

Which suggests the following reading, offered as an interpretation of the measurement rather than a result of it. A famous holdout is evidence that the question has been settled. Being able to name the person who is wrong requires a background against which they stand out, and that background is a field that has already moved. Planck's remark about science advancing one funeral at a time gets quoted as though it described the general case.[197, 198] Azoulay and colleagues tested it directly, tracking publication in fields after the unexpected death of a star scientist, and found the effect is real but narrow -- outsiders enter and the subfield's output shifts, which is a story about gatekeeping and resource allocation rather than about obstinate belief.[199, 200, 201]

The expensive failure has a different shape and no protagonist. Someone publishes the answer. It is correct, it is indexed, and it is not connected to the question it settles, because the people holding the question do not read that journal or do not recognise the result as bearing on their problem.[4, 6, 202] Nobody is being stubborn. There is no one to name. The delay is measured in decades rather than years, and the story is unwritable in the genre this article belongs to, because a story needs someone to be wrong in it and in these cases nobody is.

The arrears column supports the same reading from the other direction. Five claims here were asserted after their answers were published, and the largest arrears -- von Neumann's 211 years, Tesla's thirteen, Pauling's fourteen on the triple helix -- belong overwhelmingly to the top of the ladder. Eleven claims were made when nothing could have settled them. Of the eighteen protagonists, five accepted the correction in their lifetimes, ten did not, and three cases have no meaningful answer to that question. That last distribution is the one the genre reports, and on the evidence here it is the least informative number in the ledger.[203, 204]

09The laboratory

Every number in the prose and in the figures comes from the transcript below. It is the verbatim output of a single stdlib-only Python program, run once at build time with a fixed seed, containing eight experiments: the variance arithmetic of blending inheritance, Kelvin's cooling half-space against a stirred interior, the dot-field model of the Martian canals, an exhaustive enumeration of the numerological search space around the fine-structure constant, Newton's 1720 position-sizing surface, the wager as a decision table, the Hubble figure error and its detection probability, and the ledger itself.

Two of the eight returned results contrary to the hypothesis they were written to test, and both are reported as they came out. The enumeration in L4 was built to show that Eddington's coincidence was cheap and instead found that at his working precision the search space is empty. The ledger in L8 was built on the expectation that the rungs would predict the field clock positively and found a strong negative relation. Neither the code nor the conclusions were adjusted afterwards; the argument in section eight is the one the measurement forced.

10What the interval is for

The eighteen cases will not support a moral about humility, and the attempt to extract one is what makes the genre useless. Kelvin was not arrogant; he was using the best available physics on a problem whose governing mechanism had not been identified, and the man who identified it was ignored for a decade himself.[53, 54, 55] Darwin was not credulous; he was missing a fact about the world that nobody in his circle possessed. Pauling's triple helix was a fortnight's error by someone who had been right about almost everything else, and the correction cost him nothing except the structure.

What the cases will support is a claim about where correction time actually goes. On this evidence it is not spent overcoming resistance. It is spent in the interval between an answer being published and an answer being read by the people who need it -- a period in which no one is doing anything wrong, no one is identifiable as the obstacle, and the delay is invisible precisely because it has no protagonist. Sixty-two years for Maunder's schoolboys. Fifty-two for Mendel's snapdragons and peas. Thirty-six for Perry's convection. These are the expensive numbers in the ledger and none of them belongs to a story about a stubborn genius.

The practical consequence is unglamorous and it is not about individual epistemic virtue. If the dominant cost is unread answers, then the interventions that matter are the ones that move results between communities: indexing, review articles that cross subfield boundaries, replication of decisive experiments in venues the relevant field actually reads, and taking seriously the possibility that the answer to a live question is already in print somewhere else.[3, 5, 7] None of these produce an anecdote. All of them attack the part of the distribution where the years are.

And the reason to keep the ledger rather than the stories is that the ledger can be wrong in a way the stories cannot. Every date here is a judgement, several are contestable, and the two that most affect the result -- Mendel at 1866 or 1900, Maunder at 1903 or later -- are flagged in their plates. Move them and the correlation moves. That is the point. A story about Kelvin's arrogance cannot be checked by anybody; a claim that the field clock ran thirty-six years from Perry's paper to Holmes's dating can be checked by anyone willing to argue about the dates, which is the only kind of claim worth publishing about a question this soft.

The question at the top of this article was how long the answer sat unread. For the seventeen closed cases the median is nine years and the mean is 14.7, and the mean is the larger number because of a handful of cases in which a correct, published, entirely available result went unconnected to the problem it solved for half a century. The resistance interval, in the end, is mostly not resistance.
